## E01 PLABA-EVAL: Paraphrasing and Sentence-by-Sentence Self-Assessment

- **Design.** English biomedical abstract and vernacular versions, each rated by three different adult readers. Readers may view the full text and rate their perceived understanding of each sentence on a 1–5 scale, with 5 being highest. [Original §§3, 4.3, Fig. 5, p.54](https://aclanthology.org/2026.readi-1.4.pdf#page=6)
- **Literature observation.** Under 39 source abstracts, there are 265 original sentence pairs with alignment positions for vernacular clusters. First, average each sentence's reader score, split into multi-sentence versions, then equal-weight average the sentence scores, and finally equal-weight average the vernacular version minus the original version at the alignment positions. The paper reports a self-assessed understanding mean difference of +0.72 points, median +⅔ points, and does not report the confidence interval for this mean difference. [§4.3, Fig. 5](https://aclanthology.org/2026.readi-1.4.pdf#page=6)
- **Data audit.** Filter the pinned `annotations.csv` by `included_in_paper_sentence_analysis` and aggregate by `base_text_id`, `anchor_id`, `variant`, and `sentence_uid`. The recomputed effect is **+0.723 points**. There are 220 distinct reader IDs across 234 released reader-by-document ratings. Of those IDs, 13 appear on multiple documents and 9 span folds in the existing five-fold source-article split. That experiment evaluates unseen source articles. For joint validation on unseen articles and readers, link source articles by shared readers within the included aligned rating rows. This yields 25 connected groups for a new split. [Pinned data revision, CC BY 4.0](https://github.com/SDS-NLP/PLABA-EVAL/tree/153082e7d58b833d5e2f481b92fdcf9248c60d11) · [Original development folds](../../research/results/plaba-eval-metrics.json)
- **CogLLM utility.** Evaluated using raw sentence-level scoring and full-text visible readout training. Variants are merged and split. diagnostic comparisons derived from the same critic score are performed, and open-book responses are recorded. Full-text visible sentence-level results support the App's first-iteration sentence marking.

## E02 Zhan et al. Re-mentioned objects under free prompting

- **Design.** 50 Chinese-speaking participants each viewed 36 target items and 36 filler stimuli, and then wrote a sentence that naturally continued the first sentence according to instructions. Free prompts were not provided to continue the sentence with the pronoun. In IC-1 and IC-2, verbs were biased toward mentioning the original subject and object in separate pilot studies. Of 1,800 target trials, 1,651 were coded and included based on the author's reference and expression form. the public table presents the data after inclusion. [Experiment 1, Methods, Coding, Results](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0237012#sec008)
- **Observation and reanalysis.** Under free-listing, the proportion of readers re-mentioning the original subject under equal weighting was IC-1 **77.68%**, IC-2 **11.72%**, a difference of **65.96 percentage points**. Ratios were averaged across readers per condition at the `reMention == 'np1'` level using equal weighting, with 50 readers per group and 350, 430 valid trials per group. Standard errors were 3.25, 1.99 percentage points. The paper reports a likelihood ratio test of the verb type in a mixed-effects logistic model `p < .001`. [Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0237012.g001) · [CSV](https://osf.io/download/8zk57/) · [author analysis script](https://osf.io/download/tw5sd/)
- **Data and usage.** CSV SHA-256 is `cd04e554aea959fd075564ea432a6ccf3bbd6e88bf2fbdabc3f8abeb6b3ff4ef`, and each valid `participantID × itemNum` is unique. The same continuation instruction can be used to refer to the object as a reference auxiliary endpoint. The observation scope is limited to continuation and mention, with accuracy and question location not measured. Experiment 1 is already a development diagnosis, and Experiments 2–3 are recorded from the same material family. [OSF Project and License](https://api.osf.io/v2/nodes/t87my/) · [Stimuli](https://osf.io/download/q7bwn/)

## E03 Gu et al. explain the formulation of closed-book understanding

- **Design.** Study 1 presented English medical passages from Wikipedia and PubMed to adult participants on MTurk, with each participant reading eight passages and viewing only one version per source. After reading, participants left the main text and answered three content-based multiple-choice questions, one of which asked for the target term. The analysis excluded trials with high familiarity post-readout and the no-issue baseline condition. [Study 1, Design, Procedure, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5977593/)
- **Literature observation.** 260 reader×fragment pairs, with 132 pairs explained in plain language and 128 pairs defined technically. The average condition score across eight factor combinations under equal weighting was 72.1% for plain language and 62.6% for technical definition, a difference of +9.5 percentage points, `F(1,244) = 6.501, p < .011`. The paper did not provide confidence intervals for this comparison. [Study 1, Table 3 and Actual Difficulty](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5977593/)
- **Data and usage.** When full materials are available, compare rewrite effects under identical comprehension questions, with the single reader's query position unobserved. Partial stimuli and tables have been identified. Complete sequential files, stimuli, and reuse conditions have not yet been verified. R08 reanalyzed the same Study 1 materials and responses with a different filtering approach, merging the two papers into a single source family. [R07 Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5977593/) · [R08 §§3.1, 4.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12243615/)

## E04 Ozuru et al. Reading ability, knowledge category, and connectivity

- **Design.** 170 English university students, with biology and psychology courses serving as the high- and low-domain knowledge groups, respectively. Each participant read one high-connectivity and one low-connectivity text, with topics and order counterbalanced. Participants were stratified by pre-measured reading ability and then took a closed-book test covering textual basis, local bridging, and global bridging. Connectivity simultaneously manipulated cues, anaphors, and elaborations. [Original §§2.1–2.3, pp.231–233](https://www.academia.edu/3274508/Prior_knowledge_reading_skill_and_text_cohesion_in_the_comprehension_of_science_texts)
- **Literature observation.** The text-by-item coherence × reading ability × knowledge group interaction was `F(1,166) = 4.90, p < .05, partial η² = .03`. The two-factor interaction of knowledge group × coherence was not significant. For the 23 individuals in the high-knowledge, high-reading group, the mean score increased from .55 to .68 for low-to-high coherence items, `t(22) = 2.461, p < .05`. For the 39 individuals in the high-knowledge, low-reading group, the difference was in the opposite direction, `t(38) = −2.44, p < .05`, which contradicts the group means reported in the text and Table 3. No confidence interval was reported for the interaction effect. [§3.3, Table 3, pp.236–237](https://www.academia.edu/3274508/Prior_knowledge_reading_skill_and_text_cohesion_in_the_comprehension_of_science_texts)
- **Data and Usage.** Retain the readout and domain knowledge stratification, treating the item type as part of the endpoint. The original includes sample article excerpts and questions. complete reusable stimuli and sequential data were not found. Used for design background control. [Publisher Edition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2008.04.003)

## E05 TED-Q: Was the same question subsequently answered?

- **Design.** 111 readers read excerpts from six English speeches paragraph by paragraph, pausing after every two sentences to report new questions, and revisiting at most two unresolved questions from preceding paragraphs. Readers rated the extent to which a given question was answered on a 1–5 scale, with a score of ≥3 indicating at least partial resolution. For each paragraph, readers could highlight up to ten words as textual cues for the question or answer. [§3, Fig. 1, pp.1120–1121](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.141.pdf#page=3)
- **Observation and recalculation.** 2,412 text-anchored queries were linked to 3,669 subsequent ratings, with 1,107 rated as at least partially answered. After remapping to the original query via `worker, source, excerpt_number, prior_question`, 910 met the threshold in the next observation window, and an additional 197 met it in the second subsequent window, totaling 45.9% of the original queries. This is a descriptive statistic of a fixed observation window, unpublished sampling interval, with no follow-up measurement post-window. [§4, Table 1, Fig. 3](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.141.pdf#page=5) · [Author's sequential CSV](https://github.com/amore-upf/ted-q/blob/master/TED-Q_elicitation.csv)
- **Data and Usage.** This measurement can define the update of the same query in subsequent intervals. Circled words can locate the text readers deem relevant, while the first spontaneously confused token remains unobserved. Per TED's announcement, verbatim transcripts used for AI/ML development and evaluation require separate authorization. Currently logged as a design basis. [TED Usage Terms §§6.3–6.4](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-com-terms-of-use)

## E06 Jones & Bergen later-onset reading delay

- **Design.** Experiment 2 involved English self-paced, zone-by-zone reading with 30 passages, each comprising 8 combinations of ambiguity, antecedent order, and referent mentioned in the continuation. The explicit reference condition retained the same continuation passages. Of 205 participants, 141 were retained. of 4,230 trials, 3,947 were retained. Passage length ranged from 2 to 5 words. [Experiment 2, §§3.1–3.2, Fig. 3, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1017/langcog.2024.2)
- **Published observation.** Information conflicting with the more likely referential interpretation appears in region 4 of the continuation. The reading delay in region 5 was significant after multiple-comparison correction. The descriptive difference in Fig. 4 was +55 ms, with `p_adj = .006`. After including GPT-3 surprisal for the current region and the three preceding regions, the world-knowledge bias coefficient for region 5 was `β = .075` in log reading time per unit of bias, with `SE = .023, p_adj = .007`. Both regions occur in the same sentence. The comprehension checks probed other details. [§3.3, Tables 4–5, and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1017/langcog.2024.2)
- **Data and usage.** Retain conflict stimulus positions and measured delay positions. A readout time can be set to aid control. however, to claim a discrepancy at that location, a report observation from the same endpoint is still required. Experiments 1 and 2 share a stimulus base, with reference selection and delay occurring across different trials. The 6,150 published trials from Experiment 2, containing 30 sets of materials in JSON format, are available. Each trial includes segments such as `rt_cont` and `rt_cont_sp1` along with version IDs, but the author exclusion criteria have not yet been applied to reproduce the analysis model. Public trial files for Experiment 1 also offer an option to reply. The OSF project does not include data licensing. model usage must be confirmed. [Experiment 2 CSV](https://osf.io/download/9uy2t/) · [Experiment 2 Stimulus JSON](https://osf.io/download/ygr7x/) · [Experiment 1 CSV](https://osf.io/download/ekytu/) · [Project Metadata](https://api.osf.io/v2/nodes/v8rjm/)

## E07 Pettijohn & Radvansky: Unexpectedness of surprise and event conversion

- **Design.** Experiment 1 included 112 readers who were presented with English narratives sentence-by-sentence, asking them to rate the unexpectedness of the current sentence relative to the immediately preceding sentence, with 1 indicating fully expected and 7 indicating fully unexpected. The preceding sentence may have generally signaled a change in situation, while the target sentence was manipulated to indicate whether a genuine event transition actually occurred. This comparison used 32 non-causal transition-type stimuli, with the stimulus as the unit of analysis. [Experiment 1, Methods, Table 3, p.1069](https://memorylab.nd.edu/assets/259391/pettijohn_radvansky_2016_memory_cognition_.pdf#page=6)
- **Literature observation.** Under no-advance conditions, no-issue and prefix scores were 3.04 and 4.34, `SE = .17, .22`. Under advance conditions, scores were 3.14 and 3.40, `SE = .21, .23`. Interaction `F(1,31) = 12.43, p = .001, partial η² = .29`. Event conversion detection in Experiment 2 and recognition response in Experiment 3 belong to other endpoints. [Experiments 1–3, Tables 3–5](https://memorylab.nd.edu/assets/259391/pettijohn_radvansky_2016_memory_cognition_.pdf#page=6)
- **Data and usage.** When comparing conditions with and without earlier text prompts, we measured the unexpectedness of event transition sentences. Appendix A provides stimulus examples. the full, unverified stimuli and sequential reports are available upon request and were used for design rationale. This result allows us to verify whether the app correctly presents "unexpected" and "plausible resolution" conditions. [Appendix and publication information](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-016-0619-6)
