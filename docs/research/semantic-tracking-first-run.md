# Locating questions, earlier references, and later updates

In existing public data, Qwen3.5-2B improves the reader's readout of the first key press, also assigning more probability mass to the human-annotated earlier text. The answer position can be traced to the text line via the same narrative question's annotation, yet the precise token of the reader's immediate understanding remains unmarked. ## Data and model alignment

### Where to start reading and where to resume reading

We used the public second experiment from Paape and Vasishth. Each reading presented one experimental pre-clipping segment, with some segments containing only a single word. The risk set comprised trials where the reader first arrived at the segment and had not yet performed a backward glance. We compared the next action: pressing the back-button or continuing forward. We validated the condition using 64 held-out material groups and 16 groups, with both experimental versions grouped together, allowing readers to cross materials. We compared the back-button press frequency against prior, position, and length baselines, and against an additional model incorporating Qwen state. We also reported segments where only one word was displayed. For observable backward trials, we retained the first landing point and the furthest reached segment in consecutive back-glances. This word-segment dataset covers keypress behavior, where readers may experience difficulty before pressing. The registration page for the prototype stop-and-reread study does not include trial-by-trial data. [Original Study Second Experiment](https://doi.org/10.5070/G601139) · [Data Actually Used](https://osf.io/j8cbh/) · [Another Prototype Registration](https://osf.io/p56j8/) · [Event Construction](../../research/semantic_tracking/onset_prepare.py)

### Where do the known contradictions point?

Utilize contradictory annotations within SCARECROW. Annotators highlight contradictory segments and their corresponding earlier text when the full text is visible. Inputs are restricted to publicly released model continuations, excluding original news citations and earlier text pointing to trials within the original news. Retain 1,175 precisely aligned annotations, split into 949 training and 226 test samples, grouped by shared news prompts and identical continuations. Provide only the end position of the highlighted contradiction. candidate scoring includes words preceding the highlighted segment. Report readout baselines with uniform distribution and distance/word feature fitting, plus readout augmented with two Qwen state interaction features. The primary metric is the negative log-likelihood of the total probability of the highlighted segment, alongside a count of whether the highest-scoring word falls within the highlighted region. This comparison is limited to cases where the contradiction location is known. [Original study §§4.1–4.4](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.501.pdf#page=5) · [Raw data](https://yao-dou.github.io/scarecrow/) · [Data construction](../../research/semantic_tracking/source_prepare.py) · [Pointing model](../../research/semantic_tracking/source_model.py)

Using the same frozen Qwen3.5-2B model on Colab L4, perform a single FP32 causal forward pass per text segment without autoregressive generation. Preserve the reading stop equivalent to the native subword prefix, utilizing only current and historical position embeddings. Test annotations are retained locally. sealed snapshot probabilities are merged and scored afterward. ### When is the same question resolved?

Verify the German narrative data from Onea et al. The annotation team first marks the text line where each plot question opens, then re-reads to mark the answer line. Each story's question annotations support paired open and close positions, with suspense intensity rated by a separate group of annotators. Uncertain questions ending without confirmation are right-censored. Three stories with original text-line annotations are held-out per story. Compare the fixed per-line end probability with the probability binned by the number of already-opened question lines. Each fold estimates the probability from the numerical coordinates of other stories, conditioned on human-annotated start points. The fourth author's processing file extends the original position to word order, so only descriptive verification is performed. The model reuse weight for original stories versus translations remains unverified. this round uses coordinates and annotation status for calculation. TED-Q features more direct reader continuation ratings, typically every two sentences, stopping tracking once the response degree meets the threshold. ## held-out results

### First readout

In the 16 unseen materials, there were 8,226 valid reading stops and 381 first rereads. The average precision of reread events (higher is better, range 0–1) increased from 0.066 to 0.301 based on position and length baselines. The Brier error of event probabilities decreased from 0.04402 to 0.04183, a difference of −0.00219, with a 95% paired-material confidence interval [−0.00296, −0.00158]. Brier score represents the mean squared difference between predicted probabilities and whether a reread occurred (range 0–1, lower is better). In word display segments, there were 3,313 valid reading stops and 144 rereads. average precision increased from 0.103 to 0.248, with a Brier difference of −0.00142 [−0.00374, −0.00007]. [First reread score](../../research/results/semantic-tracking-first-run/onset-scores.json)

Among the 236 stops at single-word critical verbs manipulated by the authors, there were 15 rereading events. The Brier error difference was +0.00005 [−0.00031, +0.00040]. Among 335 rereading events with at least two eligible earlier regions, top-ranked accuracy for the farthest destination increased from 48.1% with the distance baseline to 51.0%. The origin and farthest destination were both single-word regions in only seven cases. Neither readout had a hit in those cases. In the raw records, readers moved beyond the original stop after 303 first rereading events. The keypress data did not record whether the same question had been resolved. [Critical verbs and rereading destinations](../../research/results/semantic-tracking-first-run/onset-scores.json)

### The earlier text to which the contradiction refers

The 226 held-out references had a median of 59 candidate words, and the median human-selected span covered 6 candidates. With Qwen features, the negative log-likelihood of the probability assigned to the human-selected span fell from **2.077 to 2.027 nats**. The difference against the distance and lexical baseline was −0.0496 [−0.0713, −0.0284]. A lower value means the model assigns more probability to the selected span. The highest-ranked word fell inside that span in 31.0% of cases, up from 27.4%, a difference of +3.54 percentage points with an interval of [−1.71, +8.76]. The model shows a ranking signal, with substantial error in its first choice. [Earlier-text reference scores](../../research/results/semantic-tracking-first-run/source-scores.json)

### The location of the answer to the story plot question

Three corpus-level datasets contain 196 annotated questions, of which 183 have solved rows. Per-corpus held-out paired data cover 14,945 unsolved questions × rows. Estimating the end probability using the time since opening, the average negative log-likelihood across three equal-weighted corporuses decreases from a fixed baseline of 0.07410 to 0.07242 nats (lower is better), while average precision increases from 0.0140 to 0.0239 (higher is better). The negative log-likelihood decreases in two corporuses and increases in one. This represents a known-question start-time baseline with no article semantic input. [Per-corpus results and re-run data](../../research/results/semantic-tracking-resolution/narrative-metadata.json) · [Computation script](../../research/semantic_tracking/resolution_prepare.py)

Across the four datasets, 115 instances were marked as resolved while other unresolved questions spanned across that position. 79 of these could not be reproduced by a strict LIFO rule, indicating that simultaneous parallelization is permitted. This result suggests that narrative annotations allow earlier questions to conclude first. These answer positions were annotated post-re-reading, with resolution constrained by the original text line. Even with word-level uncertainty scores, the reader's specific token where the answer was noticed cannot be determined from this score. [Question combination, constraints, and matching statistics](../../research/results/semantic-tracking-resolution/narrative-metadata.json)

## Execution and Reproducibility

The NVIDIA L4 retrieved 654 text segments, 71,534 non-padded tokens, and 51,770 original stop positions using a fixed Qwen3.5-2B revision, with no tokenizer alignment exclusion. 12 prefixes passed. forward and readout times were 30.31 seconds. Source readout and readout preparation fit times within the program were 5.33 and 3.53 seconds, respectively. Peak retention consumed 7.63 GiB of GPU memory. From configuration confirmation to stop request, the total elapsed time was 167.23 seconds, and L4 has confirmed release. The account simultaneously has other active assignments, so the account difference cannot be attributed solely to this job.
