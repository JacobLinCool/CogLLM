# Reader background and English lexical difficulty

This experiment examines whether the difference in selection proportions between two groups for the same English candidate segment can be read out more accurately by the interaction of background and text. Background refers to whether the annotator is a native English speaker. Annotators viewed the context and selected words or phrases that might be difficult for a specified reader group, as per task instructions. Each candidate segment stores vote counts from 10 annotators per group. The observational unit is the candidate segment along with its two group vote counts. [Research Hypothesis] The interaction can more accurately recover the difference in selection proportions between the two groups for the same candidate compared to leaving background empty or using a fixed background offset. [Yimam et al. 2017, §3–4](https://aclanthology.org/I17-2068.pdf#page=2) · [CWI 2018 Data Documentation](https://sites.google.com/view/cwisharedtask2018/datasets)

## Data and fitting

We retained 3,850 WikiNews training candidates along with their article families, and held 1,348 development candidates for process diagnostics. The previous 1,788 WikiNews test candidates were scored and fully excluded. The new test comprises all 7,115 English Wikipedia candidates from the same release. We pre-scan exact coordinates and normalized sentences to confirm the new test shares no HIT identifiers or normalized sentences with the entire WikiNews corpus. The Wikipedia archive provides sentences and HITs, with the model input fixed to single sentences. Resampling merges identical normalized sentences within the same HIT and across different HITs into connected components. Unpublished original article provenance and individual annotator keys fall outside the scope of this partition's verifiability. Author profile data is licensed under CC BY 4.0. The reproduction package retains upstream sources and hashes, and the published readout does not include the full text. Retain the frozen Qwen3.5-2B and the original five readouts. For each WikiNews training passage and each Wikipedia test sentence, perform a single causal forward pass. Extract hidden states only at the candidate end positions that strictly match the independent prefix tokenization of the current passage. Retain the original training token frequencies, normalization, per-token binary negative log-likelihood, unpunished intercept, L2 regularization 0.002, and LBFGS with a maximum of 110 iterations. Token frequencies and feature scaling are derived solely from the WikiNews training data. This validation fixes the readouts and hyperparameters to inspect program output on the development set. Test runs are conducted on an independent sealed snapshot of the machine. test scoring is performed only after the prediction and extraction logs have been sealed. ## Acceptance

Coverage must be at least 95% of published Wikipedia candidates aligned with precise reading stops. An observed effect difference is the proportion selected in the non-native group minus the proportion selected in the native group. The primary score is the mean absolute error between the predicted difference and the observed difference, ranging from 0–2, where lower is better. Perform 10,000 paired material group resamples (seed 43) and report intervals at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. The interaction readout must show the difference in error relative to the background leave-one-out and fixed offset baselines, with both interval upper bounds required to be less than 0.

For each group, the per-ticket Brier score ranges from 0–1, with lower being better. The upper bound of the interaction readout relative to the two baselines must not exceed 0.001, i.e., one-thousandth of the full scale. Overall calibration is measured by the absolute difference between the average predicted selection rate and the average actual selection rate. The upper bound of the absolute calibration error for each group must not exceed 0.01, i.e., approximately one error per 100 tickets. These two upper bounds represent the practical tolerance limits for this release. the former constrains the overall loss of group-level probabilities, while the latter constrains the overall overestimation or underestimation. Additionally, the per-ticket negative log-likelihood (nats, lower is better) is reported to interpret error sources. The post-processing scope encompasses the given candidate English vocabulary selection and two annotation groups. Under single-sentence transfer conditions, group difference estimation is an independent research endpoint alongside individual reading responses, thematic knowledge, and discourse comprehension. Full-text arbitrary-position highlighting requires additional locking of candidate generation methods and covered observations. Prior to release, coefficients, intercepts, training data-derived feature scales, and word frequency sources must be exported, and the exported suite must be rerun through the forward and readout evaluations that passed the initial screening.
