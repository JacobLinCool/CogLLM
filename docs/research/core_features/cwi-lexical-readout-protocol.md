# Select the human-selected fit Gradio vocabulary to read out

In professional news held-out sets, the current Gradio CompLex score ranks lower than the combined baseline of sentence length and inverse word frequency. This study uses the previously fixed WikiNews article family split to evaluate whether, after estimating vocabulary readout via human selection, the CompLex score can improve ranking and vote rates for unseen material beyond literal features. The Gradio candidate, Qwen3.5-2B, word-final features, and single causal forward pass remain fixed. [Professional News Results](cwi-news-holdout-results.md) · [WikiNews Family Split](../../../research/results/core-features-first-run/cwi-source-audit.json)

Each input contains only the published original sentence. Candidates must be single, complete interface terms with character coordinates matching the original text verbatim. Normalized sentences repeated across published article families or within HITs are excluded. The training, development, and test sets are retained from the already locked WikiNews split. Annotations consist of vote counts from 10 annotators per group for each original candidate. The two groups are merged into a single candidate, yielding 20 judgments to reduce assumptions about background grouping. Annotators view the original paragraph, while the model ingests the original sentence. Professional news follows the already sealed snapshot of 100 original sentences and the same interface score, performing only a backtracking verification after fitting the weights in the sealed snapshot.

## Three fitted controls

For each candidate, the three-dimensional initial features are the Unicode character count, the natural logarithm \(-\log(1+c)\), and the CompLex score of Gradio display precision, where \(c\) is the frequency of the corresponding lemma within the deduplicated sentence set of the CompLex train/trial corresponding to the previously sealed snapshot. Literal readout uses only the first two terms, CompLex readout uses only the third term, and fused readout uses all three terms. The model is a logistic readout with an intercept. Each precisely aligned training candidate is binomial likelihood based on the vote count. The article family uses equal weighting, sentences within a family use equal weighting, and candidates within a sentence use equal weighting. Training weights simultaneously determine the feature mean and standard deviation. Standardization coefficients use ridge with \(0.05\lVert w\rVert^2/2\), with no penalty on the intercept. All three fits use only the pre-specified training family. No regularization or feature selection is adjusted based on development or test results.

The fused readout for each candidate can be computed using the current final state and frozen statistics. All candidate model scores, literal baselines, and coefficients are first sealed in a snapshot, then combined with WikiNews development, test, and professional news vote counts. Intra-sentence ranking retains candidates that cannot be aligned via the interface. all three fitting methods and the original interface uniformly place such misaligned candidates at the end of the same sentence, split by half. Comparisons also include original interface scores and an existing word-length and inverse word-frequency equal weighting baseline. Per-ticket NLL and average ticket error are computed only over the same candidate set that is alignable via all three fitting readouts.

## Threshold and Scope of Application

Three primary checks are conducted on the WikiNews test family. The fused in-sentence ranking shows higher literal readout than the baseline, and the fused in-sentence ranking shows higher literal readout than the original interface. the fused per-ticket NLL shows lower literal readout than the baseline. Rankings are aggregated using sentence pairing, sentences within the article family, and equal weighting within the article family. The per-ticket NLL aggregation uses the same weighting rules as the fused approach. For each of the three difference metrics, the 95% confidence interval must fall in the hypothesized direction, and the candidate alignment rate must reach 95%. Clusters are formed using previous article families from the study, with 10,000 resamples and seed 735972. Additionally, report the difference of the fused approach to the equal weighting baseline, the per-ticket average calibration error, results from the development family, and the backoff scores for the 22 professionally reviewed news HITs. Since the WikiNews test family has already been used with other readout models in prior experiments, this round serves as a fixed-family model comparison. Before deploying to Gradio, an independent held-out set and actual interface validation are required for any existing unknown corpus.
