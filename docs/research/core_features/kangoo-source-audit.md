# Selection of human-level vocabulary from English textbooks

Kangoo et al. selected difficult words from English textbooks, with participants comprising 767 undergraduates and 193 graduate students. Paper-based tasks required underlining words in the original text, while online tasks first presented similar passages followed by vocabulary selection blocks. Public counts were aggregated across both modalities, and individual participant keys were not retained. The resulting dataset captures the frequency with which a word was flagged as difficult. The actual number of respondents completing each page cannot be reconstructed from the public data. [Paper §§3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10661753/#sec0004) · [Author-fixed release v4](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/p2wrs7hm4z/4)

The verified author-published ten questionnaires and `dataset_english.csv` total approximately 473 KB. Each of the ten Word documents contains nine existing page markers, allowing reconstruction of ten source pages per document. In-page text, paragraph breaks, and positioning characters are retained, following the original questionnaire grouping. The entire batch comprises 100 pages and 29,880 interface tokens. The reproducible program first verifies the SHA-256 hash of each published document. [Source and Parsing Program](../../../research/core_features/kangoo_prepare.py)

CSV first merged 100 pages and deduplicated by lemma. A lemma count was reattached to the original text only when the CSV frequency was 1, the normalized lemma was unique in CSV, and unique across all questionnaires, mapping to a complete word. The lemma must have counts from its affiliated university or graduate group, the other group must be marked as unseen, and the counts must fall within the number of students reported by the original author for that semester. Of 5,368 columns, 2,808 passed, with 449 lemmas having at least one vote. There were 55 pages containing lemmas with different vote counts, totaling 10,687 pairs across ten questionnaires. This serves as the aligned sample basis, with no model scoring yet executed. [Lemma Aggregation and Group Size](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10661753/#sec0004) · [Alignment Rules](../../../research/core_features/kangoo_prepare.py)

Comparing the existing CompLex three splits, CWI 2018 English formal splits, and the evaluated MedReadMe held-out set after normalization, no complete source sentence embeddings were found for question pages with five or more words. Additionally, no source sentence matched at least 25% of distinct five-word fragments within a single question page. Whether Qwen's pre-training data has encountered these textbooks remains unknown.

Mendeley v4 is marked CC BY 4.0. The actual textbooks cited in the survey have their own publisher statements. At least two IGNOU textbooks containing identical survey text were confirmed to retain all rights and require written reproduction permission. The institution's open educational resources policy permits releasing textbooks under specified licenses, yet each source must still be verified individually. This integration is reproducible only via local cache on this machine and applies to source auditing.
