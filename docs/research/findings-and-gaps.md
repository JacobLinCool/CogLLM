## Research Claim and Current Deliverables

The goal is to run one causal forward pass with frozen Qwen3.5-2B over the article and optional reader background. Readout heads use the current and earlier prefix representations to estimate response distributions, intensities, and associated spans that map to the original text. An analysis program or the user interprets those outputs. Replacing unread later text should preserve the causal readout at the same location within numerical tolerance. When background is empty, each endpoint defines the reference reader group for interpretation. Question stems, options, and prespecified propositions appear only in the evaluations that use them. ## Verified Results and Scope

Average precision (AP) ranges from 0 to 1. Higher AP indicates that annotated items concentrate nearer the top of the ranking. Mean absolute error (MAE) uses the original scale, and negative log-likelihood (NLL) uses nats. Lower is better for both, with zero the minimum for the losses in these comparisons. Spearman rank correlation ranges from −1 to 1. Values nearer 1 indicate stronger positive agreement.

## What the Training Experiments Show

The research register contains 46 empirical papers. For four, verification was limited to an abstract or table of contents. The first datasets were PLABA-EVAL sentence ratings and difficulties, Zhan's Mandarin continuation and reference task, and OneStopQA open-book multiple choice. Subsequent work extended the existing lexical, sentence, and document endpoints. Available signals include individual human annotations, differences between study conditions, and aligned annotated intervals. Each retains its language, visible text, observation granularity, and denominator. [Registry of study observations](stage-1-observations.md) · [Initial data](stage-2-data.md)

The main experiments froze Qwen and used supervision to fit linear heads or a shared adapter. They compared per-observation losses, paired or interval ranking, and a loss for effects reported in the literature. Sharing parameters across three tasks and adding effect supervision reduced PLABA comprehension MAE from 0.7556 to 0.6963 and Zhan's per-choice Brier score from 0.2616 to 0.2474. The Brier score is squared error between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes, with lower scores better. Both intervals for improvement in effect magnitude crossed zero. The Zhan holdout estimate remained at +8.45 percentage points compared with +24.46 for humans. Later joint training on rereading, earlier-span reference, and weakly supervised resolution also did not improve all three endpoints together. [Joint effect supervision](joint-effects-run.md) · [Three semantic tasks](semantic-joint-first-run.md)

Feature comparisons covered layer 12 logits, surprisal, and attention. Adding these signals gave no consistent gain for sentence-level PLABA difficulties. Adding attention alone improved one Frick whole-article NLL comparison while leaving the effect magnitude problem open. In a retrospective Beukers diagnostic, a readout identified author-specified critical cues from representations of fixed story templates. Incorporating those cues in a task-structured readout increased the estimated difference between the two effects from 14.3 to 60.2 percentage points, compared with 78.0 points for humans. This suggests readout structure can affect whether information already in the text representations yields the correct behavioral effect magnitude. New materials are needed to test that hypothesis. [Feature comparisons](stage-3-feature-ablation.md) · [Beukers cue diagnostic](semantic_tracking/beukers-background-results.md#post-hoc-comparison-of-readout-selection-and-cue-identification)

At this snapshot, reinforcement learning and LoRA updates to the Qwen backbone remained planned comparisons. The evidence available here came from supervised readouts, with no accepted result yet for either comparison. A loss can approximate a mean effect in the literature while giving no information about where each reader's question occurs at token level. [Training protocol](training-protocol.md#訓練順序)

**Finding.** A strict most-recent-question-first rule cannot reproduce the available narrative observations. Of 115 Onea positions where one question closed and others remained open, 79 violated that rule. TED-Q also contains 13 instances in which a reader first gave an earlier question a rating of 5 while a newer one remained at 1 or 2. The design should allow multiple open questions and different orders of resolution. Reopening, setting a question aside, and a reader reaching full understanding each call for appropriate observations. ## Research App Displays and Remaining Acceptance Tests

The research prototype gives authors and editors a whole-text view immediately after they paste an article. An actual L4 request checked a four-paragraph, 2,496-character article with 26 sentence marks and 425 word marks. One word could not be aligned exactly and remained visible. Switching score scales did not run the model again. A phrase query could retrieve up to five earlier candidates, and the word-level disambiguation view could show up to three candidates per sentence. The scales and candidate ranks follow each endpoint's measurement and validation scope. [Whole-text preview](../../research/results/gradio-combined-overview-l4/reading-overview.html) · [Disambiguation preview](../../research/results/gradio-word-cue-route-l4/cue-preview.html) · [Interface and acceptance tests](gradio_app/README.md#實驗性逐詞與提問指向候選)

Final acceptance still called for one sealed set of readouts connecting verifiable question events, the corresponding earlier text, later answer intervals, and optional reader backgrounds supported by data. The design also includes prefix-by-prefix inspection, side-by-side version comparison, and research export. Long-article, reference, article replacement, and disambiguation verification accumulated over several implementation tests. A final release requires retesting them together with the final version. ## Three Research Checks for Resuming the Work

1. **Observability of the same question.** Verify whether the available data link the same reader, the same question, the text then visible, the report location, the associated earlier text, and the later state. Define missingness, absence of a report, observation cutoff, and resolution separately. If measurement stops at a sentence or two-sentence interval, evaluate at that granularity. This determines whether it is possible to test token-level claims such as “the reader first struggles at the beginning of sentence two.” [Data audit](semantic_tracking/reader-report-source-audit.md) · [Position and offset specification](mathematical-formulation.md#3-平行讀出反應位置與強度)
2. **External effects for the same candidate.** Seal the primary effects, scales, tolerances, and source families that did not inform the design. Report individual response distributions, effect magnitudes, localization, and errors. Include consistency and reader-background comparisons on the same text, with cases where evidence is present and cases where it cannot be confirmed. A single average gain cannot resolve these comparisons. [Human validity](mathematical-formulation.md#真人效度) · [Independent holdout](stage-6-holdout-plan.md#formal-validation-of-independent-studies)
3. **Agreement between research outputs and the final app.** Use the same checkpoint, tokenizer, heads, and thresholds in one end-to-end evaluation from pasted text through original offsets, partial or complete answer intervals, and output scales. Retain separate evidence for model estimates and the human effects assessed afterward. [Acceptance criteria](execution-plan.md#完成條件) · [App verification](gradio_app/README.md#核對實際模型與介面)
