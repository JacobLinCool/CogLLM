# Stage 5 · Semantic localization, question updates, and cross-sentence links

In this round of verification, three separately observable quantities are defined. The **report reading stop** is the last position displayed when the reader is required to answer during the experiment. The **targeted segment** is the segment indicated by the reader as relevant to the question. The **question update** is the re-reporting of the same question by the same reader at a subsequent reading stop. The endpoint is estimated only when there are corresponding observations and a denominator in the data. Data ending at the end of a sentence or at a two-sentence pause are given the visible prefix at the time of reporting. Tokens first noticed within a sentence still belong to the unobserved interval. ## Problem-oriented data verification and update

**INQUISITIVE has 19,814 precisely aligned selected phrases.** The author version released 19,816 verified questions, corresponding to 7,099 articles with questions × sentences at reading stops. The instruction prompts readers to ask a question and select the phrase in the sentence upon finishing reading the current sentence. Of the 7,500 scheduled reading stops, 401 lack corresponding data columns in the released question set. Reader IDs, the actual denominator of participants, and no-issue reports were not included in the file release. therefore, the question status for these 401 reading stops is retained as unknown. The author requests access to the full article. The publicly released question set also lacks at least one earlier text sentence required by 463 reading stops with reports. [Original study §§3.1–3.3](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.530.pdf#page=3) · [Author's article retrieval instructions](https://github.com/wjko2/INQUISITIVE/tree/9c364b689a3c134cc5a0bc886f0ce7ce190e6111#article-sources) · [Public recalculation](../../research/results/stage5-semantic/inquisitive-audit.json)

DCQA released an additional 719 expert response judgments on the same set of news articles, of which 422 contained parseable answer span indices. These positions serve as annotations indicating where the answer appears in the text. Whether readers detect the answer at those locations must be verified via their own subsequent reports. All 719 responses can be matched to INQUISITIVE homologous records via article and question text. when performing held-out evaluation, they must be merged by source family. [DCQA §6.1](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.806.pdf#page=7) · [Homologous Alignment Audit](../../research/results/stage5-semantic/inquisitive-audit.json)

**TED-Q supports time-constrained question updates.** The task requires posing a question at each non-final reading stop, with the denominator of the question rate constrained by this instruction. Every two sentences, there is a stop. thus, 2,412 original questions each received one subsequent rating. Of the first 910 ratings, at least 1–5 points (3 points or higher) were deemed partially answered. Of the remaining 1,502, 1,257 were rated a second time, and 197 met the threshold on the second rating. 245 were observed only at the first rating and were truncated because the passage ended. Questions reaching 3 points exited subsequent tracking. therefore, whether "at least partially answered" is fully resolved requires another observation. 40 readers' final reading stops recorded a character-end point earlier than the start point, corresponding to 66 instances that could still be ranked by reader and reading stop index. [Original study §§3–4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.141.pdf#page=3) · [Recalculated sequential ratings](../../research/results/stage5-semantic/tedq-audit.json)

Of the 1,257 same-session dual-question reading stops, 88 had only the older question reaching 3 points, 317 had only the newer question reaching the threshold, 109 had both reaching the threshold, and 743 had neither. Treating the 3-point score within the same reading stop as a partial response, the 88 instances provide counterexamples to the fixed prior hypothesis that the newer question would be answered. Additionally, 109 questions declined from a score of 2 in the first round to 1 in the second. The scoring criterion asks how much of the newly displayed segment was answered. this scale permits downward movement. Scoring every two sentences does not allow determining which token within the interval generated the update. The TED verbatim transcript terms require separate authorization for AI/ML development and evaluation. this round recalculated descriptive statistics using public fields. [Scoring Interface](https://github.com/amore-upf/ted-q/blob/10fb7e1a2b2d756f8ea190ee2ac223fecaec0f20/data_gathering/elicitation/chunk_includes/fragment_answer.html#L126-L139) · [Verbatim Terms §§6.1, 6.3–6.4](https://www.ted.com/about/our-organization/our-policies-terms/ted-com-terms-of-use#h--copyright-issues-and-licenses) · [Same-session dual-question scoring](../../research/results/stage5-semantic/tedq-audit.json)

The derivative TED-QDB releases 2,391 questions, retaining the original endpoint at which each was posed, with each question having a single answer score. QSalience's 311 human judgments assess whether the remaining body of text can answer the question. Neither release includes the original questioner's rating sequence at each reading stop. [TED-QDB fixed version](https://github.com/LeidenNLP/ted-qdb/tree/281b6d213575b5c58c6d2dd2c0bee1010e85ca69) · [QSalience §5](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1114.pdf#page=5)

## Currie et al.'s cross-sentence contradiction experiment

179 sixth-grade readers read eight-sentence English passages, advancing sentence-by-sentence, and after completion, judged whether the passage made sense. There were 24 sets of contradictory materials, each with near and far versions, varying the number of intervening sentences between the later contradictory clause and its premise. The author underlined the premise and contradictory clause in the published file. Using 4,248 existing responses from two conditions, the probability of readers selecting "makes sense" at the end of the passage was estimated. Additionally, 2,124 responses from consistent articles were recalculated for this condition's group response. Because the published file's control articles lacked an identical key to directly map each article back to its trial, the model fit used 48 explicitly numbered near/far versions. The 48 versions of the contradictory clause and its preceding sentence were independently verified for word count using original word-by-word millisecond conversion, totaling 96 positions. [Original Study Methods, Table 2](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/147782/2/Main_Document_Unblinded_27Sept2020_toSubmit.pdf#page=9) · [Public Stimuli and Data](https://osf.io/sj28g/) · [Data Audit](../../research/results/stage5-semantic/currie-audit.json)

Freeze the final layer of Qwen3.5-2B and apply causal forward pass over the same full text, capturing both the end-of-article and end-of-contradiction states. Additionally, perform independent input for the final sentence and independent input for the contradiction sentence. The model accepts the main text with an empty reader background. A single forward pass yields multiple readout reading stops. the output head directly provides probabilities. Readouts at the contradiction point also use the end-of-article score as the target, measuring the association of available information at that reading stop with the final judgment. The two sentences marked by the authors serve as stimulus positions, without individual reader-selected segments. Groups are split into six folds of 24 materials each for held-out validation, separately retaining style and two distance versions. The same reader may appear across different material folds.

At article end, the primary comparison uses three estimates from the training-fold response proportion, the final sentence in isolation, and the full article. For stimulus $i$, let $n_i$ be the number of human responses and $y_i$ the proportion of respondents who chose “makes sense.” We L2-normalize frozen states and weight each stimulus by $n_i$ divided by the mean response count across the training stimuli in its fold. We fit a ridge model with a penalty coefficient of 1 and an unpenalized intercept and clip predictions to 0–1. The primary metric is trial-level Brier score, where 0 is best and 1 is worst. Intervals use paired resampling of complete stimulus families. The secondary comparison uses stimuli with identical sentences in different orders. It checks how the inconsistent sentence in isolation and the prefix visible through that sentence estimate the same article-end response. [Forward pass and observation-stop interface](../../research/stage5/currie_extract.py)

### held-out results

**Full discourse and last-sentence-only both outperform the average report across folds.** The Brier score difference between the two text conditions is small, with confidence intervals crossing zero. Brier scores in the table are weighted by 4,248 existing judgments. lower is better. The 95% paired confidence intervals are resampled across 24 material families using fixed folds and existing reader samples. ### L4 Empirical Evaluation and Reproduction

Extracted with FP32 on NVIDIA L4 and fitted with FP64, processing 98 distinct inputs and 6,656 non-padding tokens. 48 end-of-contradiction and independent truncated prefix tokens were fully consistent. Six truncation and padding value checks passed, with maximum relative RMS difference $9.28\times10^{-7}$. The extraction subroutine took 50.55 seconds, including load, causal check, and write. The fit and evaluation subroutine took 2.67 seconds. From acquisition configuration to sending stop request was approximately 94.56 seconds, confirmed released. All four sampling accounts showed 767.96 CU, which cannot be individually attributed to this usage. ## Stage 5 validation scope

Recalculated the end-of-sentence selection, two-sentence pause update, and cross-sentence contradictory report in the public dataset. The cross-sentence model comparison has been completed on 24 precisely matched existing materials. For the end-points of semantic question selection and subsequent updates, both require alignment with the original text and availability of training and held-out data for model use. In verified observations, the point at which intra-sentence first trigger positions and questions achieve full resolution remains unidentifiable.
