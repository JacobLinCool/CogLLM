# Adaptive joint training results

Without retention constraints, adaptive weighting lowered source-balanced development negative log likelihood (NLL) for both seeds, and the established token-level curve regressed. With a bound on the shared residual and frozen token readout coefficients, both fixed and adaptive weights retained early and late cue localization. With those constraints in place, the adaptive arm's source-balanced development NLL was slightly higher for both seeds. Agreement with the directions and magnitudes of human effects on the formal evaluations remained incomplete. ## Data and adaptive weighting

Starting from the newer Qwen token readout, we reconnected 62 observation blocks from 24 existing sources to the shared state. We added the Frick token-aggregated readout, ExpMRC, and IIRC, bringing the total to 26 sources and 65 loss blocks. The existing datasets contributed 135,598 training observations and 33,208 development observations. We moved 221 earlier Frick observations to the new development split. Another 219 observations overlapping the new Frick test split stayed local for scoring. Each source retained its original scale, effective response denominator, and causal exposure points. [Measurement specification](../union_training/observation-results.md) · [Input receipt](../../../research/results/adaptive-joint-l4/input-receipt.json)

One source passage can have multiple readouts. Blocks from the same source share its weight, so the two Frick first-report heads share the original source budget. Checkpoint selection weights sources with development outcomes equally. IIRC and Qiao contribute training signals only. The adaptive rule updates weights from each endpoint's own warm-up loss scale and previously measured gradient magnitude on the shared residual. Each optimizer step uses the same total weight as the fixed arm. One backward pass provides both parameter gradients and the shared gradients needed for adaptation. Across both seeds, ExpMRC received about 0.55 times its fixed weight, IIRC received 0.50–0.52 times, and SCARECROW received 1.45–1.49 times. [Protocol](protocol.md) · [Weight statistics and source scores](../../../research/results/adaptive-joint-l4/training-comparison.json)

We reused 164,220 sealed final-layer states from 18 existing shards. After verifying the backbone revision, we projected the 2,048-dimensional states to 48 dimensions and avoided 27,167 repeat document forward passes. The L4 ran one forward pass for each of the 351 documents used by the token-level tasks. The new readout uses a shared 48×48 residual. We folded it into the existing readout projection at export and independently recomputed the scores of each exported checkpoint. The paired session took 441 seconds from allocation to release. ## Training and held-out results

| Seed | Best fixed development NLL ↓ | Best adaptive development NLL ↓ | Epoch to fixed-arm NLL target, fixed → adaptive | Development effect directions matched, fixed → adaptive |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1729 | 1.315 | 1.294 | 8 → 8 | 17/34 → 19/34 |
| 2718 | 1.314 | 1.297 | 8 → 8 | 23/34 → 23/34 |

Both fixed runs met the development stopping criterion at epoch 15. The adaptive runs stopped at epochs 18 and 19. At the matched epoch 15, the adaptive runs took 0.44 seconds less and 0.004 seconds more than their paired controls. Both methods used 160 updates and 640 backward passes per epoch. Gradient clipping rates ranged from 0.66% to 0.83% across all runs. Adaptive weighting reduced the paired mean of best development NLL by about 0.019 nats. For the 34 replayable registered development effects, the source-bootstrap 95% intervals for the difference in magnitude error crossed zero for both seeds. The formal evaluation reused historical datasets held out in full from the current fit. On SAP, direction matches at the disambiguating word changed from 3/3 to 2/3 and from 0/3 to 0/3. On Anglin, both seeds stayed at 3/6 and all six magnitudes fell outside the prespecified tolerance. On 1,440 Traditional Chinese MECO comprehension trials, NLL changed from 0.663 to 0.646 and from 0.644 to 0.614. Both paired intervals fell below zero. Both adaptive candidates scored above the source-only baseline of 0.482. These three study families had already been scored in earlier model evaluations, and the current evaluation reused the same materials and responses. Variation across seeds reflects training variability. ExpMRC interval NLL rose from 2.772 at initialization to 3.917–4.541 with adaptive weights and 4.121–4.303 with fixed weights. All arms scored 0/8 on correctly selecting and localizing both issues in the two-issue diagnostic. Both expository diagnostics use authored controls. The human endpoints use observed responses. ## Retention constraints and the matched fixed control

We froze the initial token readout coefficients and let every source update the shared 48×48 residual. Each update limited the residual's Frobenius norm to 0.10. We used development loss on the two existing token endpoints to enforce retention during checkpoint selection. All four fixed and adaptive candidates met both development retention thresholds. All selected checkpoints were at the 28-epoch budget boundary, before reaching the development stopping criterion. [Retention protocol](retention.md) · [Matched fixed-weight control](retention-control.md) · [Paired training traces](../../../research/results/adaptive-joint-retention-l4/retention-comparison.json)

| Seed with retention constraints | Fixed development NLL ↓ | Adaptive development NLL ↓ | Development effect directions matched, fixed → adaptive | Correct localization of both early and late cues in both arms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1729 | 1.1944 | 1.1956 | 14/34 → 20/34 | 6/12 |
| 2718 | 1.1974 | 1.1992 | 19/34 → 19/34 | 6/12 |

For both seeds, fixed weighting with retention reached a lower source-balanced development NLL than unconstrained fixed weighting. Both constrained procedures used 4,480 updates and 17,920 backward passes. The adaptive runs took 1.18 and 0.25 seconds longer than their paired fixed runs. Gradient clipping rates across the four runs ranged from 0.63% to 0.76%. ExpMRC interval NLL stayed near the seed's 2.772 at 2.761–2.765. The two-issue diagnostic stayed at 0/8. The fixed arm reproduced seed-curve retention. Under matched retention constraints, direction matches at the SAP disambiguating word were 1/3 for one seed and 0/3 for the other under both procedures. Anglin direction matches were 3/6. Both methods matched 0 magnitudes for seed 1729 and 1/6 for seed 2718. Traditional Chinese MECO NLL changed from 0.569 to 0.591 and from 0.519 to 0.520, from fixed to adaptive. The paired interval for seed 1729 showed higher adaptive error. The interval for seed 2718 crossed zero. We designed this follow-up after observing the first token-level regression and reused the same formal evaluation materials. ## Reproduction and artifacts

Use `research.adaptive_joint.cache` to rebuild the packet and the `prepare` and `run` commands in `research.adaptive_joint.colab` to submit training. Use default arguments for the initial comparison. For a retention run, pass `--guarded` or `--guarded-fixed` to both subcommands. Use `research.adaptive_joint.verify` to check artifact hashes and L4 release, `research.adaptive_joint.evaluate` to replay human effects locally, and `research.adaptive_joint.token_evidence` to replay token-level scoring. All three training sessions were released. The export folds the trained shared residual into the projection, and all three curve scores passed numerical replay on L4.
