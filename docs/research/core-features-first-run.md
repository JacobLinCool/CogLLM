# First experiments on lexical difficulty, rereading, and reader background

In existing human data, Qwen3.5-2B improved the ranking of candidate spans and the estimated starting point for reader re-reading. Re-reading location remained comparable to the baseline. After providing annotators' native language background, the per-vote selection prediction showed slight improvement, yet the actual gap between the two groups across spans remained difficult to estimate. [Execution Plan](execution-plan.md) · [Goal Inference Interface](training-protocol.md#模型輸入閱讀反應與分析)

## Data and held-out design

**CWI 2018 WikiNews.** The original task asked English native and non-native annotators to circle words or phrases in a set of sentences that might be difficult for a specified reader group. Each released candidate segment had readout counts from two groups of 10 annotators each. Only segments that could be reconstructed into a single continuous text by public sentence order were retained, re-tokenized by article and repeated sentence connected components. Training, threshold selection, and held-out sets comprised 3,850, 1,348, and 1,788 segments respectively, belonging to 21, 7, and 7 mutually exclusive material groups. The original data did not provide available personal keys for held-out readers. Annotators saw the full segment, while the model read out the candidate segment's prefix at its end. Vocabulary endpoint evaluation released candidate segments and their explicit zero-votes. the observation status of other original characters is unknown. [Original Experiment §3–4](https://aclanthology.org/I17-2068.pdf#page=3) · [Data and License](https://www.inf.uni-hamburg.de/en/inst/ab/lt/resources/data/complex-word-identification-dataset.html) · [Split and Align Scripts](../../research/core_features/background_prepare.py)

**BSPR first experiment.** Using 20 English discourse corpora, with two experimental versions per corpus. Reconstructed actual display segments from 1,786 trials of public action sequences. The first endpoint was defined as the reading stop at the first arrival of each segment, calculating whether the next verified action moved backward. The second endpoint was restricted to observed backward reads, estimating the earliest segment reached by a continuous backward operation. Since the immediate landing point of the preceding segment was largely determined by key rules, a fully backward sequence was formed to create a comparable destination. With a fixed hash allocation, 15 groups were used for training and 5 for testing, with both versions held out. The test set included 2,912 valid first arrivals at reading stops and 134 backward reads. The model only read to the decision position. Original event measurements can be used to locate backward read behavior, but there are no fields for direct supervision regarding the first detection and clarification of the same semantic question. [Original Study](https://doi.org/10.5070/g6011182) · [Public Events and Materials](https://osf.io/54bda/) · [Event Construction Script](../../research/core_features/readback_prepare.py)

**Additional vocabulary comparison.** Using the CompLex held-out prediction from the Stage 5 sealed snapshot, identify two segments within the same test sentence that are both evaluated, non-overlapping, and have differing human scores. This comparison checks for rare intra-sentence reordering cases. [Stage 5 sealed snapshot results](stage-5-complex-run.md#held-out-results) · [Intra-sentence recalculation](../../research/core_features/localization_complex.py)

## Model and validation

Freeze the same version of Qwen3.5-2B. Perform a single FP32 causal forward pass per text segment, without per-token generation. Use native tokenization, taking values only when the entire input segment's token prefix equals the independent tokenization to that character's reading stop. CWI takes the hidden state at the end of the candidate region. BSPR takes the state at the end of each original display region in a single pass. Preserve the character offset for the stop position and the referenced region. Uploaded evaluation data contains only segment identification and segmentation information. Reader keys and evaluation responses are retained locally. [Prefix and Retrieval Program](../../research/core_features/extract.py) · [Training and Evaluation Input](../../research/core_features/prepare.py)

- **Vocabulary Positioning.** Establish a statistical baseline using word frequency, segment length, position, and character type from training text, contrasted against Qwen-enabled no-issue readout. The readout estimates the probability of each candidate being selected. Compare ranking of same-segment positive candidates versus zero-vote candidates. The development team sets the display threshold using F1, followed by the test team reporting candidate segment precision, recall, and F1. The binary criterion is that at least one annotator has selected the candidate, and each vote's probability lies at different endpoints. The mask covers only evaluated candidates. [Scoring Program](../../research/core_features/localization_cwi_score.py)
- **Active readout.** Compare global priors, position, and length baselines, and readout events with Qwen. Additionally, compare distance, whether it is the previous segment, whether it is the beginning, and segment length baselines, and Qwen state interaction features with the current segment and candidate segments. Scoring is conditioned on actual readout occurrence, and also separately includes cases with two or more valid reading stops. [Scoring Program](../../research/core_features/readback_score.py)
- **Reader Background.** Compare the readout of Qwen with no background, with mother tongue background only, with fixed bias added background, and allowing interaction between background and text features. Check the negative log-likelihood of each vote probability, and whether the difference in human selection ratio between two candidate segments within the same region predicts more accurately. The mother tongue state can be provided before reading. Here, the conditional measurement is the labeled behavior, while the scale remains the selection ratio. [Fitting Program](../../research/core_features/fit.py) · [Group Score](../../research/core_features/localization_cwi_score.py)

Models from both datasets were fitted using the likelihood of the corresponding event and a fixed L2 penalty. After computing the sealed snapshot probability, the model was evaluated on its response. Results were interpreted based on performance with a fixed readout on held-out materials, with paired resampled material groups providing descriptive 95% confidence intervals. ## held-out results

### Vocabulary localization and highlighting

Among 21 test segments and 46,705 non-overlapping candidate pairs with differing human selection rates, Qwen achieved a ranking consistency of 76.02%, which is 3.05 percentage points higher than the 72.98% baseline based on length and word frequency, with a paired material interval [1.72, 3.93]. After fixing the threshold using development data, the candidate segment F1 score increased from 0.6501 to 0.6651, with a difference interval of [−0.0007, +0.0240]. F1 represents the harmonic mean of precision and recall, ranging from 0 to 1. Among the 956 highlighted candidates under Qwen's threshold, 568 were human-selected, yielding a precision of 59.4%. Of the 752 candidates with at least one vote, the recall was 75.5%. [ held-out scoring](../../research/results/core-features-first-run/cwi-scores.json)

Exported 21 candidate character spans, binary highlighting, and selection probabilities at each endpoint. Of the 2,761 lexical units in the source text, 1,618 (58.6%) fully fell within at least one evaluated candidate. performance at other positions could not be estimated from this test. CompLex's independent recalculation yielded 25 same-sentence evaluated pairs. agreement with human ranking was observed for 23 (target), 20 (read-to-target), and 19 (full sentence). This small control is retained as a result for specified words and phrases. [Character Mask Export](../../research/results/core-features-first-run/cwi-masks.jsonl) · [CompLex Same-Sentence Control](../../research/results/core-feature-localization/complex-within-sentence.json)

### Readout from start to destination

Among 2,912 held-out first-arrival reading stops, there were 134 regressions. Compared to the position and length baseline, Qwen reduced the Brier error for regressions from 0.04087 to 0.03694, a difference of −0.00393, with a paired material interval of [−0.00501, −0.00285]. The Brier score is the mean squared difference between event probabilities and observed binary responses, ranging from 0 to 1. The average precision for event ranking increased from 0.330 to 0.460, with a fixed prior of 0.046. [Regression event scores](../../research/results/core-features-first-run/readback-scores.json)

Among 123 readouts with two or more valid destinations, the destination first-choice hit rate was 56.9% for both the baseline and Qwen, and the fixed selection of the preceding segment was 43.9%. The destination negative log-likelihood difference for Qwen relative to the baseline was −0.0024 nats, with a paired material interval of [−0.0262, +0.0250]. In 134 observed readouts, 84 occurred after advancing to the original initiating segment. The data records readouts and reading advances, allowing readers to maintain unresolved questions during continued reading. [Readout destination score](../../research/results/core-features-first-run/readback-scores.json)

NLL is computed based on the allocation probability for each vote, with a lower bound of 0. The MAE of the difference in proportions between two groups is the absolute value of the difference between observed and model values for each segment, averaged across segments, yielding exactly 0 percentage points. Relative to the baseline, the interaction readout shows a per-vote NLL difference of −0.000700 nats, with a paired material interval of [−0.000900, −0.000538]. However, the proportion difference MAE increases by 0.495 percentage points, with an interval of [+0.399, +0.626]. Among the 650 segments where the observed proportions between the two groups differ, the interaction group's prediction direction agreement rate is 62.0%, while the fixed background offset is 55.1%. The interaction group's test average selection probability is 15.14% for native speakers and 13.90% for non-native speakers, with human annotators at 8.71% and 8.13%, respectively. The grouped results exhibit conditional signals, and calibration of probability and the magnitude of the difference between the two groups still requires improvement. The scope of application is limited to word selection and annotator background within this task. ## Execution and Reproducibility

From a single NVIDIA L4 inference, 131 text segments yielded 17,006 non-padded tokens, with all 6,517 distinct characters reading stops retained, and 12 prefix and positive control checks passed. Forward and inference latency totaled 6.84 seconds, with internal profiling time at 3.67 seconds, and GPU memory retention peak at 7.86 GiB. From configuration to request stop took approximately 115 seconds, confirming L4 release. The account simultaneously held other assignments, with readout precision at 0.01 CU, showing 765.88 CU before and after this run. ## Cross-corpus validation of reader background

We extracted in-sentence causal states from 7,115 Wikipedia held-out candidate spans, with 7,112 passing the precise reading stop check. After adding background interaction, the mean absolute difference in human selection proportions increased from 7.061 to 7.662 percentage points. The paired difference was +0.601 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval of [+0.512, +0.691]. Per-vote NLL decreased from 0.30423 to 0.30343 nats, a change measuring the probability distribution over individual selection votes. Background transfer did not pass the difference and group calibration thresholds, and the scope remains the English candidate and annotation groups specified by this study.
