### Incorporating the effect of rewriting into the development of the control group

Use the same five folds, two rating scales, inputs, and initial sentence-level distribution readout. The candidate adds a squared error for the mean rewrite effect in the training fold to the original loss. Its coefficient is fixed at 1 per training sentence multiplied by (1/2). First calculate the difference in expected ratings between versions at each aligned position, then average over aligned positions. Each fit uses only training families and human ratings from that fold. Alignment and version labels enter only the training loss. Inference on the article requires the original sentence-end state and length features. Compare held-out rewrite magnitude for the candidate and the sentence-loss baseline, resampling the 25 connected reader-and-article groups. For development acceptance, the upper bound of the paired 95% interval for the absolute error difference in the target effect must be below zero, and the upper bound for the per-sentence NLL increase must be below +0.01 nats. Report each endpoint separately and validate again on materials from an independent study.

## Reader background and re-reading conditions

The OneStop general reading group comprised 1,941 validated initial and re-reading pairs, covering 180 readers and 30 articles. Each participant viewed the same paragraph version but answered different questions in two rounds. After standardizing by paragraph version and question ID, the human re-reading accuracy improved by an average of 3.77 percentage points, with differences across three batches ranging from −0.73 to +7.52 percentage points. Using Gradio to extract the end-of-paragraph state from the same Qwen weights as the candidate, we retained scores from articles 21–30 and the third batch of new readers. The interaction between main text and re-reading readout was not found to be significantly better than re-reading priors or additive readout accuracy. the 90% confidence interval of the difference did not fully fall within ±3 percentage points. [Data Pairing](stage6/onestop-rereading-audit.md) · [L4 and held-out results](stage6/onestop-rereading-results.md)

The 30,620 item-by-item results from the French conjunction completion experiment were supplemented with observed reading exposure measures. Among the 195 adolescents with complete results in the second round, the correlation between author recognition scores and the proportion of correct responses was 0.404. This dataset, measuring conjunction selection following the blank, extends the empirical scope of reader background evidence, yet cross-round stimulus alignment and reuse conditions remain to be verified. [French data audit](stage6/french-connectives-audit.md)

## Formal validation of independent studies

The same Gradio readout candidate has been evaluated on the German ACT fixed anchor span. The ACT includes known base word coordinates and explicitly reported first-correct response steps, while the English Core Vocabulary employs a population distribution over possible paths. When the English readout is directly transferred to the ACT, the cumulative rate over the first six steps fails the magnitude acceptance test. After incorporating each dataset's own baseline, both joint and individual readouts have undergone retrospective cross-validation, confirming no gain in either study. The existing held-out data for both datasets participated in these model evaluations. subsequent reconfiguration confirmation requires a new sealed snapshot of materials.
