## First joint-training design

### Updating one model on three tasks

We use one pinned backbone and train a small shared per-position residual adapter on cached states, with separate heads for each measurement. Batches alternate among tasks. Every eligible PLABA, Zhan, and OneStop target sends gradients to the same adapter, and the optimizer updates it after accumulation. Independently fitted heads on frozen representations supply a baseline. Per-task gradients stay separate in that condition, including when the heads are packaged in one checkpoint. The text interface stays `CogLLM(article_content, knowledge_context=None)`, with readouts at all positions in parallel after one causal forward pass. The open-book evaluation provides questions and options through a separate entry point. The text interface accepts the body text and optional pre-reading background. The first joint experiment includes **5,417 unique trials**. The 858 PLABA flags are part of the 1,826 trials. Missing fields are excluded from their loss, and explicit human reports of no difficulty remain eligible. The original five folds keep rewrites, prompt variants, and experiments with a shared source in the same group. PLABA also holds out readers. Zhan and OneStop hold out materials, with some readers crossing folds. Deduplicating OneStop's 216 passage versions by body text, question, and displayed option order produces **1,872 model inputs**. The five-fold displayed-position prior gives four-way NLL of 1.3867 nats and a multiclass Brier score of 0.7502, the mean of the sum of squared probability errors over four options. Lower is better. ### Three training conditions and two levels of evaluation

1. **Fit tasks separately.** Use the same representations and loss and give each task an adapter and readout of the same capacity to establish its baseline. Preserve the existing results from the three independently fitted heads for comparison.
2. **Shared adapter with trial-level supervision.** Use the same data, five folds, and capacity. Average each task's loss over its eligible observations to prevent trial count alone from dominating. After fixing task weights, report gains and regressions for each task.
3. **Shared adapter with trial-level and literature-derived effect supervision.** Use the same subset of trial-level labels in both comparisons. Estimate aggregate targets from the remaining training-fold trials or another aligned dataset. Compute an effect loss only after every condition in that contrast can be scored. Train an additional reference on all trial-level labels. Contrasts recomputed on trials already used in the trial-level loss serve as diagnostics.

Trial-level losses use the original measurement scales. Effect losses use the contrast in the source design. The first candidate is the Zhan pronoun cue × verb bias interaction. The second is the difference in self-rated understanding between the plain-language and original PLABA versions. Each effect enters training once the fold has eligible independent direct and aggregate responses. The current +1.96 percentage point OneStop difference for the basic versus advanced version is descriptive and supports the audit. Probabilities, ordinal distributions, and their condition contrasts are differentiable, so the first experiment can use supervised and differentiable effect losses.

For each task, report held-out scores, calibration, effect direction and magnitude in the original units, and uncertainty at the group level. Lock whole material families before selecting data, features, or weights. Prespecify tolerances and estimators for each required effect. A result reproduces the effect within a tolerance only when its uncertainty interval falls inside that range. Report substantive regression on any task under the shared head even if other tasks improve. Preserve the outcome categories of reproduced, deviates, insufficient precision, and no testable data. Split the 46 papers into research family × condition × endpoint records. Each row records the material version, reader group, content available to the reader, exposure stop, response scale, denominator, estimation precision, shared-source materials, and current use. The main gap is semantic uncertainty in continuous prose. Evaluating the first token where an issue arises, the preceding text it refers to, the same issue's resolution or deferral, and local effort all require corresponding human observations. Even a perfect fit to sentence-level flags can yield different within-sentence curves. Stage 5 can first use human difficulty ratings for specified CompLex words or phrases as a local auxiliary endpoint and then audit INQUISITIVE sentence-end selections and TED-Q interval-based question updates. ROAMM mind-wandering labels and region reading times retain their respective uses. Reader background should begin with pre-reading observations. PoTeC offers ways to check major, stage of study, and match with the article's academic field. Post-reading domain questions stay in evaluation.
