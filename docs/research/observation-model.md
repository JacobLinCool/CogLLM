# Defining CogLLM's Training Signals from Human Observations

CogLLM estimates how readers may respond under specified reading conditions. The original study's presentation, sampling, and reporting rules determine the training signal for each record. Different observation models can map the same reading state to ratings, answer choices, first reports, selected spans, or differences between conditions. This design uses a single causal forward pass and existing human data. It applies the observation likelihoods in the mathematical specification to data adapters and joint training. [Mathematical specification](mathematical-formulation.md#4-人類觀測與聯合訓練)

The first round of joint training used endpoint-specific readouts. Its training interface relied mainly on categorical response counts and Gaussian sufficient statistics at fixed observation points. Paired means were supervised with the same squared-error form. This approach trains multiple endpoints, but these two interfaces discard part of the original observation structure. For example, PLABA participants could see the whole article when rating a sentence, whereas the joint readout took the causal representation at the end of the rated sentence. For OneStop, the selected option and the correctness of the same answer entered as separate training endpoints. ## Shared reading state

One causal forward pass yields a representation $h_t$ at each position. Training distinguishes two quantities derived from these representations. $E_t$ is the local demand of processing the current text. Measurements aligned to that position anchor it. $U_{t,r}$ is the probability that a question associated with an earlier candidate $r$ remains unresolved after reading through $t$. The visible preceding text, together with the question or relation, identifies $r$. The referenced text, the reader's first report, and the clue that answers the question may occur at separate positions.

First onset and first resolution use the states “not yet raised,” “unresolved,” and “resolved.” A candidate enters the risk set once the preceding text makes it identifiable. An increase in $U_{t,r}$ comes from the onset of the question for that candidate. A decrease comes from resolution of the same question. At a topic change, the model can estimate a transition response while the earlier question continues to update under its own transition rules. Estimating when a question reopens or is set aside requires observations that distinguish those states. The model scores transitions in parallel using prefix representations from one Qwen pass and computes order-preserving prefix products. [Causal state transitions](mathematical-formulation.md#5-同一疑問何時維持解開或重新出現)

The unresolved curve $U_t=\sum_r U_{t,r}$ can be expressed as the expected count of unresolved questions within the candidate set. This interpretation requires distinct candidate identities, deduplication, and evidence of probability calibration. Candidate coverage determines which questions the curve represents. $E_t$ remains anchored to its original measurement scale. A technical term within a sentence might produce a local processing spike while resolving an earlier question. The two quantities can move in opposite directions at the same position.

## Study design determines the observation model

Each data adapter records the text participants could see, the item they judged, the presentation and reporting points, the valid responses, the sampling and stopping rules, and the trial, reader, and stimulus identifiers available for linking records. Studies with the same observation mechanism can share a likelihood. Scale thresholds, base rates, response options, and decision rules follow each study's settings. The table also lists endpoints that can validate the observation model. SAP is a locked holdout.

| Human observation | Model state used | Signal for joint training |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate sentence-end ratings or PLABA sentence ratings with the full article visible | The representation at the observed sentence end for immediate ratings. For PLABA, the rated sentence and the full visible article. | Fit an ordinal response distribution for each study, preserving the order and scale of its ratings. |
| Presentation-segment times from SAP, or processing times at other observation points | Choose the state according to the actual presentation segment, task, and exposure history. | Fit the time distribution for the segment and account for applicable truncation or selection rules. Timing can constrain local processing. Reports of resolution constrain resolution events. |
| Frick's sentence-by-sentence contradiction keypresses or the first correct answer in ACT | Retain the presentation points each reader reaches in each trial. Define the first event and risk set according to the task. | Fit the full sequence of valid keypresses, or fit the first event with the applicable censoring. A likelihood over the full sequence yields the probability of the first keypress. In ACT, the event is the first correct answer. |
| Spans linked to reported questions or selected retrospectively | Use the selection range that was visible and link it to the matching report. | Use a conditional span distribution or a multilabel set likelihood, depending on the task. Sampling only trials with questions supplies localization supervision conditional on a question being asked. When readers select spans after reading the whole article, align the span they identify with the retrospective task. |
| OneStop or RACE multiple-choice comprehension responses | Use the passage, question, and options visible when the reader answered. | Fit one categorical distribution over human answer choices. The probability assigned to correct options yields the probability of a correct response. If the records contain only correctness, sum the probabilities over the options consistent with that outcome before computing the likelihood. |
| Reconstructable condition statistics published without trial-level data | Aggregate model estimates using the original study's stimuli, reader groups, response endpoint, and estimation procedure. | Use the statistic's sampling distribution to constrain a condition difference or interaction on the original scale. Covariance weighting is available when usable covariance estimates and a credible normal approximation are available. |

Suppose a reader has yet to report success at $a$ and first reports success at $b$, and the study protocol places the observed event in $(a,b]$. Define $F_r$ as the cumulative probability of first success for that task. The loss for the record is $-\log[F_r(b)-F_r(a)]$. When the reader reaches the last observation point $b$ without the event, the right-censored loss is $-\log[1-F_r(b)]$. If a study stops asking about complete success after partial success, retain that stopping rule when treating the missing observations. Report first resolution in the original observation interval when the task leaves finer positions indistinguishable. ## Using different signals in one update

Let $Z$ be the shared reading state, $\mathcal V_d$ select the state information available under study design $d$, and $p_{\psi_d}$ denote the observation distribution. The loss for a set of dependent human observations $O_d^{\rm obs}$ is

$$
-\log p_{\psi_d}\!\left(O_d^{\rm obs}\mid\mathcal V_d(Z),d\right).
$$

For an observation model with latent states, sum over the unobserved states. Use a joint distribution or conditional factorization for responses from the same trial or repeated responses from the same reader. When only the marginals can be reconstructed, document the use of a composite likelihood and how the analysis accounts for dependence. Use effects recomputed from the same trial-level data for diagnostics or explicitly stated structural constraints. Published aggregates can contribute their sampling likelihood when the underlying observation is reconstructable. Account for dependence from stimuli or readers shared with other observations. [Weak supervision through observation expectations](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/mann10a.html)

Sum the observation losses for a shared backward pass. Each gradient flows through its observation model to the shared states and parameters it uses. Suppose the first sentence introduces an unexplained premise and the third sentence supplies the answer. A sentence rating constrains the rating distribution for the first sentence. A tracking report constrains which question the reader resolves near the third sentence. Both sources update the state model at their own temporal grain and on their own scales. With a frozen backbone, training updates the shared state module and measurement heads. With trainable low-rank backbone parameters, the same objective propagates gradients to them as well.

A measurement head that anchors a state must use that state and covariates with a defined role. For example, a first-correct-answer head can use whether the same question remains unresolved and the task conditions. Giving the head unrestricted access to the full representation may let it fit the correct-response rate while bypassing the question's persistence. If studies offer overlapping measurements or common conditions, those data can calibrate the links between readouts. When each source measures a separate endpoint, a shared latent space leaves open whether the endpoints reflect a single process.

Encode established constraints first. These include agreement on a shared prefix, conservation of transition probability, and the identity of the same question across positions. Causal masks, state transitions, and sparse matching can implement them. A return to baseline at a paragraph boundary, fixed decay, and last-in-first-out ordering are research hypotheses. A regularizer encoding any of them should state what it assumes about unobserved positions. Likelihood optimization directly fits the response distribution for fixed text. An extension to choices such as rereading or skipping, where the action changes the text visible next, calls for a reading policy, an environment, and a matching learning objective.

## Scope

Online reading signals default to an explicitly defined reference reader population. If prior knowledge or background is provided, use conditions known before reading that can be calibrated in the data. Distinct latent-state models can fit all available sources and still imply the same observation distribution. A scale needs anchors for direction and magnitude, and reports of a question need to be linkable to a single identity. If a reader reports only at the ends of sentences two and four, resolution on the first token of sentence three and resolution on the last token of sentence four can have identical interval likelihoods. The token-level curve remains a model estimate in that case.

To add a source, first reconstruct one traceable trial from the original study rules. Check which model position and shared state each response uses. Confirm that the model's response distribution can reproduce the calculation of the original endpoint. These checks determine which gradients the source can supply. Entire held-out studies retain their original role, and model fitting and final evaluation follow the existing split. [Study-level holdout](union_training/holdout-plan.md)
