# Post-training verification of MECO's passage-level understanding

This holdout tests whether article text alone can estimate the probability that readers answer a postreading comprehension question correctly. The target is **probability of a correct answer**, marginal over the original study's readers and items. Each article receives one probability, applied to every reader and item for that article when scoring. Use the official horizontal-layout true/false responses, with six articles per reader and four items per article. The data contain 60 readers, 12 articles, and 1,440 responses. Include all 12 formal stimuli and all original horizontal-layout responses. Exclude practice trials, vertical presentation, and eye-tracking data. Join the two source pages in reading order, retaining punctuation from the horizontal stimulus images and removing visual line and page breaks. [MECO study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06989-8) · [Public source data](https://osf.io/jyn8s/)

Using the `onestop_rereading_first_read_correct` trained on OneStop initial reading accuracy, map the `interpretation` state at the end of the article to the probability of a correct response. Both studies present comprehension questions separately after reading. The post-article questions and answer options are not provided to the model. here, the estimation is limited to the article level. There are distributional differences between the initial reading data of English news and the readers and question types of Traditional Chinese expository texts. This validation measures zero-sample transfer under a fixed target population and question distribution for this readout. [OneStop Study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-06272-2) · [OneStop Readout Contract](observation-contracts-b.md)

First, fix the original data, all 12 end-of-document coordinates, the frozen backbone after convergence of the development set, and the joint readout, source baseline, and all 12 probabilities. The model performs a single causal forward pass per document. Using the training fold of OneStop with the same readout head, compute the fixed baseline for each document as the ratio of correct responses to total responses. Both prediction procedures use the same held-out responses throughout, and Traditional Chinese data are excluded from model selection, baseline calibration, and cutpoint adjustment.

The primary metric is the Bernoulli negative log-likelihood difference per response (CogLLM minus fixed baseline, in nats). For readers and articles, we performed resampling with replacement, multiplying each observed cell by the number of resamples in which that cell appeared, and retaining the four responses from that cell. With 6,000 resamples and seed 1729, we used the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. A two-sided 95% confidence interval upper bound below zero indicates support for improvement on this material, while a lower bound above zero indicates support for worse performance. all other cases are recorded as an indeterminate difference. The Brier score and the difference between predicted rate and observed rate serve as descriptive metrics. Given that the number of article clusters is only 12, precision should be interpreted alongside the interval.

The scoring program checks the hashes of the raw data, protocol, data preparation, training data, convergence verification, backbone projection, and locked predictions. The raw trial file is `ch_t_bi_all_acc.rda`, with SHA-256 `7ed2a73a193b3df6417d86bf18a095905dcf70db6662b9bd0d4656d5a5f52e55`. Require verified end-of-text states for all 12 passages, six horizontally laid-out passages for each of the 60 readers, four distinct true-or-false trials for every eligible reader–passage cell, and exactly 1,440 total trials. The procedure stops if any of these conditions fail.
