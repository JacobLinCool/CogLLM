# Report the joint training results of publicly observed human observations

The first round trained three readout groups on frozen Qwen3.5-2B. The shared-module development set yielded the lowest average negative log-likelihood, while individual readouts performed better at more endpoints. Adding the matching effect loss increased both the average negative log-likelihood and the average effect error. These results derive from the development set already used to select the training round. The three independent formal validation families remain unscored. ## Results from three groups with identical data and identical budgets

Each group used six training epochs and 882 readout updates. Average NLL equal weighting was computed across 69 eval endpoints, with continuous variables standardized using training fold units. Lower values indicate higher average likelihood assigned by the model to actual responses. This average includes the log of the continuous probability density, with no shared zero or percentage scale. Effect error is the absolute error across 36 development pairs, each divided by its target scale before equal weighting averaging, where zero is optimal. Direction hits exclude one human pair with zero difference.

| Readout | Selected round | Average NLL ↓ | develop effect mean absolute error ↓ | effect direction hit ↑ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Each endpoint is trained independently. | 6 | 1.05881 | 0.06095 | 29／35 |
| Joint training of shared modules | 6 | 1.05376 | 0.06952 | 29／35 |
| Shared modules and matching effect loss | 5 | 1.32172 | 0.07378 | 26／35 |

Shared relative independence reduced NLL at 25 endpoints and increased NLL at 44. PLABA's plain-text rewriting raised human sentence-level self-assessed understanding by an average of 0.427. the shared group estimated increase was 0.435, and the independent group estimated increase was 0.498. For the shared group, the per-response distribution NLL at the same endpoint rose from 1.313 to 1.347. This endpoint simultaneously exhibited a relatively precise average manipulation effect and a relatively poor response distribution fit. The effect loss used 36 difference values recalculated with training fold weighting, with weights fixed at 0.5. Results support the completion of this group's weight comparison. Remaining weights, repeated seeds, statistical uncertainty, and formal group validation are yet to be tested. Both groups attained their best NLL at epoch six, and the effect-only group's minimum was at epoch five. This comparison used a fixed training budget and leaves convergence open. The frozen backbone received zero weight updates. ## Causal forward and computational manifest

The same NVIDIA L4 made one causal forward pass for each of 27,173 inputs and read 152,378 data-aligned stops. For each of 5 inputs, one truncation and one continuation replacement were checked. All 10 tests met the predefined tolerance. The three readouts share these representations and require no text generation. The original observations determine the distribution of stops. The current report covers each study's word, sentence, region, or postreading endpoints. Direct observations of the same question across individual tokens remain unavailable. [Prefix checks](../../../research/results/union-training-l4/causality.json) · [Feature extraction receipts](../../../research/results/union-training-l4/extraction.json)

Representation extraction took 20 minutes 12 seconds. three readout fits and sealed snapshot predictions took approximately 31 seconds. L4 self-configuration to confirmed release took 23 minutes 36 seconds. After reconstructing the development package from the source family, all file hashes matched exactly. An independent program used sealed snapshot predictions to recompute 69 endpoints and 36 paired effect terms. All result files were validated via download hash verification.
