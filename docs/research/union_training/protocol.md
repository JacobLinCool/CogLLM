# Public human-observed joint training round one

Using the fixed causal prefix representation of Qwen3.5-2B, we evaluate whether a shared small-state module can improve human response prediction across multiple reading observations. We compare the paired effect of training endpoints separately, jointly supervised shared modules sequentially, and jointly sequentially augmented shared modules. The formal validation study is conducted on a separate sealed snapshot. Its original text, human trials, and effect estimates are excluded from this Colab. ## Development materials and inputs

Prepare programmatic cross-checking of formal lists against local source hashes, using published raw materials, human-validated responses, and original study scales to construct endpoints. Retain observations that have been successfully ingested and passed source, material, role, and causal reading stop checks. Compile manifests separately documenting ingested data, role counts, and responses excluded due to misalignment with reading stops. Assign materials to families for development and training data. Group by identical text, identical source version, and confirmed shared material links. Sources identifiable by only one material family are listed as training samples and do not possess their own development generalization estimates. Cross-fold shared reader data only supports claims regarding unseen materials. Formal validation remains retained at the entire study family level.

Responses were evaluated based on visible text in the original task. Questions and available options were included only when visible in the study. PLABA options used fixed lexical order of content, with outputs indicating correctness (yes/no), while retaining the limitation of the published version regarding the absence of actual random presentation order. Word-level and segment-level responses were read from the determinable Qwen token boundary readout. This supports the first exposure at each position and the specified post-exposure response. The complete exposure history during reader rereading requires the actual display sequence.

## Model and development comparison

L4 employs FP32 Qwen3.5-2B with a fixed revision to perform causal forward passes on each input, while sampling to compare exact prefixes against truncated or substituted continuations. Shared and independent models utilize identical residual modules and observation heads. Classification employs negative log-likelihood based on human response counts, while continuous variables use Gaussian likelihoods with matching units. Continuous endpoint means and scales are estimated solely from training data. Joint training alternates endpoints, matching budgets, data, and initialization. After freezing the backbone, a single cache group is reused across three model sets.

Pairing effects were derived from the predicted expectation difference at the same reading stop or task end as in the original study's homogeneous condition. training utilized only training materials and observed calculations for the target. Pairing materials were weighted equally within groups, and effects were normalized according to the original scale. Retained identical sequential data and readout capacity before and after adding the effect. The development set selected iterative points via negative log-likelihood at each endpoint, reporting separately for each endpoint the direction, magnitude, probability error, and pairing effect on the original scale. This round of inspection examined joint learning of registered endpoints, while formal sealed-snapshot study scoring was deferred until after candidate and endpoint threshold fixation.
