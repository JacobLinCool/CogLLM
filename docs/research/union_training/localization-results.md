# In Round 31, on materials with location markers observed by humans, was the location correctly identified?

In the three-way development set evaluation, the in-sentence ranking, post-editing circle selection AP, and first report whole-document similarity at round 31 outperformed round 12, while the three metrics remained behind their respective simple baselines. The most pronounced temporal misalignment occurred in the Frick sentence pair. Humans required reaching sentence 14 before a substantial number of first report contradictions emerged, whereas the model had concentrated nearly all first report probability on earlier sentences. The evaluation followed the existing development split, sealed snapshot prediction, and original study positions, requiring no re-run of the backbone. This batch of development data was used to select round 31, and the full held-out evidence is reported previously. ## Explainability at the sentence level

The PLABA development split has five material families, 16 full texts, 133 sentences, and 399 human ratings. Ranking uses 394 within-article sentence pairs whose mean human ratings differ. Calculate the metric within each article and average articles equally. Model ties earn half credit. For the hardest sentence, tied human labels are allowed and a model tie receives fractional credit based on how many tied selections are correct. The length baseline fits log word count to mean human ratings for 476 training sentences. The epoch-31 readout uses causal states at the rated sentence end and at the end of the full text, matching sentence-level self-ratings made after full-article reading. ## post-hoc word-level positioning

SCARECROW has 238 development continuations, 22,091 evaluable tokens, and 219,978 valid annotation opportunities, with 2,147 of these containing contradictory selections. Average Precision was computed per continuation, using tokens selected by at least one annotator as positive examples, and then averaged with equal weighting across the 110 continuations that contained both positive and negative examples. The difference between Round 31 and Round 12 is +0.060, suggesting a descriptive 95% confidence interval for family-pair resampling of [+0.030, +0.089]. The difference relative to the baseline of word length and position is −0.096, with interval [−0.145, −0.047]. The binary NLL for each valid selection was also minimized against the word length and position baseline. Both models estimated posterior selections at the end-of-word and end-of-document states. ## First detection of inter-sentential temporal contradiction

Frick has four story development sets, eight versions, and 384 trials. For each story set, the 14th sentence is identical across groups, with only an earlier sentence replaced to make the target sentence consistent or contradictory with the preceding context. The first-report probability is calculated with all readers as the denominator, while the conditional probability is computed using the set of readers who have not yet reported up to that sentence as the risk set. The model's 99.86% first-report quality falls before the target sentence, whereas the human versions are 8.89% and 13.24%, respectively. Among readers who have not reported by the target sentence, 6/164 (3.66%) first report in the consistent version and 120/177 (67.80%) in the contradictory version. After weighting by the same reader distribution at round 31, the estimated values for the two groups are 42.06% and 42.05%. Misalignment occurs simultaneously in early reporting and the conditional difference in the text preceding the target sentence. [per-trial, position, and risk set](../../../research/results/union-observations-localization/frick.json) · [reproduction script](../../../research/union_training/diagnose_frick_locations.py)

When we realigned the effects to the original stimuli, we found that the earlier joint-training diagnostics had computed two Frick target effects at sentence 13. The stimulus index started at 0 and the trial sentence numbers at 1. We corrected the source builder and recomputed both effects at the correct sentence 14 using archived weights. With stories weighted equally, the human inconsistency-minus-consistency difference in first reports was +55.39 percentage points. The model's difference was approximately 0. For all reports, the human difference was +61.64 percentage points and the model difference was −0.14 percentage points. Replacing the two misaligned effects in the summary gives mean standardized absolute errors across 36 effects of 0.11943 at epoch 12 and 0.11827 at epoch 31. The joint-training first-event loss uses complete sentence-level trajectories. The correction changed the position indices for the paired effects. ### Frick readout the fit at overall early stopping

The 1,416 reader trials in the training set were aggregated into 32 stimulus paths. With a batch size of 256, this dedicated readout undergoes only one gradient update per round. The weights at round 31 correspond to 4,402 total updates, including 31 direct updates to the first-reported readout coefficients. The model's conditional report probability across sentences in the training set remains concentrated between 41.94% and 42.59%. For instance, the human training rate for the first reportable sentence is 6/1,416 (0.42%), while each model version estimates approximately 42%. [Joint batching and early stopping](../../../research/union_training/fit_observations.py) · [Observation and model retention](../../../research/results/union-observations-localization/frick.json)

Perform an additional single-intercept diagnosis on the sealed snapshot prediction. Reconstruct conditional report logits by dividing the first-report probability per sentence by the survival probability entering that sentence, and add a constant selected from training stories to all logits. The optimal training offset at round 31 is −3.0921. After applying to development stories, the overall NLL drops from 7.4814 to 1.9648 nats/trial, with the sentence-order baseline at 1.7380. After calibration, the estimated first-report probabilities for both consistent and contradictory groups at sentence 14 are both approximately 2.16%, compared to observed values of 3.33% and 58.82%. Thus, reducing the overall report rate substantially improves likelihood, while the within-sentence prefix effect remains to be read out. Replay 120 Frick end-of-sequence sentences from the original L4 batch representation. After 12-dimensional `interpretation` shared states through `tanh`, 92.36% of the 1,440 absolute values exceed 0.99. Following story 11's context switch, the Qwen normalized representation for the same sentence 14 relative to L2 has a difference of 0.181, and the final readout condition report probability shifts from 42.07% to 41.99%. These values indicate that context modifications propagate to the backbone end-of-sequence representation, where the current shared transformation and readout map it into nearly identical probabilities. The representation gap itself requires marked validation to link to semantic contradiction. In Round 31, the first readout bias was −0.01767, with a sum of absolute coefficients equal to 0.33759. Since each `tanh` state lies in the range [−1, 1], the conditional readout probability lower bound for any input is `sigmoid(−0.01767 − 0.33759)`, approximately 41.21%, with an upper bound of approximately 57.93%. This sealed snapshot weight fails to express the 0.42% training baseline rate of the first reportable sentence across the entire state range permitted by the architecture. This limitation arises from the joint effect of the readout coefficients, bias, and bounded states.
