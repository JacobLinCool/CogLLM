# PLABA end-of-sentence readout for external ranking validation of German self-assessment

This test examines whether frozen PLABA end-readout rankings are preserved on German single clauses. German learners of TextComplexityDE view complete single clauses and rate both sentence complexity and their own comprehension. The former scale ranges from 1 = very easy to 7 = very complex. the latter from 1 = fully understood to 7 = not understood at all. The release provides valid counts and means for each endpoint per sentence. [Research hypothesis] End-state status improves the ranking of both human self-ratings relative to the control estimating from sentence length alone. [Original study §§II.B–C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.07733) · [Author release and fields](https://github.com/babaknaderi/TextComplexityDE#repo-details)

## sealed snapshot materials and models

We used the `source.csv`, `ratings.csv`, and `LICENSE` raw bytes published by the authors. All 1,000 scored rows received a source sentence ID. We excluded 100 rows flagged as `Leichte Sprache` lacking a Wikipedia source link, and 125 rows where the sentence strings differed between the two release versions. This retained 775 original sentences with identical full-text strings across both releases, belonging to 23 German Wikipedia articles. We filtered out duplicates using Unicode NFKC normalization, case folding, and retention of words and numbers. We kept only unique sentences that did not share the same normalized word order with the 609 published sentences from PLABA. The provenance of source articles, article titles, and links were verified via the author fields in each column. This dataset was not post-hoc selected based on human ratings or model performance. [Preprocessing script](../../../research/stage6/textcomplexityde_transfer.py) · [Author raw materials](https://github.com/babaknaderi/TextComplexityDE/tree/master/TextComplexityDE19)

The author database is licensed under MIT. Wikipedia sentences retain their original text and source links, with attribution and use under the CC BY-SA conditions of the source text. Model inputs include plain text, article authorship, and previously fixed weights. Human scores and vote counts are kept locally independent. Utilize the existing PLABA `qwen_plus_length` ordinal readout, freeze the original `weights` array. Contrast with length readouts fitted using identical PLABA development data, identical five-fold cross-validation likelihoods, and L2 regularization. Maintain character and token scales consistent with PLABA. Input each German sentence individually to Qwen3.5-2B, perform a single FP32 causal forward pass, and extract the final token state corresponding to the precise alignment. Rank the two readouts by their 1–5 expected scores. This sealed snapshot test fixes both readouts without adjusting the model, calibrating, or candidate set based on external evaluation. ## Predefined scoring

Group by source article. Within each article, compare only sentence pairs with different human mean scores, converting model scores to difficulty by direction of difference, and counting ties as half-points. Agreement ranges from 0–1, with higher being better. Articles with at least one qualifying comparison pair receive equal-weight averaging. At least 95% of target sentences must have exact reading stop and valid sealed snapshot predictions. Using seed 43, perform 10,000 resampled pairings per article. The test passes if the 95% lower bound of the percentile difference between the "end-readout minus length-readout" agreement at each endpoint exceeds zero.

This study employed German learners, Wikipedia single-sentence corpora, and group self-ratings. The conclusions drawn are limited to the relative gain in sentence length achieved by the two ranking methods on this material. PLABA's English discourse context, 1–5 probability distribution, and rewrite effect magnitude each require corresponding human observation. We do not calibrate across different scales, nor do we treat the mean scores of non-native learners as ground truth for individual prediction.

## The availability scope of other data

MedReadMe instructs annotators to first rank six sentences, assign a CEFR reading level required for each, and optionally open adjacent sentences. The study's 1,924 development and test scores represent the average of two annotators. The actual author CSV contains 4,504 rows, comprising 2,580 training, 784 development, and 1,140 test samples, which is 16 fewer training rows than described in the paper. The author CSV was used to verify PLABA's 609 sentences against full-text normalization, punctuation removal and reordering, PLABA full-text substring matching, and 5-gram coverage. No identical sentences or 5-gram coverage exceeding 25% were found. The CSV lacks individual IDs, ranking groups, discourse, or original document pairing keys, and also lacks metadata regarding the surrounding context. Consequently, the article family and actual exposure of single sentences as described in the original study cannot be confirmed from the release archive. The authors request citation and provide a data download link. Cross-source text redistribution conditions must be verified individually. This data can be used for secondary analyses of CEFR readability, which is distinct from understanding self-assessment and original family held-out. [MedReadMe §2, Appendix C/J](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12225841/) · [Author Release Archive](https://github.com/chaojiang06/medreadme/tree/main/dataset)

Iavarone et al. report 943 target sentences in a three-sentence window at the end, where readers first view the visible context before assigning difficulty ratings. This aligns with the causal reading stop visible span. Materials are drawn from the Wall Street Journal Penn Treebank, and TextComplexityDE's publicly available Wikipedia sentences and article attributions facilitate easier verification. Štajner et al.'s five-level data presents multiple paraphrased versions of the same sentence in adjacent positions. Both resources allow investigation of their specific visible conditions. This sealed snapshot includes only two self-assessed endpoints from the German Wikipedia.
