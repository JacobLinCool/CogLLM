## GENTLE cross-sentence anaphora

Fixed GENTLE UD r2.19 official orthographic variant test set. Of 26 documents, 14 (34 manually annotated instances) met the preliminary cross-paragraph criteria. Formal candidates, reading stops, and exclusion rules follow GUMBridge, scoring from the end of the manually annotated reference phrase to the end of the currently read eligible word. Candidates within the paragraph and earlier paragraphs are retained. Source text, document ID, and normalized source URL are cross-checked against the original GUM splits one by one. [Official Orthographic Variant Corpus](https://aclanthology.org/2023.law-1.17/) · [Fixed File and Alignment Audit](../../../research/results/gum-bridge-gentle-audit.json)

Fixed to the literal GUM and Qwen readout weights, scale, and projection. For position, literal, and literal-plus-Qwen groups, report equal-weighted NLL, first-hit rate, and average reciprocal rank. The new corpus validation requires at least 12 evaluable articles, with the Qwen NLL minus literal NLL paired 95% bootstrap confidence interval upper bound less than zero. The original GUM test's 20-article threshold is retained for original decision-making. This endpoint measurement captures the preceding position induced by manually calibrated phrases, without measuring whether readers find it difficult or when they resolve the ambiguity. ## Turkish word-segmented sentence pairs

Prefer valid lemmas sequentially using all v5 author-released data. Construct a graph of lemma pairs appearing within the same sentence, partition the resulting components, and generate a sealed snapshot. For formal evaluation, retain the endpoints where the main text can be reconstructed into a complete sentence from the app's fixed sentence and token boundaries, and record the excluded count and actual denominator. The primary endpoint is the probability that a sentence is selected in an easier choice condition, reporting trial NLL and Brier. [Original observations and available denominators](turkish-sentence-source.md) · [v5 release](https://zenodo.org/records/22775520)

Compare the difference using only the presentation intercept, surface baseline, and fixed-app ease alone, and the surface baseline plus fixed-app ease. The surface baseline uses character and syllable counts calculated by the authors, as well as character counts and whitespace-split token counts calculated directly by the App on input. The former follows author annotations, while the latter includes punctuation marked within the original text and separated by whitespace. This ensures that models incorporating App scores and the surface baseline share identical raw text length information. All four counts are computed as `log1p` minus the subsequent sentence. All differences are scaled by the pooled sample's root mean square, preserving zero differences and exchangeable symmetry. Ease differences are constrained to non-negative coefficients. Original App sentence-level weights are not updated. instead, only the pooled sample's scale and intercept are fitted to select probabilities. The fitting penalty is L2 0.01, with no penalty applied to the intercept. After author quality filtering, the held-out subset is reported separately with the same sealed snapshot probability, without additional fitting. [App sentence-level scoring method](../../../research/gradio_app/causal_plaba.py#L121)

The primary comparison is the held-out NLL difference for surface + App ease relative to the surface baseline. The requirement is at least six connected components with valid held-out choices and at least 100 distinct evaluable sentence pairs, with the upper bound of the 95% bootstrap confidence interval for the reader and component double group being less than zero. Resample 10,000 times, independently resampling the reader and component each time, with trial weights equal to the product of their sampling counts. Lower NLL indicates higher probability for the human-selected sentence. This experiment measures relative reading ease. since the sentence pairs did not control other linguistic features to be identical, the results cannot be interpreted as a causal effect of a specific syntactic rewrite.
