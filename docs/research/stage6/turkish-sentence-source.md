# Turkish sentence-level reading difficulty data

The Karasekreter et al. v5 release includes Turkish sentences, human difficulty ratings for pairs of sentences, and individual comprehension questions. This corpus can be used to verify which of two sentences with equal word counts is perceived as easier by humans. Author data and documentation are licensed under CC BY 4.0. code is under MIT. The authors note that permission to redistribute sentences has been obtained from the corpus compiler, but individual underlying text rights have not been verified. [v5 fixed release](https://zenodo.org/records/22775520) · [author license file](https://zenodo.org/records/22775520/files/LICENSE?download=1)

In the workbook, 787 out of 800 sentences matched exactly, sentence-by-sentence, with 22,490 human responses. Additionally, 2,960 instances involved two selectable sentences that both linked to the same reader within the same task. Of these, 2,844 instances involved sentence pairs with identical word counts, encompassing 1,589 sentence pairs and 779 sentences. Connecting sentences within the same dataset using equal-word-count sentence pairs yielded 39 connected components, with a maximum of 51 sentences per component. These components can be used to split the entire dataset, preventing the same sentence from appearing in both development and evaluation sets. These counts have been recalculated according to the publication table and have not yet been filtered by individual model trial rules. [Fields and Publication Denominator](https://zenodo.org/records/22775520/files/README.md?download=1)

The primary measure is the probability that the first presented sentence in a same-stem sentence pair is selected as the easier one. Candidate texts are processed forward one by one, predicting the sentence score difference and the presentation order term, while controlling for character and syllable count differences. The measure assesses whether the model can predict new sentence pairs. inference must account for repeated responses by the same reader and the multiple repetitions of sentences across pairs. The accuracy rate specified in the stem can serve as an additional measure. There are 40 actual stem types in T1, with the most frequent stem appearing in 324 sentences and 2,504 responses. The dataset includes stems and response correctness per response, but lacks complete option sets and answer keys. Sentences are drawn from the LexiTR corpus, without one-to-one paraphrased versions or human reports at the per-token level. [Stem types, materials, and original columns](https://zenodo.org/records/22775520/files/README.md?download=1)
