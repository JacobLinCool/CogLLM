# English abstract rewriting held-out

Random readout experiments by Kerker et al. can test whether frozen PLABA end-of-sentence understanding, when aggregated into full English abstracts, can replicate the magnitude of human self-reported understanding rewrites. The study provides 12 social psychology source families, three abstract versions, and 166 native German-speaking university students. There were 1,992 reader × article combinations, of which 1,947 had complete understanding, confusion, and frustration ratings. Each reader viewed only one random version per article family, and viewed one academic abstract, one plain-language abstract with headings, and one plain-language abstract without headings in each block. Ratings were made after reading each abstract. The primary scale was 1–8 for abstract understandability. Confusion and frustration were each 1–5 post-reading mood reports. [Original paper Method, Table 1, Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.18898) · [Author release and data dictionary](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/A9QSY)

## Pre-fixed scoring

**Primary control** is the plain-language abstract without a subheading minus the academic abstract. The primary human endpoint is `vst`, where higher scores indicate greater subjective understanding. The mixed model in Kerker et al.'s original paper Table 4 estimates this control as +0.542 points on a 1–8 scale. both conditions may vary simultaneously in syntax, vocabulary, content selection, and length. The new score uses published individual responses, with the control defined as the average difference within each family across the two arms, applying equal weighting across 12 families. This estimate is reported separately for each family's original paper mixed model control. Results are publicly available. the held-out test consists of individual responses and CogLLM predictions, with the research direction pre-determinable from the original paper. [Original paper H1, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.18898)

**Model Prediction** uses a fixed CogLLM service to generate candidates. Each summary undergoes a single causal forward pass with Qwen3.5-2B, retaining the full-text Punkt sentence segmentation from Gradio, and reads the ordered distribution `effect_calibrated_weights` of `perceived_understanding`. Scalar values are taken sentence-by-sentence as the expected value of the distribution. The baseline comparison uses the same PLABA-developed data fitted and sealed snapshot within the TextComplexityDE transfer package `length_only_weights`, applying identical sentence segmentation and sentence length normalization. The static sealed snapshot first validates candidates, inputs, and all service coordinates. Model exports are then sealed snapshot to capture complete sentence-level distributions, expected values, sentence boundaries, causal reading stops, and forward pass counts. The scoring program loads human scores only after verifying both sealed snapshots. [Fixed Candidate](../../../research/results/gradio-candidate-checkpoint/checkpoint.json) · [Sealed Snapshot Scoring Program](../../../research/stage6/kerwer_score.py) · [Sentence Length Baseline Reconstruction Method](../../../research/stage6/textcomplexityde_transfer.py)

If the probability score of sentence $s$ is $p_s(k)$, the summary prediction is $A=(1/S)\sum_{s=1}^S\sum_{k=1}^5 k p_s(k)$. The predicted difference $(A_{PLS}-A_{OSA})/4$ and the human difference $(\bar y_{PLS}-\bar y_{OSA})/7$ are scaled by their respective maximum variable ranges as units. Both scales have fixed endpoints. the summary scale is not fitted to a transformation, nor are sentence weights adjusted. Multiplying by 7 converts the model difference on this scale to "equivalent to how many points on the original study's scale." This comparison assumes an ordinal category interval, with the difference in questionnaire intent and sentence-end observation units remaining.

Report the same-direction ratio, absolute difference, and macro-average for the model versus sentence length across each family. A primary difference is considered significant when the model and human share the same direction, the human difference resampling interval excludes zero, and the normalized macro-average difference (model minus human) has a 90% resampling interval entirely contained within $[-0.5/7,0.5/7]$. This tolerance threshold corresponds to half a response category from the original questionnaire, serving as a pre-specified operational boundary without independent evidence of scale validity. Additionally, for the 12 families, assess whether the model shows paired improvement relative to sentence length in absolute difference. a claim of improvement is made only if the lower bound of the 95% resampling interval exceeds zero. Conduct 10,000 cross-group resamples, separately sampling readers and source families, while preserving all responses from the same reader and maintaining the three-arm shared-group structure within each family, re-estimating group-arm means at each iteration. Also report fixed-material reader resampling and leave-one-out sensitivity per family. The resampling intervals for the 12 families may be wider. point estimates will be reported for each family.

A white-label summary is retained in its original position in the version with labels, providing a prefix for subsequent body text visibility. Secondary structure alignment aggregates relative sentence coordinates within the same body paragraph. The first 12 body sentences of the labeled versions are merged with `Background` into a single service sentence. therefore, both arms of each family exclude this paired sentence, each retaining 181 body sentences. The 24 versions in the primary alignment comprise 277 body sentences, all aligned. Confusion and frustration maintain their respective scales and require a pre-fixed, independently validated readout before assessment.
