# Kerwer's English abstract reader background verification

Under the conditions of no reader and no study, we test whether adding pre-reading E-PA English ability scores improves the 1–8 level probability distribution of post-reading self-rated understanding of English abstracts. E-PA is completed by readers before reading any abstract. The original study reported an overall positive association for E-PA, with the interaction effect between ordinary and vernacular abstracts not reaching significance. Here, we reuse the same cohort of 166 readers and the 12 study families previously compared with rewritten versions. The new analysis estimates out-of-sample performance with a fixed sample. The scope of inference is limited to English abstracts and self-rated understanding scales within this study. [Original study Methods, Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.18898) · [Existing rewritten control](kerwer-article-holdout.md)

Articles are identified by family using published DOI. Families share a common hyphenated identifier across their standard abstracts, subheadlined vernacular abstracts, and unmarked vernacular abstracts. Readers are identified by their existing anonymous code. Of 1,947 readers with complete response data, each DOI-reader combination has exactly one random presentation version. The E-PA pretest is shared across the entire study. The primary readout for this experiment relies solely on this background. General familiarity is self-reported. no pretest measures were conducted for individual articles. [Public Data](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/A9QSY) · [Published Materials and Usage Conditions](kerwer-article-holdout.md#data-reconstruction-and-separation)

## Features and splitting

Retain the validated Gradio candidates and existing L4 prediction packets. The text score $A$ for each article is the certified understanding expectation value equal-weighted average of sentences, where each sentence's expectation value is computed from the existing 1–5 level distribution. Standard summaries and no-issue plain-language summaries retain complete sentences from the rewritten reference. headline plain-language summaries retain sentences from the existing headline reference with precise sentence coordinates. A total of 458 sentences were used, with 12 headline-inclusive sentences from the headline version retained in the model's observed text, whose scores are excluded from the text average. Sentence length baselines utilize the Unicode character count within the full text, the word count separated by whitespace, and the three summary versions. Two length features are taken from `log1p`. Summary versions use the standard summary as the reference. [Frozen Sentence Boundary](../../../research/results/kerwer-transfer-protocol.json) · [L4 Packet Validation](../../../research/results/gradio-candidate-l4/run-verification.json)

Readers were split into five folds, and DOI families into four folds. Each axis was sorted by the SHA-256 hash of its UTF-8 string, and then ranked by modulo the sort order. Strings were prefixed with `kerwer-background-audit-reader:` plus the reader code, and `kerwer-background-audit-doi:` plus the DOI. Each of the 20 cross-grid cells was fitted using trials where both the reader fold and DOI fold were different, and tested using trials where both folds were identical. Trials matching only one fold were excluded from the fit for that cell. Each trial with a complete response appeared in exactly one test cell. Folds and identifiers were used for data selection and validation. For each cell, an identity intersection check was performed on both axes before scoring.

Continuous text features were estimated using the mean and population standard deviation from each distinct summary version in the grid training data with equal weighting. E-PA was used once per grid training reader to estimate the mean and population standard deviation. When the standard deviation was zero, it was set to 1. These scale-transformed test data were standardized, and interactions were computed. Test readers were only provided with their E-PA values. Fixed candidate raw text scores and original document coordinates were retained for verification. Human ratings and anonymous reader files were retained in the local research data directory.

## Readout and blank background

Fit each readout using the same eight-category proportional-odds model. For $k=1,\ldots,7$, define $P(Y\leq k\mid x)=\sigma(\tau_k-\beta^\top x)$ and obtain category probabilities from differences of adjacent cumulative probabilities. The thresholds are strictly increasing and the coefficient vector has no separate intercept. Fit by minimizing mean negative log likelihood across training trials plus $0.01\lVert\beta\rVert^2$. Thresholds are unpenalized. Initialize slopes at zero and thresholds at the logits of cumulative training proportions in each cell. Parameterize the thresholds in increasing order and solve with L-BFGS-B for at most 400 iterations, with `ftol=1e-12` and `gtol=1e-7`. All competing readouts use the same solver, cells, and valid trials.

| Readout | It can be seen that the input |
| --- | --- |
| L | Two full-text lengths and abstract versions |
| LP | L pre-read E-PA |
| T | L frozen corpus comprehension score $A$ |
| TP | T pre-read E-PA |
| TPI | TP plus standardized E-PA and standardized $A$ interaction term |

The blank background uses the actual E-PA distribution of the trained reader. For each article, the conditional probability is computed once for each of these readers' E-PA values, then averaged with equal weighting. TP and TPI each have corresponding blank background versions. This procedure defines the model output in the absence of background. When replaying the test grid with full blank input, the same grid's same summary version must yield the same distribution.

## Pre-specified scoring and thresholds

The primary metric is the normalized ranked probability score (RPS) for each test trial. For the cumulative probability at levels 1 through 7, the squared difference is computed relative to the metric $1[Y\leq k]$, and the mean is taken. The range is 0–1, where lower is better. All held-out trials are weighted equally. Secondary reporting includes the average absolute error of the negative log-likelihood and the predicted expected value. Calibration is performed at seven cumulative cutpoints, each using fixed five probability bins `[0,.2)`, `[.2,.4)`, `[.4,.6)`, `[.6,.8)`, `[.8,1]`. Each bin compares the absolute difference between the average predicted probability and the actual occurrence proportion, weighted by the number of trials within the bin, and then averaged across the seven cutpoints. This error metric has a range of 0–1, where lower is better.

Without adjusting for the held-out prediction, compute the difference against the two-axis paired bootstrap confidence intervals. In each iteration, resample 166 readers and 12 DOIs with uniform probability, where the trial weight is the product of the two occurrence counts, and all methods share the same weights. Fix 10,000 iterations with random seed 20261121. During resampling, use the extrapolated predictions from the sealed snapshot without refitting. Report the sensitivity of the difference to the sequential removal of each DOI, as there are only 12 independent source families in the article axis.

- Additive background gain is measured as the RPS of T minus the RPS of TP, with the 95% confidence interval lower bound required to be greater than zero. The cumulative calibration error of TP minus the error of T must have its 95% confidence interval upper bound below 0.01. The value 0.01 represents a fixed cumulative probability error tolerance, equivalent to one percentage point.
- Text and background jointly contribute. additionally, validate that the 95% confidence interval lower bound of LP's RPS minus TP's RPS exceeds zero. The interaction term is validated by TP's RPS minus TPI's RPS, with the 95% confidence interval lower bound exceeding zero, and the same calibration tolerance applied to TP. Report these two results separately.
- The blank-background variants were each compared to T. The increase in RPS and the 95% upper confidence bound of the increase in cumulative calibration error must both be below 0.01. This set of operations permits a difference to be used for checking whether an observable degradation occurs in the absence of a background.

All predictions must satisfy full trial coverage, finite and non-negative class probabilities summing to 1, and verification that both axes of the corresponding cell have been observed at least once. This document seals the snapshot of the preparation and acceptance criteria, which are only validated after fitting and scoring. The analysis level is discourse self-assessment. results cannot be directly extrapolated to sentence-level understanding, word-level load, or reader domain prior knowledge.
