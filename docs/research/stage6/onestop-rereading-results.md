# OneStop main text and rereading condition held-out results

**Qwen's end-segment state plus the reread condition failed to pass the pre-specified acceptance criterion.** The interactive readout hit rate increased by 5.06 percentage points with rereading, while the human estimate increased by 4.00 percentage points. The direction of the difference was consistent, but the precision of the estimate was insufficient to confirm it fell within ±3 percentage points. There was also insufficient evidence that the per-item Brier score outperformed the statistical prior using only the reread state. The sealed snapshot dataset comprises 1,294 development pairs and 647 held-out pairs. The first two batches were used for fitting, while the third batch retained 10 articles and 60 readers. The held-out evaluation utilized 216 paragraph-version × item ID combinations, both with observed states, totaling 1,078 responses. Both the Brier score and negative log-likelihood are minimized, with 0 being optimal. The two loss metrics in the table are weighted equally by article, and the re-read difference is expressed in percentage points of the correct-response probability. From the Gradio candidate's original CUDA encoder, we extracted 69 deduplicated corpora, 53,566 tokens, and 371 end-of-segment states. Each corpus was sampled once for causal forward pass. Additionally, 12 controls were performed on truncated or substituted suffixes, with a maximum relative state difference of 0.00000108. The L4 extraction phase took 61.6 seconds, encompassing model initialization, validation, and file writing. The CUDA peak configuration was approximately 7.19 GiB. After reclaiming files, it was confirmed that L4 had been released. The local machine only reads the extracted vector fit, taking approximately 0.06 seconds, with a peak resident memory of about 93 MiB. The second fit and scoring weights, predictions, sealed snapshot, and results are bitwise identical, and all Brier losses were recomputed.
