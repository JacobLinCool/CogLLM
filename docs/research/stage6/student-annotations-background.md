# German sentence-level reader background data audit

Thome, Hertweck, and Conrad report a five-point scale of perceived difficulty for German sentences presented in isolation to German high school students. Readers first complete a questionnaire. The data include the language used at home, self-rated German proficiency, and German test scores, linked via a common reader identifier to their sentence-by-sentence ratings. Scores range from 1 (very easy to understand) to 5 (very difficult to understand). This endpoint allows direct examination of subjective difficulty ratings across different readers for the same sentence. [Original paper §3, Table 1](https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/download/886/251) · [Author-fixed release](https://github.com/Boshl/StudentAnnotations/tree/db164a0365d1398473ec7c8be4a211e03c7b9286)

The original dataset comprises 193 readers, 1,697 distinct sentences, and 3,954 ratings. Compared against the two complete raw lists from TextComplexityDE, 554 sentences matching Unicode normalization, case, and word order were removed. Additionally, near-duplicates containing at least 80% five-word n-grams were identified and excluded without adding new removals. The remaining 1,143 distinct raw sentences and 3,160 ratings align with the original paper's reporting for the career guidance subset. The working set covers 192 readers. Sentence identifiers from the guidance source were not released with the ratings. The accompanying data release includes a university department table cited in the original study but lacks sentence-to-original-page correspondence for this batch. Currently, unseen sentences and readers can only be isolated. external validation at the source article level requires additional construction. Within the same sentence, the difference in responses between the two groups can be estimated, reducing the variance caused by different sentences observed across groups. This comparison of the three groups represents an observational difference. The language used at home, self-rated ability, and topic prior knowledge each have distinct measurement implications. The authors' original model performed an 80:20 split on the dataset, and the reported RMSE did not establish performance on identical sentences and readers held out simultaneously. ## Executable readout contrast

Before seeing model results, fix four crossed reader and normalized-sentence folds. For each test cell, fit only on the intersection of the other three reader folds and other three sentence folds. Each of the sixteen cells contains 152–247 observed ratings, and each rating appears once in its test cell. All versions of a normalized sentence enter the same fold, while inference retains each original sentence. Run Qwen3.5-2B once and use the sentence-final state. Pass background only to the downstream readout. Specify three matched comparisons in advance.

1. The additive ordering of sentence length, word count, and language group readout, checking for simple, interpretable group and length effects.
2. Suffixed representation, length, and language group additive readout, checking text signals.
3. The interaction of readout with sentence-final representation and language group was examined to determine whether different reader groups exhibit unequal difficulty differences across sentences. The held-out probability of the additive model was computed by mixing group proportions observed during training, and performance with a no-issue background was also reported.

The primary metric is the negative log-likelihood of human-selected ratings, and the secondary metric is the cumulative probability squared error. The primary effect is the within-sentence average difficulty of the 179 sentences in the German group relative to other language groups, with equal weighting across sentences. For each human response, the comparison is made against the out-of-fold prediction for the same sentence. first, within-sentence group averages are computed, and then the difference between the two groups is estimated. An interaction candidate must improve the sequential negative log-likelihood on a double held-out set relative to the additive model, with the paired 95% confidence interval upper bound for the difference below zero. Magnitude acceptance requires the 95% confidence interval for the human group difference to exclude zero, the model direction to be consistent, and the difference in magnitude between the model and human estimates to have a 90% confidence interval fully contained within ±0.5 points. The tolerance difference corresponds to half a response category on the original scale, serving as a pre-specified operational boundary.

Draw 10,000 independent weighted resamples of the two axes of readers and normalized sentences, where the weight of each reader's trial and each normalized sentence's original variant is shared across resamples, enabling comparison of each sentence's observed group within every resample. When estimation precision is insufficient, retain the difference between sentence-level and average estimates along with their confidence intervals. This comparison validates German sentence-level group conditioning, while English abstracts and personal knowledge require distinct data and validation.

## Extracted input from the sealed snapshot

The fixed tokenizer for Qwen3.5-2B has been validated on 1,143 original sentence endings locally. Every Unicode reading stop aligns, with original full-text token IDs matching those of the isolated prefix. The input comprises 33,745 tokens, with the single longest sentence containing 101 tokens. The Colab L4 enclosure includes `id`, `text`, and `stops`, containing the extraction input, reading stop coordinate list, and respective file hashes. Formal extraction still requires verification of the reading stop and causal prefix using the fixed Transformers and model revision. The authors provide the data for research purposes. The original CSV remains on the local machine. Reader keys, language groups, hyphens, trial mappings, and 1–5 point responses are stored separately on the local machine and linked by the trial key during readout fitting and scoring. The extracted packet fully excludes these columns and does not include personal fields from the original data. This study involves only data organization and tokenizer verification. no model representations or readout fitting have been extracted yet.
