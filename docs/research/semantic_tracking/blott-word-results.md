# Locate using disambiguation cues from the body text only

In Blott 2021's 46 unseen sentence frames and 92 sentences, Qwen's adjacent-word state ranked the researcher-placed disambiguation word first in **69.6%** of cases, the literal model in **39.1%**, and the model using only state-change quantities in **42.4%**. Full-state performance improved over two fixed NLL thresholds. Per-word raw scores can be computed upon reading the word. Human judgments of two years were obtained at the sentence end, so this localization validation targets the researcher-specified stimulus position. NLL is the negative log-probability of the candidate marked by the researcher, with 0 being the ideal value. The NLL improvement of the complete state relative to the literal state is **0.606**, with a 95% confidence interval of **[0.483, 0.738]**, and the relative state change is **0.546 [0.432, 0.664]**. In 1,152 comparisons across the same fold-out, same word order, and different sentence frames, the win rate for the complete state is 97.4%, while the literal model is 94.7%. After further restricting to cases with identical character length, only 6 sentence frames from 60 comparisons remain, with win rates of 93.3% and 92.5%, respectively. This smaller group provides descriptive results. [Ranking and Comparison Denominator](../../../research/results/blott-word-l4/results.json)

All 88 sentences from 2023 provide disambiguation cues at the final word. The hit rate of the first position in the held-out backtracking test for the complete state is 93.2%. Both taking the last word and the word-order model trained in 2021 achieve 100% hits. The comparison of the final key uses 108 readers, 44 sentence frames, and 1,558 reader-pair matches. After aggregating the readout states, the probability estimate for the unambiguous Sense increases by **0.26 percentage points**, and the human score is **16.33**. The complete state shows an NLL relative improvement of 0.00074 nats/key in literal aggregation, with a 95% confidence interval of [−0.00029, 0.00179], and a relative improvement of 0.00207 [−0.00082, 0.00510] in uniform aggregation. neither exceeds the threshold. When extracting states using the author's marked cues, the old key prediction can be reproduced, with a maximum logit difference of $2.12\times 10^{-7}$. ## First prompt

Additionally, on the 2021 held-out sentence frames, test the first word crossing the threshold. For each fold, the model was fitted, threshold calibrated, and test sentence frames were mutually exclusive, with the calibration target being the combined early-prompt rate across both versions preceding the author-marked prompt at 10% per sentence frame. The proportion of complete states where the first prompt fell exactly on the anchor span was **53.3% (49/92)**, literal was **26.1% (24/92)**, and the state change was **33.7% (31/92)**. Complete states exceeded both controls by **27.2 [13.0, 41.3]** and **19.6 [7.6, 32.6]** percentage points, respectively. Of 46 sentence frames, 4 had early prompts in at least one version, yielding a point estimate of 8.7% with an operational upper bound of 20.8%, failing to reach the 10% threshold. Since each fold reused partial material for fitting and calibration, this upper bound is used to interpret this batch of results. None of the three methods from 2023 prompted across 88 sentences, and the 2021 threshold did not demonstrate cross-study applicability. One Colab L4 ran one forward pass for each of 276 sentences, extracting 4,533 reading stops and 3,981 scorable adjacent-word updates. All 12 backbone causal checks passed. An independent recalculation verified the Unicode offsets and Qwen token indices for 2,495 held-out word scores. It also reproduced the localization results, keypress intervals, thresholds, and 540 first-alert decisions. Removing the later text left the raw scores for already-read words bit-for-bit identical. Both scoring procedures produced bit-for-bit identical results when rerun. We confirmed that the L4 was released. The model was never loaded locally.
