# Independent validation of Czech disambiguation and residual misunderstanding

This validation separately compares two observable endpoints. The first check assesses whether the frozen English disambiguation readout reflects a condition difference after inserting the 4th word, when observing the same 7th word. The second check evaluates whether the Qwen representation can predict the reader's response to the original misinterpretation `qmis` at the end of the sentence. The original study's three responses are "yes", "no", and "unknown". Using only this question type, the three responses are retained as distinct categories, and key presses are not included in the denominator. The research question from the text is not used as a prompt input to the model. [Original research methods and hypotheses](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0288817) · [Public files and corpus verification](czech-garden-path-source.md)

## disambiguation at the same word position

Use the frozen English disambiguating-word readout sealed for the actual Gradio app. Run the same Qwen3.5-2B candidate once causally per sentence, feeding only original text and unlabeled word-end stops. Add the changes and state-difference blocks of the full readout to obtain their combined contribution $s_t$ to the raw score. Report sentence-length, word-position, and lexical blocks separately. Within each item and object animacy condition, word 4 is the only difference between versions. At word $t$, compute the between-version difference $D_t=s_{t,GP}-s_{t,control}$. The raw scores at shared words 2 and 3 must agree within the verified FP32 tolerance of 0.001. In Experiment 3, word 7 is the author-specified disambiguation point, words 5 and 6 are pre-cue controls, and words 8 and 9 describe subsequent changes. Word 7 has the same surface form in both conditions. The primary comparisons are $D_7$ and $D_7-(D_5+D_6)/2$. Both 95% intervals must have lower bounds above zero.

The localization endpoint weights the 48 items equally and weights the animate and inanimate conditions equally within each item. Intervals use grouped resampling by shared verb. As a descriptive result, we report whether the English-trained readout gives word 7 the highest score in the Czech sentence. Always choosing word 7 achieves a perfect score on this material. The original study's condition differences in word-by-word reading time identify regions of the stimuli worth inspecting. This comparison measures model responses to experimental stimuli. Direct word-level labels would be needed to determine whether readers changed their interpretation there. [Original experiment three reading times](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0288817#sec017) · [Sealed English readout](../../../research/results/blott-word-l4/word-cue-release.json)

## The three types of responses at the end of the sentence

Keep all four text versions of each item on the same side of the split. Group by the original verb at word 3 and order the groups by `sha256("cogllm-czech-garden-v1:" + verb)`. For the first 12 verb groups, fit only on keypresses from Experiment 2's whole-sentence presentation. Hold out the remaining 12 verb groups completely. Use readers in Experiment 3's word-by-word presentation for primary validation and report the same items' performance in Experiment 2 separately. The experiments share stimulus families. The verb-group split excludes all responses for the second half of the items from fitting. Objects and other words may repeat. Holdout results were excluded from weight selection, item selection, and tolerance setting.

All readout outputs the three-class probabilities at the end of the same sentence. The constant baseline assigns the same probability to each sentence. The literal baseline uses the log character lengths of the full sentence, the 4th word, and the 7th word, along with the training set class features of the last two characters of the 4th word. All training class components for unknown word tails are zero. Subsequent comparisons include only the end-of-sentence state, the difference in adjacent word states aggregated by English readout sorted by the original text, and the position contrast provided by the author's 7th word. The aggregation weights are the softmax of the frozen ranker logits of the evaluable words. The primary model uses the end-of-sentence state and the aggregated vector sorted by the sentence, sampled from the same forward pass. Known contrasts for the 7th word and the author's GP/livingness label study are reported separately. The positions common to the literal condition are given in a pre-fixed Czech sentence pattern. Probabilities are computed separately for each of the two sentence versions during prediction.

Fit with grouped multinomial cross-entropy using `no` as the reference class. Standardize continuous lexical features by the mean and standard deviation across equally weighted training sentences. Center each vector block and divide by the root mean square norm of the training vectors. Explicit categorical features retain their indicators. Apply $0.01\lVert w\rVert_2^2/2$ to all non-intercept weights. Seal training weights and held-out predictions before opening the Experiment 3 keypresses. The primary criterion requires the lower bounds of the 95% intervals for improvements in per-keypress NLL over the constant, lexical, and sentence-final-state baselines to be above zero. Also require the model's GP minus control contrast in “yes” responses to be positive and the whole 95% interval for its difference from the human contrast to lie within ±5 percentage points. First average the contrasts for animate and inanimate objects equally within each item, then weight the items equally. Use 10,000 crossed reader and verb bootstrap samples with seed 735972. Redraw any bootstrap sample without a complete pair for a selected item and record the original number of draws. The test data reproduce the stimuli and sentence-final responses. They contain no within-participant joint responses to all questions.

The authors directed the experimental tasks, response data, and analysis scripts to the public OSF project. For internal research analysis, only the public stimulus text was sent to the existing research execution environment, while human results remained on the local machine. The report retains the hash, summary, and reproducible processing scripts.
