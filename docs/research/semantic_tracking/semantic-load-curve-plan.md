# per-token unresolved semantic load training and validation

This round evaluates whether the text provides sufficient signals for CogLLM to estimate, position-by-position, the proportion of unresolved questions and the visible cues enabling their resolution. Human endpoints are drawn from publicly available existing observations. By default, only the text is input, with pre-reading background as an optional condition. Continuing from Qwen3.5-2B and Colab L4, the entire sequence is processed via a causal forward-backward pass, with all readouts sharing the representations obtained in this run.

## Curves and single forward pass

Let $h_t=f_\theta(x)_t$ denote the causal representation of reading until the $t$ th token. First, train the readout on a frozen Qwen. For each distinguishable query candidate $r$, construct an anchor representation using the visible source segment at that time, and evaluate the query's state transition at token $t$ using $h_t$ and the anchor. The initial states are unobserved, observed, unresolved, and resolved. Pending and reopened states are retained in the existing state layer and validated upon receiving observations that can distinguish the corresponding states.

$$
p_{t,r}=p_{0,r}\prod_{j=1}^{t} K_{j,r}, \qquad
L_t=\sum_r p_{t,r}(U).
$$

$K_{j,r}$ is the transition matrix, and $U$ denotes unresolved. All visible position transitions can be computed simultaneously, followed by multiplying the existing matrix prefix to obtain the state probability for each token, with no autoregressive decoding throughout. The $L_t$ in the first round represents the number of unresolved questions estimated by the model within the defined candidate range, with a weight of one per question. Two coexisting questions can be updated independently. When encountering a newline or the end of a paragraph, transitions are still determined by the main text and candidate states. Word difficulty, readability, and suspense are each retained on their original scales. [Current State Scan and Likelihood Replay](question-states-run.md)

Anchor points are evaluated in two groups. Given human anchor points that have already appeared, check when the question is resolved. The corpus group only proposes candidates from words or phrases visible at that time, independently evaluating candidate hit and closure. Candidates achieve stable identification upon first visibility. repeated references to the same question are deduplicated after determinability, while different questions within the same segment retain their own identification. The candidate budget is determined by the prefix at that time, and formal scores do not truncate candidates based on full-text ranking. Human anchors and answer ranges are saved in the scoring file. Omitted losses from limited candidates are reported separately to avoid conflating low recall with low load. Rising and falling trends are decomposed by the opening and closing probabilities of each question, allowing back-reference to the original text segment.

Let $T_r$ denote the first occurrence or resolution position of the query $r$, as defined by the data. Interval marking is scored by $-\log\Pr(a<T_r\le b)$, and $-\log\Pr(T_r>b)$ is used when no events occur up to the final observable position $b$. Missing labels retain the observation mask, and coarse-grained intervals preserve their original length. All data are tokenized back to the character range, and observation window boundaries must be verified against the actual visible prefix. In this round, public observations have not yet simultaneously measured word-level opening, continuation, resolution, and sudden topic switching on the same reader and the same query. Therefore, the main conclusions are reported by each endpoint and the original observation granularity. Multiple events at the same position may cancel each other out. acceptance retains the variation of each query along with its sum.

## Switch-topic readout and matching

Curve estimation remains unresolved. Upon encountering new content, the prefix readout determines whether the estimated event or topic has shifted, and whether the current content continues the same inquiry. A break candidate must simultaneously exhibit elevated unresolved load at the preceding position, a transition signal at the current position, and a lack of visible continuation for that inquiry. Scores prior to a topic switch must remain consistent across the shared prefix, even if different versions diverge in subsequent outcomes. The probability of an unresolved inquiry may be downgraded only after a foreshadowing is retrieved. Authorial intent relies on editors or readers to synthesize the original text for interpretation.

The same prefix is attached to three fixed suffixes: one that fully explains the situation, one that abruptly shifts to an unrelated topic, and one that defers the answer until later in the continuation, to test whether the readout retains the question identity, detects the transition point supported by evidence, and branches only when the suffix is visible. These rewritten conditions, lacking matched human data, are counted only as model behavior validation. Frick compares the first report and effect magnitude across identical target sentences with different prefixes. Hippocorpus validates the distribution of events versus non-events and provides verifiable unexpected annotations. Both offer evidence for aligning to the sentence-level human endpoint.

## Joint training experiment

1. After fixing the scheme, first perform linear and shallow readout on the same batch of causal representations, and conduct no-issue single-head matching with identical representations. Curve-state groups are supervised-fitted via opening, relevant segments, and first response or persistence. Position/age, paragraph endings, local-only words, and surprisal serve as position and surface contrasts.
2. We compare the homogeneous readout against the shared adapter. For each task, we retain the readout head and compute the loss based on its own annotations, time window, and scale. We fix the number of samples and update steps per task, along with the random seed, training budget, and development search range. When adjusting the shared capacity, we report the parameter count and include a matching control. We first compare semantically related endpoints, then sequentially add CompLex, PLABA, or in-text evidence. Cross-task homologous articles, near-duplicates, paraphrases, and different derivative annotations must fall on the same fold. Existing readout procedures, caching, and training design are reusable. readout weights are fitted separately for each fold in this study. Only weights verifiable against the training source with no overlap in the new held-out set can serve as a warm-start control.
3. After fixing the architecture, compare adding paired ranking or human study effect differentiable losses to data with matching human conditions. Use only training fold estimates for effect direction, magnitude, and data weights. The first round employs supervised readout and interval likelihood. LoRA serves as the second round architectural variant. Paired and interval targets are differentiable end-to-end, sufficient to directly test the first-round hypothesis.

## Primary validation and execution

- **Accumulation.** For each adjacent reading stop with confirmed observation, compare the paired unresolved question and opening supervision in terms of rising direction, item probabilities, or item counts. Report the first-occurring interval error, early alarm rate, and earlier text segment hits. Use annotations indicating the same item identity as the primary source. Retain intra-sentence shape without truth values as an estimate.
- **Solve.** Compare the same problem's solved correct clue versus an unrelated but equal-length clue, and which problem is solved first when both are unsolved. Report the right-side constrained interval NLL, calibration with known reading stop, pairwise ranking, and effect magnitude. Evaluate human evidence separately for the same reader's subjective solving.
- **Conversion.** Report directly verifiable event conversions or contradictions at first report location, early report rate, false alarm rate, and effect magnitude. For conversion items with unresolved questions, composite markers are accepted only if both components are validated and the same article is used as a control. Composite markers at the per-token level require human evidence at the same position to make direct validity claims.
- **A single forward pass.** When adding or replacing suffixes with the same prefix, ensure that curves, candidates, source coordinates, and breakpoints align at shared visible positions. Call the backbone once per document. Record time and peak memory separately for the backbone, candidate transitions, and scoring. First, use old features for reproducible baselines. perform new per-token extraction at L4.

The development data includes previously observed results. The primary validation employs a pre-sealed snapshot and material families not used in design, with additional research reserved when feasible. Common materials are matched via resampling at the story or article level, retaining the same group when reader links exist. In each round, the primary endpoint, minimum gain, and allowable degradation are fixed first, followed by prediction of the held-out labeled connections using the pre-sealed snapshot. Segment start-end differences, peaks, and decline positions can be utilized for post-hoc effect analysis, with no default condition of zeroing at segment ends.
