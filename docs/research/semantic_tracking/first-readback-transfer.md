# First-pass active readout of the main text input validation

Paape's two publicly available keystroke logs contain English discourse and German garden-path materials. This alignment first models the risk set of forward reading. When a reader encounters a display segment of the original experiment for the first time and has not yet pressed the backspace key, the observed next valid action is to proceed to the next segment or to go back. This endpoint follows the keyboard reading rules of the original experiment. Backspacing may also occur at other time points after noticing a discrepancy. [English original study](https://doi.org/10.5070/g6011182) · [German original study](https://doi.org/10.5070/G601139) · [Previous two experiments](../semantic-tracking-first-run.md)

We train and hold out using the fixed article families of two datasets. We employ the same Gradio candidate Qwen3.5-2B FP32 backbone, performing one causal forward pass per article. We report only the certified reading stop count from the original experiment. For a segment, multiple words may be present. we also report the results when the original experiment displays only one word per segment. Model features include the Unicode character count before the reading stop, the count of displayed whitespace-separated tokens, and the character count of the last word. these are transformed via log1p and scaled by the training position statistics. The predictor does not require knowledge of the experiment's segment ID, condition, total article length, reader key, or subsequent actions. The text candidate is augmented with a 2,048-dimensional causal state, L2-normalized.

Use three training pools: families from the same study, families from the other study, and families pooled from both. In the pooled fit, the two studies receive equal observation weight. Fit three readouts in each pool. The event-prior readout fits only a constant, the position readout adds features of the visible text layout, and the text readout also adds the Qwen state. Retain the original lookback experiment's 0.002 L2 penalty on all coefficients except the intercept. Minimize the mean trial-level Bernoulli negative log likelihood. Holdout responses for both studies stay local until the weights and predictions are fixed for scoring.

The primary scores compared the combined body and combined position of two held-out studies. The primary criterion was the body minus position per trial Brier loss, with the 95% paired material group bootstrap upper bound below zero, and the body average probability minus human key rate, with the 90% interval fully contained within ±1 percentage point. Brier is the mean squared difference between probability and binary observation, ranging from 0–1, where 0 is optimal. We also report negative log-likelihood, average precision, word segments, and results across each training range. Using family bootstrap resampling 10,000 times with seed 20261121, fixed participants and predictions. Prior results were per-study readouts. this contrast test examines whether the same set of body readouts can also maintain similar probability performance across reading materials and languages.
