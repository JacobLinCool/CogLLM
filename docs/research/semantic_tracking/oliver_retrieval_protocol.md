# Vocabulary extraction co-occurrence trajectory and held-out specification

Oliver et al.'s experiment recorded the first report after a reader completed an English definition, whether the response switched to KNOW within 15 seconds, and whether the target word was confirmed as the reader's intended word after its revelation. A retest was conducted five minutes later, with some definitions replaced by versions from another author. Publicly released keystroke trial data were used to validate the vocabulary extraction process. In ordinary discourse, the per-token onset, direction, and resolution of readers' spontaneous queries still require corresponding observation. [Original study §§3.2.2, 4](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.200) · [Public data v3](https://doi.org/10.5683/SP2/SP6BYO)

## Input, segmentation, and visibility scope

Only the raw definition text actually displayed in Test 1 is fed into the Qwen3.5-2B within the sealed snapshot. Each text version undergoes a single causal forward pass, extracting the final layer state from the end of the definition. Scoring is separately linked to the keypress of the same trial, without providing the target word, subsequent keypresses, or reader information during the forward pass. The paper does not confirm the raw character string of the answer-revealing screen. instead, it predicts the distribution of subsequent response behavior upon completion of the definition. There is no discourse text reading stop between the definition and the keypress.

Each target word and its definition version belong to the same source family. Following the `sha256("cogllm-oliver-e1-v1:" + target.strip().casefold())` ordering, the first 30 target families are used for training, while the remaining 14 are held-out. The full set is first split, then single families with corrupted definition fields in the published version are excluded. All models maintain the same reader cohort, using no reader IDs or personal history. Test 1 validates whether original definition trials enter the model's scoring, including original keystrokes later judged invalid by subsequent feedback. Trials lacking original text are retained for data auditing.

## Readout versus readout

At the end of the original definition, $A$ indicates the first keypress of KNOW, TOT, or DON'T KNOW. $D$ indicates a switch to KNOW within 15 seconds. $Y$ indicates confirmation after the target word is revealed. and $D$ in the initial KNOW path uses a structural missing value. Three factors, $p(A\mid x)$, $p(D\mid A,x)$, and $p(Y\mid A,D,x)$, are fitted. The probability of all possible response sequences is obtained from a single definition, and marginal probabilities at either endpoint are still obtainable even when no known reader keypresses are present. Actual keypresses are used only to select the corresponding path and score it. Switching to KNOW serves as a report, restoring the accuracy of the target word based on the original oral response, yet public data lacks recordings.

Compare the three stages with identical readout structures. The constant group uses only the intercept from the trajectory branch. The surface group incorporates character count, word count, average word length, digit proportion, punctuation proportion, and proportion of defined low-frequency words, with word frequency computed via training definitions. The Qwen group additionally includes the final-layer vector. Continuous features are centered using the training text, surface features are standardized per dimension, and states are standardized by the square root of the sum of squared variances across dimensions. Average sequential losses are summed with $0.01\lVert w\rVert_2^2/2$, and branch intercepts are not penalized. All three groups use the same training trials, with no held-out data for hyperparameter tuning.

We primarily compare the held-out per-response NLL. Relative to the constant-process and surface-process control conditions, Qwen's 95% confidence interval lower bound must exceed zero. Pre-tests are conducted on two human effect sizes: the difference in the probability of "change to KNOW within 15 seconds" (first TOT minus DON'T KNOW), and the difference in "target word confirmation" probability between the same two groups. The model first generates a full distribution per text, then computes the conditional difference using the group-mixed branch probabilities. during evaluation, no actual first report is provided. When testing the direction of the human effect size interval excluding zero, the model's 90% confidence interval must fall within ±5 percentage points of the human value to define the acceptable tolerance for this validation. Additionally, marginal calibration is reported for three categories: first report, change to KNOW, and target word confirmation. All intervals are computed via cross-validation over 10,000 readers × target family combinations, with seed 608315.

Test 2 re-calculated the first report's transfer and the two text conditions, preserving the distinction between "identical source family" and "alternative definition of the same target." This round was administered after participants had encountered the answer and a five-minute interval, without fitting a retest state. The paper reports retest results based on post-hoc validated trials. original key distributions and control conditions matching the author's validity criteria were recorded separately.
