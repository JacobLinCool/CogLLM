# End-of-sentence interpretation of Czech garden-path sentences

In Ceháková and Chromý's second and third experiments, readers received one designated yes-or-no question after reading a sentence to indicate how they had interpreted it. A yes on `qmis` indicates that the reader still accepted the initially plausible misinterpretation when answering. `qcor` asks whether the reader identified the actual object, and `qpos` asks whether they understood the possessive relation between the initially ambiguous noun and the object. Released keypresses include an explicit 'I don't know' (`Nevím`), which can be counted separately from a missing keypress (`NA`). Each reader saw one version of each item and answered one question. The responses support comparison of the three question types at the group level. All three judgments and word-by-word changes were not observed together within a single reading. [Original paper, Experiments Two and Three and Tables Three and Five](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0288817) · [Word-level data fields](https://osf.io/download/um9bc/)

## Material and data alignment

Each of the 48 stimulus sets has a syntactically ambiguous version and a control, each with animate and inanimate objects, giving 192 sentences of 11 words. The 96 ambiguity contrasts across the two object conditions differ only at word 4. The author specifies word 7 as the disambiguating object. The ends of words 6, 7, and 8 are the pre-cue stop, the cue stop, and the next-word stop, respectively. In Experiment 3, each keypress reveals only the next word while masking the previous one. All 7,632 released trials map to 11 presentation positions using `WordNo`. Of these, 7,167 have a numeric reading time at word 7. Word numbers denote presentation position. The sentence-end answer is copied into the rows for all words. Following the author code, take the single value from `WordNo == 1`. Both `Answer` and `Correct` contain cross-row discrepancies for 92 trials each. Taking the first nonmissing value or treating every word row as a separate answer would change the denominator. [Stimuli for Experiments 2 and 3](https://osf.io/download/6gjur/) · [Experiment 3 CSV](https://osf.io/download/cb624/) · [Experiment 3 R code](https://osf.io/download/pyv9b/)

There is a one-to-one mapping between the 48 values of `ItemNo` in the stimuli and the 48 values of `Item` in the trial table. Only two items have the same number in both tables. We reconstructed the mapping using sentence type, object animacy, question type, word order, and question text. Word order and the original question strings matched exactly for 8,238/8,352 trials in the second experiment and 7,526/7,632 in the third. The remaining trials had the same word order, and their questions matched after Unicode NFC and whitespace normalization. The mapping also held for the 6,740 texts and open questions from the fifth experiment. For holdout evaluation, keep all versions of an item in the same group. The experiments share stimuli, so splitting only by experiment number would reuse the same items. ## Available results

| Experiment | Participants and trial | The ambiguous version still accepts the original misinterpretation. | Unambiguous control version |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2. Display the full sentence at once, `qmis` the answer is | 174 individuals, 8,352 trials | 308／1,383（22.3%） | 99／1,387（7.1%） |
| 3, display word by word, `qmis` the answer is | 159 individuals, 7,632 trials | 357／1,266（28.2%） | 110／1,267（8.7%） |
| 5, word-by-word display, `qwhat` the response adheres to the original misinterpretation | 141 individuals, 6,740 trials | 148／1,686（8.8%） | 5／1,682（0.3%） |

The tables for the second and third experiments retain explicit 'I don't know' answers in the denominators and exclude trials without a recorded keypress. Across question types, these experiments had 217 and 226 explicit 'I don't know' responses and 53 and 32 missing keypresses, respectively. For the fifth experiment, the numerator counts open responses the authors coded as `ErrorSubType=gp`. This indicates whether the reader still treated word 4 as a direct object after finishing the sentence. The third experiment pairs word-level processing times with interpretations reported at sentence end. Word 7 is the experimenter-designated cue. The data do not label where individual readers first misinterpreted the sentence, changed their interpretation, or confirmed that they had resolved the ambiguity. The results cover native Czech-speaking university students and the selected syntactic structures.
