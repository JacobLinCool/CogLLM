# Representational training before and after disambiguation cues for end-consequence judgment

Fixed Gradio readout in Blott 2023 yields a sentence-final word sense re-interpretation effect of +0.09 percentage points, with human at +16.33. This round directly uses Blott 2021's Sense/Nonsense button to fit readout, testing whether Qwen representations before and after the disambiguation clue can better match human responses compared to sentence-final representations. Fixed Qwen3.5-2B and the original Gradio candidate model versions. Each inference passes the original sentence into the backbone only once. Humans judge full sentences for "makes sense." The experiment provides disambiguation clue coordinates to compare model changes before and after reading the clue. [2021 experiment and prior validation](../stage6/blott-coherence-holdout.md) · [2023 analyzed results](blott-format-results.md)

In the 2021 original coordinates, the last visible word before the end of the author's disambiguation span, the end of the disambiguation span, and the end of the complete sentence are selected. Disambiguation tokens in 2021 occur within the sentence, while those in 2023 appear at the sentence end. the difference in research and the difference in cue position cannot be disentangled in this shift. Both coherent versions of the sentence frame must share three reading stops verified by the tokenizer to enter all fitting and comparisons. 2021 `log` four versions lacking author credible coordinates can be extracted at the sentence end and excluded from this pairing fitting. Actual training is limited to two coherent versions to correspond to the known written conditions of 2023. The model input excludes responses, conditions, questions, text from the other version, or reader fields.

Let $v_t=h_t/\lVert h_t\rVert_2$, $d=v_{cue}-v_{pre}$. Compare the surface features of the two columns (full sentence character count and word count log), surface + $v_{end}$, surface + $d$, and surface + two vectors. Each surface column is equal-weighted averaged and scaled by standard deviation over the training sentences. Each vector is centered by the training mean vector and then divided by the root mean square norm of the training centered row vectors. The readout is a linear logit, with no-intercept coefficients plus $0.025\lVert w\rVert_2^2/2$. Each group minimizes the per-keyword binary negative log-likelihood on the same 2021 response. The model's end-of-sentence estimates can be computed independently per sentence, without needing to input both versions simultaneously.

The fifth readout employs a paired conditional likelihood with weights of 1 for models containing $v_{end}$ and $d$. For sentence frame $f$, let $h_A,m_A,h_U,m_U$ denote the counts of Sense and Nonsense, respectively, for the required re-solution and unambiguous coherent versions. Use $h_U m_A$ and $h_A m_U$ as paired counts in opposite directions. Independently sample one version of the reader response, conditioned on the keypresses being distinct, yielding the probability of selecting the unambiguous model as $\sigma(z_U-z_A)$. Average this negative log-likelihood over the evaluable sentence frame with equal weighting and add it to the per-key loss. In 2021, each reader answered only one version per frame, utilizing cross-reader counts, whereas in 2023, actual same-reader pairings were used solely for scoring.

First, in 2021, the original sentence frames were sorted by term name and then split into five folds in a round-robin fashion, with the entire set of two versions held-out together. We recorded the per-keyboard NLL and the effect of no-issue, prefix, source family, random seed, matching, nats, held-out, anchor span, response distribution, documentation, and retention constraints, confirming that the program did not take answers from the other version's text. All coefficients were fixed to use identical penalties and pairing weights, with no adjustments made in either held-out set. We then fitted on all valid 2021 sentence frames, preserving the original 44 complete sentence frames from 2023 and 1,558 reader-paired groups, saving coefficients and per-sentence predictions before the 2023 scoring. Since 2023 has already been used for fixed Gradio evaluation, this round is treated as a retrospective cross-study comparison. For any independent new material in the future, the weights from this sealed snapshot must be followed.

The 2023 primary comparison evaluates the readout that incorporates matching and segmental representations, relative to the re-fit end-of-sentence vector readout and the legacy Gradio calibration, each measured by their NLL improvement. The 95% confidence interval lower bound for both improvements must exceed 0. The lower bound of the effect-direction interval must be greater than 0, and the model's reduction in human error must have its 95% confidence interval fully contained within ±5 percentage points. Additionally, the NLL and effect difference before and after adding matching to the same representation are reported to allow evaluation of supervision and representation independently. The 2023 experiment used a fixed 10,000 reader-material cross-validation with seed 735972. The results were checked on end-of-sentence responses. Annotations were used solely for extraction and alignment. the App automatically determines the question position and whether the reader actually resolves it at that location, each requiring separate validation.
