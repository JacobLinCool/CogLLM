# Blott et al. (2023) proposed a re-interpretation of the final word meaning in English sentences

**Published evidence.** The full-sentence display condition in Experiment 1 tests whether a reader's sentence-end judgment that the sentence “makes sense” changes across two matched English sentences. The disambiguating cue is the final word, so the ambiguous version makes sense only if the reader uses the less frequent meaning. The control uses the same sentence frame with an unambiguous head noun and a final word that fits the context. The two versions appear in different experimental blocks but use the same presentation format for a given reader. The paired unit is a reader's two responses, one to each version of the same sentence frame. [Original paper, Experiment 1, Materials and Design](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2159992) · [Full text and Table 3](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/63934/1/Presentation%20format%20affects%20the%20behavioural%20and%20neural%20processing%20costs%20of%20sentence%20reinterpretation.pdf)

## Stimulus, human report, and time

Full-sentence condition: display the entire sentence for 4.5 seconds, then display `Sense` and `Nonsense` buttons. The authors retained 108 readers using British English. Each reader read 21 pairs of coherent sentences and 18 incoherent filler sentences in the full-sentence condition. 63 pairs of coherent experimental sentences were assigned to readers across the full-sentence, word-by-word, and audio conditions. This paper reports only the full-sentence word-by-word coherent trials. The average accuracy in the full-sentence condition in Table 3 was ambiguous 80%, unambiguous 95%. Both coherent versions had `Sense` as the correct answer. [Original paper, Experiment 1 §§Participants, Design, Procedure, Results](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2159992)

The `Item` and `Ambiguity` fields in `Exp1_TaskData.csv` identify the sentence frame and version in the materials table. The manuscript materials list 90 frames across two experiments, with 63 coherent pairs in the first table included in this comparison. All 126 `Sentence` values are wrapped in a single `<h4>` element. The displayed text contains no HTML entities that require decoding, and word order matches the materials table throughout. For each sentence, record the last nonwhitespace character before the final-word cue, the cue's endpoint, and sentence end. When the two versions differ in exactly two words and the second change is the cue, also retain the location of the earlier head noun. Readers chose only once after seeing the complete sentence. The endpoint has no observations of pre-cue sense judgments, timing of reinterpretation, or lookback location. [Materials table](https://osf.io/download/h53ed/) · [Display events and response fields](https://osf.io/download/trf8w/)

Sampled from `Modality=Vis`, `Plausibility=Plaus`, and the `Screen.Name=Response` experimental blocks. The original code listed 16 participants to exclude and applied strict `Reaction.Time > 250.00`. Thus, responses with exactly 250 ms were also excluded. After excluding 15 response times and 19 remaining timeouts, 63 stimulus sets yielded 4,502 explicit responses. The 108 readers in the public CSV had mean sentence accuracy of 79.85% and 94.95%. Recalculating reader means from the explicit trials in this paper yielded 79.53% and 94.99%, showing a difference persists even after recalculating the denominator for both public datasets. ## Source and existing training materials

The 2021 Blott trials previously served for CogLLM's English sentence-end evaluation. The two studies use related pilot methods. The 192 earlier versions (48 frames, including `bridge` and fillers) and the 126 newer versions share neither an identical normalized sentence nor an identical frame after masking the head noun and cue. In 19 new material sets, the head noun and final word appeared together in the head-noun and later-cue regions of the same earlier version, so these sets were excluded in full. The audit also records 25 original new sets with at least one five-word span in common with earlier materials. [Existing 2021 evaluation](../stage6/blott-coherence-holdout.md) · [2023 Materials and Supplementary Materials](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2159992) · [Overlap rules and manifest](../../../research/results/blott-format-source-audit.json)

[This study found] that 44 sentence frames, 88 sentences, and 3,139 explicit responses were retained. Among these, 108 readers had **1,558 pairs** of valid responses for the same sentence frame across both versions. For each group, ambiguous responses were first reduced to unambiguous ones. then, within each sentence frame, reader differences were averaged and further averaged using equal weighting across sentence frames. The ratio of "plausible" responses for the ambiguous version was **16.33 percentage points** lower. All 88 sentences retained the original word index for the final word and the sentence-end position, while the noun phrase positions in both versions were left as missing values.
