# Sentence-by-sentence contradictory reports of the held-out results by Frick and Schüler

**The complete prefix raises the report probability in the correct direction, but by only about one-thousandth of the human effect.** Across 20 held-out German stories and 1,800 text-only trajectories, human completion increased the same-target-sentence report rate by 52.06 percentage points, while the complete prefix increased Qwen's readout by 0.057 percentage points. The human probability difference for first-report-at-target was 48.27 percentage points, versus 0.051 percentage points for the model. Both endpoints passed the direction and relative-sentence-model-NLL thresholds, but neither magnitude passed the pre-specified ±10 percentage point constraint. NLL for a report at the current sentence fell from 0.157668 nats per sentence with the current-sentence model to 0.157549 with the prefix model, a difference of −0.000119 with an interval of [−0.000184, −0.000059]. First-report NLL fell from 1.73068 to 1.72932 nats per passage, a difference of −0.00136 with an interval of [−0.00232, −0.00048]. The minimum NLL is 0, and lower values mean the model assigned higher probability to the observed report. Overall mean NLL decreased slightly. The predicted probability of a first report before the target was still about 29.0%, compared with 13.4% for humans in the inconsistent condition. Evaluation therefore needs to cover both aggregate probability error and event localization. ## The role of context in frozen representations

Conduct a paired diagnostic test following the main ratings. For each fold, estimate a direction of the state difference using the remaining 19 pairs of stories, then check the projection of the state difference along that direction for each sentence in the held-out stories. The state differences of the 20 target sentences all share the same direction. 18 of these target sentences were the ones with the largest within-story split, and all 20 ranked in the top three. This pairwise diagnostic comparison of the two versions of the same story provides evidence that the effect can be read from the frozen representation. The human report probability for a single article is the endpoint that was independently rated in the previous section and did not pass. ## Validation and Resources

For each of the 40 stories and 332 sentence pairs, a single L4 causal forward pass was performed, totaling 14,085 tokens, 956 verified readout stops, and 12 independent causal checks. Among the 20 modified stories, 81 shared readout stops were present prior to modification, with the maximum relative difference to L2 being 0.00000228. The content forward and readout phase consumed 8.17 seconds, the extraction phase took 48.5 seconds, and the content batch GPU peak retention was approximately 8.42 GiB. L4 has been released. Two local evaluations each took approximately 0.6 seconds with a peak of 176 MiB, where coefficients, predictions, and scores matched bit-for-bit. Additionally, from the sealed snapshot, the two NLLs, the first event probability and the probability of no event at the end, the two target sentence effects, and the training denominator from 120 fits were recomputed.
