# Evidence location held-out in ExpMRC new papers

**Using the original QASPER weights, per-token readout passes three pairwise comparisons based on corpus-specific specificity, yet the absolute probability error remains large.** Across 698 paired items sharing the same article and reading stop, per-token readout assigns the higher cumulative probability to the item with the evidence fully present in 62.3% of pairs. Position-based comparison yields a tie at 50.0%. item-based comparison yields 54.0%. The per-token readout's gain relative to both is +12.32 percentage points (95% CI [7.88, 16.82]) and +8.31 percentage points ([1.25, 15.32]), respectively. The interval NLL difference for original-item minus swapped-item is −0.0910 nats ([−0.1050, −0.0771]). Intervals are resampled across 158 article groups. For the two error types, calculated across 165 papers and 555 questions, with 0 as optimal: the rank difference of per-token minus paragraph interaction is −0.29 percentage points, with a 95% confidence interval of [−1.54, +0.99]. The probability average of the first reading stop reported by per-token is 0.09%, while the human reference shows 4.23% having already appeared in full before this point. These two endpoints jointly constrain the current interpretation. When the main text is fixed, correct questions improve the ranking of corresponding evidence and the assigned probability, yet the absolute probability across the corpus still deviates from human annotations. The primary analysis excluded two articles that were partially used in the RACE evaluation, based on pre-recorded text overlap, totaling six items. With all 167 articles retained, the per-token readout was 62.7% across 705 matched pairs. Among the 555 items in the primary analysis, 351 had identical character-level endpoints in both reference segments, while 518 fell within the same verified reading stop. Endpoints were determined by the annotator's selected supporting segment after the known stem, options, and answer. The original study described a re-check of segment sufficiency, while the public dataset only included the selected segments. These results support text evidence ranking under specified questions. Direct records of the exact tokens where readers generated their own questions and recognized answers remain missing. [Original study §§3.1–3.2](https://ymcui.com/pdf/expmrc.pdf#page=3) · [Materials and Overlap Verification](../../../research/results/expmrc-transfer-l4/assay.json)

On a single NVIDIA L4, the frozen Qwen3.5-2B candidate was used to extract 167 articles, 561 specified prompts, and 61,181 valid tokens. Each article underwent a single forward pass with four sets of weight-reused article representations. All 3,749 verified reading stops were fully extracted, with an additional 14 sentence boundaries merged into the next alignable reading stop. All 12 causal checks passed, and the maximum relative root mean square difference of the prefix projection states was less than 0.000003. The model forward and readout extraction took 27.3 seconds, while the entire ExpMRC phase took 77.1 seconds. the Colab execution instance has been verified and released. Per-bit reruns of the score were consistent, and the four sets of NLL and Brier were recalculated using direct multiplication of survival probabilities.
