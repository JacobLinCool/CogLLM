# Supervised training of disambiguation cue representations and cross-study validation

After direct training with the human keys from Blott 2021, the Qwen state difference improved the disambiguation sense judgment probability at the end of the sentence in Blott 2023. The readout of the state difference was **0.01733 nats/key** lower than using only the end-of-sentence state, with a 95% confidence interval **[0.00731, 0.02838]**. When paired effect supervision was added, the model estimated the sense rate for the unambiguous version to be **3.64 percentage points** higher, 95% confidence interval **[2.06, 5.31]**, in the same direction as the human judgment. The human difference was **16.33 percentage points**, 95% confidence interval **[10.55, 22.70]**. The magnitude of the paired supervision group did not pass either of the two main NLL gain thresholds. NLL is the negative log-probability per human keystroke, measured in nats, with 0 as the ideal value. The effect is the difference in Sense probabilities between the unambiguous and re-solvable versions, compared within sentence frames. Training data comprises 46 groups and 2,208 human keystrokes. Within sentence frames, both versions share the same disambiguation word, and each reader views only one version. The 2023 cohort was used in the prior fixed Gradio evaluation. this time, the 44 groups and 1,558 group-reader pairings represent a retrospective transfer. In 2023, the two versions swapped the sentence-final disambiguation word. The disambiguation word positions in both studies are intra-sentence and sentence-final, respectively. All readouts utilize the available text length at the sentence end, with cue coordinates derived from the experimental materials. [2021 coordinates and denominator](../../../research/results/blott-update-2021-source-audit.json) · [2023 source](blott-format-source.md)

The 2023 same-feature comparison shows that adding matching supervision shifts the effect from +1.05 to +3.64 percentage points, but increases NLL by **0.02592 nats/key**, with a 95% interval **[0.01019, 0.04287]**. The average estimates for this group under re-solution and no-issue are 68.49% and 72.13%, respectively, while human performance is 80.04% and 96.37%. Fixing the logit difference between versions allows each sentence frame in a group to share any intercept, resulting in an average probability difference upper bound of only **5.07 percentage points**. This upper bound, averaged per group at $\tanh(\max(0,z_U-z_A)/4)$, serves as a readout for no-issue, indicating that beyond intercept calibration, the model must learn larger conditional differences. The same Qwen candidate made one causal forward pass for each of 188 sentences from the 2021 study on a single Colab L4 and extracted 556 stops. The 2023 analysis reused 350 existing FP32 states. All 12 causal checks passed. Forward passes and readouts for the 2021 materials took 1.90 seconds, and extraction including model setup took about 42 seconds. The L4 release was verified. Two local fitting and scoring runs were bitwise identical, took about 1.5 seconds in total, and reached about 168 MiB peak RSS. Independent recomputation rebuilt all five logit sets from the sealed states and verified the crossed-resampling intervals for all six comparisons. This experiment supervises sentence-end responses and extracts the model's state at the earlier cue. When readers detect and confirm the disambiguation still requires observations attributable to that location.
