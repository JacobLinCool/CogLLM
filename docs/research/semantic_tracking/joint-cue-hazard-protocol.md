# Joint learning of two sequential word-guessing observation processes

Both the English Core Vocabulary and the German ACT record the number of trials to first correct response for the same item. In English, each step presents one sentence, and the published trial lacks the sentence drawn at that time. In German, each step presents one word, with the word list order known. The accuracy rates across the first six steps differ substantially between the two studies, and fixed English readout fails across studies. This experiment examines whether shared Qwen representation coefficients can improve predictions for materials not used in each study's fit, after each study estimates its own step-wise baseline rate. [English sampling sensitivity](core-vocabulary-disjoint-results.md) · [German external transfer](act-transfer-results.md)

## material sealed snapshot

The English model is fitted to the original 64 targets, each with 8 paths. The 32 original held-out targets and 32 mutually exclusive new paths per target (disjoint from the original sample) are used solely for prediction and scoring. For German, five materials are used from the sealed snapshot, each with four wordlists. In each fold, one material is held out while the other four are used for fitting. All tasks utilize only the first six steps, with subsequent correct responses capped at step six. Trials that are successful but lack step counts in German are excluded. Two evaluations are shared across the five joint readouts generated by each German leave-one-fold configuration. German predictions are made using the held-out wordlist, while English held-out scores are computed by equal-weight averaging the seven readout categories and then taking the logarithm. This averaging is performed at the readout layer.

The three sealed snapshot representations originate from the same Qwen3.5-2B checkpoint, tokenizer, and 128-dimensional projection. The English text requires only a verified single causal forward pass. The German text can be compared against the marginalized group result of possible paths. The German text may use a single known path, with the scoring unit being the displayed cue word. The held-out trial from both studies was used for evaluation on the older model. This is a new retrospective comparison fitting. independent study confirmation requires another sealed snapshot.

## Readout and target

For the study $s$, material $f$, path $p$, and exposure step $t$, apply a sigmoid to transform $\alpha_{s,t} + u_s^{\mathsf T}x_{s,f,p,t}+v^{\mathsf T}h_{s,f,p,t}$ into the conditional probability of correct response. $x$ comprises the blank token count for the current step, cumulative token count, overlap proportion between the current step's word form and the preceding context, and the Unicode character length for both the current step and cumulatively. Count features are derived from `log1p`. First, normalize each column of the 128-dimensional projection of $h$ by unit normalization. Within each study and exposure step, subtract the training mean from each feature block. Scale literal feature dimensions by the training residual standard deviation per study, and scale the Qwen block by the root-mean-square of the training residual L2 norm per study. Retain the constant literal dimension with scale 1. All scales and means are computed from the current training material.

| Method | Study of internal parameters | Cross-study sharing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of steps | Each step intercept $\alpha_{s,t}$ | None |
| Literal | Intercept, literal slope $u_s$ | None |
| Qwen individual fit | Intercept, literal, and representational slope | None |
| Qwen joint fitting | Intercept, literal slope | Representation slope $v$ |

For each path, first compute the first-correct and right-censored seven-category probabilities, then mix them across English possible paths using equal weighting. German paths are scored based on observed single-path ratings. For each material, compute the NLL of the human event proportion. within the study, materials are equal weighted, and when combined across studies, studies are equal weighted. Apply an L2 penalty of 0.10 to slopes, with independent study slopes penalized by the study average and shared slopes penalized only once. For each study, add a balanced virtual trial with zero features to each intercept, consisting of half correct and half not-yet-correct responses, weighted by the reciprocal of the total training trials in that study. Retain records of numerical convergence and reruns for each group.

## Scoring and validation

We report seven-category NLL for English and German separately, giving the materials equal weight within each study. The primary comparison is the improvement of joint Qwen over separately fitted Qwen. We also compare joint Qwen with the lexical and step-only baselines. To conclude that shared coefficients improve held-out predictions for both tasks, each study must have a positive lower bound on the paired 95% improvement interval against both separately fitted Qwen and the lexical baseline. We also report cumulative correct-response rates at steps 1, 3, and 6. Each 95% interval for the model rate minus the human rate must lie fully within ±5 percentage points. We run 10,000 paired resamples with seed 735972, resampling English target words within the four original study conditions and resampling whole German word lists. We also run crossed resampling over readers and materials. The intervals treat held-out predictions as fixed. Readers may appear across material splits, since this evaluation targets generalization to new materials.

Position-wise validation additionally confirms that the held-out score does not alter its own predictions, that probability normalization across methods aligns with the gradient and numerical differentiation of the log-mixture, and that shared gains, hit rates, and general text query localization each require their own evidence.
