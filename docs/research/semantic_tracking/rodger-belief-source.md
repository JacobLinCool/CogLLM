# Rodger et al.'s two reading stop written belief update data

Rodger et al. (2026) Study 1 can be used to examine how a single reader alters their rating of whether **a given policy is effective** after reading a passage of policy evidence. Readers first evaluate six policies and then read the evidence assigned to each policy in a random order. Each reader encounters each of the six evidence conditions exactly once, with each policy assigned to only one condition. Readers input their thoughts when the evidence is fully presented, subsequently assess the credibility of the evidence, and finally evaluate the effectiveness and support level of the same policy. Figure 1 reports the initial ratings for each policy, while Figures 2 and Supplementary Material Tables B1–B2 report the rating changes and condition differences for the same trial. [Study 1, Materials and measures, Procedure, Figures 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2025.10028)

## Measurable reader response

The primary endpoint can be defined as the post-policy change in **effectiveness ratings** relative to pre-policy, measured on the original scale. Recalculating via CSV, the effectiveness and support mean changes for negative-case conditions are −8.75 and −6.87 points, respectively, consistent with the −8.8 and −6.9 points reported in Study 1 Results. Readers' pre-policy scores may serve as optional background information and can be used to compare the statistical baseline excluding evidence text. Policy support can be listed as a secondary endpoint and is also influenced by factors such as fairness and implementation difficulty. [Study 1, Figures 2, 4](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2025.10028) · [Verify via CSV](https://osf.io/download/36bg8/)

In the subtraction method described in the original text, the pre-test score is placed in the minuend position. Here, the calculation is re-performed as "post-test minus pre-test" on a per-item basis, yielding results consistent with the original text's symbols. The two case studies were generated by the authors based on actual public opinion using ChatGPT and subsequently reviewed by the team. Scores derive from human raters, while stimuli encompass the wording and judgment procedures of the study. [Original Study 1, Measures, and Quantitative results](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/negative-anecdotes-reduce-policy-support-evidence-from-three-experimental-studies-on-communicating-policy-ineffectiveness/9B673E6D29B154BE39D68BFBBFA5FDAA)

The freedom of the evidence page report and subsequent rationale can be used for qualitative verification. If used to annotate semantic updates, the two reports must be cross-checked to confirm they address the same issue. The study did not sentence-by-sentence interrogate questions or repeatedly measure whether they were resolved. Post-reading ratings also included a single query regarding evidence credibility. A reading stop is defined as a specified judgment after the entire evidence page, whereas which token within a sentence caused the reader to change their mind remains unmeasured. [Study 1, Procedure](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2025.10028)

Study 2 included 300 participants, building upon the policy and partial evidence from Study 1 by adding an online safety policy, revising positive cases, and incorporating stronger statistical evidence conditions. Study 3 included 307 participants, explicitly stating that it followed the stimuli from Study 2, adjusted one condition, and added evidence for diagnostic probability judgments. The three studies should collectively be regarded as a shared-material source family. The online safety policy can be cross-referenced against the held-out materials relative to Study 1. however, not all materials can be confirmed solely by study number. [Studies 2–3, Materials and measures](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2025.10028) · [Three-study materials](https://osf.io/download/vxhwj/)

## Construction of Study 1 in the reproducible framework

Construct output consisting of six separate initial policy pages and 36 initial pages with evidence text. Retain text according to QSF's `QuestionText` and in-page heading order. HTML entity decode, convert `br` to newline, remove inline styles, and merge whitespace that browsers would fold. The endpoint of each page is the exclusive Unicode character index in the output text. These two pages retain policy statements, questions, and evidence page prompts. The questionnaire's introduction, other policies, scale control items, subsequent credibility items, and post-response completion pages are excluded from the model text. The prepared file lists the original question IDs for the latter two pages. Scores, reasons, demographic fields, and free text entered by the reader are excluded from `stimuli.jsonl`. `outcomes.jsonl` retained locally contains only cluster identification codes, alignment columns, and four-item scores. [Build program](../../../research/semantic_tracking/rodger_belief_prepare.py)

`folds.json` For each fold, a held-out policy and its six evidence variants were tested against 301 existing trials. The training and test sets for each fold shared these 301 readers, constituting held-out material from the same study. Outputs from both runs were byte-identical. The fixed Qwen3.5-2B Rust tokenizer compared full inputs against truncated inputs across 78 reading stops, ensuring consistent token IDs for shared prefixes and identical endpoint tokens. [Build program](../../../research/semantic_tracking/rodger_belief_prepare.py)
