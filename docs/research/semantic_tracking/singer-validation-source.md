# Sentence-by-sentence contradiction judgments by Singer et al

Experiment 2 of Singer, Spear, and Rodrigo-Tamarit (2023) provides **target-sentence judgment statistics and individual trial correctness for 40 English stories**. After each sentence, readers press a key to judge whether it is consistent with the preceding text. The authors record the judgment and timing for the target sentence, and timing only for later sentences. The release can serve as a reference for the target-sentence effect in a separate reader sample. Evaluation of the first inconsistency keypress requires judgments from each sentence. [Original paper, Experiment 2, Procedure, Table 3, and Footnote 3](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-022-01351-w) · [Author's OSF archive](https://osf.io/a6xqk/)

## effect and the scope of applicability of materials

The accuracy in Table 3 was **93.4% and 54.8%** for concept stories under consistent and inconsistent conditions, respectively, and **92.1% and 53.6%** for character trait stories. This corresponds to **54.8% and 53.6%** of the contradictory goal sentences being reported in the inconsistent condition, while the false-alarm rate for consistent goal sentences was **6.6% and 7.9%**. These figures describe the current report of goal sentences and cannot rule out the possibility that the same reader previously reported the same goal sentence. [Original paper Experiment 2, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-022-01351-w)

Experiment 2 reuses the 40 stories from Experiment 1, including 20 conceptual stories from Singer (2006, 2017) and 20 character-trait stories from O’Brien et al. (1998). Experiment 3 also adapts these stimuli, replacing the original target sentence with a question about prior information. The audit examined all three OSF experiment directories, including data tables and analysis scripts. Table 1 gives two example stories, and Appendix A contains the questionnaire. The full set of 40 presented texts and a mapping to the table's `Passage` codes are still unavailable. [Original paper, Experiments 1–3, Materials, Table 1, and Appendix A](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-022-01351-w) · [Author OSF archive](https://osf.io/a6xqk/)

Frick and Schüler (2026) used Singer's exercises and results as the experimental design and statistical power estimation reference, explicitly listing their materials as 12 items adapted from Cook (2014) and 8 items from Frick et al. (2025). The materials listed in the two papers differ. Deduplication by item still requires Singer's complete materials. Published effect sizes can serve as a cross-study magnitude reference. If used to adjust the model's effect magnitude, the same statistic becomes the calibration basis. [Frick and Schüler, Experiment 1 Design and Materials](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7) · [Singer et al.'s experiment](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-022-01351-w)

Schüler and Frick (2024) used German text paired with images, requiring participants to respond via keypress based on cue words. Whether text and images were contradictory was manipulated, and reaction time was one of the primary measures. This provides a comparison of cross-modal interference, with endpoint and Singer's explicit contradiction judgment differing. [Original paper and open materials entry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101956)
