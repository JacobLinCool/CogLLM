# Locating the answer to the same question

On November 21, 2026, CogLLM distinguished which of two supplied questions received support in equal-length authored continuations and in passages with human-annotated ExpMRC evidence. After we added supervision at answer tokens in the training material, the model achieved lower held-out event NLL than a step-count baseline on the authored examples. The curves still separated too early in 10 of the 13 branches with unambiguous answer positions. On ACT, which records the first correct human response as clues appear, the new readout scored worse than the step-count baseline. ## Equal-length continuations after an identical prefix

We used 24 English stimulus families, with 17 for training and 7 for testing. Each shared prefix asks two explicit questions. The two branches answer one question each. The answer sentences are equally long under the pinned Qwen tokenizer, and the opening paragraphs and neutral closing sentences match. Within each branch we compare the answered and other question at the same position and check for early decreases. Both questions start with unresolved values of 1. The readout starts at the answer sentence in these authored examples, which rules out changes before its first token by design. Ties receive half credit. The initial readout scaled features together by dimensionality, effectively regularizing shared lexical coefficients more strongly in the wider readout. When refit at matched scales for each feature block, the Qwen interaction readout produced a contrast of +53.31 percentage points in the equal-length controls. The nested baseline combining local representations and lexical overlap produced +52.85. The paired difference was +0.46 percentage points, with a 95% interval across families of [−0.28, +1.18]. Before scoring, the authors marked the answer in each of the 14 test branches. They identified 13 conservative and unambiguous positions in the native tokenizer. “Upstairs resident” was ambiguous because it did not uniquely identify an apartment. The initial lexical readout diverged by 5 percentage points before the marked position in all 13 branches. The initial Qwen interaction readout never reached 5 percentage points. After feature-block scaling, the interaction readout and the nested baseline each diverged early in all 13 branches. The curves suggest that the readouts recognized which question the clue concerned before the text supplied enough information to answer it. ### Training on answer tokens

For each of the 34 training branches, we independently marked the earliest position where both the question's referent and its answer were available. We train the first event for the answered question at that token and right-censor the other at the end of the equal-length window. Held-out positions stay outside the training data. We reuse the L4 representations and nested baseline. The interaction readout lowered exact event NLL by 0.5012 nats against the step baseline, with a paired 95% interval of [0.2389, 0.7422]. In equal-length answer windows, its drop for the answered question minus its drop for the other was +37.26 percentage points, up by +1.85 percentage points [0.33, 3.60] over the nested baseline. The largest increase at one token and the first appearance of a gap are separate timing measures. The 10 branches that separated early show that the main drop often landed near the clue, while the readout still removed part of the unresolved mass prematurely. ## Human-annotated evidence and observed reading responses

**ExpMRC.** We kept two questions with nonoverlapping human-annotated evidence windows per article across 66 training and 27 held-out articles. The model saw both questions before the text. We compared the two questions on the same text at the earlier evidence window. The initial Qwen interaction readout matched the direction in 81.5% of articles. The step-count baseline tied on all questions, yielding 50%, and lexical overlap reached 72.2%. The lower bound of the 95% interval for initial Qwen versus lexical overlap was zero. There were 22 matched pairs comparing a question's evidence window with a preceding window of the same token length. For this contrast the initial Qwen difference was 2.10 percentage points below the step-count baseline, with an interval of [−3.48, −1.03]. [Original dataset](https://ymcui.com/expmrc/) · [Original scores and equal-length windows](../../../research/results/same-question-l4/metrics.json)

After feature-block scaling, held-out NLL was 1.92465 for the Qwen interaction readout, 2.11010 for step count, and 1.93184 for the nested baseline combining local and lexical features. The paired difference against the latter was −0.00719 [−0.01788, +0.00284]. Across 27 articles, curves separated by at least 5 percentage points before the annotated support window in 10 articles and within it in 6. They remained below the threshold at the end of that window in 11. NLL weights articles equally. Each article includes the early question's event, the later question's survival through the early window, and the later question's event at its own support window. The early-window survival of the later question receives weight in both paths, so the unit is a composite path. Humans marked supporting evidence after reading. [Feature-block scaling scores and paired intervals](../../../research/results/same-question-l4/block-check/metrics.json)

**ACT.** We used 20 word lists and 1,999 evaluable trials. In 1,321 trials we observed the first correct response. In 678 trials the final clue arrived without a correct response. One additional correct response has unknown timing, so we retained it outside the scoring set. We reused the sealed single-pass Qwen extraction per list and refit a low-dimensional readout on the original five folds. NLL was 2.40465 nats per trial for step count, 2.42837 for lexical length, and 2.43343 after adding Qwen's causal cue information. Qwen minus step count was +0.02878 [0.00560, 0.05604]. Across 119 comparable pairs at the same step with the same number of additional Qwen tokens, Qwen's direction agreement was 50.4% and the step baseline's was 50.0%. ACT measures the same reader's first correct answer under the original cue order. The authored continuations test changing later text after a common prefix. ## Execution

The new inputs were 48 authored branches and 93 ExpMRC articles, for 141 texts and 36,469 native tokens. We used the official Qwen3.5-2B weights in FP32 on L4. Each text used one causal forward pass across 53 batches. All 12 truncation and suffix-replacement checks passed. The largest absolute post-projection difference across the 24 shared authored prefixes was 9.90 × 10⁻⁶. L4 forward passes and projection export took 15.4 seconds, with a peak of about 7.24 GiB. Readouts ran on CPU and the main scores reproduced file for file. Allocation to confirmed release took 160.7 seconds. At the observed single-L4 rate, we estimated 0.087 compute units.
