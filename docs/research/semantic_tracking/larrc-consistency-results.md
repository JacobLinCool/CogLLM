# Cross-study held-out results for Currie's end-of-paper consistency judgment

Frick's paired readout failed all four cross-study criteria. In Currie et al., sixth-grade readers were **24.61 percentage points** more likely to report that a contradictory narrative did not make sense than to make the same report for a contradictory expository passage. The model's mean difference was **−0.22 percentage points**, and it underestimated end-of-passage reporting rates for both genres. The results cover 24 new topics, 48 English texts, and 4,248 existing postreading judgments. Currie's human responses are used solely for scoring after the sealed snapshot. ## Per-trial probability

negative log-likelihood (NLL) measured in nats/trial, with a minimum of zero, where lower values indicate higher probability for the actual answer. The paired readout NLL is 1.3812, the equal weighting readout is 1.0112, and the target sentence weighting is 0.7638. Frick's overall report rate constitutes a fixed control of 0.7154. The paired difference relative to equal weighting is +0.3699 [0.2540, 0.4890], and the difference relative to the constant is +0.6658 [0.4847, 0.8541]. both exceed the non-inferiority upper bound of +0.02. In Frick's post-hoc analysis, the increased condition effect did not yield group differences or probability calibration in this English held-out set. Both studies used English. Their texts, readers and reporting times differed. Frick's button-by-button approach yielded an average of 14.6 evaluable reading stops per text, whereas the Currie model employed seven hazard reading stops per text. Shorter discourses exhibit lower event probabilities derived from the same hazard accumulation. The experiment can only determine that this direct transfer failed, without separately manipulating text length, reader age, or button timing. These 48 Currie texts were also used in an earlier same-corpora six-fold cross-validation. The frozen Frick readout and calibration utilized a separate set of English stories. Currie's original answers measured only at the text end. ## Reproducibility and Resources

The prepared file and the original public file's hash, the 384 end-of-sequence token verifications, the original model weights, the scoring program, and the release confirmation are all sealed snapshots. The inference entry is `python3 -m research.semantic_tracking.larrc_consistency_score forecast`, reading the stimulus packet and the sealed snapshot L4 state. Subsequently, the `score` from the same module is joined with the native human answer. Both prediction and scoring are rerun once, byte-for-byte identical. Additionally, an independent recalculation of 1,008 per-sentence cumulative probabilities and the two human endpoints yields a maximum numerical difference of approximately \(2.22\times10^{-16}\).
