The 🌧 field team visited a small watershed after three days of rain. They wanted to understand why the stream became cloudy even after the wind had settled. At the first bridge, they took samples from the surface and from the water near the bed, where fine sediment can remain suspended. Each bottle had the time, the depth, and the location written on its label. A sign at the nearby café directed them to a dry shelter where they could sort the bottles before carrying them back to the laboratory. The team kept a separate log that could connect every reading to the bottle from which it came. At the laboratory, the first step was to let the sealed bottles reach room temperature. The technicians measured cloudiness by shining a beam through each sample and recording how much light scattered. They repeated the reading after gently turning each bottle. The notebook used the label <sample> & the original station code so that the notes could be compared with the export from the instrument. Some samples came from water only 10 cm deep. Those samples were collected away from submerged branches because the water there could carry small fragments of bark. A second technician checked all of the labels before the group entered the measurements in a shared table. The group then compared the dates when the samples had been collected. The darkest water was found at the bridge below a gravel road, but the difference was smaller in samples taken near the surface. A shallow ditch next to the road drained through a pipe into the stream. The team walked as far as the outlet to see whether the ditch could explain the pattern. They photographed the pipe and marked the distance to the bridge. The notes retained the original station names, including São Bento and the field sign marked नदी. The station names made it possible to match records even when the order of the measurements changed between exports. The final inspection began with a new sample at the downstream footbridge. The stream had been clear for a week, and the readings from the surface and bed were nearly the same. The team moved the equipment back to the ditch to repeat the comparison during the next storm. They drew a simple map of the proposed stations and recorded which access points were safe after rain. The next report will include the new measurements together with the original observations. The final map identifies the downstream lantern post so that another team can find the same sampling location.
