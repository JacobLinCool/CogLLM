# Does a model trained with scientists anticipate the help a lay reader requests?

Chan and colleagues gave lay readers an English medical abstract with designated terms highlighted. Readers rated familiarity while viewing the abstract, then selected the types of help they wanted for each term. The subsequent expert-written summary and comprehension quiz came after both decisions. The source collector checked the published task screens and the availability of fields, without joining any response to a prediction. Guo's source readers rated familiarity before seeing the abstract. These two measurements share direction and range but differ in timing and population.
