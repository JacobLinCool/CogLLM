# Different readers choosing opposite antecedents

In AmbiCoref a reader could explicitly select the middle answer, ambiguous. Another event, two completed questionnaires choosing opposite names for the same sentence, tests uncertainty about which reader will select which name. Following the external, frozen prior-plus-evidence protocol, we will separately predict this directly observed pair event. No AmbiCoref outcome will select an observer or update its weights. [Five-response study](transition-anchored-reference-protocol.md)

Form unordered pairs of distinct questionnaire rows that presented the same item and both gave a directional answer. Preserve the two confidence categories separately in the original primary report. Estimate each sentence's observed disagreement fraction as opposite-name pairs divided by all eligible pairs. We will score the directly implied probability 2p(1−p) using pairwise Brier, log loss, AUROC and average precision, averaged equally over eligible sentences. We will compare the external anchored update with its unchanged prior, elapsed-step control, attention update and cue-availability subgroup. A cross-family empirical constant estimated on the other lexical skeletons supplies an additional baseline. We will estimate uncertainty using crossed questionnaire-assignment and lexical-skeleton exponential bootstrap, computing pair weights from the product of the two original questionnaires' weights. Repeat the analysis when both answers are definite. Report identified proxies separately and disclose the absence of stable respondent identities in released questionnaires. No participant-level clinical assessment, effort curve or word-time labels follow from these responses.

The empirical reader-pair constant has a single rank within each training fold. Its reference AUROC is therefore .5, and its reference AP is the evaluated outcome frequency. Proper losses use each eligible sentence's leave-family-out numerical forecast.
