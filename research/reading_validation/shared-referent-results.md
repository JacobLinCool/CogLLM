# Does learning to point backward also teach a reader model when to change its mind?

A learned mapping between an earlier candidate and the current word improved the fit to authored sentence endings when its two uses shared parameters. On 279 held pairs, shared revision gave a terminal two-candidate loss of 1.051 nats, versus 1.244 for separately fitted click and revision mappings. The paired reduction was .193 [.130, .255]. Carrying the initial pointer unchanged gave 1.059. On 68 equal-length twins with an identical prefix, the shared model changed its preference in the intended direction at the first distinguishing word on 54.4% [42.6, 66.2%] of pairs. The earlier semantic-update observer reached 73.5% on the same set. At identical prefixes, the largest observed probability difference for the new shared model was .000051. Every input was an unmarked article, with 4,024 Qwen forwards for 4,024 articles. The extractor stored 1,928,701 causal earlier-word scores and 128-dimensional sketches of word-end representations. The source-only fit consumed actual click ranges and the authors' chosen referents using distinct likelihoods. Analytic gradient checks agreed with finite differences within 5 × 10⁻¹¹. Good terminal prediction on constructed pairs did not establish the timing or identity of a spontaneous human interpretation. These previously analyzed external collections support a comparison of fitting strategies; they do not provide a new independent replication of the 68-pair effect.
