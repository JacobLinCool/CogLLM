# Can a model locate disagreement about how one sentence follows another?

Two readers can find different relations between the same sentences. We predict the probability that two annotators choose different level-two discourse relations, and the distribution of choices underlying that probability. DiscoGeM elicited interpretations through connective insertion. Its 2025 reannotation used a different answer format. About 3,223 worker–item pairs were repeated across studies with a three-year interval. A reader's choice was obtained after access to the displayed context. Agreement and disagreement describe interpretations under these tasks. People can agree about a difficult argument or disagree about an easy one. Earlier studies trained directly on relation distributions; our question concerns transfer to explanatory passages, the prediction of disagreement, and whether different elicitation methods can share supervision. [Original study](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item3488237/discogem-crowdsourced-corpus-genre-mixed-implicit-discourse-relations) · [Original distribution-prediction work](https://aclanthology.org/2022.codi-1.7/) · [Reannotation study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.comedi-1.2/) · [Public release](https://github.com/merelscholman/DiscoGeM/tree/635515c06259ae40696649cba1d9f097418c8bd4)

The primary transfer holds out the entire Wikipedia genre identified by the original source and includes every eligible encyclopedia item. Train on novel and parliamentary items only. The primary model observes the original left context through its first argument, followed by the original focal second sentence. It has no access to subsequent sentences, genre ID, connective options, votes or either requested relation. Use one Qwen3.5-2B forward ending at the last observed token. Analyze all annotated English items available in both releases and disclose exclusions. Remove train sources sharing either focal sentence with the target genre and exact duplicates within a source. The explanatory corpus is held out from feature standardization, model training, regularization and calibration.

Use raw votes per unique reader and item. Analyze the forced-choice sense and step-two free-choice sense separately. Include the explicit no-relation response. Use the union of level-two labels in the two training sets and a reserved other category. For counts n_k on an item, disagreement between two different observed raters equals 1 − sum_k n_k(n_k−1)/(N(N−1)). Require at least two valid raters and report all included item counts. The primary endpoint is mean squared error on this observable quantity, averaged equally per encyclopedia article then across articles for the forced-choice instrument. Report the article-paired difference versus a supervised lexical comparator and a constant target-instrument comparator, plus alternative losses, choice distribution cross-entropy, uncertainty calibration, and correlations. Use 10,000 paired article bootstrap draws. The original annotators may have seen additional right context. A prediction is an estimate available at the end of sentence two; it supplies no individual timing labels.

Use a fixed 64-dimensional training-only principal subspace of unit-normalized Qwen sentence-end states and standardized lexical measures from the two sentences and prefix. Relations train by a multinomial log loss with grade-specific counts. For disagreement train a separate bounded logistic mean on each item's observed reader-pair disagreement, weighted equally per item, avoiding quadratic pseudo-replication. Share a coefficient direction across response formats using a separate residual per format and separate intercepts. Penalize the shared and residual slopes by fixed L2 penalties of 0.02 and 0.05 on the mean loss. Compare matched single-instrument training and the constant rate. Also compare a lexical feature head using the identical learner and an independently trained word and character n-gram comparator. The choice-distribution model also yields the distinct prediction 1 − sum_k p_k². Score both instruments and retain all comparator results, including any stronger simple method.

As an external specificity check, apply the frozen disagreement model to the existing DECOR teacher-annotated essay boundary archive, presenting exactly the same prefix and terminal sentence at each eligible boundary. Report average precision for discourse-relation errors against all reviewed boundaries and compare with the previously released human coherence head and lexical-plus-surprisal model. The DECOR outcomes have already been inspected. A null or reversed result would distinguish reader disagreement from an editorial defect. No target-genre tuning or DECOR calibration is permitted.
