# Compare the location of a human-labelled change at the resolution of the annotation

The exact-line comparison showed that the two largest human drops in Thomas fell on adjacent lines. On these three already examined works, compare the two teams' original definite changes allowing one or two printed lines of tolerance. Primary tolerance is one line; zero and two give the resolution sensitivity. Pair only changes in the same direction, use a monotone one-to-one alignment, maximize number paired and minimize the sum of distances among maximum matchings. Count a burst only once per team and position, regardless of how many questions changed. Report every denominator, the signed line offset and the matching F1 for drops and rises separately, both pooled and by work. The two workbooks annotate different sets of questions. This test measures timing overlap of net changes without asserting they concern the same question.

Use two chance controls. First circularly shift one whole team's series, disallowing shifts of fewer than ten lines in either direction. Second independently rotate that team's event positions within consecutive 30-line reading blocks, preserving the number and direction of events by story phase. Estimate one-sided randomization probabilities on 4,000 fixed-seed draws; a shared storyline can create similarities at nearby lines. Compare at all tolerances and retain the actual location of each matched pair. Finally compare the frozen text model's change against each team's largest guaranteed drop using the strongest predicted decline inside each allowed window. This inspection is an optimistic, tolerance-granted best case, unavailable to the author who has no target location. Published work, line splitting, and annotations are unchanged.
