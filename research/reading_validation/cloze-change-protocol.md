# Which incoming words increase or reduce disagreement between people?

At a printed word, a population can become more or less certain about what follows. The original UCL and Provo protocols elicited actual word completions at consecutive prefixes. This gives a directly measured, word-local update without a retrospective explanation of what readers mentally carried. Readers can disagree about the next word for reasons unrelated to difficulty. This endpoint cannot identify a particular reader's persistent question.

Before opening Provo completion counts, freeze the complete external predictions from the original population protocol. Pair only directly adjacent, causally certified printed positions in the same original article. If position t is the upcoming target, its response distribution describes the prefix before t. The difference between the distributions' disagreement at t+1 and t is therefore assigned to newly read word t. Do not interpolate across missing word positions or sentence/material boundaries in the UCL source. Retain cross-sentence steps in Provo where they occur within the same authentic paragraph. The human probability of two distinct sampled people giving different categories is estimated from observed counts with the finite-sample U-statistic. Original records have no identifiers for the people behind each aggregate response. Resample complete passage families jointly for all systems, preserving dependent neighboring differences. Report source cross-validation separately from the independent cohort.

The primary measurement uses the same 8,192 fixed spellings plus other as the fitted multicategory likelihood. Evaluate the contextual population head, its calibrated native comparison, the raw projection and the fixed frequency baseline. Predict the difference after reading word t from the one causal forward. Report Spearman correlation and mean absolute error of signed change across adjacent pairs, sign accuracy across nonzero measured changes (a zero prediction receives half credit), and separate correct directions for increases and decreases. Record the two paired changes in performance against the calibrated native readout and the raw readout using 2,000 passage bootstrap draws. For exact lexical sensitivity, evaluate human agreement using all distinct normalized spellings. The model's exact disagreement predicts a range because residual spellings are pooled. Report the fraction of positions with an identified update direction (disjoint before/after ranges), and its accuracy separately for observed rises and falls. Mark errors, true abstentions, empirical zeros and sample counts explicitly. The operating point for pronounced observed change is 0.20 in absolute disagreement probability, fixed before scoring.

For illustration choose the first complete, nonduplicated Provo article of median eligible length (deterministic numeric article-ID tie break). Plot word-by-word empirical disagreement and the fitted contextual estimate, with their difference curves in a second panel. This selection uses stimulus geometry only. No individual trajectory, entire-exposition load or return to a conceptual baseline is observed in either corpus.
