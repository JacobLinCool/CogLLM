# Which earlier statement makes the current sentence surprising?

When the same sentence is consistent in one context and inconsistent in another, the two versions can have similar average language-model activations. A reader can discriminate them by retrieving the relevant earlier statement. We test a one-pass memory readout that scores experienced words against earlier sentences, identifies the most relevant sentence, and estimates whether the incoming content conflicts with it. A separate observation model turns this evidence into a probability of a reader's first explicit inconsistency report at a sentence boundary. The previous independent cohorts and their aggregate discrepancy are known to the investigators. This is a mechanism-driven follow-up on the same families. Its estimates cannot furnish a new blind replication. [Previous model and scope](memory-protocol.md) · [Reading task](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7)

Use the previously sealed counterfactually revised NLI triples. Each triple has the same hypothesis, distractors and position, and a human-written premise for each of three relations. Use a reproducible uniform random position for the crucial premise among the earlier sentences in each distractor count stratum. This prevents a model from always pointing at the first sentence. No sentence role, target index, question or human rating enters the encoder. Fit relevance from the known source location and conditional incompatibility from the human relation. The source location is a constructed supervision label. No claim about a person's gaze or attention follows from its recovery. Report strict three-way ranking, source retrieval among at least two candidates, and the text-position and lexical comparators on the source's held-out split. The constructed no-distractor cases cannot support a source-retrieval ranking.

In a single causal Qwen forward over each text, cache the middle and final hidden states at visible word boundaries, the attention assigned from that position to already read sentences, and an external learned contradiction score. The readout compares the evidence at a word with representations of *completed* earlier sentences. A word belonging to a later sentence must never change an earlier decision. Confirm native forward counts, original character bounds and prefix equivalence. Complete sentence boundaries define the available reading response, but the model can update within a sentence. Direct supervision from human keys applies only where a completed sentence elicited a response.

Fit separate components against the observations that identify them. For each held Frick story family, use the other Experiment 1 families' risk sets to fit a sentence-end first-report hazard. The primary fitted readout jointly minimizes the sequential Bernoulli loss and the binomial conditional loss comparing first reports at the same target across the actual two conditions. In that paired loss, conditioning on different outcomes of one reader from each condition removes a shared material intercept. The signed log-odds difference for this loss uses unconditional first-report probabilities, which also account for early alarms. Ground the offset and early-event rate in the full risk sets. Each family gives equal total weight to each observation type; retain an unpaired counterpart with the same features, and the prior calibrated terminal-conflict head. Regularize slopes and use training-only scaling. Apply these heads to complete unaltered articles with no paired counterpart, target mark or trial design needed at inference.

Evaluate the two later participant cohorts with consistent data handling to the previous experiment. The main quantity is the reduction in absolute error of the human average inconsistent-minus-neutral first-report effect at the unchanged target. The main timing safeguard is non-inferiority in whole-path log loss, with an upper bound of +0.02 nats per trial, compared with the prior frozen head. Report each condition's pretarget alarms and the model-minus-human effect with a ±10-point equivalence margin. Use the same 10,000 crossed reader and story bootstrap draws as the locked experiment. Report all models including surface, surprisal and the ablation dropping paired supervision. Evaluate on all 12 families.
