# Source-verifiable split interventions

After hashing the source packet and fixing the estimator, we compared the literal intervention to the workbook's designated target. We had not evaluated any forecasts against outcomes. Of 180 paired split comparisons, 143 contained each designated phrase exactly once with identical sequences of case-normalized alphanumeric words outside the phrase. Twenty-eight pairs lacked a uniquely matched designated phrase in one or both variants. Nine had a uniquely matched phrase but different outer words. Some differences concern determiner changes or repeated mentions. In at least three source families the supplied cloze-answer nouns create implausible original sentence text, including an outbreak of “liver function blood tests”. This mismatch also appears in the workbook's four-noun completion materials. We cannot establish from the release alone which literal words participants saw on their difficulty-rating screen. Keep the primary forecast and analysis on all eligible families as specified. As a material-sensitivity analysis, retain only families whose two split contrasts, both with and without pseudowords, pass the literal phrase and unchanged-surroundings check. Use the original held-out forecasts, calculate the aggregate split and pseudoword contrasts, and use the same stratified family bootstrap. A literal filter can exclude sound editorial changes such as an added article and repeated occurrences of the designated phrase. It directly tests how the conclusion changes in the unambiguous subset. It fixes neither participant-level noise nor any ambiguity about the original elicitation. If item-level results respond differently across subsets, report both.
