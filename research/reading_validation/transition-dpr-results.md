# An event changes which earlier entity the text describes

The first changed word shifted the model toward the authored referent in 68.0% of 194 contrastive English sentence pairs (95% pair-bootstrap interval 61.3–74.7%). Both versions had the same visible text through the preceding word, named the same two possible referents and eventually favored different ones. A fixed predictor of time since the pronoun scored 50.0%. These annotations identify where the inputs begin to differ. They record the writer's two intended interpretations. A separate reader experiment below measures the choices made by people. ## When the interpretations diverge

The source comprised 659 aligned training pairs. The frozen Qwen3.5-2B exposed earlier words, the pronoun and each new event word in one article forward; a linear readout learned from the final author choices and the change across paired endings. Across all 279 aligned official test pairs, the semantic-state readout selected the author's intended entity on 64.9% of sentences, versus 62.7% for 48 native attention heads and 50.2% for the trained surface observer. Resolving both sentences correctly was harder: 39.1%, 34.4% and 0.7%, respectively. The semantic advantage over attention alone in pair success was 4.7 percentage points [−2.2, 11.1]. When every preceding word was eligible without identifying the two plausible entities, semantic top-one accuracy was 21.1%, compared with 23.3% for the surface observer. [Endpoint denominators and pair resampling](../results/reading-validation-transition-dpr/authored-pairs.json)

Of those 279 pairs, 195 had identical prefixes, the same alternatives and opposing targets. The preceding score discrepancy was at most 0.000015 in log odds. In one pair the shorter member ended before the other supplied a changed lexical word. At the first changed word in the remaining 194 pairs, the average shift favoring the intended contrast was 0.342 log-odds units [0.246, 0.444] for the semantic readout and 0.181 [0.122, 0.241] for the trained attention readout. Their paired difference was 0.160 [0.087, 0.236]. Attention distinguished the correct direction in 69.1% of pairs, comparable with the semantic readout's 68.0%. Among the 68 pairs with equal total word counts, both native readouts favored the correct direction at the first change in 73.5% [63.2, 83.8%], compared with 50.0% for the step and surface controls. At sentence end the semantic direction was correct in 73.8% [67.7, 80.0%] and both final choices were correct in 41.5% [34.9, 48.2%]. The signed change measures reallocation between two hypotheses. Absolute effort, elapsed human resolution time, and the personal intensity of difficulty were unobserved. [Word-by-word analysis](../results/reading-validation-transition-dpr/authored-pairs.json)

## Transfer to a separate sample of human readers

Thirty physical-event families from Jones and Bergen provided 1,012 choices collected from 37 readers. The original experiment counterbalanced which object was mentioned first. After the event, the semantic readout predicted the actual choices better than it had at the earlier pronoun, reducing log loss from 0.912 to 0.686. The paired difference was −0.226 [−0.314, −0.136]. Hard-choice agreement reached 62.3%, compared with 48.6% for the surface readout (difference 13.7 percentage points [0.7, 26.0]). The registered proper-score comparison with surface features was inconclusive (−0.009 [−0.099, 0.129]). Attention alone scored better than the semantic readout in log loss, 0.637 versus 0.686, with a paired difference of 0.049 [0.009, 0.094]. The separate model learned from direct reader clicks reached 0.639. All were applied to the identical original stimuli. [Reader choices](../results/reading-validation-transition-dpr/independent-reader-choices.json) · [Original experimental article](https://doi.org/10.1017/langcog.2024.2)

The 135 independent readers in the timed experiment contributed 3,777 trials. At the preselected fifth region the semantic belief correlated 0.120 [−0.182, 0.366] with the item-specific ambiguity-by-referent reading-time contrast. Added to the held-family model with region surprisal, repetition, mention order and human syntax norms, it changed mean squared error by +0.00036 [−0.00302, 0.00388]. This precision supports a causal text-interpretation trace on controlled alternatives and leaves human reading-time transfer unresolved. Source and target groups shared no training eight-word string under the registered filter; exposure during the frozen backbone's original pretraining remains unknown.
