# A first-passage model for when a reader notices a changed premise

In Frick and Schüler, the experimenters inserted one critical reference in each original story. Some readers had already pressed a key at an earlier sentence. A predictor that independently gives every sentence another chance to be the critical reference can accumulate too many false first reports. A second problem emerged in the previous moment-supervised model. It could predict 90 percentage points of difference in one family despite the much smaller difference normally possible between the observed reader hazards. We test whether conserving the probability that the critical reference has already been encountered can improve both the path probability and the human condition contrast. This is a method development on the previously examined 12 families, with the independent third reader cohort reserved from all fitting. [Human experimental source](memory-protocol.md) · [Prior effect and rare extreme forecasts](intervention-moment-results.md)

Before each sentence maintain the joint mass for still-unreported readers who may yet encounter the cue and still-unreported readers who already encountered it. A conditional hazard for the first experimental cue transfers mass from the first state into the second exactly once. The probability of reporting at an ordinary sentence, at a neutral cue, and at a conflicting cue comes from real first reports in source families. At a candidate cue, average those last two hazards by an inferred semantic condition probability. When a reader reports, remove their mass. Allow survival through the passage without encountering a target; supply no future passage length. The model can use the distribution of target positions in the *source experiment*. This study-specific component is a legitimate control for that protocol and does not support a claim about unstructured essays.

Train on the other families in experiments 1–2. For each source risk trajectory use textual evidence and an estimated position hazard from families other than its own and the external family. For the new family, use source families only. Supervise the first-cue time, the condition given a cue, and the reader's first-keypress trajectory through their separate likelihoods. A matched-pair observation of the change in unconditional first reports at the same sentence can further calibrate the fit, with source-only variance weighting. All emission probabilities are fixed from the training readers and covariates, preventing extreme out-of-sample extrapolation beyond observed state hazards. Compare the identical state model with and without that effect term, a position-only cue with 50/50 semantic polarity, and the previously sealed risk-set and unbounded-moment forecasts. Track first-response path loss, condition effect, false early reports and per-family magnitude with the original reader-by-family intervals. Preserve source fits and external predictions regardless of direction. The running state uses causal readout operations after one original encoder pass.
