# Learning the verb prior from human continuations across independent Mandarin studies

The word-level size of the measured verb effect motivates an explicit lexical prior. In the preceding Zhan et al. experiment the article-only readout underestimated the human contrast and the external author-supplied class performed better. This follow-up trains on separate published Mandarin norms and evaluates two different cohorts. The Xia results are a new source. Its original group-level findings and several table rows were inspected to establish outcome conventions. Model forecasts have not been made on those verbs.

Use the 150 rows of Zhang's Appendix B, where the rounded bias is (first minus second)/(first plus second). Exclude discontinuous constructions with intervening argument placeholders, and exclude every spelling used in either the Xia or Zhan evaluations when fitting the main lexical predictor. Retain verb duplicates as one lexeme with equally weighted source observations, recording any duplicates. The source has aggregate bias, without per-verb individual records or sample sizes. Each source verb therefore receives equal likelihood weight. [Zhang's article and table](https://www.macrothink.org/journal/index.php/ijl/article/view/22726)

The first independent endpoint is Xia's percentage selecting the subject rather than object at SVO constructions for all 49 verbs in Tables 5.5 and 5.6. The article reports 166 eligible readers. Score unadjusted predictions of aggregate NP1 choice by proper binary log loss, expected Brier score, error in the observed item probability, Spearman correlation and agreement about the majority choice. Report paired whole-verb bootstrap intervals versus source-average probability and a Chinese character n-gram control. All words in both test corpora stay out of the independent norm training even if a source norm contains an exact spelling. Report the co-verb construction secondarily, using the same verb forecasts; the isolated prior cannot express a syntax contrast. [Xia's original thesis, chapter 5, Tables 5.5–5.6](https://pure.uvt.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/68342044/Xia_Why_10_03_2023_1_.pdf)

The lexical model uses Qwen's frozen mean input embedding of the published Mandarin predicate tokenization, with equal weight on the cosine kernel and its character n-gram kernel. Center and scale on the independent source. Fit binomial logistic regression to each source's fractional response, with ridge .01 on slopes. Keep the source mean and character-only model. A prespecified secondary model substitutes equal-weight frozen middle/final contextual representations of the isolated predicate, plus the original shared orthographic kernel. Store this lexical dictionary offline. Looking up the prepared predicate prior adds zero encoder calls during passage scoring. The excerpted predicate identities come from the experimental materials. Automatic Chinese verb identification in an unseen article is outside this measurement.

For the previously inspected Zhan population, give the same unchanged independent lexical probability to both constructions of each predicate. Fit the published conditional/free joint-observation law to the Zhan training cohorts with one external predicate family omitted per fit. Add the centered independent lexical log odds to the original native/surface kernels at equal representation weight. Compare the 1,504 held overt choices with the original article-only readout, its equal-representation lexical control, and the independently rated category baseline. Report whether the externally measured IC-1 minus IC-2 magnitude improves. No hyperparameter or layer selection on either held cohort. Disclose endpoint and participant-population differences and the lack of individual response records in the norm tables.
