# Cross-model agreement reduced accuracy on the registered human-word tasks

We selected 24 directions in the frozen Qwen word representation for their reproducibility across LFM and Gemma representations of the same visible words. All transformations and observer fits used training sentences only. At deployment the fitted observer uses Qwen once. An equal-dimensional Qwen-only principal-component projection received the same training observations, outcome-specific likelihoods and surface predictors. Across held English sentences, the cross-model projection reduced mean predictive log density by 0.0141 nats per observation (95% paired sentence interval −0.0212 to −0.0071). Its individual changes were −0.0107 for the N400, −0.0157 for the observed cloze miss and −0.0159 for judged unpredictability. It did outperform the surface-only model by 0.0277 nats (0.0193–0.0361). The source-fitted cross-model projection also reduced transfer to 299 families of Spanish word readings. Spearman correlation fell from .1842 to .1739, a difference of −.0103 (95% family interval −.0169 to −.0041). In the separate nine-laboratory English study, its family-specific noun effect correlated .473 with measured effects, compared with .495 for Qwen-only residual components. Agreement among three pretrained encoders is therefore an insufficient selection criterion for this combination of observed human responses. The result concerns this explicit shared subspace and this 24-dimensional budget. It can coexist with useful joint supervision of distinct outcomes within one model.
