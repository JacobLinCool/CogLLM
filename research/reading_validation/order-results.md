# When does presenting familiar information first help a reader?

Seventy-five readers saw English passages in which the authors varied clause structure, the order of events, and where previously given information appeared. We recovered all eight rendered conditions for 61 of 64 materials. The complete usable sample contained 4,560 target-sentence times, 4,219 usable second-clause times and 1,098 answers to comprehension questions. The authors recorded actual time in regions of text. A word inside a timed clause has no separate difficulty label in this analysis. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2077971) · [Open data](https://osf.io/j3kf6/) · [Material selection and estimator](order-protocol.md)

Across the 61 eligible materials, giving familiar information first shortened total sentence reading time by **177 ms** in subordinate-first sentences (95% crossed reader/material interval 46–314 ms faster). In main-clause-first sentences the difference was 56 ms faster (179 ms faster to 67 ms slower). The estimated difference between these effects was 121 ms [−62, 313]. The second clause showed a different pattern. Its first-pass time in main-first sentences was 54 ms longer [1, 105] when familiar information came first. These responses illustrate why an author needs to know which region and which reading outcome an intervention affects. The intervals are descriptive by endpoint. ## Forecasts for new readers on new passages

We fitted the reading model with 36 material families and 45 participants and reserved 16 other materials and 19 other participants for the primary test. The article was read once in each of 488 original wordings. In 276 usable second-clause first passes from the held readers, adding the contextual readout to word surprisal worsened Gaussian log loss from **1.004 to 1.119** (paired increase 0.115 [0.058, 0.178] nats). In the 303 complete-sentence times, the same models scored 0.646 and 0.706. Optional measured reading ability and working memory worsened timing forecasts under the fitted source interactions. Only 64 comprehension responses were observed in this panel, with just three matched factorial strata per clause structure. Reporting zero observed difference in these sparse matched strata as an effect of writing order would be misleading. [Estimation and contrasts](order-estimator.md) · [Sealed forecasts](../results/reading-validation-discourse-order/forecast/seal.json)

On all readers of the same 16 held materials, every factorial stratum was represented for complete-sentence time. In subordinate-first sentences the human estimate was 136 ms faster [393 ms faster, 119 ms slower]; the contextual readout predicted 309 ms faster. Native semantic competition did not demonstrate a gain over the form or surprisal control on this intervention. A general promise that CogLLM knows how to reorder an explanation would exceed this evidence. [All-reader and crossed test panels](../results/reading-validation-discourse-order/scores.json)
