# Is a Spanish N400 readout specific to the English N400 measurement?

Apply the previously fitted Spanish contextual EEG and early-window readouts, each without updates, to all 1,726 originally displayed English words. The earlier English N400 association and the general published pattern of cross-measure language effects are known. The Spanish readouts’ associations with the other released English windows have not been calculated here. The N400 and later P600 and PNP amplitudes come from different time windows and electrodes. The English study also measured first fixation, first pass, right-bounded time, go-past time and self-paced reading. These represent different processes. A scientific interpretation of a population reading signal must distinguish them.

Define the component-specific physiological direction in advance: multiply ELAN, LAN and N400 by minus one; retain the native sign of EPNP, P600 and PNP. The primary specificity contrast compares the Spanish contextual N400 score’s English N400 correlation with its strongest correlation in the five other direction-coded windows. Also report an absolute-value comparison to allow reverse-signed associations. Compare the original trained Spanish surface-plus-surprisal readout, the earlier EEG-window contextual readout and the raw Qwen surprisal control. Calculate ranks at all exact originally displayed positions and preserve the 205 source sentence groups. Report simultaneous 95% intervals for five target-versus-other correlations and paired bootstrap inference for the maximum comparator. On authentic word-time data independently summarize the five correlations and each measure’s observed coverage, avoiding replication of the same trial. No new fits, external scores, feature selection or participant recruitment. Shared reading complexity or common voltage across windows can explain nonselective associations. Direct evidence about an individual question’s resolution remains a separate observation.
