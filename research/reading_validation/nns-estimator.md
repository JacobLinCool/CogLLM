# Estimating the chance that a reader flags a word

The earlier supervised-material scan excludes one highly replicated sentence previously used by the rewrite experiment and one lower-replication sentence related to a held sentence. There are 200 eligible dense text families and 8,926 eligible sparse sentences before alignment. The same amount of evidence was recorded for every designated word within a released sentence. The actual replication varies between 15 and 26 across dense materials. The published nominal 20 is therefore not used as a voting denominator. The ordinal reading-proficiency category is the sole reader feature. The lexical control includes length, log length, English Zipf frequency with hinges at 3 and 5, number of vowel groups, least difficult matching CEFR level, indicators for missing levels and stem-only matches, hyphenation, digits, initial and full capitals, and log prefix word position. For the CEFR lists use a case-folded whole-form match and then a Snowball stem, taking the earliest listed level across parts of speech. An unmatched word receives a separate missing indicator and a neutral value of 3.5. Add smoothed stem-specific log odds and log number of observations in the sparse training set. Smooth with 20 pseudocounts centered on the training-wide flag rate. Exclude the whole item's text family from its own stem and worldwide rates. No difficulty annotations enter word extraction.

Estimate a binomial response likelihood over the released counts. Each sparse text family has equal total weight, its sentences have equal weight within family, and its scorable judged words have equal weight within sentence. Within a word, reader groups receive weight proportional to their observed responses. When proficiency is supplied, use five ordered thresholds and interactions of the centered proficiency number with word frequency, lexical curriculum level and smoothed stem log odds; the two models that include CompLex also interact its frozen score with proficiency. The pooled arm omits all reader inputs.

For sparse internal calibration assign text families to five folds by independently hashed sorted order cycling across folds. For every fold fit lexical, frozen-human-score-extended and full contextual arms using only the other four groups. Interpolate full and lexical forecast probabilities with one constrained coefficient minimizing the mean held-sparse binomial log loss. Fit coefficients separately for pooled and proficiency-aware branches. Refit each component to all permissible sparse observations and seal predictions for every scorable dense text word at every proficiency band before looking up any dense difficulty labels. A second standalone lexical control omits the learned stem probability when evaluating the previously trained CompLex and PLABA outputs. Report strong lexical and model estimates on exactly the same eligible word locations. Optional estimated-proficiency results describe a cohort, without establishing individual identification.

The primary concordance weights each material equally. A secondary pairwise check compares the same surface word at two different dense sentences and aggregates equally by printed word. Cluster this estimate using both contributing text families, excluding pairs that share an article-equivalent family. The within-sentence study interval reflects variation across texts. Because individual identities and Wikipedia page identifiers are absent, it conditions on the observed volunteer sample and grouping algorithm. An auxiliary frequency-matched contrast compares only pairs in the same sentence with a maximum difference in external Zipf frequency of 0.25; report its smaller denominator. The estimated observation-specific effects for the optional prior use held rows, the training-only control and exactly the same source exposure policy. Model calibration and discrimination results will be reported separately.

**Open-word check.** Independently of these comparisons, rank every prefix-certified printed word in the original sentence. Assign ties equal mass. Record how often the highest-scoring word has a published, uniquely locatable judgment. For each scorable winner estimate the actual fraction of respondents who marked it. For a winner without an exact observation give lower and upper identification bounds of zero and one respectively. Average across all eligible materials, also reporting the chance of at least one person flagging the highest word conditional on it being judged. An unobserved word does not become an automatic negative. As a separate limit of the published data, compute the identifiable interval for the proportion of the same respondents who flagged at least one of the measured words, $[\max_j k_j/n,\min(1,\sum_jk_j/n)]$. The release contains per-word totals and no mapping linking responses across words within a reader. An independent-Bernoulli aggregation would give a narrower, assumption-dependent estimate.

Half the same-word pair disagreement is an unbiased estimate of the binomial response variance if respondents are exchangeable conditional on the item. This provides a pointwise Brier reference for a pooled model. A second reference predicts each held person's binary response with the fraction among the remaining respondents to the identical item. Neither calculation requires identifying that person across words.
