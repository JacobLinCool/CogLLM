# Can a later mention retrieve associated information that the author never repeats?

Direct attention to old probes recovered a goal shift in one experiment and failed the predeclared old-character-object test in another. We test a specific memory hypothesis on these now familiar materials. When a later sentence reuses a content word from an earlier event, it may reinstate other words bound to that event. A new character need not reprint a protagonist's object for the earlier object to become less accessible. We will grade this explanation as a retrospective mechanism test. [Original independent reader cohorts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4266406/) · [Earlier goal intervention](transition-goal-access-results.md)

In the introductory passage, assign each candidate earlier word an association distribution over other content words. Use the occurrences in the same printed sentence as the candidate, excluding its own spelling, and weight by inverse word distance. Average over multiple occurrences. Normalize by the total eligible association weight so words with longer descriptions have no automatic advantage. At the designated downstream recognition region, first measure access to **all** intervening content words, using the mean causal native attention across the observed region. Redistribute this access by exact matches to the earlier association distribution. Every quantity uses the visible text only, with no question, condition label or target supplied to the backbone. The source introduction and downstream probe region are supplied by the experimental design. Report a lexical-only observer assigning inverse-distance attention to the identical middle words. Both observers can propagate a focus shift through an indirect association. The contrast between the native and lexical observers isolates any extra contribution of the backbone.

A transparent readout adds a small uniform opportunity across the entire intervening vocabulary to each measured cue position. Use a fixed 50% mixture between measured and uniform cue weights and retain every original family, including cases with no reinstated cue. Define a candidate's accessibility as the sum of cue-position weights times the candidate's precondition binding weights. Keep it on its natural probability scale. Grade (a) whether the earlier associated object gains access when its protagonist is rementioned versus replaced, (b) the difference from the three prespecified original comparison words under the same manipulation, and (c) the paired difference from inverse-distance retrieval. The shared introduction and immediate recognition sentence are identical across the three arms. The name experiment and the goal experiment provide separate endpoint transfer checks for neutral versus new focus and repetition versus neutral. Never convert a probability-mass difference to a human millisecond. Report all three banks, family-level intervals, no-coverage cases and per-family values. Any positive from this follow-up requires prospective replication in other narratives.
