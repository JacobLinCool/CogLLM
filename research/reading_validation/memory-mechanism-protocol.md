# Separating recognition of a relevant detail from the reader's response to a contradiction

The first out-of-family semantic readout underestimates the contextual effect in both later Frick cohorts. In the training experiment, 50.9% of inconsistent and 3.1% of consistent trials produced their first report at the designated target. Source readers therefore supplied a large contrast. Those experimental targets had a known relation to an earlier statement; that role was available in the material lists during training. We test whether learning the role separately from the response recovers more of the held effect. The two test-cohort summaries and their earlier readouts are known. This is a mechanistic follow-up on those materials, not an additional independent replication. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7) · [Previous results](memory-results.md)

Fit each model only on Experiment 1, excluding the complete family of the article to be scored. Learn a location model that distinguishes the designated sentence from other visible sentence ends and an incompatibility model that distinguishes the two assignments *within* that recognized role. These targets describe the published stimulus construction. They do not label all naturally occurring misunderstandings. For the first model, each source article gives equal total weight and the two classes contribute equally during fitting. Restore the source fraction of target sentence ends when calculating an absolute probability. For the incompatibility model the identical target wording in each story supplies a matched source pair. Train a binary likelihood plus a matched conditional likelihood; give the two losses equal mass and regularize standardized slopes with a ridge of 0.02. Fit both a position-and-word-form reference and a model adding the fixed, external premise-revision readout at visible subwords, including within-sentence pooling. Repeat the identical estimator with separately sealed causal retrieval features once available. Original participant responses in the matching family never enter any head. No test outcome, text-pair difference or future position is a feature.

Estimate three reporting hazards using the original number of human trials still at risk. The first is the background chance of a first report before a designated target. The other two are the chances at the target under a consistent and an inconsistent premise. Give each available source family equal weight, with half-event smoothing within each source risk stratum. At test time, mix these three response probabilities using the model's inferred location and incompatibility. Compute the probability of a first report at sentence t by multiplying its hazard by survival through t−1. Report the conditional hazard and the unconditional first-event probability separately. The source used an explicitly consistent premise in one arm; the two later studies used a neutral premise in the corresponding arm.

Three diagnostics receive information an author would not have to supply to the deployed model: the true experimental target location; its true condition; or both. For the location-only diagnostic, combine the designated position with the model's estimate of incompatibility. For the condition-only diagnostic, keep inferred position and supply the relation only at the true critical position; retain inferred incompatibility at other positions. The double-oracle arm uses only the source human response frequencies and supplied design labels. Its performance diagnoses how much of the discrepancy concerns encoding versus shifts in response rates. It is not an article-only model or a mathematical upper bound. Publish all four combinations and both simpler comparators. Use the pre-existing family and participant bootstrap on all twelve repeated target families. A new improvement needs both reduced absolute error on the human target effect and the original timing non-inferiority bound of +0.02 nats on each cohort's whole-path loss. Report early alarms and held-out target/sentence rankings as separate diagnostics.
