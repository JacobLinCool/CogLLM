# Early warnings of a scene change that readers judge abrupt

A diagnostic after the essay evaluation found that the article readout detected 17 of the 112 DECOR discourse-relation errors. SNaC distinguishes abrupt transitions between scenes from unknown characters or events. This independent, secondary experiment addresses the narrow transition class. We fixed it before receiving the SNaC GPU features, scoring movie predictions, or viewing movie error labels. It reuses the same single article pass, article grouping, retained corpus and native readout projections as the referential-span experiment. [Source study, error definitions and interface](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.29/)

The primary observation unit is an original noninitial reading segment. SNaC readers encountered one segment containing approximately three sentences at a time. We predict whether any annotator marked an abrupt transition in that segment. This asks if the upcoming difficulty can be forecast after only its first five certified words have been read. It does not assume that the participant encountered the problem within those five words. A secondary comparison uses the full visible segment. The full-segment view may use a later exposure than the first-five estimate. Segments with fewer than five words use their complete available text. No later segments may contribute to either condition. A secondary robustness analysis requires at least two votes. The 39 previously withheld movie summaries remain untouched for both conditions.

Use the same group-separated book training and threshold-calibration partitions, L2-constrained binomial readout and book-development negative distributions as the word study. Flag a segment at a strict threshold targeting ten false alerts per 100 unmarked segments. The baseline pools surface and surprisal features, using their arithmetic means and maxima at words in the visible incoming segment. It also uses the completed previous segment's mean word surprisal and the target's visible word count. The local model adds the 96-dimensional mean current visible word representation. The relational candidate adds the 96-dimensional mean of the completed prior segment, the elementwise product with the new segment, their cosine, and average earlier-word antecedent features. The dimensions and penalty are fixed. The primary test is improvement in first-five-word AP of the relational candidate over the surface/surprisal and local-state comparators, with paired whole-movie bootstrap lower bounds above zero. We report absolute sensitivity, false alarms and within-movie ranking alongside all contrasts. A regional human mark provides one binary outcome per segment regardless of how many words it contains.
