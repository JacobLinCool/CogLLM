# From words readers flag to revisions readers prefer

Can a reader's report that a word is hard to understand predict which of two substitutions makes the same sentence easier? Paetzold and Specia collected these responses in separate experiments with the same paper. For the second experiment here, 300 non-native English speakers compared candidate words in a common printed context. They could choose either word or judge the words equally simple. There are 21,000 released judgments. The words and entire sentence are public. Candidate replacements passed an earlier screen for grammar and preserved meaning. The experiment measures perceived ease under substitution. [Original study, sections 2 and 5](https://aclanthology.org/C16-1069/) · [Original public release](https://ghpaetzold.github.io/data/User_Studies_NNS.zip)

**Materials and blindness.** SHA-256 identifies each exact gap context and each full substituted sentence. Keep original punctuation and spacing, replace exactly the single marked gap, verify that the source position and both candidate word boundaries match, and read only public text at inference. The public file contains 894 distinct masked contexts and 3,160 filled variants. Detect exact textual overlap, shared 12-word spans and near duplicate five-word shingle matches against the prior supervised sources and both pools of the separate self-report experiment. Exclude affected ranking families from the primary. Also connect masked contexts and original-filled variants with these same overlap criteria before assignment. Retain no more than one statistical family per connected component. Reserve every third family in SHA-256 order as a full held family, starting at index zero. Fit only on the remaining families. Do not inspect any new preference outcome or the held word report outcomes until this protocol is locked. Forecasts for each held task must then be saved before joint results are graded. Held self-report material remains the original 200-sentence reserve. Unless the source can identify repeated judges between experiments, claims generalize to unseen text families within these samples.

**Actual responses and shared direction.** Use the likelihood of the released binary self-report counts at each word end and the three-way Davidson likelihood for judged substitution pairs. If word scores $d_A,d_B$ increase with difficulty, model the probabilities of reader preference for A, preference for B and a tie by a softmax with logits $((d_B-d_A)/2,(d_A-d_B)/2,t)$. Fit the tie intercept $t$ from training judgments. Each family's total weight is equal and each measured word or replacement pair has equal weight within its context. Preserve ties explicitly in log loss. One prefix-certified pass of frozen Qwen per complete original or revised sentence yields the source CompLex and PLABA values and the registered 96 projected dimensions at the word end. No question or answer enters the language model. Audit prefix invariance and disclose any unalignable cases.

**Disjoint baselines.** Use frequency, word length, published CEFR-J / Octanove word levels, and a locked strong lexical arm. That learned lexical arm receives word-shape, prefix position and sparse-human-training stem rates with the entire fitted word's own family left out. Use the same covariates, eligible words, family weights and trainable dimensionality in each task's joint-versus-independent comparison. Include the earlier immutable self-report model applied straight to the rewrite experiment for zero-shot preference direction, alongside the individual frozen CompLex and PLABA readouts. A preference provides no direct observation of absolute unknown-word probability.

**Joint estimator.** Fit independent slopes and softly shared slopes on a single training-only standardized coordinate space. The self-report channel receives a free intercept for prevalence. The preference channel receives its own tie intercept and depends only on the difference of the two words' difficulties. Normalize both losses by the entropy of the constant model fitted on their corresponding training set, average the two losses, and use a ridge penalty of 0.003 divided by four on the sum of both squared slope norms. The joint candidate adds 0.003 divided by two times the squared difference between the two slopes. The independent candidate uses zero coupling. Estimate borrowing on four internal family-disjoint folds in both training sources. Choose the fraction of the joint and independent probabilities for each endpoint by minimizing the corresponding held-source proper score over a convex mixture. Keep the regularization and candidate set fixed. A shared lexical-only arm controls for cross-study learning from surface cues. The trained/contextual primary includes those features plus the two frozen human scores and 96 causal projected dimensions. Reader proficiency is reserved as a secondary analysis in the word study; both primary joint endpoints use article text alone.

**Tests.** On independent unseen word-report families, compare the primary joint model to its identical-feature independent arm by the paired difference in mean binary response log loss. On independent unseen ranking families, evaluate the paired difference in mean three-class log loss. Claim an improvement on both behaviors only if both family-bootstrap 95% lower bounds for independent minus joint loss exceed zero. Report directional concordance against every actual non-tie reader vote, including ½ credit for predicted ties, and panel-majority directional concordance separately. Compare zero-shot forecasts to raw frequency and the sparse-trained lexical arm; the last is the strong baseline. Report comparison counts, gain for frequency-discordant pairs, number of ties, winner probabilities, and family uncertainty. The previous direct word-report analysis keeps its original primary comparison. Resample independent families 10,000 times using seed 231907. Retain uncertainty and any negative or task-specific result.
