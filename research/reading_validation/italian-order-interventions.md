# Identifiable sentence manipulations in Italian material

The shared-task test omits the investigators' intervention labels. Before opening any of its human ratings or the new native predictions, we discovered that contiguous authors' Task 1 prompts provide verifiable sentence locations for some of the same source documents. This limited subgroup supports a distinction between genre, agreement with a human whole-passage judgment, and sensitivity to a change in sentence order. The overall Italian held-set analysis and the planned genre control are fixed independently of this reconstruction. **Recovering original order.** Read only public Task 1 training prompts. Record each of their three consecutive, verbatim author sentences at successive source-document coordinates. For positive next-sentence examples only, record the provided target as the fourth sentence. In the TED source, use the document and its offset. In Wikipedia, keep article, paragraph, and offset distinct. Require all available sentences at a given coordinate to agree on word sequence. For an original held Task 2 passage, require a unique source document under the separately frozen material-identification procedure and exactly one complete four-sentence segmentation into word sequences occurring at known locations in that document. Do not use human scores or semantic similarity. Classify consecutive positions as original, a single transposition of two positions as swapped, and replacing one of four consecutive positions by the position ten sentences later as a substitution. Compute whether the original/perturbed label, genre and document tallies vary by retrieval. Public text from a second task supplies the **construction** label. Human raters supply a whole-passage 1–5 judgment.

**Observed outcome.** On the authentic 46 identified held passages, compare published human mean ratings of the 23 originals against the 23 altered passages, retaining swaps and substitutions separately as descriptive categories. Report mean differences and Spearman concordance between each registered model and direct human grades. Prespecify the subgroup with both original and altered examples from the same source. Give each such source the same weight and compare mean human ratings and model predictions within source, with exploratory intervals given the five eligible sources. The published materials at these two conditions contain different passages even within the same source. Selection into this subset relies on public phrase overlap and will be reported.

**Exact same-word intervention.** Nineteen intact and fourteen naturally swapped held materials reproduce the original four Task 1 sentence strings **byte for byte** when joined by a single space in the observed order. Retain these 33 for a separate paired test that holds the wording and punctuation of every sentence fixed. Recover the true order for every author-swapped material. For each intact material, choose one of six possible two-sentence transpositions by the first 64 bits of SHA-256 on the public stimulus hash prefix `cogllm-italian-order-swap-v1.`, modulo six, over lexicographically ordered sentence-index pairs. Re-encode the resulting 33 alternative passages in one pass per alternative. No new natural-language text is authored. Every pair receives equal weight and bootstrap the identifiable source article or talk (10,000 paired draws, seed 378283). A fixed native model distinguishes order if the 95% lower bound for canonical > transposed choice exceeds 50%, with ties scored halfway. Compare its performance and mean paired coherence-score change against untouched independently fitted form, form-plus-surprisal, shared, and official adjacent lexical Jaccard where defined. Show the 14 restorations and 19 experimental swaps separately. Only the original version in each pair received human judgments.

**First disrupted link.** Find the earliest noninitial source sentence whose predecessor, in the permuted article, was not its true immediate predecessor. At that sentence compare the disruption score to the score on the identical sentence in its canonical context. Use the independently English-calibrated boundary logit and the corresponding source-development adaptive **English** mixture of probabilities. The Italian adaptive weight selects a whole-passage grade, not a boundary probability. Also evaluate only sites where the canonical sentence occurs after a predecessor, giving both versions a nonempty sentence prefix. Require the official source sentence in the two variants to be an exact native sentence interval. Report eligible counts and missing geometry. The structural break is authenticated; the original Italian study never asked readers to mark when they noticed it or which prior sentence they retrieved.
