# Can a biomedical sentence readout anticipate how a lay reader judges an expert summary?

Chan et al. gave lay readers a public scientific abstract, asked about familiarity with its designated terms, then showed the expert written Cochrane plain-language summary. Readers took a multi-select content quiz before submitting quality ratings. The published interface shows both abstract and summary on the rating screen. The ease prompt asks how easy the summary was to understand. Higher ratings on the five-level scale mean better perceived quality. The separate understanding dimension concerns fit to the reader's knowledge, and the explanation dimension concerns clarification of unfamiliar concepts. The registered primary is perceived simplicity. We also retain both of these latter dimensions, coherence and actual quiz correctness as distinct named outcomes. We know the published overall means and the judgment-column names. We have not joined any of these outcomes with any model output. **Independent transfer.** The primary predictor is the unweighted mean of the previously deployed PLABA sentence-ease ratings across certified English sentences in the summary. Require certified ratings covering at least 90% of the summary's words. Predict higher simplicity from higher ease. Compare with the constant predictor, with the negated authors' unpersonalized mean readability grade (Flesch–Kincaid, Gunning fog, SMOG, Coleman–Liau), with negative word count, and with the frozen matched surface-only educational grade. Report the underlying Flesch–Kincaid grade separately. The primary success criterion is above-chance ranking and improvement over the authors' readability formula, each with a strictly positive lower paired 95% confidence bound. The source-trained educational Qwen grade and the PLABA understanding column are secondary. No target outcome calibrates the source comparisons. The model input is the summary alone.

**Rows and material.** Include every complete static visit with all responses submitted from participants assigned exactly the published three static summaries. Exclude the entire released six-visit participant from the primary and repeat a sensitivity including all six. Use only anonymous numeric reader indices for weights. Track public article families via exact or strong five-gram summary overlap, identical normalized abstract titles and identical original scientific abstracts. Group different releases of the same material in all folds and intervals. Exclude strong eligible-summary overlap with the actually used PLABA/educational/CompLex supervised texts from the independent test, recording incidental twelve-word windows separately. Check prior training and development exposure of the earlier 24-source packet separately. Use only the 91 original public expert summaries in the GPU packet. Individual quiz selections, prior categories and ratings stay local and are reported in aggregate. There are no interactive or personalized texts or additional participants.

**Primary measurement.** Compare two distinct summary families read by the same person, retaining pairs with different observed simplicity. Same-family pairs never enter concordance. The unit of reporting is the reader, with all their informative pairs weighted equally. A model tie receives half credit. Use 10,000 crossed reader and material-family bootstrap samples (seed 693701), recalculating within-reader denominators using the product of both sampled family multiplicities. Apply the same draws to all methods. Report the raw 1–5 range and frequency, appropriate denominators, and single-article rating range across repeated readers. Secondary comparisons use the same unit for organization/coherence, fit to existing knowledge, effectiveness of explanation and the number of retained content questions completely correct. Corresponding frozen PLABA understanding and ease models are reported for each outcome. Also compare two respondents reading the exact same summary, conditioning an optional prior-only prediction on their mean designated-term familiarity collected before the summary while the original abstract was visible. Every summary-only head ties on this comparison. Use the same article/reader clustering and distinguish ties in the prior as well as ties in the outcome.

**Comprehension scoring.** The publication describes four expert-selected content questions, plus an attention check, and reports scores out of twelve over three visits. The release gives five stems, option strings, keyed option sets, and selected option sets. Recognize the nonmedical attention-check stems by exact normalized text (colors, fruits, animals, clothing), verify there is exactly one and record its slot. For the four content questions award one point only for an exact unordered set match, after Unicode and whitespace normalization. A wrong additional choice invalidates a response. Report per-item accuracy, the distribution of 0–4 for each summary visit, and attention-check performance separately. A secondary option-level accuracy gives equal weight to each content question's three binary option decisions. If our aggregate differs from the published mean, report the discrepancy and do not use it to select a grading scheme.

**Adaptive sharing.** For a separate prespecified within-study test, freeze source-trained human signals and fit new target measurement heads on visits from different readers and material families. Hold out a combination of one of five article-family folds and one of five reader folds, assigning both with fixed seeded hash order and cycling across five folds. No training example can share either test reader or test material. The neutral prior predictor uses a training-only empirical outcome distribution or cloze success fraction. The article-only baseline uses mean word length, words per completed sentence, and log word count. The shared arm adds only frozen data-derived human response directions from PLABA (mean sentence ease), a cross-study supervised educational readout (expected grade), and the new noun-phrase experiment (two condition-free sentence-response estimates). We will assess this final addition only if it can be reconstructed without a secondary Qwen call or an invented word-level proxy. Orient prior signals toward higher ease and retain their original target scale to calculate training-only standardization. An optional arm adds the independently obtained mean prereading term-familiarity score to both text models. Do not include help requests, the original question, quiz alternatives, personal background, post-reading judgments, participant identity or article identity as predictors.

Fit each of the four ordinal outcomes with a cumulative logistic model, ordered thresholds, training-only feature standardization and a 0.1 slope ridge on mean per-visit loss. Fit the number of exact answers as four binomial observations with a logit link and the same slope ridge. These are observation-specific likelihoods over the same fixed article input. Calibrate the *incremental reliability of the source priors* using out-of-group loss on nested predictions within the permitted training set. Inner folds use the same subject/material isolation. Choose one convex interpolation of independent and shared predicted distributions by minimizing each endpoint's proper inner score on [0, 1], using four-threshold mean squared error (ranked probability score) for ratings and the Brier score per correctness item for the quiz. An endpoint can thus borrow nothing if the source harms that endpoint. Report the independent, jointly informed and adaptive arms on the same outer observations, plus the same arms with measured priors. The second success criterion is a positive out-of-group improvement in simplicity RPS over the independent surface arm, with lower paired 95% interval above zero. Keep quiz scores separate. Improvements on this previously untouched study would support transfer of source supervision to this response, not prove all human measures share a cognitive scale. None of the previously reserved studies or the 16 reserved Guo outcomes enter any source or target fit.
