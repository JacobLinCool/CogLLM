# Would a reader accept the suggested word as part of their earlier phrase?

A reader who clicks a three-word phrase accepts all three words. A reader choosing a nonlink discourse action accepts none. We fit an independent probability of word membership, using 949,705 earlier word positions at 2,758 training pronouns in 230 source works. The native 96-parameter readout converged to an absolute maximum gradient of 6.1 × 10⁻⁹. Source sampling combined uniform visible words and the earlier conditional pointer's distribution, as specified in the protocol. [Source model](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/word-acceptance/source-policy.json) · [Native eligibility and loss](transition-word-region-utility-protocol.md)

Across 80 entirely new works and 2,613 original player actions, the direct model reduced word log loss by .0200 [.0020, .0399] versus the earlier independent gate. Its word Brier difference was +.0023 [−.0028, +.0076]. At the model's top word, observed unconditional reader acceptance was .346 [.301, .391]. Its forecast was .438, yielding .092 average overconfidence. The source-selected screen failed to establish 70% precision, and the improvement in the actual suggested word was +.002 [−.004, +.009]. The result supports assigning appropriate probabilities to multiple words in a span while leaving automatic author markings unqualified.
