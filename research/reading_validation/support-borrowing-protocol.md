# Does transfer depend on whether a study has seen similar reading material?

The earlier fit calibrated one sharing coefficient for each observation type. It cannot change its reliance on a study-specific projection when a new passage falls in a region represented mainly by other studies. We test an exposure-dependent borrowing rule on the same 24-source, 62-outcome archive. Its earlier outer development scores are known. This is an internal mechanism experiment. The criterion for useful adaptation is a lower proper held score than the original endpoint-specific coefficient and the equal-weight mixture. A new density rule that wins only against forced sharing offers a weaker conclusion.

Summarize an observed reading opportunity by the mean normalized causal Qwen projection over its recorded stops. This representation applies to the original measurement unit, including a set of candidate stops for a first-report task. It contributes to selecting an observer for that unit; a passage-level judgment remains a passage-level observation. Build one centroid per source-training material and source using unique coordinates across all compatible endpoints. Repeated rows from one material do not create independent support. At every evaluation opportunity calculate mean cosine distance to the three nearest source-family centroids (all if fewer), in its own source and in other sources permitted to update at least one of the same reading states. Center and scale their difference on the source calibration inputs alone. A positive difference means that nearby examples came largely from elsewhere.

The geometry is learned on the source fitting folds only. Keep the original independently calibrated endpoint coefficient as an intercept and adjust its sharing log odds by a nonnegative common slope times the relative distance, clipped to four calibration standard deviations. Estimate the slope with the observation-specific geometric predictive density, one unit of importance per source family up to 32 effective families per source and one half-normal prior with unit standard deviation. Learn the slope anew for each target study, excluding *all* its calibration responses from its estimate. The target's calibration is used only for its original fixed intercept and to express its support difference in familiar units. Thus a slope can transfer to an excluded study even when its endpoint has a different likelihood. Compare both original seeds. Grade the same held families, actual trials, observation-specific densities, source weighting and independent-study bootstrap as the frozen earlier experts. If slope zero is selected, the method collapses exactly to the previous positive control. Confirm this identity numerically in all ten likelihood types.
