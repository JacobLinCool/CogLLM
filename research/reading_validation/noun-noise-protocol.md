# How much of the item-level cloze error can be sampling variation?

Leroy et al. scheduled 35 evaluations per sentence, then retained 11,949 assessments across 360 conditions. A reader contributed an integer difficulty rating from one to five and a binary correct or incorrect cloze response. The accompanying workbook gives both cell means to high precision, without trial records. Before inspecting whether item denominators are recoverable or comparing any model forecast to those averages, we specify a secondary audit of information retained by this release. It leaves all primary estimators and family weights intact. [Study procedure](https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2015.1131775) · [Workbook](https://doi.org/10.25422/azu.data.21357498.v1)

For each item enumerate denominator candidates from one to 35 for which both means times that denominator differ from valid integers by less than 0.000001. Restrict candidates jointly to those compatible with the published 11,949 total, using exact forward and backward sets of attainable totals. A feasible solution does not establish a unique solution. Report the numbers of uniquely identified and ambiguous cells, and minimum and maximum feasible trial-weighted split and nonsplit error rates. Mark any disagreement between the recovered denominators and publication statistics. Do not recover respondent identities. The algorithm operates only on aggregate cell means.

For each feasible assignment, $p(1-p)/(n-1)$ estimates the sampling variance of the observed cloze error fraction in a cell with $n>1$ independent, identically distributed binary responses. Report conservative bounds on its mean across items using all individually feasible counts, permitting a wider interval than a joint global optimum. Compare this scale descriptively with the primary out-of-family cloze MSE. Participant overlap across items, fixed panels, varying reader composition and overdispersion make this a model-based noise estimate. The two published means do not determine a five-level rating variance, a split-half reliability, or the within-person association between perceived difficulty and correctness. This analysis estimates none of those quantities.
