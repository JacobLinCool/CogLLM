# Readers' source highlights transfer better than the timing of their inconsistency reports

When an annotator had marked the conflicting passage in a news continuation, a causal readout selected the indicated earlier sentence in **69.8%** of eligible marks. A trained comparator using lexical overlap, distance and word form selected it in 57.5%. The 12.3-percentage-point gain had a paired 95% interval of 1.2 to 23.4 points over prompt groups. The study includes 154 marks across 73 groups with at least two completed candidate sentences. Across all 226 available marks, 72 had too few candidates for this comparison. The readout learned source relationships on counterfactual human-authored premises, before application to the news annotation task. A writer interested in what a passage refers back to has stronger evidence when that passage has already been located. The strong source-retrieval result and weak uncued localization are compatible. Assigning an earlier source given a known cue conditions on information that an author pasting an unmarked draft cannot supply. We separately scored the joint event on unmarked articles. Each native passage received one content pass, in 623 length-batched forwards over 7,467 distinct texts and 638,068 model tokens. All four source fits met their gradient check, and independent truncated-prefix reruns checked causal equivalence. ## An earlier detail changes how readers respond to an unchanged sentence

In held Frick reading cohorts, changing an earlier detail increased first inconsistency reports at the same target sentence by **32.12 and 31.70 percentage points**. The original model predicted 4.54 points and the contextual retrieval model with a paired response likelihood predicted 4.33. Its gain in absolute effect accuracy relative to the original was −0.21 points in both cohorts. Its trajectory log loss was worse by 0.398 and 0.374 nats per reader trial. It failed both the prespecified effect-size criterion and the timing safeguard. The later cohorts reuse the same twelve story families with different readers; the families were omitted from the earlier-cohort response fit in turn. [Human experiment](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7) · [Condition effects and paired uncertainty](../results/reading-validation-retrieval/context-scores.json)

A source-only diagnostic learned how often a reader responded when the experimenters' target and condition were given explicitly. With ordinary text alone it predicted a 2.04-point effect. Giving it the location alone produced 6.46 points, the experimental condition at that location alone produced 10.67, and both pieces of design information produced 46.34. The last figure overshoots the later human effect. A successful model must distinguish a cue from ordinary text, determine how an earlier detail changes it, and allow for a different reporting rate in a new study. A third study of 179 school readers and 4,248 after-reading judgments also favored its constant reference in log loss (0.715 versus 1.049 for the paired retrieval model).
