# Locating a recorded correct human recognition response in a progressively revealed clue

A person who recognizes a referent can click a buzzer. Unlike a labeled definition or evidence span, this event has a recorded human response location. It occurs during competitive quiz answering and includes decision and motor latency. An archive of correct buzzes cannot tell us when silent readers understood the passage. We therefore estimate the word-position distribution **conditional on an observed correct response**, and test which arriving words shift its probability. The task supplies the continuous clue text only. Neither the correct answer nor reader metadata enters the model. It remains separate from the scientific-text reading assessment.

Use the public 2018 ACF Regionals question archive, pinned revision 5d86399bda90b6d7af514b6d035419016816f378. Its two source editions include canonical packets with word-number tags and anonymizable score tables. Match each log row to edition, packet, tossup number and exact published word count. Exclude missing or out-of-range positions, protests and bounce-back attempts in the primary analysis. Remove player and team information before fitting or extraction. Mark dropped source cases by reason. Exclude score-table duplicates using the original tournament/room/round/tossup/team/player key privately. Group all variants of the same question and all identical answers across train, validation and test. Allocate 60/20/20 by salted hash of the connected component of these identities. External replication uses the already audited QANTA25 release, counting only recorded correct human buzzes in a certified clue interval and bounding ambiguity to that clue. QANTA25 involves both humans and AI and combines live narration with visual clues. This is an independent source for fitted models, though its availability and aggregate totals were inspected earlier. [ACF archive and ODbL](https://github.com/quizbowl/open-data/tree/5d86399bda90b6d7af514b6d035419016816f378) · [QANTA25 audit](../semantic_tracking/qanta25-source.md) · [Related live recognition study](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.962/)

Let d(q,t) be the number of eligible correct responses at tagged word t. The observed risk set r(q,t) is the sum of correct response counts at t and later words. Learn a discrete-time logistic conditional hazard π from prefix-visible features only, minimizing a question-balanced Bernoulli risk-set likelihood over observed d and r−d. For QANTA25, score log probability assigned to the observed clue interval by summing the event masses (survival at interval start minus survival at interval end). The distribution may retain mass after the final word. The risk set conditions on eventual logged correct responding and is unsuitable for population detection sensitivity or the probability of a person eventually knowing the answer.

Fit three nested candidates: a flexible absolute-word-position and sentence-boundary timing baseline; a trainable lexical baseline adding causal unigrams, previous and current word, within-question recurrence, word/sentence form and explicitly visible tournament phrases; the same lexical features plus frozen Qwen3.5-2B mid/final states and left-context local and running changes. Only the tagged word's last visible native token is eligible to update the hazard. For reference score an offline relative-position baseline with the final question length supplied. Fix ridge candidates and select using development response-time likelihood only. Report training and validation loss, convergence and word-to-token geometry checks. No conditions or weights will be selected using either test source. Report exact word NLL in ACF, interval NLL in QANTA25, question-balanced medians of timing error and integrated Brier error over words, the cumulative probability at the stimulus's end, and paired 95% intervals resampling entire question families. NLL superiority requires a strictly positive lower bound for the reduction against both causal position and trained lexical baselines. Show performance on science questions, lengths, response coverage and held-out groups. A positive result permits a claim only about the timing of recorded correct quiz responses under the stated risk set. [Source archive](https://github.com/quizbowl/open-data)
