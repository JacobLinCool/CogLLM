# How reproducible is the human pronoun response?

When two game players both chose a previously mentioned entity, their choices differed on an estimated 4.05% of pairs on the 373 eligible pronouns in 37 articles from 35 distinct source works (article-bootstrap 95% interval 2.74–6.46%). A second, disjoint pair reading the same mention was more likely to disagree when the first pair did. The estimated pair-to-pair correlation was .188 [.053, .412]. This quantifies limited but detectable repeatable variation across mentions. Each comparison uses four distinct voters; multiple word mentions of a single expert-identified entity count as one interpretation. [Human source](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1176/) · [Results](../results/reading-validation-transition-game/reader-replication.json)

For each mention, the independent-reader sample estimates two moments. Let $D_{12}$ indicate disagreement between distinct readers 1 and 2. Compute the mean of $D_{12}$ over all ordered pairs and the mean of $D_{12}D_{34}$ over all ordered quadruples of different readers. Under exchangeable choices within a mention these estimate its pair-disagreement rate $q$ and $q^2$. The correlation across disjoint pairs follows from the across-mention variance divided by total binary response variance. The calculation uses released entity counts and was verified against exhaustive tuple enumeration on small populations. Whole articles are the uncertainty units. Three Grimm excerpts share a source volume. Shared players across articles and selection into a reading game limit population generalization. [Implementation](transition_game_reliability.py)

The expected binary Brier loss equals .04036 for the constant rate fixed on source development. Perfect knowledge of each gold mention's population disagreement rate would have an estimated loss of .03152 [.02023, .04913], because individual pairs still vary. Their difference is .00883 [.00368, .02057]. This is an estimate under the sampling assumptions, with considerable uncertainty. It gives a reference scale for the size of a measured improvement on actual held-out choices. The model receives no gold votes.
