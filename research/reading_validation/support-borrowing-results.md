# Did material-dependent sharing improve held human responses?

We estimated a borrowing rule that could favor the shared readout whenever a passage resembled other studies' training material more than its own study's training material. Its single slope was fitted entirely outside the target study for each evaluation. Among 23 studies with held observations, adding the rule changed mean loss by +0.000003 nats [−0.000013, 0.000021] in the first fit and −0.000005 [−0.000015, 0.000000] in the second. Seventeen and 22 study scores, respectively, remained unchanged at numerical precision. Approximately equal predictive performance supports retaining the simpler endpoint-specific calibration in this archive. The geometric score uses unique fitting coordinates aggregated within the original material families. The registered experts and each of the ten observation likelihoods were retained. The old endpoint-specific forecasts reproduced when the incremental slope was zero. An outcome for which no other study could update the relevant state had no exposure-dependent sharing term. A lower loss than forced sharing carries over from the original method. These data provide no evidence that local training-material density accounts for its advantage, and the original development materials had already been evaluated.
