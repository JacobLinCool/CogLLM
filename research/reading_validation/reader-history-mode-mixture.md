# Can the same reader alternate between different response modes?

The fixed-reader-type model fit authentic earlier choices but became overconfident about the exact future word. It assumes that each person has one latent sharpness for every trial. A person can choose an obvious answer in one context while entertaining a less frequent continuation in another. The following observational reanalysis models a persistent tendency to choose each of several response modes while allowing the actual mode to vary from word to word. The previous prospective and chronological results remain unchanged. All tuning information for this new response model comes from first-page calibration ballots of other people on other sentence folds.

For each of the same 25 held-person and sentence-fold combinations, use the same peer-class intercept and slope, the nine additive offsets and the seven named-choice sharpnesses as the previous analysis. Estimate the population mixture weights for EACH RESPONSE, maximizing the proper marginal log likelihood pooled over peer histories with the same symmetric Dirichlet regularization. For the named choice use only genuine named spellings. Assume each reader has a probability vector with a Dirichlet population law. For two distinct items A and B from the same person, the model's joint predictive probability is (1−λ)p(A)p(B)+λΣπ_j q_j(A)q_j(B), where π is the appropriate across-response population mixture and λ=1/(1+κ) determines the effective prior sample size κ. Estimate λ separately for the binary and conditional spelling observations by maximizing the summed pairwise log probability over all within-peer pairs on the earlier calibration pages. Use the exact [0,1] constrained maximizer. Report both estimates and their full range across folds. At a zero boundary, preserve the learned common distribution as the population model predicts. No future score, self-selected shrinkage factor or grid search enters this determination.

Compute a chronological prediction by maintaining per-reader soft counts over modes. Given a new task response, calculate the latent-mode responsibilities using the current mixture and known single-mode response likelihoods. Starting at the trained population weights and κ prior observations, add those responsibilities to the observed soft counts. For the conditional spelling, advance only when a named word was observed. This moment-matched sequential approximation retains within-person variability even after hundreds of earlier responses. At κ=0 use a limiting concentration of 1e−8 for numerical stability. Retain the same raw sentence-prefix distribution, genuine time ordering, identical history lengths for the patient comparisons, and complete exclusion of the evaluation stem fold from the preceding responses. Check that all individual and peer-marginal distributions sum to one. Log scores will be measured on the identical, locked 284,385 outcomes; cases or experience bands may not be changed by their performance. The primary estimand is the total proper scoring advantage over the experience-matched mixture of the other readers. Also report the single matched stranger and the unadapted cohort mean.
