# Can the coherence readout identify a continuation that independent writers actually selected?

Barzilay, Elhadad and McKeown asked ten people to arrange a set of English news units into a readable summary for each of ten events. Their release separates the initial randomized units from the resulting arrangements. Some units contain two or more printed sentences. Authors reduced obvious reference cues; the material still contains authentic wording and entity recurrence. This is an observed composition decision. It supplies no subjective load value, online comprehension time or negative rating of an unselected sequence. The aggregate published findings are known. Item-level arrangements have not been accessed when registering this test. Source-only bilingual readouts, their English mixture and the auxiliary training weights are frozen; the Italian and Danish tests of these weights have already been graded. [Original methods and study](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~gmw/candidacy/Barzilayetal02.pdf) · [Original material and orderings](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~noemie/ordering/coll/)

**Label-free input.** Obtain only the published randomized lists. Use each complete news unit exactly as printed, resolving HTML entities and replacing layout whitespace by one space. Enumerate all ordered pairs of distinct units per event, join them with a newline, and encode the unmarked pair in one native Qwen3.5-2B forward, no decoding. Retain the first complete printed sentence of the second unit for boundary scoring. The newline preserves the authors’ unit boundary even if the first unit has no final period. Record all overlength or unalignable units, reciprocal directions, and duplicate sets. Check exact or 12-word material overlap against the coherence training materials and previous human-supervised sources. Exclude a target event with a shared 12-word span from the new readout's training texts in the primary analysis. Use original articles only; no news or personal metadata enters Qwen.

**Fixed predictions.** For a predecessor *i* and each candidate *j*, take the complement of the fixed bilingual English probability of marking the start-unit's first completed sentence as a coherence break. Some candidates may be consistently easy wherever they appear. **Primary** subtract that candidate's average score across all other eligible predecessors from this pair score. This has a chance level of 50% under random human ordering and asks whether the specific predecessor helps. The reference using a fixed score and no centering is secondary. Compare with the same contextual adjustment to the earlier English-only native model, form + surprisal, pure average first-sentence word surprisal, pairwise TF–IDF cosine and the English projection after source-order supervision. The TF–IDF score uses case-folded alphabetic tokens, document frequency over all provided units without labels, and smoothed IDF log[(N+1)/(df+1)]+1. Compare to the native cosine between the boundary and the preceding unit's final sentence as an additional reference. Corpus display order is deliberately randomized. One independent human's order per event, chosen by SHA-256 of the event name before inspecting judgments (integer first 16 hex digits mod number of source rows), supplies the human reference. Give immediate successors largest preference via minus the absolute displacement from their position just after the predecessor in that reference. This reference can use a real human arrangement, unlike the text-only predictors. Remove that person's order in full from the tested choice sample. Readers may have been indexed differently between pages; do not assert global reader identity without published identifiers.

**Direct human evaluation.** For each remaining observed order, at every step after the first unit, rank the actually chosen next unit against each other still-unplaced candidate with the same immediate predecessor. Ignore forced final choices. Count ties as half. Within a position give each eligible competitor equal weight, average positions within a writer's order, then average tested writers within an event and events equally. Also report exact top-one choice with ties split, and the material-specific random top-one rate given the actual candidate set size. Resample the events as clusters, keeping both El Salvador sets together automatically whether or not their wording overlaps. Every interval is conditional on the released panel. Report distributions by event and an exact one-sided signed randomization sensitivity across the at most nine conjoined event families. For three primary paired statements (original adjusted native better than 50%, better than centered raw surprisal, and augmented better than original native), give nominal and Bonferroni simultaneous 95% intervals. A comparison to the independent human reference quantifies the competing observed arrangements possible for the same wording. If the point estimate favors a method and its interval includes zero, describe the numerical difference and uncertainty. Predictions are frozen before obtaining any of the order tables. This test is limited to the immediately preceding unit and the real agreed next-choice context; it makes no claim of a generally preferred unique full article.
