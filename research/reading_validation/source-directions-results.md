# How much of the fitted response model should studies share?

Twenty-four archived human-response studies supplied 62 observation endpoints with ten likelihood families. Twenty-three studies provided held outcomes in the historical and second material partition. The remaining single-material study contributed to training. We tested whether each study should be allowed to change the orientation of the shared reading states along one learned direction. This uses 12,200 trainable readout parameters, compared with 8,639 in the completely shared and 24,515 in the private formulation. Each likelihood still evaluates its original supported position or interval. Entries are held negative log likelihood per measured trial, averaged within an endpoint and equally across studies. The historical partition had 33,208 held response rows. The second partition reserved 26,512 rows drawn from material available in the historical experiment and refit the heads and scaling on a new inner partition. The four source-direction fits reached their tuning plateaus. On the second seed of the second partition they worsened loss by .01521 [.00360, .03442] relative to separately calibrated mixing. The other three paired intervals included equality. The original controls reproduced the archived scores exactly. These comparisons favor deciding how much of two already fitted forecasts to retain. They give limited support for learning a common direction in which the language representation itself should move for every source.
