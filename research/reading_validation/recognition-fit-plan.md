# Fitting and identifying recorded response time

All four logistic designs use a constant, log absolute word position, sentence index, distance from the latest sentence boundary, and positive-part absolute word splines at 10, 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175 and 220 words. The offline length-informed oracle additionally receives final length through the fraction read. The lexical comparator adds hash-mapped current/previous word, preceding bigram, suffix, visible quiz phrases, orthography, recurrence and punctuation. The contextual candidate adds 192 fixed projected language dimensions at the arriving word, their trailing five-word mean and changes, interaction with the previous article mean and four causal native-attention concentration measures. Predictions depend only on a revealed prefix. The lexical features intentionally give baselines access to question style. Standardize weighted feature second moments on training cases and select a single ridge penalty from 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, and 1 by minimum family-weighted development NLL. Every fit must have a maximum absolute penalized gradient below 0.00015. Run an analytical-gradient check and compare predictions from complete and truncated histories. For ACF, a scorekeeper supplied a canonical numbered word for each answer. Human speech, online visual rendering and motor response introduce measurement differences relative to subwords. For the external QANTA25 study score released clue intervals only. The primary NLL gives equal weight first to the responses within a question, then to each question variant within its family, then to families. Integrated Brier compares the predicted CDF at each observed resolution to the empirical status of a uniformly sampled recorded correct response. Absolute median location error uses the eligible word/clue positions and an additional category if cumulative prediction never reaches one half. Science and length strata use the authors' subject metadata and the median training question length.
