# Can the article tell us who will ask for a definition?

For two people reading the same term in the same medical abstract, the person reporting lower familiarity was more likely to request a definition. A readout fitted to scientists' choices ranked these lay-reader pairs correctly 85.3% of the time. Ranking solely by reported familiarity gave exactly the same result. When readers had equally familiar terms in the same article, the contextual readout did not improve the choice between a definition and an example. The last row was the prespecified primary comparison. The paired difference was −9.9 percentage points [−25.0, 8.3]. Widening the context limit increased coverage to 62 pairs in 18 articles, with a difference of −1.5 points [−14.4, 15.3]. All intervals use crossed resampling of articles and readers. Word identities and the source study's trained coefficients were fixed before the target ratings were evaluated. Ties count one half. The primary cohort had 43 readers, 38 abstracts, and 563 usable term judgments. Different questions use different subsets of these observations. Among two people who gave the same familiarity score for the same term, all these deterministic readouts tied on the 42 discordant definition-request pairs. [Source training measurements](local-help-results.md) · [Target construction](relay_data.py) · [Scoring code](relay_score.py)

The source task asked scientists about prior familiarity. In ReLay the lay readers had the medical abstract visible when they rated their familiarity, before they indicated the help they wanted. Their rating provides optional information a writer's article-only analysis would not possess. The model can therefore expose what a population typically requests, and an explicitly provided familiarity score can refine that forecast. These data cannot separately attribute that refinement to language-model reasoning. The Qwen3.5-2B run processed 89 distinct source abstracts in 89 forwards, covering 94,394 tokens and scoring 752 distinct article–term combinations. Thirty-nine abstracts met the source calibration's 1,024-token range. Fifty additional abstracts met the 2,048-token extended range. Two exceeded that limit. Of 50 unique people, 49 had the regular three completed visits. The one person with six visits was included only in a declared sensitivity analysis.
