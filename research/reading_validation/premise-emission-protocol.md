# Competing interpretations of a sentence and the probability of a reader's first response

A previous mechanism analysis correctly retrieved both manipulated nouns at 15 of 22 available retained variants, yet it assigned many reports to earlier innocuous sentences. In the training likelihood, the one target sentence had received as much weight as the other at-risk sentences together. This can distort the unconditional event probability throughout an article. Here, a reader is at risk up to their first report. Fit the binomial conditional hazard on the actual risk set at each sentence, without oversampling the affected sentence. A target sentence can present a consistent, general or incompatible description. An ordinary sentence has its own baseline. Estimate these response hazards separately using only human training trials, then marginalize the hazards over the causal text model's probability of occupying each state. The resulting article trajectory has an explicit probability of no report. Score the unconditional event probability at the unchanged sentence, early false reports and likelihood of the entire response path. The meaningful calibration target is the registered ten-percentage-point bound. The previously inspected material and known effects make these improvement tests exploratory, even where the reader cohort was kept out of fitting.

Within every outer story fold use only experiments 1 and 2 from other families. Supervise retrieval with source-authored changed nouns, and supervise the no-link alternative using all source sentences. A grouped softmax estimates which past noun is being referred to, while a separate finite-class model estimates whether the designer's target relation is present here. Its input can include location and a contrast between retrieved-pair evidence and diffuse alternatives, the uncertainty in the antecedent, symmetric similarity, directional category judgments learned from independent HyperLex judgments and native Qwen word features. Source target coordinates and condition labels enter source objectives alone. Optional condition-pair conditional likelihood emphasizes discriminating a change in the earlier premise when the current sentence is literally unchanged. At inference each complete original story has only one causal feature forward. Expose a source-design oracle only as a diagnostic limit on transport of human response likelihoods to the second cohort. The oracle cannot rank ordinary unannotated writing.

The controls are source position cues plus the mixture of actual response hazards, and symmetric semantic features with the same estimator. The principal comparison uses matched neutral and incompatible materials, with without-replacement story exclusions, separate previously observed and independent reader cohorts, and two-way intervals over complete stories and readers. The original word positions and data releases are unchanged. A score improvement on the repeated twelve articles does not by itself establish external generalization.
