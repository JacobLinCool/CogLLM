# Updating an existing referent belief when the article supplies a clue

An author-facing referent tracker should preserve its best estimate when it first reaches a pronoun and revise that same estimate as later evidence appears. For each preceding word, CogLLM adds the change in a separately trained event score since the pronoun to the word's original reader-click score. Normalizing these updated scores gives a distribution over the same earlier words. At the initial pronoun the update is exactly zero. The encoder reads the original article once and the readout never generates text. The gain is fixed at one. The Jones reanalysis combines outputs from two archived extractions of the same frozen encoder. The independent AmbiCoref worker extracts both signals together in one counted forward per original sentence. This construction preserves the calibrated scope of the conditional reader-click model at the initial point. [Measurement and controls](transition-anchored-reference-protocol.md)

In the 30 previously examined Jones–Bergen physical-event families, adding event evidence to the direct-reader prior reduced human choice log loss from 0.796 to 0.630. The paired difference was −0.166 [−0.280, −0.041], based on 1,012 decisions by 37 people with uncertainty over both people and event families. Updating only for the number of words read reached 0.778, 0.148 [0.023, 0.257] above the semantic update. The semantic update agreed with 68.6% of binary decisions. The attention-only update achieved 0.622 and the original reader-pointer reapplied directly at event end achieved 0.639. The score differences between these three contextual observers were uncertain. A sensitivity that averages scores within each candidate phrase instead of summing word probability mass weakened the improvement over the prior, −0.052 [−0.139, 0.036]. The separate self-paced sample provided 3,777 trials from 135 readers. At the designated fifth region, the anchored belief correlated 0.276 [−0.081, 0.473] with the ambiguity-by-referent timing interaction. Adding that belief to a model with region surprisal, lexical repetition, mention order and human syntax norms changed held-event mean squared error by −0.00135 [−0.00861, 0.00577]. Neither estimate resolved the direction. [Region scores](../results/reading-validation-transition-anchored-reference/jones-retrospective.json) · [Experimental materials](https://doi.org/10.1017/langcog.2024.2)

Among 68 held authored twin pairs with identical prefixes, opposite intended referents, and equal total word counts, applying the semantic change to the lexical reading-game prior favored the intended direction in 75.0% at the first changed word [64.7, 83.9%]. The alternatives tied on the preceding word under the numerical tolerance, and the elapsed-word-only observer remained at 50%. Across all 558 authored sentences, unrestricted top-word hits increased from 43.0% under the lexical click prior to 51.8% after the update. The anchored model's supplied-two-option log loss worsened from 0.878 to 0.941 despite an accuracy gain from 49.6% to 57.5%. The unanchored semantic model had the best supplied-two-option accuracy, 65.1%, and reached 21.1% when every earlier word competed. The retained authored activation archive permits reconstruction of the lexical game prior; a native earlier-word prior is available in the separate human experiment. [Authored pair results](../results/reading-validation-transition-anchored-reference/dpr-retrospective.json)

These known results motivated the anchoring procedure and therefore provide retrospective evidence. The separate AmbiCoref study locked its 1,682 original English sentences, 4,905 human responses and both source models before running external forecasts. Its two primary questions are whether the model predicts which referent a person chooses and whether it identifies sentences on which a person explicitly reports ambiguity. This cohort supplies completed-sentence judgments. Token-level change points in its model forecasts require their own human timing evidence.
