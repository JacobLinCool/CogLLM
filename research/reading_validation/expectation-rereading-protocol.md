# Do changes in readers' expectations predict looking back in a paragraph?

The Provo Corpus used one panel to guess the next word from successive prefixes and another to read the same short passages during eye tracking. This makes it possible to test a link between two independently measured behaviors at the same printed word. All cloze-model parameters and the denominator audit were established before inspecting eye outcomes. The aggregate performance of the cloze curve is known. No eye observations or joint results informed the choice of model. [Original corpus](https://osf.io/sjefs/) · [Definition of lexical disagreement](cloze-change-protocol.md)

Use the original interest-area table with the publisher's SHA-256 digest and certify printed word identity at its original text and word IDs. Use the adjacent pairs of directly observed prefixes from the cleaned cloze panel. The difference between prefixes before and after a printed word belongs to that word. The primary outcome is a first-pass return from the current word to an earlier region, obtained from the authors' regression-out indicator among first-pass fixated words. Words skipped on first pass or lacking a defined regression opportunity do not supply a negative. Treat looks back toward a word later in a passage, regression-path duration, and private conceptual difficulty as different measurements. Report inclusion, field definitions, denominator checks and item-level forecasting difficulty.

Preassign whole article IDs and whole reader IDs independently to five balanced hash folds. For each of the 25 crossed test cells, fit the behavioral model using neither the readers nor the passages in that cell. Keep word opportunities from the same family together. Compare the following predictors on the same observed outcomes.

1. A causal text-only control with log current/previous word length, lexical frequency, local punctuation, log word position, lexical surprisal for the observed current and previous words (where verified) and nonlinear terms for length, position and surprisal. Fit a penalized binomial regression on the count of defined opportunities. Standardize using the source partition. Use the same fixed regularization strength throughout.
2. Add the independently trained contextual population estimate of disagreement immediately before, and on completing, the word. Represent the latter by its signed difference from the former. Include separate positive and negative changes, allowing either to be related to looking back.
3. Substitute the independent panel's observed disagreement at the same certified prefixes. This diagnostic asks whether the elicited expectations themselves explain rereading beyond the lexical control. It cannot be used as an article-only prediction at deployment.

Report held log loss per first-pass opportunity, Brier score and the direction of fitted effects. For each model, also assess a diagnostic conditioned on the observer's actual time in the current first pass. This measurement is unavailable in article-only use and may mediate changes. Report paired uncertainty by resampling whole passages and readers simultaneously. Treat the model versus text-only and human versus text-only comparisons as one family using simultaneous 95% intervals. Correct denominators, word alignment or semantic errors in the original schema before estimation, and record any material exclusions. A positive result would link two behavioral responses on these passages. A null result would constrain interpreting the lexical curve as a general reading difficulty signal.
