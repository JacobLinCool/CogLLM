# Sharing a single text exposure across comprehension, repetition and grammar

Professional SEAHORSE raters first decided whether they could understand a summary. When they confirmed comprehension, they also judged unnecessary repetition and grammatical correctness before seeing the source article. A single causal encoding supported a distinct probability for each observed response. Unnecessary repetition transferred most precisely to held judgments. Sharing the feature slopes improved the smaller form model. Its benefit beyond separate readouts on the richer native representations was indistinguishable from zero. [Original interface, §2.2 and Figure 2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.584.pdf) · [Analysis specified before opening held repetition and grammar judgments](transition-seahorse-gated-protocol.md)

The analysis retained the original English training, validation and test articles and their overlap exclusions. Each input came from an earlier, outcome-free Qwen3.5-2B forward. The three training losses used 14,482 comprehension judgments, 13,860 repetition judgments and 13,859 grammar judgments. Validation supplied the task-specific calibrators from 2,156, 2,060 and 2,059 definitive responses, respectively. Every original Q1 No skipped the next two questions (211 instances on the full official test). One uncertain Q1 answer had recorded Yes responses for Q2 and Q3. The prespecified strict Q1=Yes condition excludes those two answers. In the other records, Unsure and source skips contribute no score to that question. A complete all-article claim about how often difficult summaries contain grammar or repetition errors would require those missing responses. Frozen English-to-Spanish transfer preserved the strongest within-article ranking for unnecessary repetition. Both native and matched form observers transferred that ranking, while their calibrated probabilities and other judgments changed across languages. [Independent Spanish responses](transition-seahorse-spanish-results.md)

We estimated sharing before the joint fit by learning task-specific slopes separately on two disjoint groups of training articles. A covariance derived from reproducible coefficient patterns then determined how strongly the fit penalized differences among questions. Off-diagonal correlations among the native slopes were only .09–.19, compared with .55–.85 for form slopes. Fitting the joint convex objectives reached maximum gradient residuals of 2.19 × 10⁻⁸ for native and 7.18 × 10⁻⁹ for form. The independent Q1 fits reproduced the original English held probabilities within 10⁻¹⁵. [Frozen parameters and concordance by source half](../results/reading-validation-transition-external/seahorse/gated-fit-seal.json)

We summed the observed binary log losses within each presentation and resampled whole test articles. Native sharing yielded .50406 per draft versus .50414 for independent native readouts. The difference, −.00008 (95% interval −.00042 to .00026), provides little evidence for improved native performance. Form sharing yielded .53106 versus .53364 for separate form readouts, an improvement of −.00258 (−.00488 to −.00059). The matched adaptive native-versus-form difference was −.02700 (−.03738 to −.01788). The largest contribution came from repetition (−.01609 per observed Q2, −.02301 to −.00970). Q1 had already been inspected on these texts before specifying the gated study, while Q2 and Q3 were first scored after freezing all projections. The adaptive three-output observer is a research comparison. The released English writer uses the prior Q1-specific operating point.
