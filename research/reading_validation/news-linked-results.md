# Can one unmarked article reveal both conflicting passages?

We asked the model to select both a current word and the earlier sentence it conflicts with, without giving it either location. Native Qwen representations and learned source evidence were shared across the two decisions. On 75 eligible flagged continuations grouped by 74 held prompts, the pointwise contextual model's top-ranked word and earlier sentence were jointly marked **32.4%** of the time (95% interval 21.6–43.2%). The lexical model reached 24.3%, random selections averaged 11.6%, and retaining a probabilistic source state from the preceding word reached 21.0%. An incorrect word or an incorrect earlier sentence receives zero credit. Scores condition on the presence of a human mark only when measuring the top two-sided location. The broadest evaluation includes **114,012** eligible reviewer-word observations from 231 continuations across 222 prompts (1,602 positive, 112,410 unmarked). It includes passages with no human mark. A source outside the available reading history has an unknown label for that reviewer. Words are evaluated after the reader has seen two completed earlier sentences. The joint ranking considered 33,060 possible word–source pairs, of which 1,198 had at least one source mark. Predictions were sealed for all 246 held continuations before grading. Differences are paired by original prompt with 10,000 resamples; positive-only and unmarked losses use different eligible prompt sets. In the primary all-word comparison, the planned persistent model incurred .00253 more nats than the contextual pointwise model [.00084, .00443]. Persistence lowered the penalty on unmarked words but placed too little probability on genuine marked sources. This is direct evidence against treating every reviewer's span as an uninterrupted latent issue with the current transition estimator. The positive pointwise result is a sampled ranking on retrospective annotations; its top-ranked pair was still wrong in most articles.
