# Can a one-pass observer retain the right person when the narrative changes focus?

Gernsbacher and colleagues used three different second paragraphs following the same introduction. The existing character was rementioned, a new character appeared, or neither character appeared. A common final sentence ended immediately before a recognition probe. The original name experiment reported average probe times of 556, 757 and 654 ms in these conditions, respectively, with 80 participants. A new name made the old one harder to retrieve than a neutral continuation. These published directions and aggregates are known. They provide an independent human check of a mechanism previously applied to story goals. Freeze the earlier Qwen retrieval readout, the trained unresolved-source readout, and the lexical inverse-distance control. Use every original item with a directly recoverable probe. Extract three earlier control words with the same count, position and length matching procedure used for the old-goal experiment. Compute at the authors' probe position and at the shared initial paragraph. Use the earlier opportunity and subword normalization, score only the unprompted once-encoded passage, and exclude the recognition question. Compare neutral against new and remention against neutral. The prespecified primary effect is the difference in target accessibility at the common downstream sentence, after subtracting matched control-word accessibility and inverse-distance effects. Report separate target and matched-control changes, primary item bootstrap, sign agreement, a source-mention-only sensitivity, unchanged-prefix checks and every item. Keep native attention and trained unresolved mass in their own units. Report name effects separately from earlier goal effects after auditing cross-study text overlap. With only condition means, correspondence to participant times is limited to a direction.

If the companion experiment's original object materials can be verified, use the independently reported contrast of the protagonist rementioned versus replaced by a new character. Its probes name an associated object rather than the protagonist, and human timing evidence was weaker across items. Use exactly the same geometry and estimators. The studies can reuse story families, so this is a change of probe and participant group rather than a second independent material population.
