# Does a small, separate sample from a reading task calibrate the absolute scale?

The original UCL-trained exact-spelling estimator gave helpful changes in paragraphs and systematically overestimated disagreement on isolated Peelle sentence stems. This follow-up uses previously examined public data to test a narrower, deployable calibration procedure. The parameters controlling how printed words differ remain fixed. A new reader does not need to supply a background or any prior answers. One optional, study-level intercept can be learned from an earlier panel completing that type of stimulus.

Sort the 233 eligible anonymous Peelle participants and the 3,085 stem IDs separately by SHA-256 of a fixed domain tag and the corresponding ID. Before using their answers select the first 32 people and first 64 stems for calibration. Use only intersections of those two sets to fit one log-odds offset to UCL's exact-spelling predictions. A fixed offset preserves the ordering imposed by the source model. Use every other person on every other stem for final evaluation. Do not lend responses from mixed-role cells, choose the offset by held error or infer its value from the target article. Require at least five distinct responses per included calibration stem and two per test stem. Treat every eligible stem equally. Fit the offset by matching the mean predicted and observed probability that two different people supply different complete spellings. Retain the constant learned from precisely the same calibration observations, the source-only constant and the frozen UCL paragraph estimator as comparison forecasts. The calibrated constant controls for adjusting the overall response level of the laboratory alone.

The primary outcome is mean absolute error of the exact-spelling disagreement probability at the withheld stems. Report calibrated-minus-control gains, calibration mean error, rank correlation across items, cohort sizes, and held mean prediction versus observation. Bootstrap complete calibration people, calibration stems, test people and test stems in their respective sets. Keep one reweighting per person across all their stems. For empirical pair agreement, exclude self-pairs including multiple copies of the same reader in a bootstrap draw. Refit the constant and offset in each draw and use simultaneous intervals for the two primary comparisons against the frozen model and the same-data constant. Compare the source-only and target-calibrated conditions transparently. This assesses adaptation with disjoint calibration observations inside one previously inspected study. It cannot establish zero-shot transfer to an unseen laboratory.
