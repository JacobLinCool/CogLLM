# A loss that matches a randomized human effect can damage the rest of the reading curve

At a later sentence with identical wording, 233 readers in Experiment 3 increased their first inconsistency reports by 31.7 percentage points [20.4, 42.7] when an earlier premise conflicted. On the same 12 story families, training an article-only predictor with first-report likelihood alone gave an average increase of 6.0 points [−1.2, 14.9]. Adding the original study's randomized, within-family difference to the training objective raised this to 19.0 [3.9, 37.1]. The estimated reduction in absolute effect error was 13.0 points [0.3, 24.0] against the otherwise identical likelihood-only fit. All held predictions used a single original article. The comparison of alternative versions was available in source training and evaluation. The typical response elsewhere in the article suffered. Full-path loss rose from 1.787 to 3.487 nats per reader trial [difference 1.700, 95% interval 0.821, 2.997]. The earlier directional risk-set readout scored 1.502 with a predicted critical effect of 7.7 points. Its diagnostic supplied with the true critical location and condition scored 1.303 with an effect of 32.6 points. The new moment model assigned neutral-condition early reports 12.2 points more frequently than observed. Its single largest predicted family effect was 90 points; several other families' estimates remained near zero. The held effect failed the registered ±10-point equivalence criterion. The repeated material, previously examined third-cohort aggregate, and twelve independent families preclude an independent replication claim. We retain the risk-set readout as the better full-trajectory forecast and make the tradeoff available in original units.
