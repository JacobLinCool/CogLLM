# Lexical unfamiliarity transfers more readily than a change in phrase structure

A reader can find a sentence easier and still make more mistakes when using it. In 90 families of English health-information sentences, splitting a compound noun phrase reduced mean perceived difficulty by 0.052 points on a five-point scale and increased errors on a four-noun cloze task by 2.72 percentage points. A Qwen3.5-2B readout given only the completed sentence predicted changes of −0.002 points and +0.47 percentage points, respectively. Both changes had the observed sign across all 90 families. At the level of individual families, split-direction agreement was 56.1% [47.8, 64.4] for ratings and 48.8% [39.2, 58.1] for cloze. A second intervention replacing familiar nouns with pseudowords produced larger effects, both of which the model reproduced more closely. Differences subtract the unmodified condition from the modified one at fixed levels of the other intervention. A positive cloze change means more errors. Concordance counts correctly signed changes among unequal human outcomes, giving half credit for a model tie. Each family gets equal weight. Intervals use 10,000 family bootstrap draws within the three phrase lengths, holding each fitted fold predictor fixed. For example, subjects in the same design cell receive nearly identical condition-only forecasts, so the interval for their predicted mean change can be much narrower than the human interval. The 334 workers and 11,949 retained assessments are the paper's study totals. The released observations are aggregate means for 360 sentences. The four experimental conditions were presented in different sessions and participants could return. The estimates therefore describe item-family variation. ## What the text contributes

Each fold held out all four variants of 18 families. The two measured responses shared the twelve surface features, source-trained expected educational grade, two contextual lexical measurements and 24 frozen sentence components. Separate coefficients for each response were solved in one multivariate ridge system using both levels and within-family changes. This joint numerical solution is algebraically equivalent to fitting the two response columns separately under this particular separable loss. The surface comparator used the same folds and loss with twelve features. No cloze prompt, response options, noun inventory, intervention flags or other version entered the text-only estimators. The diagnostic condition arms were explicitly given the original design cells. [Estimator specification](noun-estimator.md) · [Program and available native comparisons](noun_fit.py)

The registered perceived-difficulty MSE improvement over surface alone was −0.0016 [−0.0740, +0.0640]. For cloze it was −0.00105 [−0.00379, +0.00179]. The training mean had the smallest full-data cloze MSE. An intervention-label control predicted split changes of −0.050 difficulty points and +2.60 percentage points in cloze errors. Its success on the average shows what the training design makes predictable when the condition is supplied. The unchanged educational-grade head predicted that splitting would increase grade by 0.051, while the mean lexical head predicted a small decrease of 0.004 lexical-score units. These are source-specific scales. ## What survived the source audit

Two independent limitations affect the structural claim. First, the source's target phrase and other words passed a strict literal comparison in both pseudoword conditions for 71 of 90 families. In these verified families the model estimated a split effect of −0.004 difficulty points [−0.025, +0.017] versus the observed −0.043 [−0.093, +0.005]. Its predicted cloze increase was +0.48 percentage points [+0.02, +0.94], versus +2.57 [+0.47, +5.11] for people. [Literal criteria](noun-material-sensitivity.md) · [Estimates for all 71 families](../results/reading-validation-noun/scores.json)

Second, the public workbook gives identical paired difficulty and cloze means for every split and unsplit sentence in the 30 four-word pseudoword families. There are no informative split rankings in this subgroup. Treating the duplicated outcomes as evidence of equality would require independent trial records. We fitted the same prespecified models from scratch on the 60 two- and three-word families. On those data, the human split contrast was −0.030 difficulty points [−0.089, +0.027], while the Qwen readout predicted +0.021 [−0.003, +0.047]. The observed cloze change was +2.98 percentage points [+0.53, +5.94], versus +0.61 [+0.10, +1.18] from Qwen. The pseudoword contrast remained closer, with predicted increases of +0.300 difficulty points and +3.43 cloze-error points versus observed +0.293 and +3.89. The Qwen addition reduced rating MSE by 0.018 [−0.074, +0.117] in this restricted refit. The result depends on a different target population and does not establish an advantage in the original comparison. [Amendment recorded before inspecting complete Qwen results](noun-duplicate-amendment.md) · [Restricted refit and intervals](../results/reading-validation-noun/varying-blocks/scores.json)

The workbook and published trial-weighted cloze rates also differ more than integer reweighting alone can explain under their stated counts. Both describe higher average error after splitting. We treat all predicted values here as comparisons to the released sentence means. There is no observation of the reader's exact point of difficulty and no basis here for an editor to promise improved comprehension if a particular noun phrase is split. ## Reproducibility

The model processed all 360 completed sentences in 23 batches on an NVIDIA L4, with one pass per item and six separate numerical validation passes. It needed 8.33 seconds for the prediction batches and covered 10,782 of 10,782 word endings over 14,827 native tokens. The held-family ridge normal-equation residual was below 10⁻¹⁶. The trained extended text readout uses 39 input coordinates and two continuous response outputs. The calibration parameters are specific to these released sentence means. The saved weights omit the raw English workbook. The data repository specifies CC BY-NC 4.0.
