# A document-level term for the benefit of a visible section-heading template

The external full-state experiment recovered +0.042 against the known +0.359 mean reader benefit. This motivated one within-study adaptation to the grain of the measured outcome. No new response source is claimed. The sample is the same 12 English psychology articles, 166 participants and 1,283 ratings.

A binary input h recognizes the visible subheading template in the released English materials using article text alone. Require four successive standalone lines, allowing a numeric list prefix, matching “Background”, “Why was this study done?”, “What did the researchers do and find?” and “What do these findings mean?”. The model processes the same archived original text in one forward pass. The frozen predictor s for each document is the mean of the deployed head’s matched sentence ratings transformed by 1 + (s−1)7/4. The strong text-only comparison sets s=0, estimates the same document intercept and learns the same heading effect. Both models allow positive or negative effects. This defined template supplies no empirical estimate for arbitrary headings.

Fix five balanced participant blocks by ordering participant IDs by SHA-256 of a study-specific salt and assigning ranks modulo five. For a test presentation involving article f and a participant in block k, omit the entire DOI and all participants in k from fitting. In the remaining articles obtain each arm’s mean residual, y−s, from the remaining participants. Estimate the global heading coefficient as the sum of within-article residual contrasts divided by the number of training articles plus one. This fixed shrinkage is equivalent to one hypothetical article with zero effect. Average the residual arm midpoints equally across training articles and subtract half the fitted heading coefficient to obtain the intercept. Predict both reading conditions for each eligible held reader under their own out-of-fold coefficients. The strong intercept-plus-template comparator follows precisely the same recipe.

Primary comparisons are the average heading benefit, signed difference from the human contrast and mean absolute source-article contrast error. Also report the difference in mean absolute error between adapted native and heading-only, family residuals and held individual-rating RMSE. Use the same 12 articles with equal weight. Within each article average model counterfactual contrasts over its held readers, weighting each reader once regardless of their assigned arm. Observed article contrasts use the original arm-specific means. A secondary measure subtracts the grand mean of both predicted and observed article contrasts before computing absolute deviations, separating article-specific variation from learning a global offset.

Refit every coefficient and holdout forecast in 10,000 crossed-with-replacement reader × DOI bootstrap draws using fixed seed 784562. Repetitions of a held DOI and all members of the held participant block remain excluded from that fold's fitting. Report both the predicted mean and the errors against observed study responses; matching the mean on the same study cannot establish transfer to new kinds of organization or cognitive load at a particular token.
