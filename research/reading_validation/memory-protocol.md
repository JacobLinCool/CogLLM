# Can attention adaptation recover the human response to a changed premise?

The primary test holds the target sentence constant. Changing one earlier detail can make that same sentence contradict the story and prompt a reader to press a key. Previous models sometimes ranked versions correctly but underestimated the difference in the proportion of readers reporting a problem. We train a contextual comparison on separate human premise revisions, then estimate the mapping from that comparison to a reading response. All stimulus files and splits will be hashed before the new reading outcomes are scored. The test protocol is local and time-ordered, without third-party preregistration.

## Separate the premise from the hypothesis in training

Use the original and revised-premise releases of Kaushik et al. within their original train, development and test partitions. Retain complete triplets in which human writers changed the premise, held the hypothesis fixed, and assigned exactly one contradiction, one neutral relation and one entailment. Remove connected training families sharing a normalized focal hypothesis or premise with development or test. Each triplet is displayed with the same amount of intervening text in all conditions. A deterministic hash assigns 0, 2, 4 or 8 unrelated sentences between premise and hypothesis, chosen from a disjoint pool of training premises, with lexical-overlap exclusions. Added text is a weak construction with an intended distractor role. Report the exact coverage and score the original adjacent texts separately. No human reading times, inconsistency keys or condition identifiers enter the language model. [Premise revisions](https://github.com/acmi-lab/counterfactually-augmented-data/tree/6f232a1d2a11462a30ce08fb4825b734ab30828e/NLI) · [Original report](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12434)

Use one Qwen3.5-2B causal forward on unaltered or expanded narrative text, without a question or generated answer. The shared binary logit is a linear projection of the normalized token state. For NLI training, the loss is the mean Bernoulli cross-entropy plus half the mean pairwise logistic loss requiring the contradiction to exceed the other two versions. A ridge penalty of 0.01 regularizes the readout. Fit the frozen head first by L-BFGS to a maximum gradient below 2e−4. Starting from this solution, adapt only rank-eight updates to the query and value projections of the final global-attention layer. Use AdamW with gradient clipping, a fixed predeclared learning rate, and held-out development loss for checkpoint selection. Train for up to 12 epochs subject to elapsed accelerator budget, with patience three for improvements smaller than 0.002. Log train and development loss, validation paired margins, model state, interruption state and epoch. Report whether development performance stabilized, the selected epoch and actual parameter counts. A wall-time or resource interruption is distinct from convergence. Maintain the same original initialization, training examples, readout loss, masks and test inputs for the frozen and adapted comparison. Processing one article yields a score at each token in one forward.

## A new participant cohort with a previously unseen neutral condition

Use only nonillustrated prose from experiments 1, 2 and 3 of Frick and Schüler. The actual stories are in English; the data columns bear German names. The second and third cohorts include 12 story families from the earlier experiment. Their neutral versions replace a critical earlier term with a broader one. Each nonillustrated story is read sentence by sentence; a participant actively judges the current sentence's consistency. Train all reading-task calibration on Experiment 1, withholding every version of the matching story family when forecasting either later cohort. Thus materials share story families across the original experiments, but no reading calibration uses the evaluated family's judgments, and no parameters use the new cohorts' responses. Derive the cross-experiment family identity from the exact target sentence and checked surrounding original text. The 12 shared families limit material-level precision. The new participants afford a cohort replication. [Original paper, experiments 1–3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7) · [Authors' data archive](https://osf.io/pg67h/)

Calibrate the probability of a reader's first contradiction report at each completed sentence, given they have not reported earlier. All readings receive the same sentence-end features independent of condition: elapsed word count, sentence and word position, number of words in the current sentence, overlap with the earlier passage, and the original deployed token signals. Add the fixed premise-comparison logit, its difference from the previous sentence and its excess over prior logit peaks. Make three matched predictions: the controls alone, controls plus frozen premise comparison, and controls plus adapted comparison. Fit logistic hazards using only the other Experiment 1 families. Weight the whole-path risk-set likelihood and target hazard calibration equally so an infrequent but large response has appropriate influence. Use a fixed ridge of 0.02 on training-standardized slopes and no unpenalized per-family intercept. Hypotheses are directional. Preserve signed condition differences and declare a magnitude match only if the entire interval for model minus observed effect lies within ±10 percentage points. Report performance against the original token signal, human early reports, and whole-path log likelihood alongside magnitude to reveal indiscriminate alarm inflation. Retain both cohorts and all comparators.

The primary comparison is the paired reduction in absolute error of the inconsistent-minus-neutral first-report difference at the identical target, adapted versus frozen, on Experiment 2. Estimate the same quantity independently on Experiment 3 with its additional example in the instructions. Use crossed participant–family bootstrap draws, grouping participants within text condition. Report 95% intervals and sample sizes. Use the decisions before the first report to score timing across the full story. A response identifies a completed sentence where a reader reported inconsistency. The study supplies no within-sentence report times or later key indicating that the same issue was resolved. No new participants or material are solicited.
