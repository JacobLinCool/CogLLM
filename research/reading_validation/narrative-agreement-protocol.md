# How reproducible are the locations where open plot questions rise or fall?

The original German narrative study supplies independent annotation workbooks for the same lines in three complete stories. A fourth story has one workbook. Previously examined models underpredict a large closure in one of these stories. We ask whether a second team also identifies large changes at the same lines and how much of their magnitude the trained causal readout recovers. These works informed earlier analysis. This protocol defines a measurement diagnostic on known material. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1017/langcog.2025.10057) · [Frozen article forecasts](stock-process-results.md)

Read the original definite and possibly open labels for every numbered question. Certify both teams received identical lines, the released workbook hashes match, and the fitted forecast uses precisely those lines. At each line form a lower count of definite questions and an upper count including provisional questions. The conservative interval for a change is the current count interval minus the preceding interval. Exclude the structurally empty start of each story. Flag a definite increase or decrease only when the appropriate endpoint is strictly above or below zero.

For every story and target team report the number of determinate human changes, the fraction for which the other team is determinate, direction agreement among those, and the success fraction when the other team abstains on indeterminate changes. Report the causal count forecast and elapsed-duration control on exactly the same human-selected changes. Also compare the predictions on every strict human decline. For the largest decline of each team select the first tied maximum measured by the guaranteed decrease, then compare target bounds, the independent team's bounds and both article-only forecasts at this same line. The size is in questions per printed line. Ties and all denominators are retained. Compare curve levels by interval squared error, applying one positive affine scale per team learned from the *other* two works for the peer diagnostic. Report the raw peer comparator alongside it. Give equal weight to each story and each annotated team. These teams need not have elicited the same questions; this diagnostic cannot establish question identity. The three shared works do not support population confidence intervals.
