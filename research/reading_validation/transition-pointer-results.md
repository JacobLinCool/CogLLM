# Which earlier words did people actually choose?

On a new English reading-game evaluation, CogLLM’s highest-ranked earlier word fell inside the phrase a reader actually clicked 78.4% of the time. A control trained on word distance, capitalization, repetition, POS and pronoun agreement reached 58.7%. The paired difference was **+19.7 percentage points** [9.1, 30.9]. When the source-calibrated model offered a link only at confident positions, precision rose to **91.6%** [86.1, 95.6] with 46.9% article-weighted coverage. These are expectations for pronouns where at least four eligible players chose a previous entity. The training signal was the literal prior region a reader selected. A choice of a multiword phrase rewards the sum of probabilities on its admissible words. No earlier candidate list, identity chain, future words or comprehension question is an input at inference. Qwen3.5-2B produced the observed article states and native attention rows in one causal forward per original text. The source contained 8,283 choices from 433 people at 1,306 pronouns across 230 training texts. Twelve development texts supplied 602 choices at 93 pronouns for separate calibration. The model scored all 757 eligible-form pronouns in 44 complete gold texts, before matching outcomes from 2,196 distinct reader–pronoun responses by 120 people at 366 pronouns in 37 texts. The other immediately preceding words remained eligible candidates even if nobody marked them. [Source audit](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/source-seal.json) · [Blinded numerical receipt](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/receipt.json)

The full observer used seven position cues, 39 additional lexical/POS cues, four pooled semantic/attention measures and 48 individual attention intensities, across six native full-attention layers. A single L4 processed 286 full texts, formed 880,770 query–candidate scores, and fitted all four source arms in 120 seconds. Every final source gradient was below 5.1 × 10⁻⁷. Aggregating the reconstructed heads matched the original attention summaries to 1.8 × 10⁻⁷, and changing a later suffix changed common-prefix features by at most 1.7 × 10⁻⁶. Individual heads improved clicked-region log loss over pooling by .132 [.059, .215]; the top-one difference was +3.2 points [−2.1, 8.6]. ## What remains difficult?

The average original clicked phrase covered 2.53 prior words. Its end was 11.2 words before the pronoun on average. For the 817 choices at least 10 words back, the native model reached 49.5% top-one containment against 23.8% for lexical/POS, a difference of +25.8 points [13.7, 39.4]. For the 1,265 single-word choices, the two methods reached 63.8% and 63.7%, a difference of +.2 points [−12.0, 11.9]. Part of the overall location gain therefore depends on a target phrase having multiple acceptable words. Among the 38 disputed-entity pronouns, the native observer assigned higher probability to the actual clicks than the lexical control, while its top-one gain was uncertain. [Subgroup estimates](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/scores.json)

The difference from lexical/POS remained +19.7 points [8.5, 31.6] after placing three Grimm excerpts in one Gutenberg work and resampling 35 distinct releases. Twenty people had made no original interpretation anywhere in source training. They supplied 32 gold choices across 23 pronouns and 13 texts; corresponding top-one rates were 72.3% native and 29.5% lexical/POS. This small subset cannot support precise generalization to previously unseen readers. A word sampled uniformly from a *different* eligible reader’s clicked phrase matched the target reader’s phrase 86.6% [83.0, 90.0]. Two different readers chose the same exact phrase 84.2% of the time and the same expert-adjudicated entity 93.2% of the time under article-equal aggregation. ## Offering a link to a writer

The rule was fixed using source development alone, before the external labels were loaded. The threshold of .80 gave 48 suggestions across nine source development texts, with estimated precision 90.9% and a simultaneous one-sided lower bound of 71.6%. On the held-out game texts it proposed 191 links among 366 evaluated pronouns. Giving each text equal weight, it offered a suggestion at 46.9% of positions; 91.6% of those suggestions fell within a reader's clicked phrase. It produced 3.96 incorrect suggestions per 100 eligible pronouns. The lexical/POS model did not meet the source calibration criterion. These figures apply where the game recorded a preceding-entity choice. Antecedents outside the text and discourse-new or impersonal readings remain important limitations for use on unrestricted prose. [Selection rule](transition-pointer-selective-protocol.md) · [Source decision](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/selective/source-selection.json) · [Held-out policy](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/selective/game.json)

**When a reader can decline to link the pronoun, the same source policy has 54.6% precision [48.3, 60.7].** The broader audit included 4,880 actual choices by 164 people at 631 pronouns in 41 texts. Among the responses, 3,137 selected a preceding entity, 1,517 labeled the expression discourse-new, 146 nonreferring and 80 predicative. The latter three actions count as misses for a suggested link. Across 313 suggested positions, article-weighted coverage was 42.4% and wrong suggestions numbered 19.3 per 100 eligible pronouns [14.9, 23.8]. Of all 757 visible forms, the head suggested 363 links, including 50 without a sufficiently mapped human outcome. Even if *every* unauditable link were correct, equal-pronoun precision over this complete set would be at most 64.3%; the lower bound is 50.5%. The 91.6% estimate answers the narrower question posed after readers have already chosen an antecedent. ## Do competing pointer locations measure readers’ disagreement?

The same pointer distribution predicted earlier-word locations much more reliably than it predicted which pronouns would lead two readers to disagree. After source-only calibration, native word-location dispersion ranked the more disputed literal span correctly in .509 [.379, .640] of informative within-text comparisons from 23 distinct works. Dispersion over prior word types ranked entity disagreement at .396 [.200, .599] in 11 informative works. Neither expected reader-pair Brier contrast against lexical/POS was conclusive. A location forecast and a forecast of ambiguity have different observable targets. [Protocol](transition-pointer-disagreement-protocol.md) · [Source fits](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/pointer-disagreement-calibration.json) · [Held-out disagreement](../results/reading-validation-transition-pointer/pointer-disagreement.json)

Recursively following earlier pronouns reduced literal top-one containment from 78.4% to 59.8%; 23.0% of the human clicked regions had no terminal word after this transformation. The same transformation can be evaluated when a separate study asks for a person’s name, because that observation differs from being asked to point to the latest mention. Neither transformation identifies the moment a reader felt difficulty.
