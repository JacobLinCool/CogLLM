# Full native states recover missing narrative context on three language models

Readers of SNaC summaries mark words that introduce an unexplained character or assume an unexplained event or object. A linear observer of the middle and final states of a frozen causal language model localized both response types in held-out movies. All three backbones improved over the strong named-entity, POS, lexical and native-surprisal control on words with unique human annotation locations. The improvement transferred less reliably to unclear pronouns. [Human study](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.29/) · [Study protocol](transition-direct-protocol.md)

The source contained two summaries per book across 44 training titles and 11 development titles. The movie split contained 39 independent movie summaries. Each backbone processed each of the same 193 original SNaC and reading-game texts once. The source fits averaged observations within titles. Qwen3.5-2B and LFM3-1.7B-Base each supplied 2,048 coordinates per layer; Gemma 4 E2B supplied 1,536. The legacy projection used 48 coordinates per layer learned in earlier experiments. The full-state and projected observers had the same source-only normalization, fixed ridge coefficient and endpoint-specific masks. Maximum absolute gradient was below 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ in every source fit. Complete L4 workers took 170, 140 and 193 seconds for Qwen, LFM and Gemma, respectively. These are measurements of frozen-feature extraction and readout fitting on one accelerator per sequential run. ## All 26,417 movie words

Across all placements consistent with the original character annotations, the exact AP differences between full states and NER were +.0316 to +.2029 for Qwen, +.0132 to +.1973 for LFM, and −.0264 to +.1416 for Gemma. Full native states improved on Gemma’s 48-coordinate projection under **every** admissible placement, with an AP difference between +.0458 and +.1185. Gemma’s improvement over NER on uniquely identifiable spans therefore coexists with an unresolved ordering against NER over the entire text. The ranges describe identification within these movies. They do not incorporate sampling a different population of films. On Qwen the full-state observer versus the separate 128-feature, annotation-marginalized writer ranged from −.0297 to +.0585. The production release uses that separately verified writer. ## Do annotations of unclear pronouns predict disagreements between readers?

Phrase Detectives supplied 373 eligible pronouns in 37 texts from 35 distinct releases, with 2,242 original person–pronoun assignments and at least four readers choosing a preceding referent per pronoun. On 38 pronouns, readers chose different expert-adjudicated entities. Thirteen texts from 11 releases provided 1,554 pairs of pronouns with unequal disagreement rates. A correct prediction ranked the more frequently disputed pronoun higher within its article. Source-trained full-state scores achieved concordance .581 [.439, .719] on Qwen, .414 [.233, .595] on LFM and .461 [.302, .621] on Gemma. Within exact pronoun form, only nine articles supplied 262 qualifying pairs. Every interval included chance. The independent response has repeatable variation, but the book-trained word observers did not show reliable transfer to it. The source books and held-out films for the first two response types had previously been examined with other observers. Full-state comparisons on those films are a matched architectural replication. Giving their book one vote and resampling 11 distinct releases among the informative texts yielded .590 [.418, .750] on Qwen, .381 [.178, .593] on LFM and .444 [.262, .636] on Gemma. This sensitivity supports the same conclusion about uncertainty in the book-to-game transfer.
