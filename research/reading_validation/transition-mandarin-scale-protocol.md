# How much human calibration shifts an external Mandarin norm

Zhang and Xia elicited different overall preferences. The zero-shot source-calibrated observer ranks Xia’s SVO verbs moderately well but assigned less than .5 probability to the first person on every verb. A source neutral reference of .5 yields lower unconditioned log loss. This diagnostic was designed after observing those external outcomes. The original zero-shot model and report remain fixed.

First report the neutral and empirical cross-study prior in the same units as the original scores. As a scale sensitivity, center each model's log odds by its mean prediction on its **training** verbs and assign an intercept of zero. This specifies a neutral reader choice when the predicate has no source-relative influence and uses no Xia response in its calculation. Source-mean centering is a newly proposed decision following inspection of the Xia experiment.

Next, for each held SVO verb, fit only the intercept of the frozen logistic word-norm predictor on the other 48 verbs' SCOV outcome proportions. Exchange SVO and SCOV when predicting SCOV. Use all original verbs once as test words. This excludes the actual held predicate as well as the held construction. Use the mean of the same 48 calibration proportions as comparator, and calibrate the orthography control by exactly the same procedure. Refit the leave-one-verb intercept in each whole-verb Bayesian bootstrap to propagate calibration uncertainty. Cohort membership may overlap across conditions; only aggregate per-verb outcomes are available. No fitting of slopes or source representations on Xia.
