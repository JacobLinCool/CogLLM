# Can a shared difficulty readout reduce the number of new human ratings required?

The three-study difficulty direction generalized to ReadMe++, but the pooled ordinal model lost to a target-only fit at full sample size. Its predictions combine a ranking of sentences with numeric thresholds specific to the original raters. We test whether other studies can supply a useful ranking while a small number of new human responses set its scale and correct its errors. We selected this mechanism after inspecting ReadMe++. The two public CEFR-SP partitions have not been used to score or choose this mechanism. The trained source direction uses PLABA, CLEAR, and German student annotations. ReadMe++ labels are excluded.

Use both public portions of [CEFR-SP](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.416/), SCoRE and Wiki-Auto, [revision 2ba4a50](https://github.com/yukiar/CEFR-SP/tree/2ba4a5005fbff68ab0f863891cf977c50fdcda29/CEFR-SP). Two educators supplied separate 1–6 proficiency judgments on each sentence. Use both votes. Fit an independent target head per corpus and retain both official test sets. Keep a text packet free of scores. Extract the terminal Qwen vector from one presentation of each sentence. All forecast models use the frozen source-study projection and feature normalization.

Group exact normalized strings and near duplicates before analysis. Two sentences sharing at least two five-word shingles and at least 80% of the shorter set form one connected group. Exclude CEFR-SP groups overlapping the three source studies under this rule. Exclude CEFR-SP training and development groups represented in either official test set. Keep one copy of an exact string per partition and corpus, and retain both published votes for that observation. Report the coverage and any remaining limitations in identifying the original articles. Split sizes of 64, 256, and the full eligible training partition are nested, selected by a predetermined hash of the material with no access to ratings. The primary low-data endpoint uses 64 sentences per target corpus.

Let x contain standardized lexical features and frozen language model features. Predict the probability of a rating above level k with a cumulative logistic model with intercept b_k. A target-only model estimates a coefficient vector v from the new judgments. The transfer model uses v + a·s, where s is the already learned three-study direction scaled to unit variability over target training sentences, and a is a nonnegative coefficient learned from those same sentences. Penalize v with (0.01 + 4/n) times half the squared norm and use a fixed weak penalty of 0.005 times half the squared value on a. Fit all parameters simultaneously with convex optimization of mean cumulative logistic loss. Stabilize rare threshold intercepts with one symmetric pseudo-observation per threshold, in both models. Compare with matched target-only estimation, target-only estimation with the original fixed ridge of 0.01, source direction plus intercepts and a free scale alone, and a surface-only transfer calculation. The base readout remains frozen so that adapting to one target leaves the earlier endpoints unchanged.

The primary statistic is the equal-corpus mean change in ranked probability score per educator on the official test items at 64 training sentences, versus the matched target-only Qwen model. Lower is better. Report separate corpora, both target-only comparisons, the two larger training sizes, absolute error, and the source-direction coefficient. Use stratified 10,000-sample bootstraps of connected item groups with seed 71306, retaining both educators' votes together. Two annotators cannot estimate uncertainty over the population of educators. Our nominal budget counts sentences, each with two judgments. This is a test of statistical efficiency for estimates of perceived sentence difficulty.
