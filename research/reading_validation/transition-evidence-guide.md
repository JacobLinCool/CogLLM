# Which reading responses can this observer reproduce?

A score at a word or sentence becomes interpretable when it predicts a recorded human action at that same position. This project has different amounts of evidence for detecting an unexplained name, recovering the person referred to by a pronoun, identifying an awkward transition and measuring effort. The comparisons below each preserve the original human task and its unit of observation.

| A writer's question | Measured human response | What held-out data support |
|---|---|---|
| Can someone understand this short English summary? | Professional English-language raters answered whether they could read and understand a news or how-to summary, before seeing its source. | In 4,349 scored held judgments, the summary-only observer reached AUROC .842 and ranked the difficult draft higher in 85.3% of same-article comparisons. Its source-selected threshold caught 92 of 206 difficult drafts and flagged 184 of 4,143 understandable ones. These responses locate difficulty at the whole-summary level. [Summary comprehension](transition-seahorse-results.md) |
| Where is a character introduced without enough context? | Readers highlighted words in narrative summaries. | Across 39 movie summaries, 413 of 22,133 uniquely interpretable words were marked. The shipped balanced Qwen observer achieved .782 precision, .816 recall and .799 F1, versus .687 F1 for named entities, text cues and surprisal, and .322 for a book-trained T5 specialist. A separately reserved 40th film supported the direction of the comparison with named entities on the portion within context range. [Word-level operating points](transition-writer-balanced-results.md) · [T5 protocol and results](transition-t5-results.md) |
| Is this finding specific to Qwen? | The same human span records on the same book/movie split, with separate source heads. | Direct full-state readouts on Qwen, LFM and Gemma achieved average precision of .846, .845 and .737 for unexplained characters. For omitted event or object context, the corresponding values were .252, .256 and .231. All six exceeded their matched entity/POS/surprisal controls. Unclear-pronoun results were much weaker. [Three-backbone replication](transition-direct-results.md) |
| Where did a reader lose the connection between sentences? | Individual people reading English stories incrementally marked a newly visible sentence. | On 770 judgments of 293 sentence stops from 49 stories, the source threshold found 77 of 123 marks and falsely flagged 48 of 647 unmarked observations. It transferred poorly to student essays. Its risk becomes available after the full sentence. The beginning of a reading difficulty within that sentence is unmeasured. [Story and essay comparison](transition-external-results.md) |
| Can a score trained on judgments also identify an expert's revision? | Experts marked original essay sentences, tagged reasons and wrote replacements under the same context. | Across eight held-topic rotations and 203 actual changed passages, a model trained on both judgments and comparisons preferred 66.5% of the expert changes, versus 60.1% using original judgments only. At the 1,352 original sentence boundaries its log loss was .579. The probabilities of expert decisions and the probabilities of personal difficulty have different meanings. [Matched joint-training study](transition-essay-rewrite-results.md) |
| If I select a pronoun, which earlier words could it refer to? | Game players clicked a particular earlier phrase or chose a different discourse action. Annotators in a separate Wikipedia study linked pronouns to names. | With all earlier words available to the source-trained conditional pointer, 64.4% of suitable independent Wikipedia examples landed inside the gold name, versus 29.3% for a lexical/POS pointer. Recovering an entire selected phrase improved from 27.2% to 63.7% on held game data. In new game documents, considering every possible player action reduced unconditional acceptance of the suggested word to .346. The writer control therefore displays a conditional suggestion on request. [Wikipedia transfer](transition-pointer-gap-results.md) · [Exact phrase recovery](transition-pointer-phrase-results.md) · [Unconditional acceptance](transition-word-region-utility-results.md) |
| Does a new predicate resolve the same ambiguity that a human resolves? | Readers chose referents, produced continuations or explicitly marked a sentence ambiguous. | In one controlled English experiment later event words improved prediction of 1,012 referent choices. A separate study with 4,905 actual ratings failed to confirm the proposed ambiguity update. In Mandarin, independent verb knowledge captured part of the relative shift between candidate referents. Absolute probability and effect magnitude varied by task. [Controlled English choice and timing](transition-jones-results.md) · [Independent ambiguity experiment](transition-ambicoref-results.md) · [Mandarin transfer](transition-mandarin-lexical-prior-results.md) |

The calibrated narrative editor marks, expert-sentence probabilities and selected-pronoun suggestions all read existing states from a single causal pass. A training likelihood can use token spans, binary classifications, competing referents or paired revisions while retaining the source measurement's scale. A universal token-by-token measure of experienced cognitive effort would additionally require independent evidence for the same reader, event and time point at which difficulty began and resolved. These studies provide local evidence for particular observable reading behaviors. [Single-pass native replay](../results/reading-validation-transition-partial/serving/balanced-l4-replay.json) · [Whole-topic essay readout](transition-essay-reasons-results.md)

A separate blind evaluation identified a practical limit for editing. The model selected the expert-preferred wording in just 84 of 256 directional choices between two actual rewrites, despite preferring 66.5% of supplied corrections over their original problem sentence. In an independent 32-story memory experiment, a new story goal reduced frozen Qwen's access to the old goal word beyond recency and changes to other equally old words. Human readers took 74 ms longer to recognize that old word. The latter result concerns continued accessibility across a paragraph.
