# Agreement among readers who marked a character reference

The frozen Qwen head was trained on whether any reader marked a character word. This secondary analysis asks whether its score carries additional information once a word is known to have been marked. The corpus paper describes aggregating three annotators. The released records include some span counts of four and give neither the total number of readers assigned to each segment nor stable reader identifiers. Only compare vote counts directly inside the same released segment for the primary association: those locations share the annotation item and reader pool. Relative vote counts cannot be interpreted as individually calibrated probabilities without denominators. [Original annotation procedure](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.29.pdf) · [Public release](https://github.com/tagoyal/snac)

Use the original 39 held-out movies and unambiguous word eligibility. At a word covered by overlapping span records, retain the largest recorded vote count as in the primary SNaC outcome. For each segment with at least one three-or-more-vote word and one one-vote word, compare their frozen scores. The statistic is the probability of ranking the more agreed-upon location above the less agreed-upon location with half credit for ties. Average first over within-segment pairs, then over qualifying segments within a movie, then across these movies with equal weights. Use 10,000 paired movie-cluster bootstrap draws. Report the result alongside the pretrained NER/POS and native-surprisal predictor. Repeat on recognized first PERSON mentions if there are at least two independently qualifying movies; otherwise report that restriction's actual support. Record the numbers of qualifying movies, segments, and word pairs and the distribution of source vote counts. As a sensitivity check, use all ordered unequal positive vote counts within a segment. Provide a descriptive ranking across all marked movie words with no within-segment matching as a separate statistic, explicitly sensitive to unknown opportunity to vote. This is a retrospective restricted-range test of reader agreement after segment completion. It does not measure private effort, a discrete mental event, or the magnitude of cognitive load.
