# Linking mathematical explanations to earlier symbols in scientific prose

We tested a frozen causal Qwen3.5-2B reader on original scientific paragraphs containing multiple symbols before their explanations. Annotations linked particular source spans to particular later primary descriptions. The model was trained on 56 Symlink papers, calibrated on 15, and tested on another 24. The independent SymDef corpus contained 21 other papers. A total of 4,084 unique presentations contributed 781,298 native tokens. Replaying 480 truncated prefixes preserved all 12,638 eligible notation proposals and their associated source identities. The live GPU extractor additionally checked state and attention invariance before suffixes. **Can a specific explanation be attributed to the correct already-visible symbol?** Among 109 held-out pairs with two distinct symbols introduced before either explanation, the contextual readout assigned the higher score to the correct source on 78.4% of cue–source comparisons (paper-bootstrap 95% interval 73.8–85.4%). The corresponding trained surface model scored 67.4%. The paired difference was 11.0 percentage points (3.9–15.5). For this endpoint the evaluation supplied the two correct source spans and measured both models at the same annotated evidence. Only nine held papers contained eligible pairs. On the six additional official development pairs, the contextual readout scored 50.0% against 66.7% for the surface model. [Held papers](../results/reading-validation-symbols/held-scores.json) · [Official development papers](../results/reading-validation-symbols/published_dev-scores.json)

**Can the model find both the original source and its first later explanation with no question or marked spans?** On the 24 held papers it produced 791 alerts, 48 of which had the correct original source and arrival inside the first annotated explanation. There were 392 annotated events, yielding precision 6.1%, recall 12.2%, and F1 0.081. An identically trained surface head scored 0.043. The paired F1 improvement was 0.038 (0.014–0.069). The notation parser covered 80.9% of eligible source spans. On 1,216 math sentences in 21 external SymDef papers, the contextual readout produced 187 alerts, 65 correct against 360 gold events, F1 0.238, versus 0.055 from the surface head. The external paired F1 improvement was 0.182 (0.129–0.239). These apertures identify an explanation interval made available by the author. A person’s moment of comprehension is a separate measurement. [External results](../results/reading-validation-symbols/external-scores.json)

**Can it reliably retain one variable while the other is explained, then release both correctly?** The contextual decoder localized both source identities and both release intervals in zero of 109 held dual pairs. The literal remention baseline succeeded on three. The contextual decoder retained the second already-proposed symbol at a correctly localized first closure in five of 109. The independent corpus had only five eligible dual pairs and yielded zero complete paths. The fitted contextual features support source attribution at known explanation intervals and improve text-only discovery; the complete two-source lifecycle failed under this decoder. A registered follow-up separated three observations with distinct likelihoods: whether an already-visible source was eligible for a later definition, whether an explanation began at the current token, and which earlier source a particular explanation belonged to. It recovered both actual source spans and both first definition intervals for 2 of 109 held pairs. Ordinary event recall rose from 48/392 to 78/392, while the number of alerts rose from 791 to 1,682; F1 fell from 0.081 to 0.075. The paper-wise interval for the improvement in complete paired lifecycles includes zero (0.000–0.056). The external SymDef F1 fell from 0.238 to 0.154 and neither decoder recovered a complete trajectory among its five pairs. Both changes followed inspection of the original results. These findings constrain deployment to source ranking or first-definition suggestions. A source-specific time-to-first-explanation likelihood treated each annotated explanation as an interval. A source that received no later formal explanation contributed the probability of surviving the observed passage. This used 971 intervals and 9,193 censored paths across 56 source papers. Each active symbol could close at the same token. On whole held papers it recovered 66 of 392 events from 712 alerts, F1 0.120 (paper-bootstrap 95% interval 0.052–0.202). On the independent SymDef papers it recovered 89 of 360 events from 255 alerts, F1 0.289 (0.234–0.343). Complete trajectories remained at zero of 109 and zero of five, respectively. **What goes wrong before two symbols can be closed correctly?** In the original decoder, the two exact source spans were both available in 72 of the 109 paired cases. At the true evidence, the median rank of the correct proposed span was third among 23 eligible earlier spans. Both exact source spans ranked first in zero pairs. Even with both correct sources supplied, a separately tuned causal threshold could close both at the right time in only 18 of the 72 representable pairs. A single threshold could succeed in 12. The ordinary article-only decoder also raised alerts in 124 of 424 paragraphs without any forward explanation. The 109 dual cases come from 35 paragraphs in nine papers; 73 additional pairs had overlapping first descriptions and were outside the registered distinct-interval comparison. [Failure decomposition and denominators](../results/reading-validation-symbols/timing-diagnostic-held.json)

Learning typed source, description onset, continuation and relation scores raised held-paper F1 to 0.168 (0.069–0.283), with 61 correct alerts among 333. It recovered both identities and both release intervals for one of 109 pairs. A threshold chosen to deliver at least 50% precision on calibration papers subsequently gave seven correct alerts among ten on held papers, covering 1.8% of the 392 first explanations. It flagged two of 424 paragraphs with no forward explanation at that operating point and 64 at the broader operating point. The learned source filter retained only 13 of 360 exact sources in the separately encoded SymDef corpus; this reduced external F1 to 0.016. The unfiltered notation grammar covered all 360. An independent prefix audit covered 366,205 proposals at 1,558 truncations in 121 paragraphs. Keeping every structural source proposal available to the interval decoder restored exact source coverage in SymDef to 360/360. The retained-source system made 47 correct alerts among 117, giving precision 0.402 (paper-bootstrap 95% interval 0.295–0.513), recall 0.131 (0.093–0.170), and F1 0.197 (0.144–0.251). The independent interval-survival specification remained stronger there at F1 0.289. In the held Symlink papers the retained-source system found 64 of 392 explanations in 335 alerts, with F1 0.176 (0.072–0.288). It located the first source and timing in 23/109 two-source cases and retained the other open source in 15/109. It recovered both identities and both intervals in 3/109, tied with the literal-remention comparison. The failure of simultaneous recovery persists when multiple source identities may close at the same word.
