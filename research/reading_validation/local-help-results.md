# Distinguishing a request for a definition from a request for an example

After a research abstract, which term would its reader single out for a definition, and which would they single out for an example? When both terms had the same initial familiarity for that reader, the full article model chose the right ordering for 62.2% of pairs. Its crossed reader–article 95% interval was 43.9–78.3%. A lexical model given the same familiarity rating reached 63.0%. The paired difference was −0.8 percentage points [−13.1, 16.5]. Nine readers contributed informative pairs to this primary comparison. The evidence leaves a wide range of possible effects from adding article context. ## What each person reported

Guo et al. asked eleven computer science graduate researchers about designated terms in scientific abstracts. Starting familiarity ranged from never having heard of the term to understanding it deeply. The survey showed five categories numbered 1–5, which the released data encode as 0–4 in increasing order. After reading, each participant could independently indicate a need for a definition, background or an example. The candidate terms were prepared in advance with language-model assistance. This procedure measures the reported kind of help a reader wanted for a supplied term. A naturally occurring click or the moment of difficulty would require a different measurement. [Guo et al., NAACL 2024, §§3–4](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.255/) · [Source release](https://github.com/talaugust/PersonalizedJargon/tree/22add4cc00274de199cc74bf0dbd629d05151b08/data)

The analysis used 5,247 term–reader observations for 477 target terms across 78 abstracts. Sixteen further eligible abstracts remained reserved for this response task. The primary comparison required a definition-only response for one term and an example-only response for the other; background could be checked for either. Both terms had to occur in the same paper and receive the same initial rating from the same person. These restrictions left 129 pairs in 65 reader–article cells. Terms marked both ways or neither way still contributed to the separate binary endpoints as appropriate. Candidate geometry and aggregated eligibility counts were available when the analysis was specified. The project had previously evaluated other responses from the Guo source. The example requirement and mutually exclusive preference are new endpoints within that source. ## Held-out reading behavior

The models below withheld both the test reader and the test article fold. Each comparison gives equal weight to articles, then readers within an article, then the unequal pairs for that reader. Ties received half credit. The intervals resample readers and articles independently over 10,000 draws. The separate binary endpoints compare a term marked yes and a term marked no for the designated response. [Estimator and split](local_help_fit.py) · [Score calculation](local_help_score.py)

Allowing the two terms to have different initial ratings changes the task. For any definition need, the lexical model rose from 73.5% to 84.2% when given that reader's own initial ratings, a paired gain of 10.8 points [5.8, 16.3]. The complete article model with those ratings reached 84.8% [80.1, 88.8] on 4,300 pairs across 68 articles. Its 0.6-point gain over lexical + prior had an interval of −0.7 to 3.0 points. For exclusive help type the full model reached 77.9% [68.5, 87.4] on 484 pairs from 54 articles, and for example need it reached 60.6% [54.2, 67.3] on 3,663 pairs from 68 articles. Thus the clearest incremental result concerns knowing a particular reader's initial familiarity when predicting where that reader wanted a definition. The added explanatory features did not clear the prespecified superiority threshold. [Every model arm, raw counts and paired comparisons](../results/reading-validation-local/help-choice/scores.json)

## What an independent study can run

The released heads integrate nine measurements, in order. The first five are the frozen CompLex lexical score, a fitted form/frequency score, the natural logarithm of one plus term word count, the natural logarithm of one plus term character count, and the term's character start divided by article character length. The remaining four are the strongest subsequent DEFT surface definition-link probability, the PLABA local phrase score, the DEFT semantic term-proposal logit, and the strongest subsequent DEFT semantic definition-link probability. A later definition can influence the final two link measurements for an earlier term. The full-article outputs therefore pertain to retrospective editorial review. [Feature implementation](local_help_article.py) · [Feature and coefficient seals](../results/reading-validation-local/help-choice/l4-worker-package.json)

An independent study supplies original English article text plus its candidate terms and exact half-open Unicode character intervals. Each target must align to the local phrase grid and a one- to eight-word DEFT span. The worker reports excluded targets and a reason. An original article of at most 1,024 native tokens is within the registered source feature range. Complete articles of 1,025–2,048 tokens receive an extrapolation flag, and longer articles are excluded without truncation. The builder consumes mid-layer states, final-layer states and global attention summaries from a single causal Qwen3.5-2B forward on each distinct full article. The primary Guo comparison conditioned on a supplied inventory and used measured familiarity for each designated term. [Response-free worker](local_help_l4.py) · [Exact input, code and weight digests](../results/reading-validation-local/help-choice/l4-worker-package.json)

`HelpReadout().predict(features, endpoint=..., arm=..., familiarity_before=...)` accepts a numeric matrix with one row per term and returns log odds and a probability. The endpoint names are `definition`, `example`, and `definition_over_example`. The `lexical` and `lexical_prior` arms use the first five columns; `lexical_deft_prior` uses the first six. The `article` and `article_prior` arms use all nine. Select a fitted text-only arm when no relevant reader measurement is available. Prior-aware arms require one 0–4 rating per term. A fixed population intercept learned on Guo assigns the reference odds for a previously unseen reader. It uses equal weight per article and equal weight per reader within article. Survey responses from a new population can have different frequencies, so absolute calibration requires separate evaluation. [Public serving contract](local_help_inference.py) · [Frozen source weights](../results/reading-validation-local/help-choice/weights.json)

All 840 convex fits, including the final source refits, reached a maximum absolute gradient below 10⁻⁷ in at most six Newton updates. A reconstruction from archived Qwen and attention features for all 477 eligible term rows preserved every column within 1.2 × 10⁻⁷. Across all three response types, five model arms and all five familiarity levels, the largest change in predicted probability was 1.93 × 10⁻⁸. These are checks on training and implementation. Held-out comparisons in the table measure behavioral agreement. A separate live L4 replay used three unreserved Guo articles and 22 targets selected by material length before the run. It observed exactly three forwards over 583 tokens and recovered all 22 targets. The largest absolute difference across all response probabilities was 3.09 × 10⁻⁶, within the fixed 0.003 tolerance. The largest feature discrepancy was 7.94 × 10⁻⁵ on a DEFT term-proposal logit.
