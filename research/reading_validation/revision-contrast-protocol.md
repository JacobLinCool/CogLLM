# Similar length and conflicting revision preferences

Before the SimpDA 2022 responses are opened, we specify two secondary comparisons that answer editorial questions left open by the overall preference rate. Both use the immutable source–candidate forecasts from the primary revision study, with no further tweaking.

First, compare correctly ranked simplicity within source across (i) identical word counts and (ii) word counts within 10% of the two candidates’ mean length. Use whitespace-delimited count, matching the existing ASSET comparison. Identical counts also enter the within-10% set. A human tie contributes no directional observation; a predictor tie earns half credit. Report the number of informative pairs and sources for each stratum and the word-count, trained-edit, earlier CEFR and adaptive edit-plus-Qwen comparisons.

Second, among pairs with different mean human scores on both simplicity and meaning, distinguish aligned preferences from a trade-off. A trade-off occurs when the easier variant has a lower meaning-preservation score. Evaluate both preferences together using the product of the two direction credits. A predictor with a tie in one outcome receives half credit in that outcome. A model that applies exactly the same simplicity ordering to both aspects cannot select both preferred variants in a trade-off unless a prediction ties. Compare the adaptive two-head model, the two-head edit baseline, the shared CEFR ordering and the adaptive model’s simplicity ranking reused for meaning. Report paired intervals by resampling complete source sentences and, as a sensitivity analysis, by resampling both sources and raters and recomputing the human directions and eligible strata. Report each stratum separately and document any sparse estimates. The human means serve as the reference, with three independent assigned judgments per variant. The analysis does not imply that one member of a trade-off is universally preferable. [Primary methods](revision-protocol.md)
