# Do population word readouts distinguish an expected article from an expected noun?

Nieuwland and colleagues preregistered a direct nine-laboratory replication in 334 English readers. They reported a substantial relation between human cloze and the noun N400. The larger article effect from the earlier study did not replicate. These published directions motivated the present study and were known before registration. Individual trial values, model forecasts for these materials, paired target coordinates and per-item effects have not been inspected. This protocol uses the authors’ publicly released experimental materials, language norms and the aggregate single-trial measurements used in their mixed-effects analysis. The study is independent of the Spanish and English cohorts previously used to fit CogLLM. [Original study and participant methods](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.33468) · [Authors’ data and stimuli](https://osf.io/eyzaq/)

Reconstruct both authentic alternative presentations for each source item with the exact article and critical noun. Certify their common preceding word sequence and the separate last native token of each printed target. Supply only complete text to the frozen Qwen backbone once per alternative. Retain direct whole-word surprisal, the original unresolved curve and the frozen English and Spanish N400-trained readouts at both originally displayed target words. Retrieve the original author-provided cloze and N400 response records on the assessment machine after the model inputs, coefficients and forecasts are locked. Distinguish a first-word response from the noun response after the visible choice of article. Use participant, item and laboratory counts separately; preserve differing exclusions for the two targets. Validate source units, sign of the authors’ amplifier data, item coding and trial inclusion against their own R analyses. Restrict to item variants that have verified displayed text and unambiguous trial-to-condition matches. Report eligible coverage and all exclusions. Experimentally supplied labels stay outside model inputs.

Primary comparison is the difference of the mean within-item unexpected-minus-expected contrast at noun versus preceding article. For the assigned experimental condition use the authors’ article probability designation even if an item's noun has the reverse cloze ordering; also report continuous cloze separately by target class. Rank the true measured condition differences across lexical families, same-cloze controls, and cases where higher or lower human variation limits identification. Report separately the proportion of noun contrast expressed at the article and the paired human confidence interval. Do not infer that a nonsignificant article effect is zero. Estimate clustered intervals over original stimulus families, resample entire readers and laboratories when identifiers support it, and give leave-one-laboratory-out direction. Cross-study EEG voltages have independent scales. Present contrast ratios and consistent position of the larger effect; retain laboratory microvolts for human responses. A model may predict high surprise at a low-probability article and still miss the observed electrophysiological selectivity. Compare the fixed physiological readouts with raw surprisal, surface+surprisal, and a constant-location baseline. An affirmative localized claim requires aligned direction, correct relative locus and informative agreement beyond a hard-coded noun preference. If the corpus does not expose both true target locations and interruption-free original text, do not substitute inferred participant trajectories or invented continuations.
