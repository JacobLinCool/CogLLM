# Can an article-only model locate words that readers identify as unexplained character references?

Goyal, Li and Durrett released SNaC, a human study in which readers marked exact text spans in book and movie summaries with coherence defects. Readers completed a segment before advancing. A segment contains about 2.7 book sentences or three movie sentences. The span locates the passage readers identified after reading the segment. It does not date the onset of a private mental event. Our source search found no prior CogLLM SNaC evaluation. We inspected the file structure and the first book's annotation format before writing this plan. The files contain aggregated span votes without global individual-reader identifiers. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.29/) · [Authors' data release](https://github.com/tagoyal/snac)

We selected SNaC after observing that calibrated surprisal matched or exceeded the broad transition predictor on CoheSentia and DECOR. Localizing a specific relation supplies a stronger semantic hypothesis. An unexplained character is often a new name, so a valid evaluation must compare it with lexical novelty, ordinary word surprisal and a model of local contextual representation. No SNaC model or movie outcome was calculated before freezing this protocol.

The primary task is locating every source word that overlaps an unambiguously matched CharE span with at least one human vote. The secondary task repeats this definition for RefE, an unexplained reference to an event or object. The span match uses the exact characters of the visible segment after whitespace normalization. If a target span has zero or multiple matches, omit the whole segment for that target category and report the omission. Other categories do not remove otherwise eligible negatives. A mark from two or more readers defines a sensitivity analysis for which locations still receive credit. We do not count consensus votes as distinct independent subjects. We retain exact original word offsets and each model sees only source tokens through a scored word. When the complete word and its standalone prefix tokenize differently, omit the word. Where no complete summary fits in the validated 2,048-token window we exclude the document before scoring. We never use error labels or their locations to select input texts or words to extract.

The 110 book summaries from two generation systems provide training and calibration. All 40 movie summaries remain a separate test domain. We group the two book systems by the numeric released identifier, and reserve 20% of those families for threshold calibration by a SHA-256 ordering with salt 'cogllm-snac-books-v1:'. We audit exact prefix and repeated source text across domains. The movie system differs as well as the genre. The only trained parameters are convex binomial readout heads, with standardized train-only predictors, a free intercept and 0.01 L2 penalty. One feature row represents one reading word. A fixed development threshold targets no more than five false alarms per 100 unmarked words. Report absolute counts, precision, sensitivity, AP, AUROC and uncertainty from 10,000 whole-movie bootstrap samples. Train and analyze each target independently.

Four nested arms independently test (1) surface cues including upper/lower case, position, length, repetition and distance to past mentions, (2) the same cues plus frozen causal subword surprisal summed at the word, (3) the previous controls and projected causal current-word middle/final states, and (4) the same representation plus native attention and semantic similarity between the current word and its completed possible antecedents. The latter considers prior appearances of the same lexical form, earlier distinct capitalized words and other earlier content words. Fully normalized contextual cosines and native attention to earlier lexical candidates provide direct relation cues. Every arm receives a single article-only model pass or a subset of its outputs. The pretrained CogLLM projections are fixed. Primary evidence for a semantic relation contribution requires the full model to improve movie CharE AP over the surprisal and local-state arms with paired 95% intervals above zero. Independently, report localization among capitalized words, and whether the highest-scoring word falls on a marked phrase within each annotated segment. A failure of the incremental criterion does not invalidate prediction against simple text controls. These comparisons are secondary to the prior CoheSentia and DECOR releases.
