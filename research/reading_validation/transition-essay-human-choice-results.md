# An essay-revision score fails to select between two plausible rewrites

A score trained on expert annotations and original-versus-replacement examples agreed with an independent expert on 84 of 256 directional choices between two actual rewrites (32.8%, 95% topic-and-essay bootstrap interval 21.5–45.6%). This was a new judgment by a different person on essays from the same DECOR source. Each essay topic was held out from fitting its corresponding readout. The earlier success choosing a supplied replacement over its problematic original therefore has a narrower use than selecting the better of two proposed repairs. [Original blind preference study, §5.3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.639/) · [Target and split registered before scoring](transition-essay-human-choice-protocol.md)

The release has 100 target passages from 57 essays in eight topics. For each target, the expert made three blind comparisons, separately comparing a human reference, Llama trained with explanations and Llama trained without explanations against GPT-4. The expert saw the original problem and its stated reason. The model read each revised passage in its original context. In 299 pairs containing actual sentence rewrites, the evaluator selected the first 128 times, the second 128 times, and a tie 43 times. One literal editing instruction accounts for the difference from the 300 released comparisons. The import reproduced the paper’s 74/100 preferences for a human rewrite in its comparison with GPT-4. The 348 distinct candidate texts were each encoded once. The native-minus-form difference in agreement was −3.91 percentage points (−10.04 to +1.15) and in log loss was +.1420 (+.0524 to +.3973). Agreement stayed below 34% in each of the three systems' comparisons. On a conditional check comparing how well scores distinguish choices within each fixed generator pairing, AUROCs were .502, .430 and .494. Reversing the source score after viewing labels would therefore mostly learn which generator this expert generally preferred. With other-topic choices available for calibration, three-way log loss was .804 for a native observer including generator information versus .812 for the generator-origin prior (difference −.008, interval −.036 to +.014). These intervals describe sensitivity to the included essays and topics. They cannot estimate variation between evaluators because only one was recorded. [Paired comparisons, sample sizes and calibrated secondary scores](../results/reading-validation-transition-external/essay-expert-choice/scores.json)

On the separate original–replacement task, the source joint model preferred 66.5% of 203 actual expert changes and exceeded its original-judgment-only version by 6.4 points. That result supports ranking a proposed repair against its flagged original in this corpus. Editorial recommendations choosing among competing repairs require separate human preferences.
