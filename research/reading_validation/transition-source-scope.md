# Public evidence for an author inspecting an explanatory text

The Carter–Hoffman, CoheSentia and DECOR studies have open records matching specific passages or sentence boundaries to human judgments. Their materials comprise constructed short passages, generated stories and student argumentative essays, respectively. SNaC supplies independently collected human spans in narrative summaries. These sources cover several forms of coherence judgment. Scientific explanations written for knowledgeable and novice readers remain a separate evidence requirement.

A 2025 expository-text study in Buenos Aires tested 48 university students in its first experiment. The four Spanish passages were about memory, language comprehension, telescopes and particle physics. Participants marked incongruent sentences on a directed second reading. Its journal-linked view-only repository contains three Word documents listed as original expository texts, modified monitoring texts and comprehension questions. It supplies the experimental stimuli, but its public root contains no participant-level marked-span table. Imposed sentence inconsistencies would measure a model's ability to detect experimental manipulations. They cannot be counted as successful localization of a reader's response without the reader's mark. [Study methods](https://journals.copmadrid.org/psed/art/psed2025a6) · [Authors' source repository](https://osf.io/xwy9g/?view_only=20c506036747473e8c9ea80d1cf45631)

Bai et al.'s hierarchical resource-rational reading model already links word, sentence and discourse behavior. Their 2026 study uses McNamara et al.'s coherence-by-knowledge results as a text-level comprehension test. The present project already lists the Bai dataset under R20 for the separate reading-under-time-pressure observation. The public human-data folder contains a 72,472-byte comprehension CSV, a 13,955,696-byte corrected eye-movement JSON and a README. Simulation files occupy separate figure folders. These recorded outcomes address comprehension and eye movements rather than a per-word reader-marked explanatory gap. A one-pass model's incremental reading estimates should be compared with these prior computational results using aligned endpoints. The existing evidence does not establish priority for modeling reading behavior across multiple levels. [Bai et al., Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02534-0) · [Public source and data](https://osf.io/q2dm6/)
