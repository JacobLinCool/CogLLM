# Recognizing a clue that resolves several outstanding questions

One piece of news can settle several questions at the same reading line. The previous model gave every source its own smooth lifetime. Its error was largest where many questions closed together. We investigate a shared, line-specific discharge opportunity using the same four previously examined German narratives. Within each source fold fit a causal clue probability g(t), and a separate conditional closing probability q(s,t) for each preceding source. When no clue occurs, all earlier questions survive. When a clue does occur, each earlier question can resolve with its own probability. The marginal human-issue hazard is g(t)q(s,t); the shared clue introduces positive conditional dependence between closures at a line. An independently parameterized comparison uses the same textual information per pair and has no shared gate. Both predict openings, the probability that a specific prior issue survives, its closure mass and the conserved expected count. End each estimate at the last visible token on that original line. Place no prior on ending the story near zero and supply no future length.

For a team with known issue outcomes at a line, directly score the joint probability of its observed set of closures including lines where none of the at-risk questions closed. Sum over the hidden gate on zero-event lines. Uncertain and right-censored spans preserve their observed survivals; an interval closure contributes the probability of closing somewhere in its permitted interval. With independent hazards these observations reduce to Bernoulli survival terms. A within-state count objective uses squared distance to the permitted interval scaled on source stories; it makes no Poisson distributional claim in the presence of dependent closures. Each source story, then annotation team, has equal weight in fitting. Features use progress so far, current wording, change in native line state from the preceding line and the earlier local setting, and similarities between earlier and current lines. All scales and textual axes are fitted exclusively on the other three complete stories.

The published AA workbooks record more questions than JE on every story they both annotated. Model the team's different reporting propensity with a source-estimated opening intercept and closing intercept. Predict both known team styles at every line of every held text. The article-only output always averages them equally; it cannot use which team actually annotated the held story. Compare the article-only average and the optional known-team forecast against the *same* published lines. This is a measurement-context test for two returning annotation teams. It does not personalize arbitrary readers. Keep all four story results, report signed and absolute changes at the largest human closing bursts, whole-count error, candidate identification and proper lifetime score. Mark the biggest burst using human outcomes only in scoring, never in forecasting. The more demanding comparison is the otherwise identical independent-hazard observer.
