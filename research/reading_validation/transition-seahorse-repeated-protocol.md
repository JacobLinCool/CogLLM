# Estimating disagreement between readers who rated the same summary

The published test supplement contains up to three English Q1 votes for the same presentation. An analysis of these votes found 2.85% observed pairwise disagreement versus 7.13% expected from the original binary observer under independent draws. The following analysis was specified after seeing that discrepancy. Its empirical transfer test uses previously inspected summaries. The English production observer, its parameters and its threshold stay fixed. [Original response study](transition-seahorse-results.md) · [SEAHORSE multiple-annotation files](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/seahorse)

Use definitive Yes/No ballots grouped by exact summary text, original article identifier and generating system. Require at least two ballots and a corresponding key in the canonical partition. Preserve the actual group size. The released fields include the language of the worker but no unique worker identifier. The design supports an exchangeable group-count likelihood and uncertainty by original article. It cannot estimate stable individual differences or establish that particular people recur across partitions.

Train the native or form probability on original training articles after omitting all source articles that contain an eligible train repeated-ballot group. The canonical grade for one row can have been selected from its repeated ballots. Whole-article exclusion prevents using that grade twice in estimating the source probability and the dispersion. Recalibrate the mean on the original validation groups used by the earlier observer. Check every source exclusion against the prior exact/12-word rules. Use held repeated ballots only in scoring. For completeness compare the original all-training frozen probabilities on the same test groups with the parameters fitted to the source-held-out mean. Primary results use a consistent mean fit for source and test.

Let a group of n observed judgments contain k No responses. Give each summary its model-based mean p. Under an exchangeable beta-binomial model the latent probability of No has mean p and variance ρp(1−p), giving pairwise disagreement 2(1−ρ)p(1−p). Fit ρ on original source repeated groups by maximizing their article-balanced beta-binomial count likelihood. Evaluate the probability of every observed held count, the Brier score of each actual pairwise disagreement indicator, and the mean discrepancy between expected and observed pair disagreements. Compare (i) source-specific mean with independent draws, (ii) the same mean with fitted ρ, (iii) an intercept-adjusted mean under independence, (iv) a source-fitted intercept and ρ, and (v) a constant beta-binomial source prevalence and ρ. Include the matched form observer in the same treatments. An overall constant estimate of observed source pair disagreement controls for calibration on a low base rate. The primary contrast is native count log-loss change from estimating ρ at the unchanged mean. The pooled-independence comparisons and mean adjustment assess distinct alternatives. Intervals resample complete original articles for the same fixed held forecasts, and condition on estimated source coefficients.

The test measures exchangeability of published aggregate votes. Shared wording, rater-selection procedures or variation omitted by the model can all contribute to a positive ρ. The original papers do not identify a physiological or causal interpretation of this parameter.
