# Lexical complexity across two reader populations

Can the existing Qwen3.5-2B lexical head rank isolated English words by the difficulty reported by individual readers outside CompLex? The Word Complexity Lexicon provides 1–6 ratings from eleven fluent English L2 readers. Alonzo et al. collected the same rating from eleven deaf or hard-of-hearing (DHH) adults on the same word inventory. These are distinct reader cohorts with shared materials. Neither label records difficulty during article reading. The public surveys do not establish a proficiency gradient across these readers. [Maddela and Xu, 2018](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1410/) · [Alonzo et al., dataset](https://github.com/oliveralonzo/DHH-lexical-dataset/tree/71ef53d851a9c2ded9ec989b3308afdca0d137a5)

Freeze scores before opening the individual ratings. Intersect the two studies with word forms for which the archived Qwen target-only feature exists. Use single English alphabetic words, match case-insensitively, remove ambiguous source entries, and prefer the lowercase native stimulus where several capitalizations are available. For the primary analysis exclude every case-folded form used as a CompLex training or trial target, including component words of multiword targets. Also evaluate the complete matched lexicon as a secondary analysis. Report coverage explicitly. Restricting evaluation to words with historical Qwen features changes the population of words, especially its frequency range.

The Qwen candidate is the existing full-training CompLex target-only ridge head. Compare a frequency-only predictor (negative log subtitle frequency) and a surface predictor fit on the original CompLex training observations using character length, lengths on a log scale, vowel count, and log subtitle frequency and its square. Use an intercept and ridge penalty one on standardized features. Clip CompLex model scores to their original 0–1 output bounds for parity with the released head. The surface calibration and Qwen head receive no labels from either new cohort. The 2018 English subtitle frequencies come from the pinned FrequencyWords English release. [FrequencyWords](https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/tree/525f9b560de45753a5ea01069454e72e9aa541c6/content/2018/en)

For each source compute the mean within-reader Spearman correlation, using only the words each reader rated. The primary contrast is the paired Qwen minus surface correlation on novel forms within each cohort. Report the frequency-only contrast and direct human inter-reader agreement as context. Resample words and readers independently in the same bootstrap draws for all systems, recomputing ranks and handling ties each time (2,000 draws). Also report a word-only interval conditional on this particular reader sample. The two primary populations are reported together regardless of the sign of the results. Do not treat a shared ordering of words as a prediction of which particular reader will find one word difficult. Source versions, prediction hashes, exact eligibility and scoring code accompany the results.
