# Which earlier words do readers choose when a noun refers back?

Across **91 previously unseen works**, the model assigned more probability to people's actual reference decisions than a trained word-form and distance model. Readers made 11,309 decisions about 1,639 ordinary noun or determiner–noun expressions. They clicked an earlier phrase 2,194 times. For the linked cases where the current noun had never appeared earlier with identical wording, the contextual model's highest-ranked word fell inside the selected phrase **38.0%** of the time, compared with **28.6%** for form. The paired improvement was 9.4 percentage points [0.9, 17.8] across the 68 works contributing such a case. These noun decisions were unavailable to the extractor and to the source-trained predictors. The full-event loss reduction of the learned model against form was .0690 nats [.0514, .0875]. The conditional loss reduction against fixed copying was .0314 [.0140, .0500]. Against context alone the conditional reduction was .0084 [−.0175, .0339]. Thus text-sensitive retrieval transfers to new works. The extra learned copy weight improved on the fixed copying policy and added little to unmodified context. Many players introduced a new item or chose a nonlink action. The 9.3% in the final row quantifies the limitation of offering an earlier word at every queried noun.

### How the observations entered the model

We used separate proper scores for a player's four available actions and for their selected range when they chose a backward link. The source archive contained 58,903 initial actions in training. A frozen Qwen3.5-2B supplied contextual states at the end of each printed word in a single article pass. Every eligible earlier word was a candidate. The optional copy gate was calibrated on 570 linked development choices at 121 mentions in 12 works. Decisions from the same person on the same mention were deduplicated. We averaged people within mention, then mentions within original work. The 95% intervals resample whole works; they condition on the observed player pool. In the original gold material, 9,726 decisions in 42 works gave a contextual full-event loss of .876 versus .946 for form. With no prior literal match, contextual retrieval hit 34.8% versus 22.0%. The later copy-gate follow-up used this already examined gold panel. Before grading the additional intake, we sealed 48,729 query positions with 16,077,106 possible earlier-word candidates across 201 article records. Two additional replay articles reproduced their original word sketches exactly. We excluded works or passages sharing a consecutive 20-word sequence with the earlier gold panel. Of 92 remaining articles, 91 supplied four-reader-qualified observations. A separate check restricted to previously unseen game players then retained only 47 decisions in seven works, including 16 backward clicks. Its full-event gain over form had a 95% interval spanning zero. The supported replication concerns new works within the same game population.
