# Can an article-only model recover the reading consequences of introducing familiar information first?

Scholman and colleagues crossed three manipulations of the same short English passage: presenting given or new material in the first clause, placing the main or subordinate clause first, and narrating events in chronological or reversed order. The source includes the actual total fixation time on the target sentence, second-clause first-pass and total time, optional question accuracy, and separately measured word-reading efficiency and working memory. This is an experimentally manipulated writing-order question. Times measure processing duration; answered questions concern comprehension. Neither supplies individual subword confusion labels. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1080/23273798.2022.2077971) · [Released materials and responses](https://osf.io/j3kf6/)

Give the primary analysis only complete eight-condition material families for which every clause has exactly one matching occurrence, the entire target sentence is identifiable, and before/after and the two clause orders agree with the recorded condition. Document the excluded words/families. Do not silently rewrite source material to make it match an eye-tracking record. The model sees ordinary complete passages. Reserve 25% of readers independently by hash, then grade both new articles and new readers together. Report test-material results on all original readers separately for statistical precision. Find the condition effects and their interaction independently in the complete observations and in the held materials. The primary writer-facing contrast compares given-first versus given-second ordering within an otherwise identical event and clause-structure condition. The primary times are total target-sentence time and second-clause first-pass time (milliseconds, plus model fits in log time); report separate effects for subordinate-first and main-first and their difference. Include the published trial outlier flag in the main second-clause analysis. Report all designated regions and the distribution of available question opportunities. The questions serve only as secondary outcomes for the participants actually asked.

Fit separate time, question response, and measured-reader adjustments using the endpoint's own loss. Preserve a form-and-position baseline including length, before/after, preceding overlap and position. The measured word-reading and working-memory covariates are optional, training-calibrated reader information. An experiment-design comparator using the supplied condition labels is an oracle for engineering diagnosis. Compare held response likelihood, given-new effect magnitude in the original units, and effect direction by material with grouped confidence intervals. A numerical advantage for surface order cues alone is not an advance in understanding a reader. The published directions were known before this analysis; no held material-conditioned scores have been inspected. This study predates the present models and does not exclude pretraining exposure.
