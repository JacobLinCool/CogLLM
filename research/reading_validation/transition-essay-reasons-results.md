# What helps identify a broken connection in a student essay?

English essay experts marked 446 of 1,352 sentence transitions as incoherent in 100 student essays on eight assigned topics. A reader can identify a sentence for several reasons, so each observed reason supervised its own risk. Each essay entered the frozen language model once together with its assigned title. The judgment became available at the sentence end. A new topic was held out on each of eight rotations. Six other topics provided training examples and one topic set all decision thresholds. Intervals resample complete topics and essays. [DECOR study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.639/) · [Measurement protocol](transition-essay-reasons-protocol.md)

| Observer at the completed sentence | Log loss ↓ | Average precision ↑ | AUROC ↑ | Source-threshold F1 ↑ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Story-trained observer, applied unchanged | .750 | .451 | .616 | .330 |
| Essay-trained, binary coherence label and native reading states | .832 | .468 | .622 | .477 |
| Same native states, seven observed reasons | .668 | .506 | .653 | .504 |
| Sentence position, lengths, repetition and surprisal | .592 | .510 | .674 | .519 |
| Seven reason hazards anchored to the preceding lexical observer | .580 | .554 | .686 | .509 |

The unconstrained reason observer gained .164 log loss [−.350, −.043 for its difference] against the matched binary model. The six-feature lexical observer still predicted the observed decisions more accurately. The next fit started each reason with a share of this lexical risk, learned a multiplier per reason from native representations, and estimated the reliability of the total adjustment on the development topic. Reliability ranged from .523 to 1.000. Every residual fit reached maximum absolute gradient below 2 × 10⁻⁸. [Direct reason results](../results/reading-validation-transition-external/essay-cross-topic/scores.json) · [Anchored estimator](transition-essay-hazard-protocol.md)

The anchored observer improved average precision by .043 [.005, .076] over the matched lexical observer. Seven of eight topics showed a ranking improvement. Mean log loss decreased by .012 [−.026, +.002]. The interval includes no difference. The source threshold maximizing F1 produced 38.0 false alarms per 100 expert-unmarked sentences. A second threshold targeting 10% source false positives produced 9.7 per 100 on the held topics, precision .589 and recall .283. The corresponding lexical control achieved 10.6 false alarms, precision .583 and recall .300. These operating points support ranking sentences for review with considerable residual error. [Held topics, calibration and thresholds](../results/reading-validation-transition-external/essay-cross-topic/source-anchored-hazard-scores.json)

The corpus had already been examined in the transfer from stories to essays. The anchored formulation followed inspection of the first essay-trained observer. Each individual test fold excludes its judgments from training and calibration. The choice of estimator therefore still requires a new corpus to assess generalization. Its eight-source inference ensemble and lexical control reproduced every archived topic-specific held forecast within 7.2 × 10⁻¹¹ before their independent test on summaries of news. The essay annotations establish sentence-level expert judgments. They do not establish at which word a reader felt difficulty or whether readers would prefer a revision.
