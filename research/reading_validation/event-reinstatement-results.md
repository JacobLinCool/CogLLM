# When a protagonist returns, does the model retrieve the same object as readers?

Readers recognized an earlier, unrepeated object 41 ms faster after its protagonist was rementioned than after a switch to someone new. We tested an explicit model of indirect retrieval. At the final sentence, attention to intervening words was propagated to words from their original introductory event. The accelerator saw the original text through the recognition region once and received no probe or condition label. The 96 presentations per bank yielded 283 distinct article exposures after merging identical input sequences across the banks. The model was specified after inspecting the original direct-attention results on these materials. Values are differences in allocated retrieval probability, with 95% family-bootstrap intervals. Exactly 31 of 32 unrepeated objects gained access under protagonist remention. In 23 new-protagonist continuations and only one remention continuation, none of the target object's old associates recurred. Inverse distance alone recovered the same raw direction in 31 families. The three earlier comparison words gained even more native access on average. The target-specific effect consequently reversed, and the paired comparison with distance admitted zero. The positive native-minus-distance goal contrast reflects a smaller reversal than distance after adjustment. It supplies no evidence of a selective object-memory recovery in the native model.
