# Locating a named difficult expression in a complete abstract

The initial PLABA cross-fits found that strong within-sentence retrieval did not carry over to whole abstracts. The scoring rule was fitted on candidate spans within a supplied sentence. It did not learn which sentence a reader might single out. This follow-up adds a sentence selection probability to the conditional within-sentence span probability. It reuses the same previously scored materials and groups. It is a retrospective test.

We train on the same 94 distinct named expressions. Within an abstract containing at least one such expression, its count in each sentence supplies a conditional target distribution. An unmentioned sentence remains eligible for a mark. This models a recorded act of naming an expression conditional on an abstract yielding at least one mark. Use the original reader/article-connected component holdouts. Fit every sentence-level normalization and coefficient within each training fold. Compare a uniformly probable sentence, the literal length/frequency/position sentence model, and the local biomedical and Qwen sentence model. Use the initial form and local span heads refit outside the held-out component. The primary comparison is whole-article nonoverlapping top-five recovery for the hierarchical local model versus the hierarchical form model. Report the change from the initial local unnormalized logit ranking, paired intervals under the original 12-component bootstrap, and every fitted alternative. The new ranking can be used after reading to find candidates for review. It does not estimate the onset of a reader's difficulty and should not determine the height of a causal online curve.
