# How much should a reading outcome borrow from other studies?

Each observer reports in the units of a study. Agreement between a passage rating and a timed word response is an empirical question. We compare two readers of the same frozen Qwen features. One learns shared projections for the states each endpoint is allowed to update. The other allows a separate copy of those projections for each source. Both fit exactly the same ten measurement likelihoods to the same training rows. The original article is encoded once whichever readout is used.

## Materials and decisions preceding fitting

The original observation archive contains 62 measurements in 24 sources, with 135,598 historical training and 33,208 historical development rows. Five endpoints from the single-family Qiao study have no development observations. All 27,159 distinct exposure identifiers occur in only one source, material family and historical partition. These identifiers certify the archived splits, not independence from backbone pretraining. The existing development partition has previously been studied. The three dense token tasks from the separate original training archive are outside this particular measurement experiment. It does not replace their evaluation.

Within each source, hash-sort original training families with a fixed salt. Reserve approximately 15% for selecting a training epoch, an additional 15% for learning how much to borrow, and the rest for fitting. Keep the single-family source in training. Recompute observed-value standardization on expert-fitting families only. Use identical initialized readouts and observation order for both experts. Each source has equal weight, as do endpoints within the source. Retrospective word counts retain the original material balancing. Monitor tuning likelihood separately for each expert, halve the learning rate on a plateau and persist the best inner-validation checkpoint. Report stop conditions. Fit a second seed if time permits. Do not tune using outer development responses.

For each endpoint, combine the shared and source-private forecasts as a normalized geometric mean of their predictive densities. For categorical events this combines every log probability, including the option of no event. For scalar and bivariate normal distributions it combines precision and precision-weighted mean. The known sampling-error distribution uses the reported sampling variance before pooling. A group of continuous observations supplies its exact Gaussian sufficient statistics. A material family receives one vote when estimating its endpoint's borrowing coefficient. Regularize the coefficient toward 0.5 by adding one symmetric beta(2,2) log prior per endpoint. If a source provides no calibration families, use the neutral coefficient 0.5 and mark it unestimated. Coefficients are fixed before the outer measurements are scored.

The primary quantity is the change in held-out negative log likelihood per observed trial, equally weighted over available endpoints within each source and over sources. Compare calibrated pooling to full sharing, source-private fitting, and the fixed midpoint. Bootstrap whole sources with 4,000 draws and report the number improving as well as the mean. Report each endpoint and source. A lower mean than the private expert alone could support useful borrowing; a specific advantage for adapting the coefficient requires improvement over the fixed midpoint. Repeat identity checks at coefficients zero and one, and verify coverage of full-source folds and partitions before interpreting improvements.

The initial numerical budget of 100 passes ended with both inner-validation curves still falling. Before scoring development forecasts or estimating borrowing, we extended both identical schedules to a 1,500-pass guard with the same early-stopping rule. Histories retain each update and the selected epoch.
