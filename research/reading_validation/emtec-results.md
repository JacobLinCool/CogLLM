# The frozen unresolved-stock curve adds little to reading ratings and rereads in EMTeC

Does a passage feel harder when the model carries more unresolved material, and does a given word's local stock predict a return fixation? In a human reading experiment spanning 107 native English readers and 41 prompt families, adding the selected CogLLM curve to a strong common model produced no clear gain on either held outcome. Mean stock had a descriptive correlation of 0.113 with the numerical difficulty rating after removing reader and prompt means. Word length, native surprisal, position and separate source-trained lexical features already explained much of the rankable rereading signal. Positive gains favor the added stock. The control includes PLABA mean sentence ease for the passage endpoint and CompLex for the word endpoint. The word model adds local stock and its positive change. All sixteen fitted rereading models gave a positive coefficient for stock after these adjustments (0.041–0.109 on the log-odds scale per training standard deviation), but the estimate did not yield a discernible held ranking gain. In the passage models the stock coefficient ranged from −0.005 to 0.143. Intervals use 10,000 crossed resamples of readers and prompts and equal weight per prompt. People provided 4,368 ratings on the numerical 1–5 scale. The counts in ascending response order were 1,049, 1,645, 1,216, 413 and 45. The original report documents the range and the post-reading difficulty question. We have not independently verified which printed pole corresponds to 5 and therefore report signed associations with the numerical response. The proper-score conclusions hold under a consistent reversal of all categories. Rereading was observed at 83,018 of 377,873 presented reader-word positions (21.97%). The researchers designed the separate four-choice item to maintain attention. Its 84.39% accuracy is descriptive. [Original procedure and measurement definitions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12134054/)

Every test fold withheld both the prompt and reader, across sixteen crossed folds. Each reader saw one of the variants for any given prompt. A model saw the exact public introductory header and full passage. One Qwen forward for each of 518 unique header–body sequences produced 44,705 prefix-certified word ends from 62,810 model tokens on the L4 in 98.7 seconds. All 4,367 visits with gaze had a complete and exactly matched sequence of original measured word positions, and one rated visit had no word records. Historical supervision shared a twelve-word passage with variants of one prompt, so its complete family was excluded in advance.
