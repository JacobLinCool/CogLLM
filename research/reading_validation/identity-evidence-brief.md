# What the article alone reveals about a reader

At a newly read word, a causal model can estimate how much readers will diverge in their next-word expectations. It can also identify some connections between terms and their definitions. These observations come from different human tasks. The strongest evidence for a rising and falling word-level signal comes from actual written completions at adjacent prefixes. Exact simultaneous identification of two pending scientific explanations remains difficult. All article-level inference uses the visible prefix, with one encoder invocation for each presented text. Source-weighted parameter fitting, resampling uncertainty and analyses of fixed reader records can run on CPU. The associated project used one owned Colab L4 at a time. The release audit lists 42 closed reservations, 5.939 allocation-hours and 11.522 estimated compute units at the observed rates. No reservation remained active. These totals cover the shared project, including engineering recovery and research from the other study groups.
