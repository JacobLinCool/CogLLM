# Sentence ease did not transfer reliably to readers' judgments of expert-written summaries

Among 49 lay readers of 89 expert-written Cochrane summaries, the frozen biomedical sentence-ease head correctly ordered 60.8% of pairs on which the same reader gave two summaries different simplicity ratings. The original authors' four-formula readability measure reached 62.1%. The paired difference was −1.3 percentage points (crossed 95% interval −26.9 to 25.0). The source ease model ordered actual comprehension quiz performance at 49.2%, against 63.3% for the formulas. The data give little precision about which summary an individual reader will find easier. Each of the 147 visits included four multi-select content questions, a separate attention check and ordinal ratings. Readers provided a rating of 4 or 5 for simplicity in 129 visits. Of 37 summaries read by at least two people, 22 received different simplicity ratings and one spanned the full 1–5 scale. Just two of 145 different pairs of articles shown to one person were also seen by a second person. Replication is insufficient for a human–human ceiling on that pair-ranking task. For the last column we compare different readers of an identical summary using their self-reported familiarity with designated terms while reading the original abstract, collected before exposure to the summary. Fifty-four unequal ease comparisons from 22 summaries contribute. A summary-only estimator necessarily gives each reader the same score on that comparison. [Raw distributions, denominators and family construction](../results/reading-validation-relay-summary/scores.json)

For the content quiz, an item was correct when the selected set exactly equalled the keyed set. The primary cohort answered 439/588 items correctly (74.7%), passed 146/147 attention checks and classified 87.4% of individual options correctly. This reconstructs 8.96/12 questions per three visits. Including all six visits from one additional participant also gives 8.96 after standardizing to three visits. The publication reports 9.03/12 for static visits; the discrepancy remains, and we retain the registered exact-set rule. The original interface shows both the summary and abstract during rating. Quiz content differs by article, so observed quiz performance combines the difficulty of those questions with how well readers learned the material. ## Can other human studies help?

A new target measurement head with three surface features achieved a ranked probability score of 0.10546 on simplicity (lower is better). Adding four fitted source signals under an adaptively weighted model gave 0.10568. The paired improvement was −0.00022 [−0.00247, 0.00195]. For quiz accuracy the Brier score changed from 0.18954 to 0.18924, an improvement of 0.00030 [−0.00098, 0.00185]. Every test visit belonged to both a person and a material fold excluded from fitting and calibration. The adaptive method gave the additional source signals zero median weight across the 25 primary simplicity folds. All 5,250 fits including sensitivity analyses converged, with a maximum gradient component of 4.65 × 10⁻⁷. [Calibration method and verification](relay-summary-estimator.md) · [Fitting diagnostics](../results/reading-validation-relay-summary/trained-seal.json)

We separately tested transfer of fitted directions from 2,196 sentence or passage observations in PLABA, CLEAR and StudentAnnotations. Each source contributed its own response likelihood to a common 36-feature fit. We transferred both the learned slope and a curvature matrix measuring which combinations of features the source data constrained. On simplicity, target-only training achieved 0.10253, transfer of curvature alone 0.10764, and adaptive transfer of the learned slope within that curvature 0.10566. Its improvement relative to target only was −0.00313 [−0.00912, 0.00299]. Its improvement over curvature alone was 0.00198 [−0.00159, 0.00572]. The matched quiz Brier scores were 0.18759, 0.18938 and 0.18779. A separate control transferred only the learned center with an isotropic penalty and gave 0.10103 for simplicity and 0.18493 for the quiz. Both paired intervals against target only included zero. All 650 fits converged, with a maximum gradient of 4.70 × 10⁻⁷. This test leaves general gains from sharing supervision across studies unconfirmed. ## Reproduction

Use the pinned public participant release and text-only packet in the source archive manifest. The frozen Qwen3.5-2B L4 extraction used one backbone call per summary, 91 calls and 48,144 tokens. All 1,668 sentence endpoints were certified and every summary met the 90% word-coverage threshold. The backbone received no quiz, reader profile or rating. The source noun-phrase readout was trained on isolated sentences. At evaluation it read each sentence with its preceding summary context. There were zero strong content matches against the documented supervised learning material. The article-level forecast file contains only public article IDs and text-derived features. Participation data remain in ignored local storage.
