# Event words change the inferred referent; prediction of a later slowdown remains uncertain

A pointer trained only on earlier-word clicks transferred to an independent experiment on how people interpret physical events. Reading the predicate after a pronoun lowered the model's log loss on 1,012 reader choices from **0.796 to 0.639**, a paired difference of **−0.157 [−0.260, −0.042]**. Among 30 physical situations, it selected the same object after an order reversal in 24 cases at the event endpoint, compared with seven at the pronoun. It processed all 60 isolated sentences and all 240 longer experimental presentations in one forward each. Scores for the same visible prefix differed by at most 0.0000026 probability when later text changed. This delayed use of the weights transfers a source pointer to a newly visible predicate. After the event, the native pointer agreed with individuals 12.8 percentage points more often than the surface observer [0.8, 24.1]. The primary paired log-loss comparison to surface was −0.089 [−0.188, +0.026]. The stronger claim of a reliable log-loss gain over that surface comparator remains open. The event-versus-pronoun loss change persisted when the one family with reversed survey-option ordering was removed (−0.174) and under the paper-described rather than script-expanded human exclusions (−0.158). A consensus predictor that used other readers on the exact same item reached 85.7% accuracy. ## A different cohort read the later contradiction

For each original item and order, the timing test subtracted the explicit-reference condition from the ambiguous-reference condition, then contrasted the two possible contradictory continuations. The primary endpoint was the first region after the contradiction, following the original report. Among 135 participants contributing 3,777 eligible presentations, the source pointer's prediction correlated 0.258 [−0.082, 0.449] with the pace-adjusted contrast. The estimated interaction in raw milliseconds aligned to its prediction was +33.3 [−28.5, 97.2]. A positive control calculated from independent people's physical plausibility choices gave a correlation of 0.523 [0.116, 0.631]. [Regional timing analysis](../results/reading-validation-transition-jones/later-reading.json) · [Published experimental design and data](https://osf.io/v8rjm/)

Leaving both orders of each physical family out, the model with native semantic belief reduced mean squared error in the log-time contrast from 0.03487 to 0.03402 relative to a control with native region surprisal, lexical repetition, order and syntactic norms. The paired difference was **−0.00084 [−0.00655, +0.00458]**. Independent halves of the 135-reader cohort showed a median item-order timing correlation of only 0.108 at that region across 5,000 splits (2.5th–97.5th split percentiles −0.089 to 0.316). Directional improvement in individual-choice forecasts therefore supplies stronger evidence than locating a human timing spike on a particular example. The publication and its released processing scripts retain different numbers of trials. The latter reproduce the authors' rendered analysis outputs, with 37 readers and 1,012 main choices in Experiment 1, and 135 readers and 3,777 trials in Experiment 2. Applying only the paper-described exclusions gives 41/1,079 and 141/3,947. Both versions are preserved. Intervals for model effects resample readers and whole physical families together. The item-wise timing error intervals are conditional on fitted training folds, and the separate reliability split quantiles are descriptive. Participants made explicit choices after the complete event in Experiment 1. These observations cannot supply a personal word-by-word cognitive-effort curve.
