# A new human test of sentence-to-context coherence

The current preview's transition scores barely distinguished two-sentence preambles that made the *same* next sentence more or less coherent. A compact readout of the same model's surprisal, contextual similarity and attention did not recover the size of the human difference. This was the project's first model evaluation on Carter and Hoffman's materials. The study's published aggregate results informed its selection. The new readout was fitted on 96 article families within the study and evaluated on 64 different families. Each family provided four versions, crossing contextual coherence with word predictability. ## How much the context changed human judgments

Readers in the norming study rated each complete passage on a 1–5 scale. In the 64 held-out families they rated low-coherence contexts 1.372 points lower than their high-coherence counterparts (95% interval −1.540 to −1.198). The checkpoint's transition channel, calibrated on the training items, predicted a −0.0002-point difference. The integration readout predicted −0.036 (−0.079 to +0.007). The absolute agreement intervals in this report use 10,000 paired family bootstrap draws and condition on the participating readers. The study selected stimuli with a clear coherence contrast during norming. Zero is the best RMSE. There were 128 paired comparisons, two per family. The integration-minus-lexical RMSE difference was −0.0004 points (−0.0170 to +0.0168). The corresponding display-channel comparison was −0.0037 (−0.0251 to +0.0173). The prespecified improvement criterion was unmet. The integration readout assigned a score below the fixed midpoint of 3 to **45 of 128** experimental low-coherence passages and **35 of 128** high-coherence passages. Against the actual norming means, it flagged 36 of 134 passages whose human score was at least 3 and missed 78 of 122 whose score was below 3. ## Online reading

The independent self-paced reading task recorded a latency for each revealed word after its context. The held-out test covered 5,293 reader–passage trials and 43,401 timed words before the manipulated critical word. Among the 84 readers in the original study's filtered file, the held-out low-minus-high difference was +0.0146 natural log milliseconds per word (−0.0025 to +0.0314). The integration readout estimated +0.0013 (−0.0010 to +0.0036). Its RMSE was 0.0696 log milliseconds, compared with 0.0721 for length and lexical controls. The difference of −0.0026 (−0.0044 to −0.0007) favors the integration readout on this auxiliary endpoint. Against the calibrated display channel, the RMSE difference was −0.0011 (−0.0040 to +0.0018). Norming ratings and reading times answer different questions. Nothing in these responses assigns subjective confusion to a specific token.

We aligned every recorded word string with the published text and certified the original precritical region in both word-predictability conditions. One calibration item lacked timing outcomes for condition c. Its full quartet stayed in the norming endpoint and was omitted in timing calibration. The 64 holdout item families were complete for both endpoints. One L4 backbone pass per passage produced the nine readout features at each target prefix. Fitting required only a convex ridge solve for each observation and uses calibration features alone for centering, normalization and coefficients. A local rerun reproduced the scores. After the primary comparison we checked the reproducibility of the norming judgments themselves. The 256 held-out passages each have 13–17 raw ratings, totaling 3,776. Splitting the ratings within each passage into two nonoverlapping halves 1,000 times gave a median between-half correlation of 0.777 and a median between-half RMSE of 0.613 points. The halves contain different numbers of ratings from the full comparison, and their correlation does not specify a ceiling for latent single-reader accuracy.
