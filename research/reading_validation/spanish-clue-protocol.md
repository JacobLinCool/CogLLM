# Does incoming context resolve the meaning of the same unknown word?

## Design

The independent Spanish experiment presented three seven-word sentences sequentially. Sentence one introduced a pseudoword. An informative or unrelated second sentence followed. The fourth word of the third sentence named its superordinate category. A real-word control changed the first sentence. At the end of each block, participants chose a meaning for the pseudoword from four alternatives. The published report also describes physiological condition comparisons. Public aggregate results were known at registration. No corpus-specific model parameters or participant responses have been inspected for this study. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.117159) · [Authors’ materials and data](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/AVY4W)

Recover the actual original Spanish words, sentence order, target string and response alternatives from the authors' E-Prime log design fields; retain their inclusion list for the human comparison. Match complete exposure and choice records from the same block by literal pseudoword, allowing normalization of case, accents and punctuation only for matching and preserving displayed text. Report ambiguous and unmatched trials; verify the authors' answer key against independently released pseudoword meanings. Use high/low pairs with identical first and third sentences and different second sentences. Keep differing token lengths and use exact original character-to-token coordinates. Score explicitly how many families also have equal native second-sentence length. Real words are processed separately. The realized trial-level outputs, including actual keypresses, will be read only after input, model and scoring directions are frozen. Anonymous outcomes remain on the host.

## One forward and three endpoints

1. **Article-only next-word expectation.** At the originally shown superordinate noun in sentence three, compute its actual whole-word log probability from causal logits and its first-piece probability relative to the corpus's other superordinate onsets. Compare the prespecified informative-minus-uninformative contrast for the same unknown word and identical category sentence; test sign across lexical families. Also test *specificity* by subtracting the average change for other plausible category onsets. Collapse duplicate onset token IDs. Show changes at matched positions before intervention and at the seven word boundaries of sentence two. Identical raw prefixes must produce identical values within the verified numerical tolerance. Sentence-level measurements give no exact human token of insight.
2. **Unprompted stock and events.** Run the frozen released article readout on those same raw positions, using a single encoding of the original article. Summarize stock and resolution at the last native token of each of the 21 original words. Contrast stock at first sentence, end of second sentence, immediately before category, and article end. The prediction of lower post-clue unresolved stock under informative continuation is a falsifiable conjecture. Use actual mixed success if a next-word effect does not appear in stock. The study's null slow-voltage context effect remains part of the interpretation.
3. **External human semantic choice.** After the complete raw text, append the identical Spanish readout template `En este texto, «{written pseudoword}» significa` in the same single causal pass. The first next-token logits of the four printed alternatives create a forced-choice distribution without generating a response or conditioning the article on the question. Test predictions for all recovered people and items. Report four-way human-choice NLL versus uniform, relative candidate position, a training-only lexical-frequency preference, and a condition-only correct-answer rate (which knows the experimental answer key). Compare the model distribution at the language-model scale and after a single temperature learned without test targets or readers. Report chance, argmax accuracy to the official key, and association with actual human success within each context condition. This specified-word task supplies the locus and test alternatives. It differs from an article-only load curve. Words sharing their first native token receive equal score; report frequency and a strict distinguishable-onset sensitivity.

## Inference

Use the original correct-item family and reader group for independent uncertainty summaries. Train any calibration parameters in crossed five-fold word-by-reader splits, excluding each evaluated target and reader from that fit. Average one estimate per original pseudoword for the High/Low condition contrasts; bootstrap original words, while human predictive NLL comparisons additionally bootstrap readers. Use Holm control for the two directional primary tests (category surprisal and actual-choice NLL against the learned lexical-frequency baseline). Preserve stronger offline oracle comparisons, missingness, per-family forecasts, convergence, and negative results. Raw human ERP recordings are unnecessary for these endpoints; the original paper's group results are cited as physiology and will not be represented as a new EEG analysis.
