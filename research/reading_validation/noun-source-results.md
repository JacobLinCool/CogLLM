# What can be reconstructed from the English noun-phrase study?

The 2016 study measured two outcomes on each experimental item. A respondent rated sentence difficulty from 1 (very easy) to 5 (very difficult) and completed an adjusted cloze task by choosing one of five possible orderings of four hidden nouns. The 360 released rows cross three phrase lengths, refactoring of the phrase, and replacement of two words with pseudowords. Each length stratum has 30 families and all four variants per family. The investigators scheduled 35 assessments per item, then excluded 19 workers, reporting 11,949 retained assessments from 334 workers. The four split-by-pseudoword conditions ran at different sessions at least a week apart. Workers could return for multiple sessions. The public file contains condition means for individual sentences, without participant-level responses. [Original methods](https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2015.1131775) · [Workbook, documentation and CC BY-NC 4.0 license](https://doi.org/10.25422/azu.data.21357498.v1)

All 360 released full sentences have the same sequence of case-normalized alphanumeric words as the workbook's cloze prompt restored with its indicated correct answer. This includes unexpected word substitutions. For example, a row designating a phrase about an infectious disease contains a phrase about liver function blood tests in the released sentence about outbreaks. In another row the restored nonsplit text contains the unusual sequence “grip tests conduction nerve”. The agreement between fields means that restoring the blanked prompt would reproduce these cases. It does not establish which text was presented during rating. The primary study uses the released completed text exactly. [Source restoration check](../results/reading-validation-noun/source-restoration.json)

We separately required the indicated split and original phrase to appear uniquely in their respective sentences, with identical normalized text around the target. Of 180 split comparisons, 143 passed, 28 lacked a unique match, and nine differed outside the indicated phrase. Determiner changes, repeated targets, typos and unrelated substitutions can all affect this strict check. Both comparisons passed in 71 of 90 families. The sensitivity analysis retains these families with their original held-out forecasts. No exact or twelve-consecutive-word material match appeared in the training inputs for the frozen lexical and educational heads or in the previously audited historical training and development packet. The stronger five-gram check also found no match to the heads actually used. [All affected family IDs and source exposure](../results/reading-validation-noun/material-audit.json) · [Material-sensitivity criteria](noun-material-sensitivity.md)

The equal-family mean cloze errors are 9.36% for unsplit text and 12.07% for split text. The paper reports 8.5% and 10.9%, respectively. To test whether weighting by trial count could explain the difference, we enumerated every cell size from one to 35 compatible with both observed means, the ordinal or binary response ranges, and the published total of 11,949. This identified 293 cell sizes uniquely and left 67 ambiguous. Dynamic programming gives bounds of 9.015–9.898% for the unsplit condition and 11.701–12.715% for the split condition across all compatible assignments. An independent mixed-integer solver constructed a compatible assignment of all 360 cell counts for each bound. The unsplit bounds are attained by 540 errors in 5,990 trials and 580 in 5,860, and the split bounds by 690 in 5,897 and 732 in 5,757. Every assignment sums to the documented study total. Trial weighting alone cannot reproduce the two published levels under these assumptions. The direction of change agrees, and the analysis does not identify the source of the discrepancy. A feasible sample size of one for some cells also leaves the prespecified unbiased binomial noise calculation undefined for the full collection. Equal family weighting gives a split-induced change of −0.052 [−0.094, −0.011] points in perceived difficulty and +2.72 [+0.74, +5.03] percentage points in cloze errors. These 95% intervals resample families within phrase-length strata. On the 71 strictly matched families the estimates are −0.043 [−0.093, +0.005] and +2.57 [+0.47, +5.11] respectively. The global and restricted results agree on the sign, with weaker precision for perceived difficulty in the restricted subset. Reader identity, reading time and within-person changes cannot be recovered from the public rows. [Registered family-level weighting and intervals](noun-protocol.md)
