# Does agreement with the meaning of readers’ alternative words explain the N400?

Freeze a single population word-distribution experiment in English with previously verified public UCL responses and an independent Provo test. At each prefix estimate the distribution of 8,192 known next words and an explicit other category. On the next printed word, measure its semantic dissimilarity to expected alternative words in Qwen's candidate embedding space. Archive the normalized static vector for each observed spelling, measured independently of its expectedness. For comparison the native Qwen first-token distribution goes through exactly the same candidate geometry. All source, Provo and 160 multi-site material presentations are encoded once before scoring. The previously reported group-level article–noun dissociation is known. No original multi-site voltage values or candidate performance on these measures have been opened. Release the independent voltage outcome for all models only after this physiological readout and the three separate backbone comparisons are sealed. [Replication materials](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.33468) · [Earlier semantic variability work](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2025.104653) · [Incremental alternative sampling](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2025.104715)

Map each complete lexical form to the mean of its native piece embeddings, use the first sixteen standardized dictionary principal coordinates and normalize that vector. Given an article-only preceding distribution q, exclude the other bin and the current lexeme itself. Let m be the remaining probability-weighted mean unit vector and let v be the incoming form's unit vector. The scalar 1−vᵀm, where the remaining weights sum to one, estimates dissimilarity to distributed alternatives independently of probability on the target itself. It can represent a close synonym even when no reader supplied the literal printed target. The incoming word is observed at the last token of its true printed span. A zero vector or insufficient alternative mass is an explicit availability failure. A semantic score produced by Qwen's coordinates and human completion data remains an operational measure of meaning.

Use identical source ridge penalties (precision 1/0.35² on centered standardized predictors) across nested EEG readouts. The calibrated baseline contains five printed surface cues and natural whole-word native surprisal, the current word's 16 fixed semantic coordinates, human-modeled disagreement, its modeled lexical surprise, and whether its form belongs to the response alphabet. Fitted English word EEG negativity is the outcome. The raw semantic comparison adds the conditional-other semantic dissimilarity using the original native word distribution. The population semantic comparison also adds the dissimilarity using the full UCL-trained human response predictor. Word distributions for held English EEG predictions use a cloze model trained without those held sentences. For training the EEG readout, compute its inputs with that fold’s cloze predictor. All test sentences are disjoint from both fits. For the external evaluation only the final, all-UCL cloze and EEG readouts are used. Report English held N400 correlation and mean squared error changes against both comparators. Score all 80 original nine-laboratory item pairs at the true article and noun. Report group contrast and whether larger measured item differences are tracked across readers and materials; a group advantage without family discrimination supports only average localization. The other category cannot provide lexical probabilities for individual outside words; use the available exact whole-word surprisal, other-category probability and a separate coverage indicator there. Archive every endpoint and source fit before opening the measured multi-site voltage.
