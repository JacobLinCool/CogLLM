# Calibrating shared and study-specific models of human reading

On two globally separated sets of reading materials, a calibration rule that resamples material families assigned higher mean probability density to the observed human responses than either equal sharing or a target-specific Beta penalty. The gains were small. The corrected third split improved by **0.00525 nats per observed response unit over equal sharing**. The previously ungraded fourth split improved by **0.00233 nats**. In both cases, simultaneous intervals that account for linked sources and material sampling included zero. This establishes a reproducible estimator and a repeated direction of effect. The magnitude of benefit on further reading studies remains uncertain. ## The calibration rule

Let q₀ and q₁ denote the source-private and shared predictive densities for one registered measurement and let a denote how strongly it draws on the shared expert. Their normalized geometric mixture is

```text
qₐ(y | x) = exp((1 − a) log q₀(y | x) + a log q₁(y | x) − Aₓ(a)).
```

For each calibration family f, form C_f(a), its mean negative log likelihood over its actual recorded response units. For a family with a categorical count table, this uses every observed count. For grouped Gaussian measurements, it uses the count, mean and within-group variance. For correlated pairs, it preserves covariance. Each material family supplies one curve, regardless of how many times that item happened to be observed. [Exact likelihoods and grouping](borrowing_score.py)

Consider the change in family loss after subtracting its tangent at equal mixing.

```text
K_f(a) = C_f(a) − C_f(½) − (a − ½) C′_f(½).
```

The outcome-dependent term of the geometric pool's log density is affine in a. Subtracting its tangent leaves the normalizer. By the exponential-family identity, K_f is the family's mean KL divergence from q½ to qₐ. This permits a neutral penalty in the units of the actual disagreement between the fitted experts. If they predict the same thing for a material, moving the weight costs zero there. For known-error measurements, the reported sampling variance defines the observation channel. We checked this identity independently against closed-form univariate and bivariate Gaussian KL and exact finite-category sums for all ten likelihoods. Across checked third-split calibration rows the largest absolute difference was 4.8 × 10⁻¹¹ nats per observation. [Analytic checks by channel](../results/reading-validation-family-bootstrap/third/audit.json)

Add one neutral pseudo-family whose loss at a is the mean K_f(a) over observed calibration families. Draw Dirichlet(1,…,1) weights on the actual families and this one neutral family. Minimize each weighted sum of the original loss curves on [0,1], then use the mean minimizing a in the predictive density. We used 2,048 pairs of antithetic draws per endpoint with a fixed seed. The midpoint applies when no target calibration family exists. The bootstrap is used to estimate the coefficient. Separate material and source resampling quantifies uncertainty in held performance. ## Corrected comparison and confirmation

The main comparisons use globally separated parent articles and the known reader and material links among sources. RACE and OneStopQA share 970 participant identifiers. OneStopQA and OneStop rereading share parent articles. We therefore treat these three source datasets as a connected group, average represented sources within it, then give each connected group equal weight. Within a held resample, overlapping OneStop articles receive the same resampling weight. We resample 20,000 paired combinations of source group and held materials and keep both fitted seeds together. Registered simultaneous intervals account for the two comparator choices. Reader-level uncertainty remains conditional where the released archive omits individual identifiers. ## What changed when parent articles and readers were linked?

The originally registered third split separated exact experimental page strings and families *within* each source. An audit found 20 parent news articles used by both OneStop studies. Article 18 entered fitting in one source and evaluation in the other. Article 11 entered coefficient calibration in one source and evaluation in the other. Article 5 was held in both, producing 1,073 source–family combinations from 1,072 distinct parent family identifiers. The correction kept every original held outcome and quarantined 365 fitting, 86 tuning and 104 calibration rows that had a more protected appearance elsewhere. Both expert models were independently reinitialized for both seeds. The global audit now verifies that every active registered parent family has exactly one role. This comparison corrects already-scored material. The fourth split separately tests the unchanged estimator on parents whose every occurrence had been confined to expert fitting in all three preceding partitions. Older fitted models had seen those materials. The new four models were trained without them. Neither split recruits new readers. ## Optimization and interpretation

On the globally corrected third split the selected shared checkpoints occurred at epochs 212 and 229 and the private checkpoints at 186 and 191. On the fourth split the corresponding selections were 193 and 188 for shared weights and 199 and 177 for private weights. All eight runs stopped on the held tuning plateau, with the selected checkpoint restored. Training these four small expert models took a total of 115 seconds on the third split and 100 seconds on the fourth, excluding feature extraction and calibration. The midpoint-tangent check and independent likelihood replay did not find a numerical discrepancy that could explain the effect. The released forecasts retain the random seeds and posterior Monte Carlo diagnostics for every calibrated endpoint. [Third-split optimization, seed 1729](../results/reading-validation-family-bootstrap/global/seed-1729/fit.json) · [Seed 2718](../results/reading-validation-family-bootstrap/global/seed-2718/fit.json) · [Fourth-split coefficients](../results/reading-validation-family-bootstrap/confirmation/forecast-freeze.json)

These experiments support using each human measurement's own likelihood while learning how much to share across sources. They separate optimization success from uncertainty over new material. The test grades predicted votes, choices, reaction summaries and other recorded responses at their observed positions. It supplies no direct human label for a continuous, universal token-by-token burden curve and no identification of a reader's unobserved doubts. Those claims need their own time-resolved, content-specific observations.
