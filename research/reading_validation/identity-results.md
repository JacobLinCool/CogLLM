# Reading evidence for a previously identified question

A single forward pass can help match an arriving passage to one of two questions a reader has already raised. The test represents each question as a separate candidate. The same passage can supply evidence for either question. Human annotations indicate which passages contain relevant evidence. Approaching certainty at the end of a paragraph and correctly localizing a person's moment of understanding are distinct measurements in these datasets. [Scientific QA study](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.365/) · [Multi-sentence QA study](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1023/)

## Does the semantic readout outperform word matching?

Each number below is the percentage of documents where the two questions both outranked their rival at their own human-marked evidence passage. Ties receive partial credit. Differences use paired resampling at document level. QASPER's training-fold predictions use different model weights per fold. Its development and test predictions and all MultiRC predictions use one model trained on the QASPER, IIRC and ExpMRC training partitions. The lexical control uses the same training examples, objective and optimizer. [Prespecified analysis](identity-protocol.md) · [Transfer protocol](identity-transfer-protocol.md)

The trained semantic readout beat the lexical control by 12.0 percentage points in the out-of-fold analysis (95% paired interval 3.7 to 20.7). The difference was −18.2 points on the official QASPER test (−45.5 to 0.0). Only eleven eligible new test papers remained after the exclusion of papers used previously. On MultiRC the advantage over a conventional BM25 retriever was 9.2 points (5.0 to 13.5), and the advantage over the trained lexical control was 0.1 points (−4.0 to 4.1). We found no shared sequence of twelve normalized words between any of these MultiRC passages and the QASPER training articles screened by the protocol. Both registered tests required superiority to the trained lexical model and the released readout. Neither met that condition. The QASPER test interval loss decreased from 1.570 to 1.175 nats per question relative to the lexical control, a paired difference of −0.395 (−0.558 to −0.220). On MultiRC the corresponding difference was −0.023 (−0.126 to 0.082). Many combinations of within-paragraph token hazards can yield the same interval probability. This loss cannot identify the particular token at which an individual understood the answer. ## Can the same readout locate an explanatory cue in prose?

The retrospective matched early/late cue test was stricter. The new readout found the correct timing on both branches in one of twelve families, compared with six for the released model and eleven for the lexical control. In the eight two-issue families, both the new and released models obtained zero complete successes. Across sixteen branches, the new readout had positive sham-adjusted selectivity on eight, reached the decisive target-over-sham threshold on one, crossed the differential threshold inside the annotated window on one, and kept off-target change below threshold on fifteen. These are author-constructed cues with known textual boundaries. No human subject was observed reading these particular passages. The design required joint identity, timing, minimum advantage and low off-target responses on both branches. [Original scoring rules and test](../results/reading-validation-identity/paper-scores.json)

The single-pass features involved 198,194 tokens in 518 passages on a Colab L4. Two additional technical forwards tested one prefix against its extension. The maximum absolute feature difference was 0.00000275. All twelve semantic and lexical heads across the main fit and five folds converged numerically. Finite differences on a separate simulation agreed with the analytical objective gradient to below 0.000000001. These checks validate the computations independently of the data's ability to identify human mental states.
