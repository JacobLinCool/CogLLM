# When does changing phrase structure help readers?

Leroy, Kauchak and Hogue split English compound noun phrases in health information and separately replaced two nouns with pseudowords. Readers rated the difficulty of a completed sentence. They also performed a modified cloze task selecting the correct permutation of four masked nouns. The source reports that splitting improved perceived ease overall but increased cloze errors. Its data release contains 360 cell means across 90 sentence families and 11,949 retained assessments from 334 workers. Analysts know the published aggregate effects. The held-family item-level predictions and their relationship to the released means have yet to be calculated. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1080/10810730.2015.1131775) · [Data and license](https://doi.org/10.25422/azu.data.21357498.v1)

Encode each unique completed sentence with Qwen3.5-2B once. The deployed sentence predictor must work on ordinary prose, without an answer, experimental labels or masked target. Keep the two human endpoints separate throughout. For positive orientation use greater felt difficulty and greater cloze error (one minus the source mean correctness). Preserve the four split-by-pseudoword conditions and the two-, three- and four-word phrase strata. Verify source ordering by head noun, target phrase and unchanged surrounding words. Hold each family and all variants together. Group identical sentence stimuli if they cross a proposed split. Hash the material-only inputs before analysis.

First apply unchanged the ReadMe++ educational difficulty score, the matched surface-only head, and mean frozen contextual lexical difficulty. For each endpoint, report the within-family concordance on splitting, with and without pseudowords, and on pseudoword substitution at fixed syntax. Give half credit for ties in model output and exclude ties in the human outcome from ranks. Weight the two levels of the other factor equally within each family when informative and each family equally. Report signed mean human and model changes with their native units. A zero-shot educational scale has no conversion to cloze percentage points.

Next estimate the shared human response to words and structure using both measured endpoints from the training families. Use five fixed family folds balanced by phrase length. Compare a training-mean prediction, a diagnostic model with the experimental condition design, a plain text regression using length and lexical frequency features, and a text regression which adds the frozen source-trained grade, contextual lexical scores and the existing 24-dimensional source-trained sentence projection. All text regressions use identical ridge regularization after training-only normalization and predict each response in original units. Estimate the relationship on the within-family change as well as the level by adding centered within-family examples with the same total weight as the original texts. At inference, each forecast uses the completed sentence alone. All contrasts and confidence intervals use strictly out-of-family predictions. A separate arm adds the known experimental conditions to both text methods to test contextual knowledge beyond the published intervention.

The primary comparison is out-of-family squared error for perceived difficulty versus text-only surface training. Require a lower paired 95% confidence bound above zero for an improvement claim. Secondary outcomes are cloze error, split-contrast concordance, the pseudoword contrast, the sign of each corpus effect, and the two effects within each phrase-length stratum. Compare models on all these quantities even when the lexical baseline wins. Use 10,000 family bootstrap replicates within the three length strata, seed 726031. The released means cannot support crossed person-level intervals. Cell means weight sentences equally, whereas the original publication reports trial-weighted means. None of these ratings determine the exact word a reader noticed.
