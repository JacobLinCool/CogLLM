# Can an editor tell whether a researcher wants a definition or an example?

Guo et al. asked eleven researchers whether each designated scientific term warranted a definition, background or an example. These were elicited information needs. A reported need does not demonstrate that a reader clicked a help control or that a particular intervention improved understanding. The original study modeled the three responses as separate binary labels. In CogLLM the previous Guo experiments used familiarity, definitions and background. No CogLLM model has been fitted to the example response. This study asks which of two terms, equally familiar to the same reader before the same abstract, was singled out for a definition while the other was singled out for an example. The respondent may also have requested background. Terms with both or neither need are excluded from this primary comparison. We include single-response definition and example rankings as secondary endpoints. [Original study, including the authors' F1 benchmarks in Table 5](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.255/) · [Pinned data](https://github.com/talaugust/PersonalizedJargon/tree/22add4cc00274de199cc74bf0dbd629d05151b08/data)

Before registering the test, we inspected the response-column names and counted categories and eligibility without producing model-linked outcome scores. Across the 6,391 records for 581 unique, grid-aligned terms, the complete release supports 156 unequal term pairs for the exclusive type preference across 45 papers at a common initial familiarity. The analogous counts are 973 unequal pairs for definition choice and 1,132 for example choice. Material from this study entered prior experiments on other responses. We reserve all papers whose SHA-256 hash of the literal string `guo-help-reserve-v1/` plus the original paper identifier (including the `jargon/` prefix) is divisible by five. No responses from that reserve may enter fitting, tuning, forecast scoring, or final plots for this study. The earlier project has seen other response types from these papers. This is a reserved outcome on familiar materials; there is no independent second cohort with the same design in this protocol.

For the remaining papers, make one prediction for each eligible researcher, term and article with both the reader and the article fold excluded from training. Five article folds derive from the SHA-256 hash of the prefix `guo-help-fit-v1/` plus the paper key modulo five. A model sees only the original abstract, the source character span of a candidate term and, for the personalized version, that reader's term-specific prereading rating. Never use postreading ratings, other readers' choices for the test abstract, a participant ID, citations, or paper identity as predictors. The human task supplied a term inventory, so the primary endpoint conditions on a candidate being present. It does not establish open-vocabulary retrieval of every concept the reader might want to look up.

The lexical comparison includes the already frozen CompLex score, a word form and frequency score, word count and length of the term, and relative position in the abstract. A prior-aware comparator adds one centered ordinal prereading score and its interactions with those features. The extended model adds four fixed article features: the PLABA-trained phrase score, the frozen DEFT semantic term-proposal score at the mention, its strongest later definition-link probability for this term, and the matched DEFT lexical link score. The dependency of the latter two features on later prose is permitted only for retrospective editorial review of a completed abstract. The DEFT and PLABA weights are frozen and previously trained on separate materials. The earlier Guo example-response labels played no role in them.

As a feature-specific control, add the DEFT lexical link alone to the lexical-plus-prior comparator. Report its paired difference against the extended model to distinguish contextual features from lexical features learned on an additional text collection. Separately report the fixed, unadjusted CompLex difference, using greater lexical difficulty to predict the need for a definition, an example, or a definition rather than an example. Its orientation is fixed before fitting the new target.

Fit three separate conditional logistic ranking heads with the same protocol, one for the exclusive preference and one for each binary choice. Train on every unequal pair within a researcher and abstract, including pairs with different prereading ratings. Use pair orientation positive minus negative, give each observed researcher–article cell equal total weight within each article and each article equal total weight within training, and use training-only feature scales. Minimize mean ranked logistic loss plus 0.05 times the squared coefficient norm divided by two. Use no validation-driven parameter selection. At each test compare half-credit pair concordance within the same initial familiarity category. Average over each reader–article cell, then researchers within each article and finally articles. Publish the paired 95% intervals from 10,000 crossed reader/article bootstrap draws. Preserve independent scores for lexical-only, lexical-plus-prior, extended article-only, and extended-plus-prior. To claim an incremental primary gain, the extended-plus-prior lower bound must exceed zero against the lexical-plus-prior comparator and its own lower accuracy bound must exceed 0.5. Report the secondary binary outcomes whichever way their results go, along with sample sizes and any excluded ambiguous records. Report a secondary same-reader, same-abstract comparison without equalizing prior. The original authors' binary F1 estimates use a different target and cannot be substituted for these paired controls.

### Serving a new reader or a new study

A conditional ranking score has an arbitrary offset. With each fitted slope and standardization fixed, fit a single population intercept on the training observations alone to match their weighted binary prevalence. Weight each article equally, each observed reader within its article equally, and eligible terms within a reader–article equally. The same rule applies within each held-out reader and article fold. A previously unseen reader receives this population intercept. Use a separate text-only fit if a pre-reading rating is unavailable. For future evaluation freeze the complete set of coefficients, centers, scales, intercepts and feature definitions on all permitted non-reserve Guo papers. Neither the reserved outcomes nor an external cohort may calibrate these parameters. Offer log-odds and probabilities for absolute comparisons and preserve log-odds differences for relative comparisons. External outcomes such as actual help-seeking can have a different prevalence from the survey’s declared information needs.
