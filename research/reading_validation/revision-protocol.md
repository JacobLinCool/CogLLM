# Predicting which revision is simpler and which preserves the source

A writer comparing two revisions needs to distinguish reduced difficulty from changes in the information conveyed. In the earlier ASSET analysis, the absolute ReadMe++ head selected the human-preferred simpler variant less frequently than choosing the shorter variant. The present experiment uses ASSET as development data and trains on explicit judgments about revisions. The new target is the independent SimpDA 2022 direct-assessment panel. The primary comparison and analysis below were written after inspecting the ASSET results and before reading any SimpDA response values. Primary data permit the following observations. Eleven anonymous raters assigned three separate scores to rewritten passages. The release contains three judgments per candidate and six candidates for each of 60 source IDs. Several source strings have superficial variation within an ID. Preserve the precise text each rater was shown and group by source ID. ## Frozen information and endpoints

* The unit is an original English passage and one complete revised candidate. Each unique passage receives one Qwen3.5-2B forward. Share the cached original across alternative drafts. Encoder inputs contain only the visible passage. Compare their native token patterns, terminal states and independently computed lexical or CEFR readouts after the two passages have been encoded. This is one pass for a draft when the source has been cached. Processing an uncached original plus one draft requires two independent encodings.
* Predict distinct preferences for simplicity, preservation of meaning, and fluency. The primary end point is the relative human simplicity of two candidates for the same source. Compute the mean across the three direct-assessment votes per version, exclude equal human means, award half-credit to a model tie, and macro-average first within one source and then across source IDs. Report meaning and fluency separately. Also report per-worker directional ordering where that worker rated both variants, per-source and pooled rank correlation, coverage, and the within-panel reproducibility of preferences. Resample source IDs for 95% intervals and use a subject-and-source bootstrap as a sensitivity check, reporting draws with missing comparisons. Include both comparison baselines regardless of statistical significance. No positive conclusions about one endpoint are inferred from the others.
* Use ASSET direct pair votes and ratings, the original Simplicity-DA/WikiDA human simplicity, meaning and fluency scores, and SimpEval-past overall quality ratings in training. The latter rates a distinct composite response and may only enter through a separately parameterized auxiliary task. In holding out development source groups, match normalized original strings and near-identical originals across training studies before assigning folds. Drop complete test source IDs if any original or variant exactly matches, or has at least two distinct shared five-word shingles covering 80% or more of the shorter passage against any training text. Include the known earlier ReadMe++ and auxiliary human training materials in the external overlap check. Native model pretraining exposure remains unknown. [ASSET study](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.424/) · [WikiDA release](https://github.com/feralvam/metaeval-simplification/tree/7703c461858cb2557ff1d8ce0a20c63d8a3d280f) · [SimpEval-past](https://github.com/Yao-Dou/LENS/blob/8fa5b149f099f0c75563ee8344a2411b0bffbc37/data/simpeval_past.csv)
* Before opening test scores lock predictions for every eligible candidate and every comparator. Baselines include shorter length, exact lexical overlap/retained content, the absolute ReadMe++ head used on ASSET, and a supervised edit model trained on the same development observations. That edit baseline includes length ratio, lexical overlap, protected strings such as numbers, content-word loss, and splits, additions and reordering. The full model adds source-to-rewrite contextual token alignment in both directions, frozen terminal changes, and lexical difficulty among preserved, inserted and removed words. Fit with separate supervised objectives for graded judgments and actual paired votes and regularize to prevent unstable high-dimensional fits. All scaling, calibration and model selection use development families alone. Compare the chosen model to both the strong supervised edit model and the shorter-text heuristic. Retain diagnostic forecasts for each contribution and report the tuning rule and selected weights.
* Store numerical predictions and hashes without redistributing passage text or worker IDs. Never interpret a revision preference as an online measurement of difficulty at a particular word. Results on the development corpora have a separate role from the single fixed transfer to SimpDA 2022.
