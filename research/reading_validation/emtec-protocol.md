# Can a single reading trace distinguish how difficult a passage feels and where people reread?

EMTeC is an external English reading experiment with 107 native readers. Each reader saw up to 42 short machine-written passages. The experiment balanced 14 generator and decoding conditions across 42 topic and task prompts using a Latin square. Each person saw one variant per prompt. Readers saw a short introductory header, the passage, an attention question, and finally rated perceived difficulty and engagement from 1 to 5. During the passage the study recorded word-level rereading. We ask whether the same causal model signal improves prediction of two distinct human measurements: how a reader rated the passage and which word they revisited. The design supplies no observation of the instant of semantic confusion. The single attention question was not designed to measure comprehension. No demographic or question fields enter a predictor. [Study design, rating questions and corrected word measures](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02677-4) · [Authors' software and data](https://github.com/DiLi-Lab/EMTeC/tree/83c64a2020f5127598138143317fde993e41bf6f)

**External materials.** Use the final released texts in `gen_seq_trunc` and the exact `introductory_screen_item` that preceded each passage. Both fields agree between the GitHub input table and the authors' OSF final stimuli for every one of the 588 item–list combinations. Every word in the header and passage agrees with the experimental log for all 4,474 visits. The log sometimes escaped line breaks or omitted punctuation. Model input is the final published header, two newline characters representing the change of screen, and the full original body. Word offsets and scores refer to the body only. A twelve-word phrase in seven variants overlapped earlier supervised development material. Exclude that entire prompt and all its variants before inference. This leaves 518 distinct bodies from 41 topics. Check that every whitespace-delimited area of interest terminates at an unchanged tokenizer prefix. Compare the corresponding released word ID and spelling in the corrected rereading file; retain the full audit of any mismatch. Model pretraining exposure is unknown. [Exact stimulus and overlap audit](../results/reading-validation-emtec/material.json)

**Model signal.** Freeze the original selected Qwen3.5-2B and its calibrated four-output readout. Extract one native forward for each unique header–body sequence. For each measured word take remaining unresolved mass at its last token. Define its increase as the positive part of the change since the preceding word end, using the last token of the header as the reference for the first word. For perceived difficulty the prespecified curve feature is mean unresolved mass across body words; retain 90th percentile, the mean positive increase, opening, resolution and transition values as descriptive diagnostics. For a reread the primary expanded model adds the local stock and its positive change at that word. Also calculate the Qwen negative log probability of the complete word, and the English PLABA passage/phrase readouts from these same token states. These are distinct source-trained estimates. Retain analytical references for the fixed curve alone, source PLABA scores alone, native surprisal and conventional formulae. No test-set head selection is performed.

**Comparisons with strong controls.** For passage ratings, a baseline receives log number of body words, mean word length, mean English Zipf frequency, mean words per sentence, approximate position of the average word end in the native sequence, mean word surprisal and 90th-percentile word surprisal. The stronger control adds the frozen PLABA mean sentence ease score and its coverage indicator. Evaluate an identical model that additionally receives one scalar, the normalized-by-word-count sum of unresolved stock. Within each source fitting fold, center all predictors and fit a shared-slope proportional-odds readout by the mean of four binary cross-entropies across the four ordered cut points, with unpenalized intercepts and a ridge of 0.01 on the standardized mean loss. Score the held reader's observed rating using ranked probability score averaged over the four thresholds. Raw numerical responses and known source scale anchors should always be reported. If original response-pole instructions cannot independently establish whether 5 is harder than 1, the signed direction of numerical response remains uninterpreted; the proper-score comparison remains valid under reversal of the scale. Report raw partial association with the numeric rating after removing respondent and prompt means, with this sign qualification when applicable.

For the binary corrected reread outcome (RR), predict its conditional probability at each original word position. The baseline uses current and previous word lengths, English Zipf frequencies and Qwen whole-word surprisals, native token index, squared and log native index, proportional position in passage, final-in-line marker from the authors, and a log body length. Add the frozen CompLex score as an additional lexical reference. The expanded model receives two more numbers from the selected curve, word-end stock and positive stock change. Fit a ridge-binomial model on the original measurement counts with the same 0.01 penalty and source-only standardization; pool identical word predictors and reader-fold counts before fitting. All methods retain the same scorable locations. The primary score is Bernoulli log loss averaged over words in a visit, then over visits. Report the direction of the stock coefficient conditional on the controls and the mean within-visit ability to rank reread versus non-reread words. This measures an observed eye return, which can also serve motivations besides difficulty. The post-reading rating provides the separate direct observation. Do not train on gaze to score the passage outcome or use the eventual rating to score word outcomes.

**Splits and inference.** Form four balanced folds of prompts and four balanced folds of readers by separately hashing their canonical identifiers. In each of sixteen crossed tests, train on observations with neither the held prompt nor the held reader fold. Test their intersection. All variants from a prompt and all visits by a reader remain together across the relevant split. The same original word or rating can contribute to only one held forecast. A constant predictor trained on the same remaining observations is a reference. The primary contrast for each outcome is held proper loss of the strongest baseline minus that of the same model with the registered curve feature(s). Report equal-prompt means of the within-prompt mean visits, and 10,000 matched two-way bootstrap replicates (sample readers and the 41 prompt families independently with replacement using seed 919841). Claim the added curve transfers across both observations only if both 95% lower bounds exceed zero. Use two-sided intervals and include failures. These contrasts compare variants seen by different people within prompts and adjust for people through cross-fitting and resampling. An individual never saw two variants of the same prompt. No online stock value is trained from EMTeC, only calibration slopes for the two measurement instruments. The frozen feature packets and the mappings from signals to outcomes are locked before grading.
