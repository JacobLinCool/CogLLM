# Does an explicit reader prior improve estimates of familiarity and support needs?

Guo et al.'s release provides a separate prereading rating for a designated term and a postreading rating from the same surveyed researcher. We ask how much knowing the reader's measured familiarity contributes beyond the article text and whether the article readout adds information beyond the supplied prior. The study supplied a term list to participants. A prereading rating is a valid input only when a future user actually supplies it. [Measurement and materials](https://github.com/talaugust/PersonalizedJargon/blob/22add4cc00274de199cc74bf0dbd629d05151b08/data/README.md)

This experiment is specified before opening the Guo response columns. It will use the eligible terms and checksummed predictions from the independent transfer, with the same unique-span and overlap rules. For each fold, hold out one anonymous reader and one of five deterministic abstract groups defined by SHA-256 of the source article ID. Train on other readers and other article groups jointly. Each observation therefore receives one prediction from a fit that excluded both that reader and article. When repeated reader–term rows have conflicting outcomes or prior scores, report and exclude the ambiguous key. All scores are conditional on the original respondents and the uniquely alignable terms.

Use a regularized ordered-rating model for the five postreading familiarity levels and binary logistic models for a request for a definition and a request for background. Train-only means and scales determine feature normalization. Compare the training response distribution, the article-only surface score, the frozen independent lexical score and PLABA localizer scores together, the reader's own target-specific prereading score, and combined text and prior. Exclude article or reader IDs, the person's future ratings, and other respondents' judgments of the test paper from all features. The prereading-only arm uses a complete categorical representation of the five response levels so that a text increment must improve upon a flexible source-appropriate baseline. Report mean held-out negative log likelihood, error in the rating expectation or Brier score for binary responses, and a two-way reader/article percentile interval on paired differences. Freeze zero-shot predictions first and report this cross-fitted transfer separately. The first unit here is a reader, a full abstract and a designated term. Neither response timing nor a perceptual onset token was observed.
