# Recover a reader's earlier phrase from a predicted word

The frozen English pronoun-location observer supplies one word that may belong to a longer clicked phrase. In the original 8,283 source clicks, median region length was one word, the 95th percentile was six and the maximum was 34. The pointer's existing word-hit results and interpretation denominators are known. The ability to recover entire region boundaries has not been scored. Keep the existing source/frozen forecasts and gold splits. This retrospective extension changes the requested measurement from “a word inside the clicked phrase” to “the earlier phrase ending and beginning where this particular reader clicked.” [Source click measurements](transition-pointer-protocol.md) · [Independent external names](transition-pointer-gap-protocol.md)

For each exact training click and each possible anchor word within it, create leftward and rightward continuation decisions. Weight the click by the original number of people, normalized across responses to its pronoun, pronouns per source work, and works. Average uniformly over words within the training click. On each side use a binary hazard for extending past each successive word, ending at the clicked boundary. At the physical edge of the previously read prefix the stop is deterministic and contributes no fitted event. Learn two standardized ridge-logistic hazards, one per direction, penalty .01 on slopes and unpenalized intercept. Require gradient below 10⁻⁶. A length-only hazard sees only distance from the anchor. The full model additionally sees prefix-only English POS, visible word forms, determiner, noun, proper-name and function-word relations, capitalization, sentence boundaries and intervening punctuation. At inference maximize the probability of the two boundaries subject to total width at most the source maximum. The full phrase must contain the frozen top word and precede its target pronoun. Keep its Unicode character endpoints on the unmodified article.

Use the original frozen top word by itself, a fixed noun-phrase POS chunker and a source length-only expander as comparators. The deterministic chunker uses optional determiner/possessive, adjectives and consecutive nouns, with consecutive proper names as a special case. Score the full observed distribution of source-game readers' clicks, giving each article equal weight and retaining cases where the initial anchor was wrong. Report exact boundary match, intersection over union, token precision/recall, length and exact match on multiword regions. Report the source .80 selected positions separately. On GAP keep all 1,107 eligible earlier-person choices and score the same word/phrase metrics against the verified bare name spans, with whole Wikipedia article resampling. Source annotated phrases may include determiners and titles outside GAP's original name annotation. The method has no free license to use those gold boundaries during inference.

Use this as an optional display extension only if it improves primary expected intersection over union on both the game and GAP relative to returning the original word, and has no clearly supported loss to the fixed grammatical chunker on either primary result. All comparisons receive the same frozen anchor; a domain-specific calibration study is still required for the separate question of whether to offer a link. The underlying word location remains reproducible even if this expansion fails the display criterion.
