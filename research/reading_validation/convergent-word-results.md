# Which word-level reading signals transfer between people, languages and laboratories?

We fitted one Qwen3.5-2B word readout on 122,036 usable Spanish word presentations across 8,640 distinct word contexts, using the negative voltage in the 300–500 ms window. The fitted coefficients were then applied unchanged to an independent English reading experiment. The primary joined analysis contains 1,546 word positions in 186 sentences. For each position we verified that the complete visible English prefix in the model matched the original laboratory sentence, including punctuation. Separate groups judged predictability and supplied cloze responses. All intervals below resample complete English sentences. [Spanish source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.117159) · [English EEG study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bandl.2014.10.006) · [English cloze and predictability study](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-023-02261-8) · [Registered protocol](convergent-word-protocol.md)

The contextual readout correlated with measured English word-level N400 negativity at 0.322 (95% interval 0.283–0.365). It improved on raw Qwen surprisal by 0.070 (0.020–0.115). A Spanish-fitted readout of just word form, position and Qwen surprisal reached 0.324 (0.286–0.363). After cross-fitted adjustment for lexical frequency and surface properties, the residual contextual association was 0.080 (0.034–0.125). Including native surprisal reduced it to 0.023 (−0.026–0.071). The external experiment supports generalization of lexical reading-response signals. The incremental contribution from richer hidden states remains uncertain. With English training data from separate sentence folds, the contextual head reached 0.408 for N400 negativity, 0.805 for human cloze surprisal, and 0.764 for rated unpredictability. A surface and GPT-2 XL comparator reached 0.384, 0.824 and 0.776 on the same endpoints. Constraining the three contextual readouts to a common rank-two subspace gave 0.407, 0.783 and 0.757. Unconstrained joint squared-error training was algebraically equivalent to separate regressions in this complete-label design. The experiment therefore provides no support for a general gain from forcing the endpoints into a shared two-dimensional representation. The laboratory exposure and the later behavioral stimulus release differed in apostrophe normalization in 27 sentences. The primary comparison excluded 180 positions affected by a change before or at their measured word. The exact spelling of all 27 original EEG sentences has now been reconstructed, with all target boundaries verified; the unchanged-coefficient replay is scored separately. [Exposure registration](convergent-word-geometry.md) · [Verified full-coverage text input](../results/reading-validation-convergent/erp-exposure-seal.json)

The exact-exposure replay recovered all 180 previously unmatched words. On these words the unchanged Spanish contextual head reached ρ = 0.431 (0.311–0.551) compared with 0.149 (0.017–0.279) for raw Qwen surprisal. Across the full 1,726 words in all 205 English sentences, the contextual head reached 0.333 (0.293–0.373), raw surprisal 0.242 (0.196–0.286), and Spanish-trained surface plus surprisal 0.331 (0.294–0.370). Relative to the trained comparator, the paired difference was 0.002 (−0.035–0.038). The 28 common prefixes in the replay agreed to within 0.000004 across all feature channels. [Full coverage and held original-exposure estimates](../results/reading-validation-convergent/original-exposure-scores.json)

Training with the observation law for each response improved held English log density for all three endpoints over surface plus surprisal. Estimated coupling between readouts provided a small benefit when only ten training sentences had EEG. The calibrated low-dimensional model remained the strongest sparse-EEG comparator.
