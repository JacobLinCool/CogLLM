# Can passage judgments in Italian and incremental English reports share a reading model?

Native Italian speakers rated the coherence of complete Wikipedia or TED passages from 1 (minimum) to 5 (maximum). The researchers retained 10,567 individual ratings across 1,064 materials, averaging about ten per item. Original passages, swapped sentences and within-document substitutions are represented. The shared-task CSV exposes one panel mean per passage. CoheSentia reports when an English story reader marked the sentence they had just seen. DECOR reports the judgments of expert readers on English learner essays. Italian ratings provide the independent endpoint. The two English test panels were previously graded for earlier CogLLM checkpoints, so their use for new weights is a design-informed follow-up. [Human Italian study](https://aclanthology.org/2023.clicit-1.40/) · [Official baseline](https://github.com/davidecolla/DisCoTex/blob/e113774f1b5f9c1eb4ea5581767429241ebb8cc9/src/run/run_baseline_t2.py) · [English observations and earlier readouts](transition-external-results.md) · [Parent protocol](state-transport-estimator.md)

**Splits and source separation.** Preserve all 200 official anonymous Italian test IDs. Group official training passages by the document identifier preceding the first `#`; connect passages sharing any 12 consecutive case-folded words. Remove the entire training component if it touches a held passage. Split the remaining training groups deterministically 80:20 by a salted SHA-256 of the component's minimum source key. Fit on the source portion and choose scalar sharing weights on development only. Keep the project's existing CoheSentia train, development and official test stories, and keep all DECOR judgments for external evaluation. No participant IDs span individual English stories. The Italian test masks source identities; report its published passage-weighted correlations and errors with passage bootstrap intervals, and give any detected cross-test shared text separately. Disclose historical exposure separately from the frozen backbone's unknown pretraining history.

**Inputs.** Read the complete article in original Unicode order, once. Form features at each boundary use position in the visible prefix, word and character counts, punctuation, type diversity, repetition in the immediately preceding and earlier visible text, and unsupervised lexical overlap. Stronger reference features add mean, upper-tail and initial observed Qwen word surprisal and differences from its preceding sentence. The representation model adds fixed coordinates of current word states and their mismatch to earlier word states, similarity to previously visible sentences, and prefix-only native attention. Inputs shared by the two languages use separately standardized source-train distributions. English queries use their released boundary coordinates. Italian uses visible punctuation-derived spans and pools the boundary scores at all noninitial segments. For a passage with one detected segment, use its completion; explicitly count these occurrences. Titles and unscored prefix context remain visible. No label, candidate edit, source type or annotator identity is supplied to the backbone. Keep the historical English story readout and the form-plus-surprisal reference on the identical eligible boundaries. Give extraction coverage by test role; do not truncate or silently replace unavailable passages with lexical predictions.

**Endpoints and fitting.** For English fit a Bernoulli likelihood for actual mark counts, with equal total weight per story. For Italian let the difficulty at boundary t be the standardized feature vector times a slope. Pool difficulties by log-mean-exp. Predict the completed passage rating as one plus four times the logistic of the Italian intercept minus a positive scale times this pooled difficulty. Fit squared error of the published 1–5 mean. No latent boundary label comes from this mean. Compare independent slopes and a slope shared across the two studies under equal task weights; divide each fitting loss by its training-only constant-model loss and penalize squared slope norm with coefficient 0.01/2. The Italian log scale has a zero-centered quadratic penalty 0.1/2 and bounds equivalent to a scale of 0.25–4. Fit intercepts independently and use numerical gradients only for validation. Use the same loss, penalties, features and training groups for matched models. Learn a single convex mixture weight of their predictive probabilities or means separately per task using only its development loss. Preserve untouched independent and shared predictions, including endpoint solutions of the calibration. Retain separate form and form-plus-native-surprisal fitted comparators. The official Italian baseline uses its own English-default NLTK tokenization, training vocabulary, adjacent binary Jaccard and special period/question-mark splitting exactly as released. Its native score is used directly for rank. A positive-slope affine mapping fitted on source groups allows interpretation on the 1–5 scale.

**Scoring.** Primary Italian outcomes are mean squared error and Spearman correlation on the 200 original test passages, with Pearson also reported. The primary model must improve error over the matched independent representation fit to support a sharing gain, and over the strong form-plus-surprisal fit to support added representation information. Report against the official reference separately. For the English holdout and untouched external essay judgments, report actual Bernoulli log loss, mean calibration, and within-document ordering. Bootstrap complete articles (Italian passages on anonymous test), 10,000 paired draws, seed 378283. Use the same test cases and draw weights for all systems. A two-language benefit requires a positive lower paired 95% bound for improvement in indexed primary loss in both languages. Record source development weights, convergence, controls, group counts and exclusions. These tests constitute an additional research family beyond the earlier 32-comparison local audit.

**Official program coverage, recorded before joining target ratings.** The authors' unmodified scorer raised `StatisticsError` on test ID `task_2_test_108`. Its splitter yields a single nonempty unit because the punctuation in this passage does not provide a recognized adjacent pair. We leave the official prediction undefined for this one item, evaluate the other methods on all 200 as planned and compare every method to the published reference on the same 199 scored records. The inspected reference implementation and author package versions are saved along with the predicted values. No imputed Jaccard value enters calibration.
