# Which predictions identify a reader's exact spelling?

The population model names 8,192 spellings and sends residual mass to an other category. A top-ranked other category does not name a word. A correct coarse category may therefore overstate exact lexical agreement. Before opening independent responses, add a transparent support analysis. First compare the highest-probability category with the true modal spelling of the observed responses, retaining ties and counting every forecast of other as an abstention and a miss. Second calculate the empirical probability that two different respondents give different normalized spellings, including distinct spellings in other. The observation corrects for drawing without replacement from the measured counts. If a fitted probability vector gives residual mass q to other, its exact-disagreement probability lies between one minus the sum of squares of all category probabilities and that quantity plus q squared. The lower end places all residual mass on one unlisted word. The upper bound is the supremum when residual mass spreads across arbitrarily many alternatives. Report the mean lower and upper bounds, mean measured exact disagreement and mean distance of each measured point to the predicted interval. Sampling noise can place the observed point outside its population interval. Source and target procedures fix the same case and whitespace normalization before opening the observations.

A strong native sensitivity restricts both predictions and observed choices to the 7,275 spellings encoded by one complete Qwen token with a distinct first token among all dictionary entries. Renormalize the fixed trained and native probabilities over this identical set. This removes the within-dictionary frequency split for shared first tokens. Report choice-weighted log loss by arm, retained human choices and paired whole-article uncertainty for the gain relative to the calibrated native and raw model. The quantity is conditional on the actual choice lying in this identifiable fixed subset.
