# Can measured choices from one reader predict that person's later choices?

A population word distribution can fit average choices while losing stable differences between readers. Take two observable properties of literal response choice. Some people systematically select a sharper or flatter set of common alternatives than others. People can also differ in how often they choose spellings outside a fixed response lexicon. Fit both with a finite population mixing distribution inferred from repeated, genuine responses by each anonymous reader. The model can marginalize the reader when no history is given. It can update the same prior from twenty genuine, earlier-observed responses by that reader. The article encoder remains fixed. No participant identifier, demographics, or grade is passed to Qwen or used as an input feature. History gives observations of choosing words. It need not indicate an individual's prior factual knowledge, general comprehension or felt effort. [Original UCL norms](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-023-02261-8) · [Independent original sentence-completion study](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-020-01351-1)

At a visible prefix i, let p_i(w) be the source-calibrated Qwen distribution. Conditional on reader parameters b and g, set q_i(w) proportional to p_i(w)^b times exp(g I[w=other]). Use the same 8,192 spellings and other as the prior population study. A fixed 7-by-7 quadrature grid covers b=2^(k/3), g=k/2, k=-3,…,+3. The grid integrates possible reader types. It is shared across words, folds and sites. Estimate its 49 probability masses by maximum marginal likelihood of all training responses, grouped by actual respondent. Use symmetric Dirichlet regularization with total one extra count over the full grid. Fit the underlying calibrated 19-coefficient word distribution to the available training responses first. This choice retains genuine lexical conditioning and isolates the new latent parameters in a small reproducible model. Require a converged likelihood for both source estimators and a verified numerical probability sum of one.

For UCL, assign complete anonymous people to the fit set (75%) or a strictly held group (25%) by reproducible pseudorandom sampling within each of the eight published list cohorts. If one pseudonym occurs twice, keep that person's records in the same side. Use the same independently fixed five sentence folds as the word-population study. For each fold, train both stages on fit readers and training sentences only. For each held person, select twenty nonempty history observations from training sentences by a fixed outcome-independent hash of sentence/item identity (include the reader's first/earliest responses only if true order is provided). Forecast all that reader's held sentences without updating on their evaluation answers. Compare the same person's scores under the correct history, a cyclic permutation assigning another eligible person’s history from the same list, the exact mixture marginal over unknown readers, and the fitted single calibrated head. These tests address the utility of observed reader-specific choices even on text and people unseen by both training stages. Report per-response negative log probabilities, accuracy in predicting whether each response fell outside the known lexicon, paired differences and reader-by-sentence crossed bootstrap intervals. Source corpus data from prior population analyses were known in aggregate. All individual conditioning and actual third-study response values were unexamined when registering.

Refit the two source estimators on every eligible UCL reader and sentence. Evaluate this fixed model on Peelle et al.'s public independent sentence-completion study. If original chronology is available, take the first twenty eligible responses in the released order for each person, score subsequent nonoverlapping items, and distinguish verified time from arbitrary presentation order. Otherwise preassign twenty history items by material hash and describe only cross-item conditioning. Do not use held scores to choose history length, quadrature, data inclusion or fitting. Count genuine nonempty held responses; do not score a missing response as an other word. When each respondent's questionnaire block differs, use the sorted previous participant with the maximum overlap of available sentence set for the shuffled-history comparator, breaking ties deterministically. Disclose overlap. Estimate uncertainties with whole readers crossed with whole sentence stems. Keep the model's normalized probability distribution over printed alternatives independent of held grades except for the twenty expressly supplied personal observations.
