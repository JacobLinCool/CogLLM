# What transfers between a reader's local coherence judgment and a passage rating?

Training on two different human observations improved one held-out behavior. A shared English–Italian model reduced log loss for experts’ sentence-level coherence decisions in 100 English learner essays from 0.6372 to 0.6013. The paired reduction was 0.0358, with a simultaneous 95% interval of 0.0140–0.0591 across ten registered comparisons. This result concerns the assigned probabilities of expert judgments, including their calibration. Within-essay ranking accuracy was 65.4% for the shared readout and 67.3% for the form-plus-surprisal control. Multiple unlinked texts from the same source would make these intervals too narrow. The frozen Italian model scored the Danish texts without any target calibration. For Danish texts the original table supplies a single value for each text, limiting inference about an individual reader. The English evaluation collections had already been scored on earlier model versions. The Italian and Danish target grades remained sealed during this study's model fitting. The original Italian word-overlap baseline reproducibly gave ρ = 0.138 and Pearson *r* = 0.105 on its 199 scorable test passages. On the same 199 the adaptive readout gave ρ = 0.438 and *r* = 0.446. The published 7B LoRA competition system reported ρ = 0.65 and *r* = 0.66 on the official test. Its per-passage forecasts were unavailable for a paired comparison. In 62 passages traceable to TED talks, the readout's ρ was 0.401 (0.200–0.567). The eight identifiable Wikipedia passages provide little precision. The form-plus-surprisal reference had lower MSE on all 200, and the paired gain over inferred genre plus form was 0.0081 (−0.0674 to 0.0872). These observations support some within-genre association and leave the added predictive value of native context uncertain. ## What changes when identical sentences exchange places?

Independent public records identified the original four sentence positions of 46 rated Italian passages. Mean human grades were 3.373 for 23 originals, 3.053 for 18 transpositions and 2.712 for five substitutions. The mean original-minus-altered difference was 0.394 (−0.214 to 0.953). Five source documents contained both altered and intact observed passages, with different wording. Across all 228 reconstructible training and test texts, no two graded passages shared even three of their original sentence positions. Among the 18 held transpositions, the human difference between neighboring and more distant swaps was −0.062 (−0.976 to 0.928). This small recovered sample leaves the original study's distance effect unresolved. [Original study](https://aclanthology.org/2023.clicit-1.40/) · [Observed ratings](../results/reading-validation-italian-interventions/authentic-scores.json) · [Paired-rating coverage](../results/reading-validation-italian-interventions/paired-ratings-coverage.json) · [Swap distance](../results/reading-validation-italian-interventions/distance-scores.json)

Thirty-three held passages from 21 source documents reproduced the published wording verbatim and could be re-encoded in two orders. Fourteen restored the authentic original of a previously swapped passage. Nineteen swapped two sentences in an authentic original. The adaptive model preferred the source order in 22/33 (66.7%, 52.9–82.1%), equal to the form-only control's rate. It preferred the source order for 8/14 genuine restorations. At the first affected noninitial sentence it assigned greater disruption to the swapped version in 19/33. Restricting both contexts to sentences with predecessors gave 15/29 (51.7%, 35.3–69.2%). The source-order advantage over 50% had an interval of −2.8 to +38.0 percentage points in the ten-comparison family. Only one version in each matched pair was scored by human readers. ## What the order-training objective changed

The additional loss used 89 independent source-training pairs, including 77 first disrupted sentences whose comparison preserved a predecessor on both sides. Its weight was fixed at 0.25. The optimization converged after 94 steps, with a maximum projected gradient of 3.0 × 10⁻⁸ and a finite-difference error of 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰. During fitting, the structural loss fell from 0.971 to 0.778 while the regularized human component rose from 0.694 to 0.714. Holding the predictions fixed, passage preference declined from 22/33 to 20/33 and the narrower held-site structural diagnostic rose from 15/29 to 20/29. The paired site increase was 17.2 percentage points (nominal 95% CI 4.2–28.6). The augmented system's own site accuracy interval was 48.4–85.7%. [Registered training criterion](italian-contrast-protocol.md) · [Source optimization and frozen forecast](../results/reading-validation-order-training/prediction-seal.json) · [All intervention outcomes](../results/reading-validation-order-training/scores.json)

On actual held observations, augmentation changed Italian MSE from 0.6167 to 0.6171, English story log loss from 0.3564 to 0.3506, English essay log loss from 0.6013 to 0.6144 and Danish concordance from 53.3% to 51.9%. None of the four registered superiority contrasts for augmentation had a simultaneous lower bound above zero. Source-restoration training shifted the local structural response without improving how accurately the model predicted human coherence judgments across these studies. A timed response or explicit reading difficulty mark under both exact orders would be needed to locate where people felt the switch. Other published experiments have directly measured the effects of reordering on comprehension judgments, regressions and reading time.
