# When did a successful contestant recognize the answer?

Qwen's causal states improved the likelihood assigned to the observed moment of a correct quiz response in two archives. On 1,849 recorded correct first responses from 77 ACF texts in 65 held-out question families, the contextual model improved word-level negative log likelihood by 0.181 nats relative to a trained word/phrase baseline (paired 95% family bootstrap interval 0.096–0.268) and by 0.465 relative to the position baseline (0.340–0.589). On 155 independent QANTA25 responses across 93 questions the improvement was 0.207 nats per released clue interval relative to lexical features (0.132–0.284) and 0.282 relative to position (0.208–0.356). ACF is largely an oral quiz and QANTA25 combines visual and narrated clues. Both endpoints condition on an observed correct response. The archive lacks exposure denominators for players who stayed silent. The offline final-length comparator further limits interpretation. It ran ahead of the contextual model on external mean likelihood by 0.095 nats (paired interval −0.013 to 0.202) and on external Brier and timing error. The contextual model captured where responses concentrated beyond several prefix-visible controls. Its external timing accuracy remained coarse. A science-only ACF comparison of 130 responses in eight families gave a 0.212-nat gain over lexical features, with an interval spanning zero (−0.080 to 0.547). External science-category labels were unavailable. The study establishes timing evidence for competitive clue recognition. Spontaneous uncertainty about a scientific article, silent comprehension, and the identity of a concern remain separate endpoints. [Scoring and subgroup estimates](../results/reading-validation-recognition/scores.json)

Training used 5,612 ACF responses, with a further 2,237 for validation. No answer, annotation, final length or participant field entered the causal contextual model. Each arriving complete word carried a discrete-time conditional hazard using a single whole-question Qwen3.5-2B pass. All families that shared a question or an answer stayed in one split. The optimizer initially failed the registered maximum-gradient condition for one high-dimensional fit. A damped Newton refinement solved the same penalized likelihood on the same training and validation data before held-out scores were calculated. The sealed forecasts and fit histories include all 16 penalties across the four models.
