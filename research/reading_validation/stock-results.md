# Measuring an unresolved terminology curve

The probability that a specific term is still awaiting explanation can be represented by two learnable components. A source starts with its own estimated probability of being a term that receives a forward definition. Each subsequent word can reduce that source's mass through an event hazard trained with the original definition interval. The probability of an explanation arriving in the interval equals survival up to entry multiplied by the chance of at least one event before exit. Summing the surviving probabilities gives an expected count at every word. Exact conservation of opening, surviving and closing mass is verified at inference. An independently selected common multiplier brings expected counts into the psychology development distribution without changing which source supplied the evidence. On a preselected 500-passage subset of unseen textbook disciplines, the original author-preview curve found 33 of 394 exact terms within their own later definitions and emitted 2,467 closures (F1 0.023). Its native fixed-width pointer could match at most 134 of the 394 human term spans, regardless of its scores. The variable-length contextual term readout found 99 true events among 319 emitted (F1 0.278). Across source files, its F1 gain was 0.268 (paired 95% interval 0.216–0.326). Even with partial credit for source overlap, the old precision was 4.5% compared with 36.1% for the new readout. On the same 315 texts with nonconstant allowed counts and two nonconstant predictions, within-text rank correlation with the midpoint gold count averaged −0.114 [−0.201, −0.034] for the original curve and 0.222 [0.174, 0.275] for the continuous source-linked model. The original integer terminology ledger in this table is the **new term/definition system** using hard unit decisions. The older author-preview baseline was separately scored above. Interval MSE is the mean squared distance to the range allowed by closing at definition entry or exit; smaller is better. AP is average precision for certainly pending versus certainly empty words; uncertain positions are excluded. The mean contextual count at certainly pending and empty words was 0.132 and 0.063 in textbooks, and 0.100 and 0.059 in the external ACL texts. On these linguistic structures the continuous model ranked likely pending locations more effectively. Its absolute count did not independently surpass the strongest simple comparator on W00. The always-zero mean absolute error was also lower than the contextual error in both. The external W00 archive provided 2,184 physical lines and 2,178 unique texts after deduplication. The primary subset comprised 568 single forward-linked and 1,426 negative research sentences. Four positive annotations did not map to separate forward word intervals, giving 564 eligible gold events. Without a supplied term or answer window, the jointly fitted DEFT event decoder achieved F1 0.114 (55 correct of 404 alerts); the separate contextual decoder scored 0.061 (26/293), the surface decoder 0.077 (70/1,264), the position decoder 0.008 (13/2,496), and the exact-name decoder 0.006 (2/151). The ordering of separate versus joint models reversed across domains. The archive lacks stable source-paper identifiers; results are available under both sentence and section resampling. The article-only terminology API returns original character offsets, stable mention IDs and per-token counts, openings and closures. Ten complete passages replayed cached events and stock exactly, with the same prefix verified after truncation. The feature extractor took one Qwen pass for each of 750 complete articles and ten separate prefix checks. Maximum evidence-score discrepancies were 0.0000200 against the frozen offline references and 0.0000234 after truncation. These results ground an artifact for studying *where a text explains an earlier term*. A reader's unresolved mental workload is an additional, experimentally distinct quantity.
