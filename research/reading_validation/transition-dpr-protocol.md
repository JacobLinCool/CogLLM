# Referent revision after an event reveals which interpretation fits

The same pronoun can evoke different interpretations as more of its sentence is read. Rahman and Ng published 943 pairs of English sentences composed by 30 students. Each original record gives the pronoun, two candidate antecedents and its author’s intended choice. These are authored benchmark targets. Independent choices and reading times in [Jones and Bergen](transition-jones-protocol.md) provide the human-response endpoint. The source originally fixed 661 training pairs and 282 test pairs. Keep whole pairs together. Establish identity from unique earlier matched words, permitting case and original apostrophe normalization. Omit ambiguous geometry for both twins. Ignore authored test labels throughout fitting and retain both scoreable and excluded counts. Audit all source/target eight-word strings and remove any source-training family sharing a complete experimental sentence or an eight-word word sequence with Jones. Report residual task similarity and possible exposure during backbone pretraining. ## A single score for each earlier word as the evidence changes

The unprompted Qwen3.5-2B receives each original text once, with causal native attention. At any earlier pronoun q and subsequent word k, assign an unrestricted utility s(q,k,i) to every word i before q. Neither annotated candidate is privileged in this operation. Compare named alternatives in the experiments by averaging these same utilities within each supplied span. Differences of those two mean utilities identify the log odds for the second alternative. An input to this measurement has two specified spans; the companion full-prefix ranking also evaluates the earlier-word forecast with no provided names.

Compare three frozen source-trained/readout models with fixed capacity. A surface observer reads the 46 original earlier-word lexical, recency and prefix-only POS cues at q. A trained-attention comparator adds the two middle/final cosine similarities and all 48 true global-attention-head masses at q and k. This is a causal-decoder adaptation of published supervised attention baselines. The primary semantic observer adds candidate-word × newly read word products separately in the normalized middle and final state spaces, alongside distance-weighted changes to those same two global event representations. The latter terms allow evidence for an event to favor a grammatical position. An event endpoint is the final lexical word of each original source sentence. At transfer it is the predesignated final word of the Jones critical predication. The feature function and fitted weights apply unchanged at every intermediate word. Candidate and event states see only visible prefixes. L2-normalize each native feature block for dimension using the same 48-dimensional effective width as in the [direct-state readout](transition-direct-protocol.md). No activations are changed and no language-model decoding occurs. [Prior attention classifier](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.310/)

Within each training pair average the binary conditional-choice log loss over its two final readings. Where the authors changed the intended answer, additionally score the direction of the change between twin interpretations, aligned to the same candidate identity. The change loss gives equal weight to both items and cannot be satisfied by a lexical prior invariant across the two versions. Give individual and change losses equal weights when both are available, and use the individual loss for same-answer or nonalignable pairs. Centering/intercepts must preserve candidate-exchange antisymmetry. Scale by source root mean square, use ridge .01, converge to maximum gradient below 10⁻⁶. No training labels attach to the initial pronoun or supposed “moment of resolution.” The earliest changed text is observed. Actual time of human resolution is unknown.
