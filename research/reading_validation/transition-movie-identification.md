# What survives uncertain human span locations?

In the 39 movie summaries, every released human character-error string appeared in its source segment. There were 554 such records. A literal string had exactly one match for 431 records, two matches for 87, three for 31, four for four, and five for one. Removing every segment containing a repeated string previously removed 4,284 words. Only 285 of those word labels actually depend on the placement. This analysis recovers all 26,417 original word positions without assigning a preferred occurrence to a reader. The ranges give attainable minima and maxima on this fixed movie set. They quantify uncertainty in the human locations. They do not quantify how the result would vary across other movies. The original word AP advantage on the 22,133 wholly unambiguous words was +.137, with a 95% movie bootstrap interval of [.068, .210]. On the full 26,417 words, the **sharp paired AP difference** ranges from −.00470 to +.18038. The paired calculation uses the same hidden human placement for both policies. A reversal of roughly half a percentage point is possible. At the operating threshold, the Qwen policy finds at least 79.4% of marked words for every admissible placement. The precision of its alerts stays below 38% throughout. ## Assumptions and verification

The release aggregates records by span string. We assumed that each record corresponds to one of its literal occurrences within that same segment. All words intersecting the chosen span are positive for the binary analysis. The resulting number of positive words ranges from 629 to 632. When a pooled record combines votes cast at different occurrences, its precise word-level label requires original reader coordinates. A separate relaxation allows all combinations of ambiguous matching words and preserves the guaranteed words. That weaker assumption widens the bounds. The original corpus does not test whether these reader marks represent moment-by-moment effort. [Released study and task](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.29/)

For every segment we enumerated all distinct permitted word masks. Operating-point extrema are obtained by fractional optimization over these masks. For average precision, the numerator is a quadratic function of the binary word labels, including exact score ties. We optimized this function over the segment masks, with the common positive-word count as denominator. Strict dominance eliminates a candidate only if another placement improves the objective for every possible configuration of all other segments. Any residual configurations are handled by branch and bound. Exact search was verified against brute-force enumerations containing score ties, and each extremizing placement was replayed with a separate conventional AP calculation on the full movie set. The extra factor-dual bounds also enclose the extrema. These are mathematical sensitivity checks on previously evaluated texts.
