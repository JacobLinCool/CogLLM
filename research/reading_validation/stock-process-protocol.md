# Estimating outstanding questions and suspense from the same underlying reading path

The original German study records both story-level annotations of when a *particular* plot question was unresolved and a separate panel's continuous suspense reports. A prior model fitted opening rates and survival likelihoods, but its expected question count missed the trajectories in four held stories. A count is an observable marginal of an event process. We now fit the count to that same conserved process, then ask whether the independently recorded suspense responses help estimate it. This is a new, explicitly retrospective model test on the same four stories. Four books cannot establish generalization to literature as a whole. [Previous estimates and primary source](narrative-survival-results.md) · [Response geometry](narrative-survival-protocol.md)

At each line, model openings as a Poisson process with rate depending on its current text. An opened issue has a latent short or long lifetime. For each admissible earlier opening, the two component hazards depend on age and on the relationship between the current and earlier text in the one original causal Qwen pass. Give the short component a larger hazard at the same evidence. Conservation is exact. Under independent thinning of the source Poisson issues, the number still outstanding at any observation has Poisson mean equal to the sum of the surviving source-specific masses. The observed count may lie in an interval; use the log probability of the whole allowed integer range. Exact, censored and interval closures retain their own event likelihoods. Never train a separate free curve capable of disagreeing with the issue memory. Do not give the held model any actual plot question or source start.

The dense count and individual lifetimes derive from the same team annotations. Their equally weighted composite risk targets two different predictive uses of these data; it does not multiply the number of independent observations. A third likelihood concerns the genuinely distinct suspense ratings. Model the average change in reported suspense between adjacent observed lines as a function of immediate textual evidence and change in expected count. Compare a readout fitted to the frozen count with joint gradients into the identical count process. Center the empirical *scale* of suspense change on training stories; avoid a held-story calibration from future ratings or a final-page countdown. Compare (1) the original elapsed-duration and wording forecasts, (2) the two-timescale process that fits just openings/lifetimes, (3) the same process additionally trained on count intervals, and (4) the count-constrained state updated also by real suspense changes. Fit only the three other originals, rotating the original four-story split. Primary is mean squared distance to the allowed outstanding count; report all four stories, not line-level significance. Grade conservation, expected count direction, cause and timing, and suspense change. Source weights are set before inspecting new forecasts. The conditional meaning of a story annotation and that of a reader's suspense rating remain distinct.
