# Independent test of a word-by-word referent update

The frozen referent update did not generalize from the original choice and click corpora to AmbiCoref readers. It assigned too much probability to the second offered antecedent. This failure is material for any claim that the displayed uncertainty reflects a reader's unresolved question. [Prospective protocol](transition-anchored-reference-protocol.md) · [Original observations](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-eacl.75/)

The public questionnaires provided 4,905 five-way judgments on 1,682 supported English sentences across 564 questionnaire assignments. Participants selected definitely first 1,310 times, probably first 1,224 times, ambiguous 810 times, probably second 1,032 times, and definitely second 529 times. Each native input contained only the original full sentence. The external readout used the two supplied noun regions for evaluating the relevant probabilities. No external judgment entered source training or the 1,682 causal forwards.

| Frozen observer | Directional log loss ↓ | Agreement with a directional answer | AUROC for an explicit ambiguous answer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Source pointer held fixed at the pronoun | 1.207 | .388 | .483 |
| Pointer plus elapsed-step change | 1.275 | .384 | .485 |
| Pointer plus attention change | 1.385 | .406 | .472 |
| Pointer plus externally trained semantic evidence change | 1.471 | .391 | .436 |
| Semantic evidence component on its own | .837 | .561 | .537 |

Adding the semantic evidence change increased directional log loss by .264 [.225, .308] relative to the frozen pronoun-time prior. Its explicit-ambiguity AUROC fell by .046 [.018, .074]; its AP was .145 at an observed rate of .165. It assigned mean second-name probabilities between .79 and .84 in **every** original response category. These are externally evaluated probabilities. The primary update was fixed before the first AmbiCoref native pass. Whole lexical skeletons and questionnaire assignments define the crossed uncertainty calculation. The public assignments cannot identify a participant who responded on multiple sheets. [All original categories, strata and comparisons](../results/reading-validation-transition-anchored-reference/ambicoref-confirmation.json)

**Where did the signal change?** The article's original classification identified 3,772 minimal comparisons among 470 distinct sentences. Every comparison changed one source word and preserved all other original characters. Shared sentences link these pairs into only 16 independent text components, the largest comprising 594 comparisons. The maximum preceding probability difference was 6.8 × 10⁻⁷. Humans selected the explicit ambiguity response 15.7 percentage points more often in the ambiguous condition [.100, .214]. At the changed word, the frozen update's uncertainty shifted −.039 [−.092, +.020]. At sentence end it shifted −.045 [−.089, −.004], opposite the reader contrast. Its semantic evidence component alone shifted +.062 [.033, .097] at sentence end. The original independent result rests on the full update. The source component is a diagnostic for future external replication. The questionnaires only identify the readers' final decisions. [Character-matched check and crossed uncertainty](../results/reading-validation-transition-anchored-reference/letter-exact-pair-confirmation.json)

**Did different readers choose different names?** Among 1,485 sentences with two or more directional responses, 3,391 pairs of distinct questionnaires supplied 1,068 opposite-name decisions. The update implied each sentence's probability of such disagreement as 2p(1−p). Its equal-sentence Brier score was .261 [.247, .275], versus .241 for the unchanged pointer and .223 for the rate from other lexical families. Its difference from that constant was +.038 [.029, .047]. Explicitly reporting ambiguity and disagreeing with another respondent are distinct observations. Both expose limitations of the source-trained prior here. [Reader-pair analysis](../results/reading-validation-transition-anchored-reference/independent-reader-pair-choice.json)

A secondary experiment trained a proper five-category response distribution using other AmbiCoref constructions and other questionnaire blocks. Across the 1,670 classified sentences and 4,869 judgments, the native-update classifier reached five-way log loss 1.711 versus 1.572 for source category frequencies. The paired increase was .139 [.109, .169]. This supervised model also failed to establish generalization to an unseen syntactic construction.
