# Incremental human coherence judgments on unseen texts

We will test two frozen transfer claims. A reader encounters each additional sentence of a generated story, sees only the article so far and marks whether the new sentence has a coherence problem. CoheSentia recorded this judgment before revealing the next sentence. DECOR later obtained expert annotations of incoherence on public context–sentence pairs from student essays and expert corrections for a subset. These implement sentence-level judgments, with prompting and different populations and genres. We found no prior CogLLM score on either benchmark. The CoheSentia and DECOR published protocols and full-sample descriptive summaries informed this design. All methods below were selected before scoring any model on these material families. [CoheSentia methods, §3.2.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.324/) · [DECOR methods, §3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.639/)

## Primary external tests

1. Apply the frozen eight-hour project's Carter–Hoffman readout without target-study calibration to the official CoheSentia test stories. Score all sentence boundaries from the second sentence onward. Include title and preceding text, exactly the information raters received. Estimate the comparison with the lexical and deployed-channel Carter calibrations. Use the fixed Carter rating midpoint <3 to flag a review. Report the sensitivity to marked boundaries, false alerts per 100 unmarked boundaries, average precision relative to prevalence, and AUROC. Compare judgments from individual annotators where their trial-level indicators are exposed, reporting story-cluster uncertainty. We treat local annotator indices as within-story identifiers. For an additional consensus view, at least half of the available readers must have marked the sentence. A positive and a negative reader can thus both be represented in the primary trial-level denominator.

2. Train a compact causal sentence readout on the official CoheSentia training split, then freeze its feature normalization, model and alert threshold prior to reading test responses. Evaluate on the CoheSentia official test and, without fitting any DECOR labels, on every evaluable sentence boundary of the separate official DECOR detection test. Record all input and group exclusions. The DECOR prompt is visible to the judge and can serve as article title when that field is available. If only context–sentence pairs can be reconstructed, process each distinct full original essay or longest verified common prefix once and select the matching original endpoints. Represent alternate human rewrites as separate texts with identical original context. The primary outcome is average precision at marked boundaries; we compare with the strongest matched lexical and deployed-channel models using a paired story/essay bootstrap with 10,000 replicates and fixed seed 9271. For formal success both differences must favor the proposed model with intervals entirely above zero. We require the lower confidence bound for AUROC to exceed 0.5 and report sensitivity and false alerts alongside it. Retain all tests even when this criterion fails.

3. On DECOR's official expert-rewritten test pairs, report the fraction for which a fixed readout assigns a lower incoherence probability to the revised sentence, the average change, ties, and uncertainty by essay. Rewrites authored by experts do not provide independent human judgments of the rewritten version's ease. Any preference claim applies to this edit comparison. DECOR and CoheSentia originals remain in an ignored cache. We do not upload judgments or model training answers.

## Representations and training

Each complete article is run once through the selected frozen Qwen3.5-2B. All boundaries use the hidden states, attention and surprisal of words seen through that sentence. We collect the same nine Carter features at sentence ends. The trainable readout also receives projected final- and middle-layer current sentence and previous-context averages and their paired elementwise product, based on the shared frozen CogLLM input projection. We omit missing titles and use previously read sentences as context. One readout uses only position and lexical controls, one uses only the displayed transition (with sentence position), one uses lexical controls plus surprisal, one uses local features including the current sentence states without explicit interactions with the previous sentences, and the proposed full readout combines them with the contextual interactions. The current sentence states are themselves causal and contextual. Feature vectors contain the log sentence index. We use reader-level binomial likelihood with L2 penalty 0.01 on training-standardized coefficients and a free intercept. We fix this penalty and require numerical convergence. We expose no author condition or local diagnostic annotation to the model.

Thresholds for the newly fitted binary readouts are set on the last 20% of official training story families ranked by SHA-256 with prefix 'cogllm-incremental-calibration-v1:'. The first 80% train the weights. Alert when the score strictly exceeds the empirical 90th percentile of calibration negative reader trials, targeting no more than ten false alerts per 100 negative readings. No holdout result adjusts a threshold or model. The Carter transfers keep their original midpoint. Evaluation retains the released official test set in its entirety apart from prespecified boundaries with no preceding article sentence, missing source observations, or materially ambiguous reconstruction. We identify duplicate normalized training/test boundaries or sources before scoring and report attrition. These tests cover discourse coherence at supplied sentence stops, without token-level annotations of the exact word at which a reader first noticed a problem.
