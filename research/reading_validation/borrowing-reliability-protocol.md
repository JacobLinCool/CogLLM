# Does relative training instability identify which expert to trust at a particular text?

The two frozen random initializations of shared and private observation readouts provide an outcome-free measure of prediction stability on the same material. The preceding source-level prior did not improve reliably over a fixed half share. This follow-up tests one specific alternative, whether a material-specific change can exploit an independently trained replica's prediction. Historical and fresh evaluation outcomes have been examined before for endpoint-level observers, and the present result is an internally registered retrospective comparison.

For each study endpoint and row, compute squared Hellinger distance between predictive distributions from the two shared replicas and separately between the private replicas. Calculate overlap on the original categories, or the exact Gaussian Bhattacharyya coefficient in one or two dimensions. For known observation sampling errors, use each head's predicted latent scale in this reliability computation; withhold the human estimate and its standard error from the reliability gate. The evaluative scoring model retains the source's known-error likelihood. Named response choices, their validity masks and printed positions are supplied by the original observation design. No human response count, category, value, rating, error estimate or test decision enters the gate.

For a given endpoint, estimate the usual Hellinger distances m_private and m_shared on the calibration *inputs*, with equal weight per material family and equal rows within a family. Each test item contributes one additional material-specific discrepancy v to the usual endpoint discrepancy. Multiply the source-calibrated odds of shared reliance by

```
r_i = (1 + v_private,i / m_private) / (1 + v_shared,i / m_shared).
a_i = a_endpoint × r_i / (1 − a_endpoint + a_endpoint × r_i).
```

This proposal treats one observed two-seed discrepancy as one additional estimate alongside the endpoint mean. It makes no equivalence between retraining variability and human disagreement. Relative instability on the private side increases sharing for this row. An everywhere-deterministic expert on the calibration inputs has no within-expert scale for a relative comparison. When either reference mean is smaller than 10⁻¹², or no calibration material exists, assign neutral odds r=1 and record the count. The original registered per-endpoint Beta(2,2) mode supplies the main a_endpoint for each seed. Each human response still receives the original normalized predictive distribution and its original likelihood. A single backbone encoding supplies all four heads at inference. The two seed-wise losses are averaged as repeated fits on the same observed responses. This experiment does not train another backbone.

The two primary comparisons pool the same sources, split families and seeds as the hierarchical-prior analysis. Compare the proposed row-varying pool against (i) the identical endpoint coefficient, and (ii) the identical set of row-varying multipliers after a prespecified, outcome-independent cyclic assignment within the endpoint and valid option-count group. Order the rows in each such group by the SHA-256 of a fixed prefix, material family and projection coordinate sequence, with stable original row indices to break full duplicates. Shift by floor(group-size/2), excluding the original pairing when group size exceeds one. All three predictors use the same test rows, experts and label support. The second control checks whether locating the disagreement on the right material matters beyond changing the average mixture. Report the other study-excluded coefficient under the same gate as a secondary reference. Export the actual per-row coefficients and predictions' digests before scoring. Use 10,000 paired whole-source resamples with 95% simultaneous intervals for the two primary contrasts; score source mean nats per observed trial. Give separate split and seed results, true counts, reliability dispersion and held source-specific values. No searched gate or selected threshold.
