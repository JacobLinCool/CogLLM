# A Mandarin verb predicts which person readers mention next

Independent human verb norms supplied the model with a lexical prior. The source table reported first- and second-person-reference bias for 150 predicates from 99 participants. After excluding 15 discontinuous predicates and every spelling shared with either evaluation, 106 verbs remained for training. A logistic readout learned the published fractional response for each source verb. Its kernel weighted orthography and the frozen Qwen word embedding equally. The experimental predicate identities were available in the source materials. A dictionary of the 188 distinct predicates required 188 short offline passes and added no pass to the original sentence inference. A different study reported mean subject choices per verb for 166 Mandarin readers. On its 49 unseen verbs in subject–verb–object sentences, predicted and observed rates correlated at Spearman ρ = .502 [.229, .712]. The orthography control reached .429 [.154, .654], and mean mid/final representations of the isolated verb reached .151 [−.162, .442]. The kernel using the input embedding achieved log loss .779 versus .788 for orthography and .801 for the source-base-rate predictor. Its paired advantage over orthography was −.0085 [−.0150, −.0023]. In the other syntactic construction the same frozen verb forecasts gave ρ = .388 [.103, .625]. These measures represent the relative ordering of verbs and average choice loss calculated from the available rounded proportions. Individual ballots and per-verb denominators were unavailable. The source model assigned every target predicate a probability below .5 of referring to the first person. Its absolute probabilities yielded worse log loss than a constant .5 (.693) in both constructions. First-person rates averaged .563 and .591 in the target study. A follow-up diagnostic centered each lexical logit by the mean logit on its source training words and set the resulting neutral logit to zero. This specification was chosen after inspecting the original test result and required no target responses to calculate its scores. Its log loss improved over .5 by −.0172 [−.0252, −.0098] and −.0149 [−.0226, −.0081]. It also gained over equally centered orthography by −.0054 [−.0105, −.0012] and −.0063 [−.0120, −.0013]. When the intercept was fitted to 48 other verbs in the other construction, the gain over the calibrated orthography predictor included zero in both constructions. The same 49 verbs appear in both conditions. On the previously examined Zhan cohort, adding the independent verb prior to the native passage model increased the predicted IC-1 minus IC-2 difference in first-person choices from 5.8 to 11.0 percentage points for active sentences, and from 1.6 to 9.3 points for 把 sentences. The corresponding human contrasts were 26.5 and 23.7 points. On the 1,504 overt choices from 87 readers the combined model had log loss .634, compared with .644 for the matched orthographic-prior model, .643 for the passage-only readout, and .619 for the published semantic-class predictor. The paired difference against the orthographic-prior model was −.0101 [−.0207, −.0006]. The interval for the difference against the passage-only readout was −.0231 to +.0025. Thus independent semantic norms recover part of a human lexical effect; accurate magnitude and absolute probability remain open.
