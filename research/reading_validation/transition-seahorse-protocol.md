# Can a score for coherent news predict whether a reader can understand a new summary?

SEAHORSE question 1 asks a professional rater whether the summary can be read and understood. The rater sees only the summary for this judgment. The source article first appears at question 4. Our input is the identical displayed summary, without a reference, article, question, or system identifier. This outcome concerns a whole summary and its rater. It does not identify a particular misunderstood word. The English US subset has three corpora (XSum, English XL-Sum and WikiLingua) and multiple generating systems. Preserve the original train, validation and test assignments. The independent public source is CC BY 4.0. [Clark et al., §2.2 and Appendix C](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.584/) · [Official dataset, license and duplicate warnings](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/seahorse)

Before inspecting individual response values, extract train, validation and test inputs, deduplicated by exact text. Reserve the entire official test for final scoring. Keep all votes, corpus, source story and generator labels off the accelerator. The primary binary outcome distinguishes “No” from “Yes”. Describe “Unsure” and missing responses and report them separately. No duplicate-file judgments enter training, validation or parameter selection. The multiple-ballot test subset measures between-reader agreement. Count exact input coverage and all exclusions. Each model receives one causal pass through each unique text. A training article with any exact printed draft or a normalized consecutive 12-word match to a test or validation draft is withheld from training. Validation similarly gives precedence to test. Quantify observed source overlap with the earlier transferred news model and report sensitivity excluding the complete affected test articles. Retain original test versions for comparability.

The first frozen prediction is the news-coherence ballot utility fitted on the earlier SummEval news cohort, with higher utility predicting an easier summary. Compare it with that study's matched form and word-surprisal utility. Report Q1 discrimination, precision–recall for a reader reporting difficulty, and concordance on pairs of competing summaries of the same article that received opposite responses. The primary transfer comparison is the difference in within-article concordance of native versus form scores. Treat a model tie as half correct; summarize across informative articles and bootstrap complete articles. There is no calibration on test. Report the performance by generator and by news versus instructions, including units and denominators.

A separate observer is trained only on official English training responses with typed binary log likelihood. Its baseline has 14 document form measurements and average frozen-model observed word surprisal. The native version adds the earlier source's 86 standardized prefix measurements pooled by their mean and log mean exponential. Fit a convex standardized ridge logistic readout with mean log loss and a fixed L2 coefficient of .002, using equal total contribution per original article. Recalibrate intercept and a nonnegative slope on official validation. A generator-by-corpus metadata model and metadata-plus-form versus metadata-plus-native fits diagnose sources that can be predicted from system identity. The main supervised comparison is native against form plus surprisal using held article binary negative log likelihood. Report AUROC, rare-response average precision, Brier score and same-source concordance for the same rows. Select the difficult-summary threshold by validation F1, and report precision/recall/false alarms with the number of supported held outputs. Freeze all predictions and operating points before evaluating held responses. Use two-sided paired article-bootstrap intervals. As sensitivity, rank tips within the same generator and corpus. Preserve all registered source strata even if intervals are broad.

When the held duplicate records refer to the same displayed summary and model, compare the trained probability with the actual separate responses. Estimate observed pairwise rater disagreement among the three-level responses and additionally among definitive yes/no judgments. The release omits stable reader identities. No independence between panels or latent truth is assumed. The primary independent source item can have a human- or model-authored summary, and eligibility will be reported for both.
