# Holding the printed word constant across different passages

Two contexts can produce different expectations for the same word. Across 12,449 same-word comparisons from 185 English sentences, the source-trained Qwen readout identified the context with the larger fraction of unexpected completions on 71.4% of eligible comparisons. It identified the context people rated as less predictable on 72.2%. Matching the exact printed spelling controls word length and many stable lexical differences. Both complete sentences in each pair were excluded from the fit used to score that pair. Each printed word type contributes the same weight. Intervals resample lexical types and source sentences. Human ties are omitted per endpoint. For cloze, the new objective conditioned the reader choice counts on their two-context total. This removes a separate shared intercept for every printed word. Gaussian contrast losses used the corresponding difference of human means for the rating and EEG. A constant lexical predictor ties every comparison and receives 50%. At matched capacity, none of the contextual conditional model's three changes from the conventional contextual head excluded zero. The calibrated comparator explains much of the behavioral ordering. Independent numerical validation reproduced the noncentral hypergeometric likelihood to 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ and gave identical slopes after reversing all context pairs. Applied without modification to 112 Spanish novel-word families, the conditional contextual head predicted an increase in N400 negativity of 0.113 in the English source’s fitted units after the less informative continuation (family interval 0.078–0.150). The preceding shared word had a maximum model discrepancy of 0.0000024. The registered external Spanish reader response increased by 1.016 µV at the common category noun. The correlation of the predicted effect sizes with individual family-level EEG contrasts was 0.032 (family interval −0.147–0.222). Thus the new objective recovered a group ordering at the relevant later word, while reliable ranking of which specific family would produce more effort remains unresolved. These readers’ cross-half reliability for family ranking was also low. [Spanish comparison and original measurements](spanish-clue-results.md)
