# Can a predicted change in meaning anticipate what readers can answer?

We freeze the existing source-relative revision models from SimpDA before any new individual response is inspected. Agrawal and Carpuat collected 1,980 answers from 112 English-speaking participants who read English OneStopQA paragraphs in one of eleven forms. Each paragraph version was seen by one participant, who answered three questions. Each participant was assigned six passages from the same condition. The authors' release includes the text, choice, correct option, and explicit indication that the text supplied insufficient information. The public paper reports aggregate results that the investigators have seen. The released table contains no stable participant identifier. Use all 60 paragraph families from 30 articles. For each, pair the advanced original with each candidate, using exactly the displayed text including original punctuation. The primary comparison is across the ten human or automatic revisions, all compared for the same question and paragraph. Each distinct paragraph receives exactly one frozen Qwen3.5-2B forward; source/draft comparison uses cached encodings. Exclude question wording, options, answers, author system, and reported correctness from all features. The annotator IDs needed for within-person effects are unavailable. The original will serve as a second, separately reported version with its own observed reader responses.

For every informative pair of revisions answering the same question, compare the frozen **meaning-preservation** ranking with the two observed binary correctness outcomes. Give half credit to predicted ties. Average first over eligible comparisons within a question, then over eligible questions within a paragraph, paragraphs in an article, and all eligible articles. This is discrimination against a noisy observation from one person per version; it does not identify the reader's moment of understanding or predict which item is lost. Report score, pair, question, paragraph and article counts; 10,000 paired article-bootstrap draws with seed 486130. The primary comparison is the fixed Qwen-assisted preservation score versus each of (1) source-content retention weighted by word frequency, (2) the trained edit-only preservation score, and (3) retained length, defined as closeness in word count to the original. Establish superiority only if all three paired lower 95% bounds exceed zero. Report absolute ReadMe++ ease, shorter length, pretrained simpler-draft and fluency scores, and source–draft contextual coverage as separate controls. Consider the secondary explicit answerability endpoint, preserving its distinction from correctness. For a complementary real editing comparison report the concordance with an *observed* original-to-rewrite difference on the same question. Splits on lexical retention or systems are descriptive only.

Screen source/draft strings against all earlier supervised materials that actually shaped the frozen projections and revision weights, including the frozen CompLex head and the older ReadMe++ effect studies. Use exact equality or two distinct five-word shingles covering at least 80% of the shorter item; flag complete article families for a primary supervised-exposure exclusion. Check the historical portable reader's broader OneStop materials separately. Applying an isolated frozen Qwen feature extractor does not use that reader's trained parameters. Record every exclusion and repeat on all available articles. Since the present data reuse OneStopQA originals, the broader CogLLM research program has prior familiarity with the stimuli. Backbone pretraining exposure cannot be excluded. No result on this dataset will trigger model selection or retraining. ### Amendment after inspecting text and question geometry

Two unsupervised MUSS entries have identical input text, question triplets and condition identifiers within a paragraph (articles 17 and 26, paragraph 1). Two further paragraph families lack this system. We found these discrepancies when checking the geometry, before viewing any answer, annotation or model score. Assign a single text forecast to each displayed version. Exclude the two ambiguously repeated version assignments from the main comparison. Preserve both paragraph families and every other eligible version. Include a sensitivity analysis averaging the two released outcomes per question for each ambiguous version and allowing fractional outcomes in directional comparisons.
