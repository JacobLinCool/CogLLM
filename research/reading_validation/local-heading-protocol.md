# Can a sentence readout recover the effect of section headings?

Kerwer et al. presented plain-language research summaries with and without section headings and collected article-level ratings of comprehensibility. Earlier CogLLM experiments examined this assignment. The new comparison is an external application of PLABA-fitted coefficients to an already examined study. Use the same 12 DOI pairs and 181 verbatim body-sentence pairs in their published order. Do not fit on Kerwer. The known positive average human contrast motivated this diagnostic. [Kerwer et al., original study](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.18898)

Predict the expected PLABA understanding rating at each verified source sentence stop. Average equally over matched sentences within each document. Convert the paired heading-minus-no-heading difference from the 1–5 PLABA scale to the 1–8 Kerwer response scale by multiplying by 7/4. The two scales measure related but distinct self-reports. Compare the new pre-existing text-form, medical, 32-dimensional local representation, and local-plus-trace readouts. Include the separately archived frozen sentence head as a historical comparator. The entire 2,048-dimensional deployed head can be scored only if its native full states are available for both heading assignments. Do not reconstruct them from 32 projections. Evaluate all arms using identical human responses. Report the mean prediction, signed deviation from the human contrast and 95% paired intervals resampling readers and DOI families. No adjudication of individual heading boundaries follows from a document-level rating.
