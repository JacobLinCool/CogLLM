# Learning a response-style prior from other readers' earlier pages

The untouched transfer result was known before this follow-up. Twenty historical Peelle completions improved a source-fitted mixture's predictions over unknown history, but a matched stranger's earlier choices also captured almost all of this advantage. An external participant selected a spelling outside the English source response lexicon on 25% of later choices, compared with 6% in the UCL holdout. We therefore separate two directly observed response processes in the new likelihood: choosing outside the fixed lexicon, and choosing the identity of a named spelling conditional on choosing inside it. This analysis measures adaptation to the elicitation task in this cohort. Its performance on this dataset is a retrospective test.

Keep the external source texts, observed words, chronology and 20 history responses identical to the primary prospective analysis. Preserve both the original external score and its complete prediction lock. Assign readers and literal sentence stems to fixed independent folds using only their anonymous ordinal and their original verified text fingerprint. Train new parameters exclusively on designated first-page histories of other participants and restrict these training items to the four stem folds outside the held fold. Fit five crossed reader folds by five stem folds. Neither the held reader's name nor that person's future choice enters the peer prior. Training labels come exclusively from earlier history pages. Do not use other people's later scored responses. Once it has been fitted, the peer prior is available even when the new reader's own history is absent.

For the outside/inside decision, use a logistic observation with an intercept, a slope for the source log odds of an outside response, and an additive reader offset. Learn the two population coefficients on peer history responses using regularized Binomial likelihood (normal prior with standard deviation four, centered at intercept zero and slope one). Model the reader offset using a discrete zero-centered 9-point grid from -3 to +3 in 0.75 steps. For the named spelling, reweight the fixed source model's conditional named probability by a reader-dependent positive exponent. Use the seven previously registered sharpnesses 2^(k/3), k=-3,...,3. For each of these two processes, fit the population weights by maximum marginal likelihood over peer readers with the same symmetric Dirichlet regularizer and convergence condition as the original study. A missing named choice provides no information about its spelling. Estimate each new reader's posterior from exactly those twenty historical choices; the conditional class probability and named-spelling distributions each sum to one. Their product gives a proper probability law for every named spelling and the single outside category. Integrate over both priors when individual history is absent.

The primary comparison is the paired total log gain from transferring the correct new person's twenty choices rather than the original same-material yoked stranger's twenty responses. Both arms must share the identical cross-fitted population prior and base distributions. Also compare with that identical unknown-reader prior, the original all-source single head, and the already recorded zero-shot predictions. Report crossed reader-by-sentence intervals and decompose gain into outside propensity and exact named spelling. All enrolled first-page histories stay disjoint from their owner's later assignments. The matched stranger is fixed by the original exposure-only lock. Preserve overall and per-reader median gain, membership coverage and converged optimizer receipts. Treat any positive finding as conditional on observing sample first pages from an additional cohort. Do not use it to assert an unobserved person's factual subject knowledge.
