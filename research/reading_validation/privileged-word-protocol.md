# A single-encoder readout of word information shared by three language models

Three frozen encoders supply a different view of the same printed word in its causal context. We test whether selecting contextual directions reproducible across alternative model architectures helps one deployed encoder match measured reading responses. The human behavioral and neurophysiological outcomes do not define this shared subspace. The study is registered before original Qwen, LFM and Gemma return their new human score comparisons or the nine-laboratory voltage measurements are opened. The previously published direction of that laboratory experiment and the earlier sparse-panel findings are known.

Use the exact source words, masks, folds, pretrained checkpoints and native states in the three-backbone study. All transformation and cross-view estimation must take place on training sentences only. First compute the six elementary word/surprisal predictors for each backbone. For that backbone, estimate and subtract the component of each native hidden channel linearly explained by those predictors and the intercept. Standardize the residual dimensions on the same source words. Retain 96 whitened principal coordinates of each training view. For Qwen whitened residual coordinates \(U_Q\), calculate their cross-covariances with LFM and Gemma \(C_{Qv}=U_Q^\top U_v/n\). Project Qwen onto the leading 24 eigenvectors of \((C_{QL}C_{QL}^\top+C_{QG}C_{QG}^\top)/2\). This selects the Qwen directions whose trained linear predictions from the other two views account for the most variance on identical human word exposures.

The comparator retains the leading 24 Qwen-only residual principal directions. Both methods receive the same six native-Qwen surface and surprisal predictors and the same 24-dimensional contextual allowance, observation laws, data, training steps and covariance estimation. A more basic surface-only fit remains available from the three-architecture study. The two non-Qwen models are training-time measurements only. A deployed model multiplies the native state at the current Qwen token by a fixed linear transform and computes the three readouts. Independent Spanish and nine-laboratory human assessments use the trained Qwen route alone. Do not use extra language-model calls, a learned architecture gate on held words or target-domain normalization.

The primary comparison is the mean held log-density gain over three prespecified English measures, relative to the dimension-matched Qwen-only residual comparator, with a paired 95% sentence-bootstrap interval and all three endpoint-specific gains. Report the gain over surface only as a separate comparison, the three held correlations, Spanish EEG and the comparison between the article and its following noun. Use the same dense source training followed by a prespecified ten-sentence EEG sensitivity, if it fits the closed budget. Keep full rank and principal angles for a numerical audit. A valid one-forward projection at runtime is required, even if results are inconclusive.
