# Does the sentence-difficulty model select the rewrite that people find easier?

Readers judging a draft can prefer one of two revisions of the same source sentence. We test this prediction on [ASSET](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.424/), using its [published judgment files](https://github.com/facebookresearch/asset/tree/9d659040d0d8942dbc4cd65cf357563b43fd9ab4/human_ratings). This analysis uses the fitted ReadMe++ difficulty model without adaptation. We chose this source after finishing the ReadMe++ analysis. No ASSET preferences, ratings or outcome-linked predictions have been inspected. The lab previously used PLABA paired rewrites. A repository search found no earlier ASSET scoring. ASSET and ReadMe++ include material drawn from Wikipedia. Check normalized target strings, exact source strings and five-word shingles against all ReadMe++ train/validation and auxiliary training materials before scoring. Report coverage. Exclude a source family if any of its passages has (a) an exact normalized match or (b) at least two five-word shingles and at least 80% of the shorter passage's distinct five-word shingles in common with a training item.

## Primary comparison

The authors asked which of two simplifications of the same sentence is easier to read and understand. Their form permits a tie. For each unique pair, choose the alternative with the lower predicted expected English CEFR level from the fixed source-adaptive Qwen readout. Compare with the correspondingly trained surface-only readout, and with simply choosing the shorter alternative (word count). In each case an exact numerical tie receives an expected half credit when readers express a preference. For each source family average agreement across its non-tied human votes, then average equally over families. Summarize human ties explicitly and report how often human votes were available. An all-tied family provides no directional observations. Bootstrap whole source families 10,000 times with seed 40617 for 95% intervals and paired differences. Report the recorded two comparison sets separately and pooled. No choices use the dataset of origin of a simplified variant. Compare to an always-choose-ASSET method as a metadata prevalence control.

We process each presented text as a single unit, including variants rewritten into several shorter sentences. The score combines the frozen causal state at its last visible token and surface features of the entire unit. The model receives only the variant, without a question, options or generation. Both revisions are assessed independently. The single-sentence subset is a sensitivity check. Test meaning preservation and fluency preferences separately on the same frozen ranking as specificity checks. A lower predicted difficulty cannot certify that a rewrite preserved meaning. No changes to the deployed readouts are selected from this dataset.

## Absolute ratings

The second published file contains selected system rewrites with human simplicity, fluency, and meaning ratings. For each source, score the original and its rewritten text. Report Spearman agreement between predicted difficulty reduction (original minus rewrite) and the mean human simplicity rating of the rewrite. Also report rank agreement for its predicted final ease. The human score rates the presented rewrite in a comparison with the original. The magnitude units differ from CEFR levels. A correlation across source sentences does not establish within-pair preference performance.

Both analyses use the same previously sealed ReadMe++-supervised and auxiliary-only readout weights. Model comparison uses the supervised head as primary, with transfer from auxiliary studies as secondary. We report the outcome of every declared contrast. These materials assess user preference, not moment-by-moment comprehension.
