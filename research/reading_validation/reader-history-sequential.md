# Time-respecting updates from each newly completed page

The prespecified twenty-observation study found a clear zero-shot benefit of exposure to the target cohort but limited reader specificity. After source-independent peer calibration, the correct twenty-answer history beats a stranger and falls behind the calibrated unknown-reader model. A static short history may produce a noisy personalized estimate. This follow-up assesses whether subsequent completed questionnaires improve the same parameter estimates without changing the model family or selecting a history length on the target scores. Aggregate results from this cohort are known, so this is a retrospective and time-respecting test.

Reuse the 25 fixed peer priors and their original source/personal folds. For each held person, predict a future answer using only that person's observed, nonempty responses to other stimuli in assignments already submitted when the new assignment was accepted. Withhold the entire evaluation sentence fold from all prior updates, include the originally registered twenty on the first page, and then admit previously scored answers only after their assignments' genuine submission time. Repeated target exposures remain ineligible. No item response times within an open page are inferred. Use the whole available history, capped by the number of observations available from that person's previously fixed same-material stranger. Require at least twenty prior distinct word choices with a matched control. Break simultaneous completion ties by fixed stem identity. The matched stranger supplies the same number of disjoint non-test-fold responses in their own chronological order. No answer to the current or any other evaluation-fold stem can enter any profile.

Use the already fitted, conditionally independent random-effects distribution for outside-lexicon propensity and named-word sharpness. Compare the target person's exact posterior, its matched stranger, the unchanged peer-cohort prior, and an experience-matched population. Form the latter as a mixture over every other originally eligible person with at least as many past choices, using exactly that many responses from each. Average full joint response probabilities over the other people, preserving cross-person dependence between choosing outside the lexicon and naming a word. The primary estimand is mean proper log-score gain of the actual personal history over this experience-matched population. Secondary prespecified controls are the single matched stranger and fixed unknown-reader prior. Report reader-by-stem intervals, named versus residual decomposition, total coverage, future queries excluded for short calibrations, and gains by experience bands (20–49, 50–199, 200–799, 800+ earlier responses). The sign of a result in a chosen band does not replace the full-data primary estimand. No feedback updates the encoder. Each text still has one forward regardless of the number of observed previous responses. [Fixed source data and primary analysis](reader-history-results.md)
