# Human readers respond more strongly to a changed premise than the frozen conflict readout

Two groups of readers judged each sentence of an English story. In a fixed target sentence, changing one earlier detail increased the proportion giving their **first** inconsistency response there by 32.1 percentage points in Experiment 2 and 31.7 points in Experiment 3. CogLLM predicted a 4.54-point increase for the same passages. It detected a contextual difference but substantially underestimated the change in human behavior. The 12 original story families repeat across cohorts. A deterministic model gives the same mean condition effect for both cohorts. Each reading calibration omitted the corresponding Experiment 1 family and the strict analysis discarded the original joint reader's channels. The authors released 280 and 239 complete raw records with all 24 comprehension checks. Applying their exclusion threshold of at least 40% incorrect checks removes four and six readers, reproducing the published cohorts exactly. A participant read six text-only stories in one condition. Crossed 95% intervals resample readers within condition and the twelve story families. An early alarm removes a reader from the risk set for a first report at a later sentence. The frozen semantic model overpredicted the proportion reporting before the target by 12.1–16.6 percentage points across the four condition-by-cohort cells. [Design and participant exclusions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-026-01900-7#Sec20) · [Family exposure control](memory-exposure-amendment.md)

The semantic readout trained on 1,446 human-written premise-revision triples. A matched development split contained 199 triples and the test set contained 400. On held-out texts with intervening distractors, it ranked the contradiction above both other variants in 83.0% of families [79.25, 86.50]. Adjacent statements gave 89.25% [86.0, 92.25]. Training the frozen 2,049-parameter projection took 384 L-BFGS iterations with final maximum gradient below 0.000001. The competing adapter added 69,632 query and value parameters in the last global attention layer. Across three epochs its training loss fell from 0.552 to 0.515, but development loss was 0.665, 0.634 and 0.640 against 0.615 at initialization. The prespecified patience rule selected epoch zero. Both saved models therefore give the same predictions. This run does not justify a population-wide claim about LoRA or language-model weights. The GPU run used one NVIDIA L4 for 820 seconds of training and evaluation with 8.33 GB peak allocated device memory. Each of 55 distinct articles passed through the frozen encoder once per model condition. A trimmed-prefix check gave relative error 0.0000013. Study endpoints are keypresses at completed sentences. A reader could notice a word before giving a response. The data do not time that earlier realization or identify where a misunderstanding was subsequently resolved.
