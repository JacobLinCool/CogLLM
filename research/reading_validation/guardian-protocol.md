# Can article-wide estimates anticipate readers' perceived understanding and coherence?

Thirty participants in the Guardian Reading Dataset each read eighteen newspaper excerpts and gave seven-point appraisals. Each excerpt included the title followed by a body truncated at 1,200 characters and a terminal ellipsis. Texts were originally selected at three levels using a readability algorithm. The release gives each body's first and last approximately 50 words, source URL, topical familiarity and separate semantic differentials for difficulty reading, difficulty understanding, incomprehensibility and incoherence. Reader identifiers were removed. The 17 recovered original bodies and the broken link were inspected without any response fields or model comparisons. Freeze the following outcome directions and analyses before scoring. [Original human experiment](https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.24657) · [Official tables and text excerpts](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.27057958.v1)

For each source article recover a unique ordered pair of *complete* lexical anchors in the public publisher's article body and standfirst. Normalize word case and straight/curly apostrophes for matching. Preserve the publisher's internal words and punctuation in the recovered passage. Restrict to continuous unique spans of 1,150–1,210 characters excluding the final ellipsis. Record source URL, ingress and egress, length, hash and retrieval route. Readers saw a title separately from the truncated body. The annotated source's 100-word quotation covers the body; its historical title and original whitespace cannot be independently certified. Prespecify the reconstructed body plus the documented terminal ellipsis as the primary model input. Run a sensitivity analysis including the currently published headline followed by the same body. Keep all news text in the local research cache and release only traces/derived numbers and links. The article labeled as a broken URL is unavailable unless both anchors can be verified without using any outcome.

Use the shipped English sentence-understanding head trained on prior scientific-text reader ratings, the shipped coherence head trained on narrative sentence judgments, and the original token-wise forward readout. All formulas are frozen outside Guardian. One native Qwen forward per complete presentation returns all readouts. For each article, average the sentence-level understanding ratings equally over certified sentences, reversing the 1–5 ease/understanding scale so high means difficult. This is the primary difficulty predictor. The primary coherence predictor is the unweighted mean of the calibrated transition probabilities over all eligible sentence boundaries after the opening. Record sentence/word coverage. As diagnostic comparisons include the highest transition risk, the average and 90th percentile of unresolved mass, and the mean word surprisal using the same native pass. Include two surface baselines, automated readability index (4.71·letters-per-word + 0.5·words-per-sentence − 21.43), and Coleman–Liau (0.0588·letters-per-100-words − 0.296·sentences-per-100-words − 15.8). These comparisons are equal-source and independent of this study's reader responses.

The difficulty endpoint is the article mean of the three author-designated semantic differentials easy-to-understand versus hard-to-understand (60), comprehensible versus incomprehensible (61), and coherent versus incoherent (62), oriented from easier to harder. The coherence endpoint is item 62 alone in its increasing-incoherence direction. Check the raw endpoint coding independently against the authors' documented reverse scoring: the difficulty composite should be inversely monotonic with released appraised comprehensibility. If that sign cannot be established from the release, omit the problematic endpoint until the codebook discrepancy is explained. The secondary reading-ease endpoint is the single easy-to-read versus difficult-to-read item 59. Do not select a direction from correlations with a model.

Use rank correlations across recovered articles (maximum 17 independent stimulus units) and 10,000 article bootstrap intervals. Report paired correlation differences from both readability baselines on the same resamples. With two primary tests, report familywise-adjusted two-sided article-permutation p-values by Holm correction alongside independent bootstrap intervals. Recompute consumer-facing metrics including the current title and using high- versus low-reported topical familiarity, using a within-stimulus median split with tied cases omitted. Report numbers of sessions in each analysis. Anonymous readers cannot be independently clustered across their readings; intervals describe the observed stimulus sample. A correlation across articles supplies no token-level truth and no confirmation of a reader's point of confusion inside a particular article.
