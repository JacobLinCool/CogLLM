# Local difficulty in English biomedical abstracts

We test whether the content readers single out as difficult can be located more accurately by distinguishing local lexical difficulty from a persistent unresolved issue. PLABA-EVAL includes original and plain-language biomedical abstracts, five-category sentence ratings, and optional written comments. The sentences and both article versions were visible during annotation. A subsequently audited inventory contains 94 distinct expressions that readers explicitly mentioned, in 19 article families. Earlier project runs used these materials. This is a retrospective development experiment with held-out predictions during fitting. It cannot establish an online time of onset or a negative label for unmentioned words. [Original data](https://github.com/SDS-NLP/PLABA-EVAL/tree/153082e7d58b833d5e2f481b92fdcf9248c60d11) · [Observation audit](../results/plaba-explicit-span-audit.json)

## Models and evaluation

1. Apply the exact selected Qwen3.5-2B curve checkpoint (SHA-256 beginning `c1704d44`) and the previously trained CompLex target-end coefficients to the same native forward for every abstract. Save per-token unresolved mass, increases in that mass, and source offsets. Save independent local lexical scores and the small shared projections. Recompute the frozen lexical scores on word-final states only for words whose source endpoint is certified by the tokenizer. Align all readouts to the original Unicode character coordinates.
2. Rank candidate source spans of one through eight consecutive words within a supplied sentence. Score raw displayed unresolved mass and raw one-step increases at the word level as separate diagnostic arms. As a benchmark train a source-span ranking model using length, position and word frequency (from CompLex training material). An external medical-vocabulary predictor estimated using only the published MedReadMe training split supplements the surface benchmark. The local candidate adds frozen CompLex difficulty and a regularized representation of the source span. An additional persistent-curve arm tests whether the selected model adds information. Evaluate properly separated local difficulty and persistence individually. Raw app readouts and shared projection variants inherit the historical exposure of the jointly trained model to PLABA.
3. Fit span heads by leaving out the entire article/reader connected component for the named-span endpoint. A reader's named source span supplies positive mass within a supplied sentence. Use a conditional likelihood over the candidate spans rather than labeling all unmentioned words easy. The target distribution is uniform over candidates with the largest character intersection over union with a named span. A span is recovered when character intersection over union is at least 0.5. The primary outcome is top-five recovery with spatially disjoint proposals. Report top-one, exact hits, and mean reciprocal rank additionally. Report whole-document top-five recovery as a separate diagnostic. Report intervals from resampling reader-connected article groups, using a common bootstrap draw for competing models. Fixed seeds, feature budgets and ridge penalties are set in the executable. Where a component only contributes to evaluation, hold its labels out of training, scaling, and calibration.
4. On the original five PLABA folds, retain both versions of each article and their connected readers in one fold. Test whether local difficulty features improve prediction of ease and self-rated comprehension beyond length, position, and article condition. Use real category counts and score category log likelihood, calibration of the expected rating, and pair-level mean absolute error for human rewrite effects. Include the existing frozen sentence-head forecasts as a separate historical comparator. Treat fine temporal claims and any calibration of absolute load as unresolved. Current-app features trained historically on some study records are diagnostic only. Freeze the fold-specific predictions and write their checksums.
5. Keep the current curves for the public demo unchanged. Record execution, hashes of data and weights, optional GPU availability, all comparisons (including negative results), and any difference between the planned and implemented assays. No new participants are recruited.

The raw checkpoint, CompLex training data, and prior PLABA, Kerwer, MedReadMe and article-group experiments were available before this protocol. No new predictions or comparison results were accessed when writing it.
