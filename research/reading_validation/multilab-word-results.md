# Where an unexpected continuation affects measured reading physiology

Nine laboratories tested 334 readers on 80 English passage pairs. Each pair contained an expected or less expected noun and its preceding indefinite article. Across 25,844 original article responses and 25,710 verified noun responses, unexpected endings produced 0.200 µV more N400 negativity at the article (95% crossed interval −0.368 to 0.770) and 1.602 µV more at the noun (0.935–2.291). The excess noun effect was 1.402 µV (0.503–2.274). Leaving out each laboratory in turn preserved this difference, at 1.34–1.52 µV. This reanalysis used the investigators’ original response window, coding and exact printed alternatives. A Qwen contextual readout trained on a separate English EEG study predicted the larger response at the noun. Across the 80 pairs, its predictions correlated with the actual size of each noun contrast at ρ = 0.423 (0.013–0.547). The corresponding article correlation was 0.060 (−0.223–0.344). Raw Qwen surprise reached 0.341 at the noun and a statistical readout of surface features plus native surprise reached 0.355. The contextual increment over the latter was 0.068, with a paired crossed interval of −0.078 to 0.189. The running unresolved-issue score reached 0.326 at nouns and −0.067 at articles. Correct average localization alone can be obtained by always choosing the noun. The positive noun-item association for the source-trained contextual head addresses variation among materials that a fixed positional rule cannot express. It still leaves substantial variation and a competitive simpler baseline. The human article-to-noun contrast ratio was 0.125 (−0.250–0.541). Qwen's independent English-trained EEG readout gave 0.245, compared with 0.228 for raw Qwen surprise. Raw lexical surprise thus also localizes much of the average response difference. The human split-half reliability of individual item contrasts was 0.146 at the article and 0.475 at the noun. These values measure agreement among independent reader halves and limit the interpretation of strongly differing correlations across the two positions. Bootstrap inference sampled entire laboratories, people nested in laboratories and original passage families. Two of 3,000 resamples lacked a verified observation for a required cell. The primary analysis excluded 138 noun trials with a discrepancy between their original randomized list and the published cloze value; including them by the reconstructed list changed the mean noun effect from 1.602 to 1.602 µV. ## Do explicit meanings of readers’ alternative words add explanatory power?

We fixed a position-specific next-word distribution using 135,905 actual choices from an English norming study, then measured the semantic distance between the incoming word and alternative expected words. The comparison removed probability on the actual target, retained its exact native whole-word surprise, print features and static semantics, and evaluated the readout on held source sentences and the independent nine-laboratory data. Among 1,546 source words, adding the population-based semantic distance moved N400 correlation from 0.4281 to 0.4280 (change −0.0001, 95% interval −0.0033–0.0034). Independent noun-item correlation changed from 0.4163 to 0.4136 (−0.0026, −0.0240–0.0208). This sixteen-coordinate semantic representation did not establish added physiological predictive value, even though its underlying word probabilities tracked actual changes in reader guesses.
