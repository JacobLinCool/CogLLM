# Verification of detection labels and exposure for the edit comparison

The official DECOR test tables encode coherent passages with label 1.0 and blank reason and rewrite fields. Every original labeled 0.0 contains a rewrite or an instruction to edit the text. We map 0.0 to the incoherence event and verify the complementary fields row by row. The README describes the empty fields and the correspondence between expert corrections and incoherent source sentences. One field R1 is −1.0 and is treated as missing for the reason-specific endpoint. Its main detection judgment remains available. We verified the published schema and marginal counts before fitting the contextual models or scoring their held-out predictions. These counts play no part in fitting, threshold selection or model choice. [Official data documentation](https://github.com/BillyZhang24kobe/writing2coherence/tree/382b4fa086351202f5ac514fcd2b86a5479882e1#decor-data-format)

In the first extraction the raw versions shared identical article prefixes on all 213 original–rewrite pairs. In the auxiliary directed format some original documents contained blank response markers at earlier sentence boundaries that did not appear in the edited excerpt. We identified this mismatch before scoring. The dedicated matched packet puts the same earlier turn markers into both versions, asserts exact equality of the original text before the edit, and scores only the final target. Two source/edit entries are textually identical, giving 424 distinct forward calls. We report the paired directed estimate only from this packet. [Preparation](transition_matched.py) · [Forecast implementation](transition_matched_forecast.py)

The benchmark paper reports that exactly 906 sentences are coherent and 446 are incoherent and compares weighted F1 on these essays. We will also report this quantity at our fixed, source-calibrated alert threshold. Its operating rule differs from the original methods. For an author inspecting one article, cross-article performance could reflect genre or whole-document quality. Before looking at the contextual forecasts we added a within-article check. We compare pairs of different sentences within the same essay or story with one human problem mark and one nonproblem mark. A correct ordering receives one point and a tie half a point. Each eligible document contributes one mean. We resample documents for uncertainty. [DECOR, §5 and Table 3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.639.pdf)

Of the 213 expert-edit rows, nine contain a target identical to the source after whitespace normalization. Seven of those occur in separate complete/excerpted document records. Their common source prefix permits an empirical numerical invariance check. One further row supplies the editorial instruction “Delete second half of sentence” as its revision. We report all 213 registered source rows and a sensitivity analysis of the 203 changed-text pairs that supply a replacement sentence. Identical texts count as a tie after checking their numerical differences. Each model's raw deviations on these matching endpoint texts are reported separately. The 424 distinct input identifiers distinguish source records even when two complete input strings happen to match. The additional analyses were specified before scoring either held-out benchmark.

For context on individual reader variability we will also ask whether the other readers of that same CoheSentia sentence anticipate a selected reader's mark. For each reader trial, the peer score is the fraction of the remaining local readers who marked a coherence problem. At least two valid readers are required. This diagnostic has access to human answers for the same held-out material and is an agreement measure, not a deployable text baseline. We use the same story resampling and report its AP and AUROC.
