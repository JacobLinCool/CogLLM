# Locating the words a reader says they did not understand

Paetzold and Specia asked non-native English speakers to mark every content word they personally could not understand while reading an English sentence. They could explicitly say they understood all words, including in a sentence with difficult-looking vocabulary. This procedure provides both positive and negative observations about specific printed words. The original authors report 400 volunteers, 200 planned multiply rated sentences, and 9,000 with one rater. The public release contains 158,624 rows and 9,128 distinct printed strings. Exactly 201 strings have 15–26 judgments for each designated word. For 8,927 strings every designated word has 1–5 judgments. We checked these counts without comparing any judgment to a forecast. The public file provides self-reported English proficiency and no persistent reader or Wikipedia article ID. [Experiment 1, original prompts and selection](https://aclanthology.org/C16-1069/) · [Authors' archive](https://ghpaetzold.github.io/data/User_Studies_NNS.zip)

**Evaluation material.** Reserve all 201 higher-replication sentences for model evaluation. Only the lower-replication pool may contribute judgments for training, validation of pooling, or term priors. Normalize text for duplicate detection, retain original Unicode for scoring, group exact equivalents and close five-word-shingle matches, and omit suspected material overlap from the primary. Compare to previous CompLex, PLABA, MedReadMe, ReadMe, CWI-2018 and earlier supervised source ranges, including model-selection texts when identifiable. A 12-word shared contiguous span excludes both members of a sparse–dense pair from simultaneous use. The primary drops dense source matches and removes conflicting sparse items for direct sparse–dense overlap. The remaining target and source text families are disjoint. Available public data cannot establish the absence of unsupervised language-model pretraining exposure or identify unseen individual readers.

**Location.** Map the observed target string to one complete, unique printed word inside the exact released sentence, with Unicode case folding only. Drop repeated instances of that string, different tokenizations and words missing exact prefix certification. Publish coverage at each step. The model receives original text only when the optional reader proficiency is absent. It processes each original sentence once in a causal, length-sorted batch. Every inference position must have identical tokenization to the prefix ending at the final character. Check direct neural prefix equivalence on a fixed subset. Only the end of an observed word carries the measured result. The study does not observe the precise instant at which the reader recognized an unfamiliar word.

**Primary.** Within each held sentence compare every pair of uniquely aligned, designated words whose observed fraction of readers reporting difficulty differs. Count 1 for a correct ordering, 0 for a reversed ordering and ½ for a tie. Average within sentence, then within independent material family, then equally across families. A constant gives 50%. The registered primary comparison is text-only adapted CogLLM versus the sparse-trained lexical and curriculum baseline. Require a positive lower bound on the paired 95% interval from 10,000 family bootstrap resamples (seed 631402). Report mean Bernoulli log loss, expected Brier loss against actual 0/1 responses, and average precision on the item-level binary outcome of any reader flagging a word separately. A discrimination claim requires the primary criterion. A calibration claim additionally requires an improvement in log loss over the same baseline.

**Comparisons.** Report fixed CompLex and fixed PLABA single-word scores independently versus raw English Zipf frequency, word length, and published CEFR vocabulary levels. In addition, fit a strong baseline on the sparse release using English Zipf frequency, length and morphology measures, the published English CEFR-J A1–B2 and Octanove C1–C2 vocabulary profile (including coverage), prefix word position and a training-only smoothed word-type rate. Exclude the entire source material family when assembling its own word prior. The extended model adds the frozen CompLex score, frozen PLABA single-word logit, native 32D PLABA-era random projected hidden state and separate fixed 64D projection from Qwen. Use the same source rows, training weights, regularizer and fold structures in all matched arms. A third intermediate arm adds just the two frozen human-supervised estimates. Select how much the full contextual model can borrow by a single convex interpolation of its probabilities with the baseline; estimate this weight only on held sparse-family predictions under log loss, then refit both readouts on the whole sparse study. No model selection uses dense judgments.

Fit a regularized binomial likelihood to the actual counts, with every designated measurement from the same sentence receiving equal weight within its text and equal total weight per sparse family. Center and scale feature columns on the fitting partition. Fix the ridge at 0.003 on mean binomial loss. For the optional proficiency analysis combine the same features with the reader's reported A1–C2 group and its interactions with word familiarity, curriculum position, and fixed CompLex. Use the actual response totals per proficiency stratum, never reader age, first language, education or inferred identity. Compare conditional and unconditional models on identical target groups. Also compare two contexts containing the same folded word whenever the observed rate differs; report denominators, finite uncertainty and results for rare or unseen types. Outcomes may diverge by proficiency or by context even when overall within-sentence ranking is strong.

**Human agreement.** Estimate the probability two distinct respondents disagree on the identical word in the identical sentence as $2k(n-k)/(n(n-1))$ from its released count $k$ among $n$ respondents. Report the distribution of words with unanimous versus mixed responses and programmatically check nominal group counts. Contrast variance across and within proficiency strata, retaining sparse A1 denominators. The missing persistent reader ID precludes a valid individual split-half reliability ceiling across word rankings. The reported pointwise human disagreement measures a separate quantity. Article drafts and personal information are unnecessary for GPU extraction. All raw judgments stay in the local cache.
