# What do the writer's widening and narrowing marks predict?

On 1,566 consecutive words in 46 Provo paragraphs, the contextual observer marked 239 words as widening readers' expectations and 243 as narrowing them. Of these 482 marks, **422 matched the direction of the change in people's actual next-word answers** (87.6%, 95% paragraph-bootstrap interval 83.8–91.1%). At 315 marked words the human change also reached the stated magnitude of 20 percentage points (65.4%, 59.8–71.3%). The rule recovered 315 of the 614 large human changes and left 1,084 positions unmarked. A highlight has an observable interpretation at a particular word. It carries no direct measure of how hard the word or sentence felt to read. ## Why a large change can disappear when unknown words are counted correctly

At each prefix the model gives individual probabilities for 8,192 named spellings and a single probability r for everything else. Let D be disagreement over these 8,193 categories. If the unlisted spellings have probabilities u₁, u₂, … summing to r, full-spelling disagreement equals D + r² − Σu². Its possible range is [D, D + r²]. Concentrating r on one spelling gives the lower end. Splitting it among many spellings approaches the upper end. This is a range permitted by the model probabilities, with no claim of statistical calibration to a population of readers. [Derivation and registered endpoint](word-mark-certification-protocol.md) · [Numerical identity checks](../results/reading-validation-word-marks/scores.json)

Subtracting consecutive ranges gives [L_after − U_before, U_after − L_before]. Mark a widening only when the lower endpoint reaches +0.20 and a narrowing only when the upper endpoint reaches −0.20. None of the 188 overlapping contextual level ranges produced a mark. An explicit check split residual mass among 1, 2, 8 and 10,000 hypothetical spellings up to r = 0.96. The formula agreed to 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵. The archived and live Qwen forecasts selected identical marks at all 1,566 decisions. The largest difference in their predicted change bounds was 0.000101. [Decision implementation](word_mark_certification.py) · [Existing native serving validation](../results/reading-validation-cloze-population/native-serving-verification.json)

One example shows why an unmarked word can still matter. Among the 83 marks removed by this check, the largest residual mass occurred after *United* in paragraph 48. The pooled model predicted a fall of 0.810, with 99.4% of the next-word probability assigned to unlisted spellings. The full-spelling change could range from −0.975 to +0.179. The readers' observed change was −0.745. The model therefore abstained on a genuine large human narrowing. This example was selected by residual mass alone. Requiring the full-spelling bound improved precision over the old contextual point rule by **6.06 percentage points** [3.75, 8.77] for a change large enough and in the marked direction. Direction-only precision improved by 3.13 points [1.24, 5.32]. Recall of large human changes decreased by 3.26 points [−5.60, −1.31]. The 83 discarded marks comprised 63 that failed the size-and-direction criterion and 20 that met it. Raw Qwen received the same vocabulary projection and the same bound. The trained contextual observer changed large-event precision by +1.28 points [−2.24, +5.19] and recall by −0.98 points [−4.21, +2.21] relative to that stronger comparator. The results support the display rule itself. A distinct advantage from the learned head remains uncertain at this operating point. ## Measurement and replay

People supplied actual next words at individual prefixes. We recomputed empirical disagreement from the complete normalized spelling counts with the finite-sample formula 1 − Σ n_w(n_w − 1) / (n(n − 1)). The 1,791 original denominator-verified prefixes include 71,647 nonempty answers. Every included pair comprises consecutive printed positions in the same original paragraph. At a target ordinal t, the observation describes the prefix *before* that word. Its change at t is therefore the distribution before t + 1 minus the distribution before t. Native input tokens, character offsets, printed words and single-space adjacency all had to agree. The last position of a paragraph has no measured continuation change. The earlier scores on this Provo cohort were already known. The new flags and complete-spelling size-and-direction endpoint were fixed before their evaluation. The signed change describes different response distributions at consecutive prompts. Individual evolving beliefs remain unobserved.
