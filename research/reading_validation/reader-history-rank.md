# Can personal history change the order of specific expected spellings?

Every positive mixture of positive powers of the same word-probability vector preserves its ranking. The earlier reader model could change how much probability it assigns to the person's actual word but could never rank a different named candidate first. This follow-up tests a single new reader axis capable of reversing word order. Some readers may preferentially choose more familiar, common words, even among words assigned similar probabilities by the given sentence. The objective is the probability of genuinely chosen named spellings, conditional on an answer belonging to the independent named dictionary. The previously scored English and German response cohorts and results are known. All existing primary outcomes, model weights and scopes are preserved.

Use the same English 25 reader and stem folds, the same strictly later English assignments and their exact matched quantity of previously supplied responses. German calibration uses the identical 133 auditory people; test uses the 98 visual people and the 140 written-only materials, the exact previously measured shared-stem conditioning sets, and the same matched control. Freeze the source-calibrated lexical logits, the response-mode sharpnesses and their population weights π. In each language standardize log independent wordfreq probability across the sealed named dictionary. For frequency preference γ = −1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, +1, define a named response distribution q_γ(w|i) = Σ_b π_b p_i(w)^b exp(γ z(w)) / Σ_v [p_i(v)^b exp(γ z(v))], with normalization inside each β term. Thus each mode adds a bounded global preference for word commonness in competition with its genuinely context-sensitive probability. All five functions are capable of assigning mass to every named spelling. Fit their population weights and individual mixture dependence with the previously registered per-response likelihood and genuine within-reader pair likelihood. A learner's online responsibilities can shift mass between modes; neither its encoder nor its base probability table changes.

The primary endpoint is conditional named-word log gain of each person's frequency-mode forecast over the exact same observer marginalized across other people with matched amounts of history. Report the same amount of history assigned from a single matched stranger, the fitted cohort mode prior, and the prior model's population sharpness alone. Report the occurrence-weighted share of personal named-word argmax changes against its matched population, and the unconditional difference in top-word correctness on the same named response observations. Conditional performance where the two argmaxes disagree is a descriptive secondary endpoint; with very few reversals say explicitly how few. Compute exact top predictions, or certify them using rigorous upper bounds on each unexamined word from the strongest and weakest component scores. Provide uncertainty over independent reader and sentence clusters, and separate English from German. Avoid converting a probability calibration gain into a claim of changed individual word identity unless the actual forecasts and human choices support it. The empirical cohorts have been examined for a different mechanism; this analysis can identify whether a rank-changing correction is useful and cannot be advertised as a fresh cohort replication.
