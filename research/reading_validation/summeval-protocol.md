# Which draft would readers judge more coherent for the same source story?

The public SummEval study contains 16 system-produced summaries of each of 100 news stories with three expert and five crowd assessments of coherence, consistency, fluency and relevance in the original design. The endpoint is a human judgment about the organization of the whole draft. The two estimated source models previously used actual incremental English boundary marks and retrospective Italian passage ratings, or independent judges’ local coherence reasons in English essays. This third English cohort has no role in either original model. The original study and previously published large-model results are known. We register the following before inspecting any item-level rating. [Original study](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.24/) · [Official data](https://github.com/Yale-LILY/SummEval) · [Published G-Eval comparator](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153/)

**Article groups.** Group all 16 drafts by the underlying source story. Merge groups sharing an exact draft or any 12 consecutive normalized words across different stories, and audit direct overlap with the previously fitted English/Italian materials. Assign source-only calibration, primary confirmation and future sealed groups by deterministic SHA-256 order of source-group ID prefixed `cogllm-summeval-public-v1/`, in proportions 40%, 50%, 10% (floor the first two group counts). Report realized numbers of stories. Keep every ballot in the future reserve closed. Within an article, combine exact duplicate drafts and their coherence observations before comparing distinct wordings. Submit all distinct raw `decoded` strings, including display-tokenizer punctuation spaces, in one unmarked Qwen forward per draft. A raw missing or ambiguous segmentation is counted, and the source story remains within its assigned split. Individual ratings, rater IDs, task prompts, reference summaries and source news stories stay off the GPU. No new human data collection.

**Frozen forecasts and comparators.** Use the existing Italian-trained readout's expected article coherence as the primary frozen cross-study predictor. Source controls are the exact same Italian form, form + surprisal, and source-order augmented head. The second pre-existing model predicts a human expert marking an English essay break at an individual boundary. Average the complement of its hazard over all word-certified noninitial stops. Compare it to the corresponding lexical + probability model on the identical eligible noninitial-sentence summaries. Assess mean and maximum risk only if explicitly labelled secondary. Compare higher coherence between unequal expert means for two eligible summaries of the *same* story (ties in predictions receive half), averaging first within story then across eligible stories. Report all counts. Head-to-head comparisons restrict to the same draft pairs; a one-sentence draft cannot supply an essay transition. Report draft-only correlations and expert- vs crowd-rated coherence separately. No goal of checking factual consistency from isolated summaries.

**Anchors and ceiling.** Absolute lexical/format cues include word and sentence count, words per sentence, mean word length, unique-word proportion, repeated bigram/trigram fractions, duplicated sentence fraction, across-sentence word recurrence, punctuation markers and mean native word surprisal. Fit a fixed ridge (penalty 0.01 with unit-scale features, intercept unpenalized) to average expert coherence on the source calibration articles, weighting each source article equally. A complementary source-system reference estimates the mean grade of each known summarization system using calibration stories only. A source-aware reference measures maximum and mean unigram and bigram F1 against the 11 original published references and adds them to the source-only lexical fit. The independent G-Eval authors publicly release their GPT-4 coherence scores. Align them on original story/system ID and draft wording, apply their original parser, and report a paired within-story comparison with matched coverage. Its rubric can see the full source article and uses multiple outputs. Keep this costly source-aware reference separate from single-forward text-only inference. Rank crowd and expert outcomes independently. For a direct human reference, take a randomly isolated expert's score on each draft against the other expert judgments on the same two drafts, using each of the 3 × 3 possible pairs of held expert draws; report model performance on the identical remaining-two target comparisons. Use a deterministic single original row per duplicate wording in this human-agreement diagnostic. No assumption of a shared annotator ID across rows.

**Calibrated transfer.** On calibration stories only, fit the same unit-scale ridge once for lexical/form cues and once adding fixed native Italian/English source scores, including summary-level 1–5 expected Italian coherence and, when available on the same drafts, the mean calibrated English hazard and its source lexical equivalent. The raw bilingual and raw essay predictors stay intact. A new third-task fit adds a summary-level 1–5 observation link to the original unmodified 86-feature English/Italian objective, using actual calibration experts’ mean, a separate study intercept and scale and the existing English feature normalization. Share native slopes across all three cohorts. Estimate the same architecture independently on the same calibration summaries as a comparator. Retain the published bounds and penalties, start and end objective, finite-difference gradient and projected-gradient checks. Reuse exactly the same eligible boundaries and pooling in both native arms. Fit-only comparisons and all confirmatory estimates will be archived separately. The sharpest comparison asks whether the frozen or adapted human-reading signals improve prediction of human coherence within a news story beyond simple properties and native word probability.

**Inference.** Resample source families, preserving all drafts, expert votes and pairs. Use 10,000 draws. Prespecified three-comparison 95% simultaneous percentile intervals cover (i) frozen bilingual over its form + surprisal readout, (ii) frozen essay hazard over its matched native lexical baseline, and (iii) three-task shared fit over the identical single-task native fit. Give paired 95% intervals for calibrated reference, G-Eval, human crowd/peer and chance separately. Source-controlled comparisons use article-specific true response variation. Treat associations at whole-summary resolution and comparisons of complete competing drafts separately from local reports of when an individual reader felt a problem.
