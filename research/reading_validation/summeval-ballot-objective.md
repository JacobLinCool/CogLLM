# Comparing two drafts written about the same source article

We register this secondary objective before opening any SummEval ballot. The primary three comparisons, calibration, confirmation and future stories remain as specified in the original protocol. Article-level mean ratings answer a different statistical question from identifying the clearer of two drafts of the same news story. Actual draft scores share a topic. A reader's score can also differ from another reader's score for the same draft. We can use both observations directly when fitting the within-story criterion.

For each distinct pair of drafts of a calibration story, draw one expert rating on each independently from their empirical ballots, including all three observations from every original row of an identical wording. The target is the fraction of draws in which the first exceeds the second, with half credit for equal ratings. Fit a logistic comparison model by soft Bernoulli cross-entropy on the difference in the two feature vectors. Weight the pairs equally within story and stories equally. All pairs are retained, including those for which the empirical target is one half. Ballot draws and draft pairs from the same story are dependent. All held confidence intervals resample complete stories.

Fit two source-only models. The first uses the exact summary form and surprisal features in the main protocol. The second uses those same columns plus per-draft means and coordinatewise log-mean-exp of the 86 English-normalized causal boundary signals, capped at ±8 per boundary before aggregation. Including both average and high boundary signals allows ordinary local difficulties to accumulate within a draft while also representing unusually difficult regions. The additional input contains only the original text. Use the original source English scales for native boundary inputs and calibration-story-weighted means and unit scales for article inputs. Centering and standardization are estimated anew inside training. Source and confirmation articles have disjoint text.

Compare sample-size-matched linear ridge regression on absolute mean expert grade and logistic soft-choice regression on the same features. Use the same fixed 0.01 L2 coefficient convention as the main unit-scaled linear fit. For the adaptive model, compute one mixture of the two logits (form and native). Select the coefficient in [0, 1] by held calibration soft-choice cross-entropy on eight of the 40 calibration articles, selected by SHA-256 order of the group ID with prefix `cogllm-summeval-pair-mixture-v1/`. The voting target of those eight groups stays out of the two provisional fits. Refit the underlying two observers on all 40 calibration stories and retain the selected coefficient. Confirm the optimizer objective decrease, finite directional gradients and final gradient. Keep the ten future stories' outcomes closed. Export the actual fitting and mixture diagnostics.

Use two secondary contrasts on the 50 previously designated confirmation groups. Compare (i) the soft-choice native model with absolute-grade ridge on exactly the same raw article features using within-story draft ordering and (ii) the form/native adaptive mix with form-only soft-choice on held Bernoulli cross-entropy. Resample complete stories 10,000 times with simultaneous 95% intervals across these two directional contrasts. Also report score differences from the three-task shared head, imported prior readouts, published article-aware G-Eval, constant 0.5 and held-human agreement; these are comparisons of different input and judgment regimes. For cross-entropy compare the pairwise observers and uniform with native logits; the equally scaled absolute-grade prediction is not a calibrated logit. Distinguish the experts' independent whole-draft grade draws from choices explicitly elicited while viewing two versions side by side.
