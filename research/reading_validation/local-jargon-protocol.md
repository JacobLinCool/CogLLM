# Prospective transfer to familiarity with terms in scientific abstracts

Guo et al. asked eleven computer science researchers about the familiarity of terms in scientific abstracts and their need for supplementary explanations. The release includes term familiarity ratings before and after reading, requests for specific kinds of support, the original text and candidate terms. We will use only the original texts, IDs, term strings and source geometry before sealing predictions. The annotators' ratings and requests remain outside model training and forecast generation. This release was located during the present experiment, after the PLABA fitting plan was written and before any scores on these materials. [Original study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.255/) · [Release schema](https://github.com/talaugust/PersonalizedJargon/blob/22add4cc00274de199cc74bf0dbd629d05151b08/data/README.md)

## Primary hypothesis

Within one abstract read by one researcher, terms receiving a lower postreading familiarity rating should rank higher under the local difficulty readout. The primary predictor is the `local` span head specified in the PLABA protocol, fitted on all PLABA development components. It combines length/frequency, train-only medical terminology, the frozen CompLex rating, and a 32-dimensional projection of the Qwen state. The prespecified comparator is the PLABA-trained `form` head, with identical span candidates and without biomedical or LM signals. Secondary comparators are the previously served unresolved mass, the increase in that mass, the frozen CompLex score, the biomedical lexicon arm and the joint readout projection, as declared in the preceding protocol. These methods are all reported. We will not adapt coefficients or method selection to Guo outcomes.

We map a released term to its unique case-insensitive exact occurrence at Unicode word boundaries within the abstract. For the primary sample the term must start and end on the common word-span grid, have at most eight words, and lie within one sentence. We use fixed punctuation sentence boundaries for the new corpus. We report every exclusion and the retained reader, term, abstract and pair counts. A term absent, repeated, or outside this support receives no assigned score. Any sampled text whose article or sentence overlaps appreciably with fitting material will be excluded under the prespecified 25% distinct word five-gram rule at the article level for PLABA and sentence level for MedReadMe and CompLex.

For each reader and abstract we compare all pairs of eligible terms with unequal observed ratings. Concordance is the share ordered correctly, with 0.5 for a predicted tie. We average within reader/abstract, then over readers for each abstract, then equally over abstracts. Report paired differences and 95% two-way bootstrap intervals over anonymous readers and articles, and a document-only interval conditional on these surveyed readers. Fixed seed 60821, 10,000 draws. This is a test of ranking among the supplied terms. Participants rated designated terms and postreading familiarity. The study did not measure spontaneous token-level difficulty or the instant when an explanation took effect.

## Secondary tests

Repeat the ranking calculation on prereading familiarity, on binary requests for a definition and background (if the release supports unambiguous binary coding), and on the mean of reader postreading difficulty for each term. Report each outcome separately. For records with both familiarity ratings, compare postreading predictions to a reader's own prereading score and report the frequency and magnitude of change. An optional quantitative calibration may use article/reader-separated fits with all held-out forecasts first locked. Keep such within-study fits separate from the primary zero-shot transfer. Repeated ratings by one person do not constitute independent replications.

Only a one-pass text-only inference runs on the L4. No personal histories or reader-specific judgments go to the GPU. We will archive the pinned revision, local input hashes, coverage, raw predictions without participant text, fitting weights, and all outcome summaries.
