# Do preferences for complete news drafts locate actual expert-marked essay transitions?

This retrospective transport uses already published DECOR expert observations on 100 English learner essays. Prior CogLLM studies have examined these labels and their baseline rates. The target is the original judge-coded coherence problem at a noninitial sentence boundary. The 40 different SummEval calibration articles supplied the only news ballots used to fit the scalar ranking coefficients. We specify this transport before inspecting confirmation performance of that observer. Fit no coefficient, cutoff or mixture against DECOR.

Apply each locked news reader to the exact prefix ending at each completed sentence, retaining original text and token coordinates. Compute global form and word-surprisal inputs on the visible prefix. For two and more sentences, pool all earlier certified noninitial boundary vectors available in the original native archive, according to the training definition. For a one-sentence prefix, use its one initial sentence, as in training. Require the last sentence and immediately preceding prefix to have at least one certified boundary. Use one causal feature bank per full essay and explicit subword offset checks for every included prefix. Capture sentence-boundary scores at sentence ends. These data provide no individual-word onset annotation.

The primary local risk is the previous completed prefix score minus the current completed prefix score. A positive change indicates that the current sentence makes the observed prefix less like a coherently judged news draft. Apply the same difference to the fixed form and contextual word-probability model. Retain one static baseline for proportional position in the essay and the original frozen supervised English form-plus-word-probability boundary estimates on their matched source coordinates. Report the source-trained essay local expert head as a same-study benchmark when geometries and source-exclusion are verifiable. The primary source observation is sorted by original essay and target sentence. Compare a real human-marked target with a human-unmarked target from the same essay. Average comparisons within essay and essays equally. Give two simultaneous cluster-bootstrap intervals across (i) native/form calibrated news score change over chance and (ii) news score change over its identical form/surprisal-only prefix change. Include a separate comparison to the previous English local observer and disclose failure anywhere. Reuse the original calibration-selected native weight and observations in all work. The already published DECOR judgments and direct English controls make this a retrospective study-level transfer, separate from new-story confirmation in news.
