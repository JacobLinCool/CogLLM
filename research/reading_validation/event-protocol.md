# Which earlier event does a later sentence help explain?

A reader can understand that a particular fact explains one earlier event without having seen a written question. [TellMeWhy](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-acl.53/) records the source action behind each mechanically constructed why-question and the sentences that individual workers selected as helpful when answering it. The workers saw the whole story and the questions. We ask whether a model shown **only the five narrative sentences**, without a question, options or indicated event, can recover which two parts of the text they related. The data measure retrospective judgments of supporting content. They leave the timing of a reader's own spontaneous question unobserved.

Use the three official splits of the [authors' Hugging Face release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/StonyBrookNLP/tellmewhy/tree/662fce7ab3d2e18087973b1f15470b1dfaf81f9e/data). The model scores all ten pairs of earlier and later sentences in a five-sentence story. Each pair receives two scores. One estimates that the later sentence explains the earlier action. The other estimates that the earlier sentence helps explain the later action. A worker who was asked about an action and did not select a particular candidate supplies an observed negative for that candidate. Unasked actions supply no loss. Use each worker's own selections separately. Exclude the question's own sentence as candidate. The input contains every sentence, even when no worker was asked about it. Do not supply narrative IDs, question indices, or human-selected text as model features. Check normalized full-story and sentence matches across official partitions. Exclude train/dev stories sharing a full story or any contiguous two narrative sentences with an official test story. Disclose single-sentence overlap and source alignment failures.

The entire story passes through frozen Qwen once. For each already visible sentence pair, extract its distance, absolute sentence positions, length and word overlap, native global attention targeting the earlier sentence, similarity between matched content tokens, and projected sentence representations. Every feature used at sentence j must depend only on the prefix through j. The reader supplies no anchor at inference. Use a pooled human-vote Bernoulli loss, keeping earlier-explains-later and later-explains-earlier observations distinct. Fit three nested competitors with the same train-only feature normalization and ridge 0.02 on average Bernoulli loss: positions and lengths; positions, lengths, lexical overlap and token form; those lexical controls plus contextual features. Also report position-only ranking and untrained content-overlap ranking. Sharing the sentence projections is permitted for both directions. A second score adds the reverse-task direction as a trainable scalar prior estimated without the forward-task labels as a source of transferable relation information. Use a fixed training-only projection and compare the two Qwen fits. The primary is the contextual forward-event classifier without the reverse-task prior.

For the primary comparison fix a late sentence in a held-out story and ask which earlier action it helped explain. Include comparable pairs of earlier actions asked about in the release where at least two of three workers selected the late sentence for one action and no worker selected it for the other. Ties receive half credit. Average first within story and then across stories. Report the primary accuracy and a paired difference from the learned lexical comparator with a 10,000-story percentile bootstrap. The same procedure for earlier evidence supporting a later action is a secondary endpoint. Report mean grouped held-out vote log loss and precision–recall curves for each direction using every eligible annotated pair. Also report localization of a later majority support sentence among candidate later sentences for a fixed earlier action, along with the distance baseline. A directional gain requires the lower bound on the paired primary difference to exceed zero.

Screening **training only** found 320 majority later-to-earlier links among 294 stories, with only two stories exhibiting two majority later clues selectively paired to two earlier events. Two simultaneous independent resolutions in the same story will be reported descriptively where present. The primary within-cue comparison needs only one selective support and a rival action. No test counts or assignments were inspected when choosing these conditions. A sparsely observed endpoint will retain its prespecified comparison and uncertainty. This is a new article-only study for CogLLM.
