# What did readers actually expect to come next?

Human cloze participants supply a word after seeing an exact prefix. The historical project reduced that genuine response distribution to a binary indicator of whether a response matched the author’s continuation. Recover the original alternative counts, use their multinomial likelihood at each measured prefix, and test the learned distribution in an independent laboratory and on new passages. The independent Provo source comprises short news, popular science and fiction paragraphs. Its previously published whole-word norming results and earlier language-model comparisons are known. Earlier work measured poorly calibrated full distributions and better cloze prediction with larger models. Our test asks whether a small source-trained, normalized, single-forward readout can improve held cross-collection human choice log score beyond a model with calibrated lexical frequency and native surprisal. No external Provo response count, empirical distribution or performance statistic has been examined before this registration. Its materials, headers and word-number alignment were inspected to ensure genuine exposures are available. The source UCL target hit counts and omission rate were previously audited; its full alternative distributions have not been modeled. Fix an English response alphabet independently of the measured readers. Take the 8,192 most frequent distinct, lowercase alphabetic English spellings with optional internal apostrophes supplied by wordfreq 3.1.1, keeping the published frequency ordering. Normalize curly apostrophes and case for responses, and map all reportable responses outside the list to one explicit “other” outcome. Report raw and vocabulary-covered decisions, omissions, representable words, and held unseen spellings. Do not impute unobserved answers. The external word–prefix mapping keeps original white-space word indices up to the first unverified lexeme in an article. Verify each word against the article, allowing the source’s transparent contractions and punctuation removal. Give a whole-article subset with no alignment interruption. The model sees the authentic full article once; its estimate before word i uses only the final native state immediately preceding that whole word, including its original prefix punctuation. Independent suffix-removal tests must reproduce earlier predictions. No question, reader answer or held choices are supplied to the encoder.

The normalized reference uses native first-subword probability for each spelling and splits the mass of shared first subwords using fixed relative background word frequency. The complement of the retained first-token support is assigned to the other category. The calibrated baseline receives source-trained logits for native evidence, log external English frequency and an other-category offset. A context-independent multinomial comparator estimates smoothed English source response frequencies, holding out each sentence’s labels. The contextual head keeps the calibrated predictors and adds a low-rank bilinear correction between sixteen preceding-state and sixteen static candidate coordinates. The latter use only Qwen’s frozen input embeddings of complete dictionary spellings. A source-trained candidate bias is represented by the same sixteen geometric coordinates and is also present in the matched calibrated baseline, eliminating an artificial lexical-prior advantage. Source transformations use source training prefixes only. Optimize the exact aggregate multinomial log likelihood (no candidate subsampling), normalize each participant-response loss per observed choice, and apply a fixed ridge penalty to correction coefficients, scaled to one effective observation per parameter. Convergence requires an independent gradient residual check. Use five original English sentence folds, a context-independent reference, raw first-piece mapping and calibrated comparator. Then freeze the full-English coefficients and all forecast files before opening the Provo outcomes. Both corpora must meet the same character-level prefix and tokenizer geometry requirements.

Primary external outcome is true independent response negative log likelihood, retaining all observable answers in the primary 8,193-category space. Quantify uncertainty over whole Provo passages and report accuracy of the most frequently selected response at each position, error in the finite-sample probability that two independent responses disagree, and the probability of supplying the actual printed word within the vocabulary. When a printed word is outside the fixed dictionary its probability cannot be distinguished from other unlisted responses and is omitted only from this last endpoint. Give a separate calibrated-surface-versus-context effect. A prediction of disagreement in a cloze completion task describes disagreement over productions. It does not directly measure an individual reader’s comprehension, fatigue or unresolved questions.
