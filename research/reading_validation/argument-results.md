# Which earlier claim is a writer supporting?

Supervision on human-annotated argument structure generalized to independently collected student essays. When a sentence had an annotated backward link, the one-pass native readout selected the correct earlier sentence at **66.0%** of opportunities in Darmstadt versus **59.9%** for the trained text-form and recurrence control and **46.6%** for the nearest earlier sentence. The paired improvement over form was 6.0 points [3.5, 8.6]. The endpoint comprised 2,253 backward-link opportunities in 381 essays. Native attention and context helped on both supports (65.9%) and attacks (60.2% across 137 annotated opportunities). These were relations the experts identified in the text. Whether a reader had to stop at that sentence was not observed. [Original structural study](https://aclanthology.org/J17-3005/) · [Source and estimation](argument-estimator.md) · [Models, denominators and paired differences](../results/reading-validation-argument/scores-native.json)

The primary within-source evaluation reserved 480 essays on four writing prompts. On 2,079 linked sentences the native model chose the earlier parent 46.2% of the time, versus 43.7% for text form (increase 2.4 points [0.1, 4.8]). Gains were observed in three of the four prompts. In a whole unmarked essay, the model must also decide whether a link exists. Across 6,713 external sentence opportunities its development threshold emitted 1,415 links; 412 matched the current sentence and its own earlier source (2,253 possible, pooled F1 .225 versus .178 for form). The native ranking improved while its probability of a link became overconfident in the external domain: the occurrence log loss was .760 versus .692. An interface which shows a likely source after a writer chooses a sentence has a different operating point from an automatic explanation warning. ## Do joint losses improve the editorial judgment?

A single causal pass supplied all models with the same context. We fitted a categorical distribution over earlier sentences, a Bernoulli probability of a backward link, and an ordered likelihood for each human effectiveness rating. In the joint model, integrating over the possible earlier claim changed the distribution of effectiveness grades. Gradients from observed grades could therefore update the source pointer. A comparator kept the same conditional effectiveness model while blocking those gradients. The exact analytic gradients agreed with numerical differences within 4×10⁻¹², and each optimizer reached a maximum gradient below 10⁻⁷. Development-calibrated shared fractions were .39 for source choice, .50 for link presence and .47 for effectiveness. On independent essays, full joint fitting worsened source-choice log loss by .0020 [.0004, .0037] and occurrence loss by .0075 [.0069, .0080] relative to the blocked-gradient control. Source-side task relatedness alone did not establish useful transfer. [Registered joint observation model](argument-joint-protocol.md) · [Joint held estimates](../results/reading-validation-argument/scores-joint-native.json)

On 3,895 held source effectiveness judgments, native information worsened ordinal log loss from .597 to .632. Applying this score to a different question, whether there is enough evidence for a conclusion, produced an AUC of .523 in 1,022 published judgments, versus .658 for form. Among 433 paragraphs rated by three people with available individual votes, AUC for the 1,299 original votes was .480 versus .662. In 18 of the 433 texts the published binary label differed from the majority of the three released votes. All 433 paragraph fingerprints agreed across releases. We therefore preserve the published label, vote majority and original choices as three separate outcomes. These findings motivate direct sufficiency supervision and independent tests of how experts judge the adequacy of an explanation.
