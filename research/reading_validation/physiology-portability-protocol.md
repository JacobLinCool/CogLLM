# Do three small causal backbones predict the same human word response?

The frozen Qwen, LFM and Gemma backbones have previously been compared on narrative annotations. Existing Qwen studies established correspondence with some word-level human outcomes and limited gains over calibrated surprisal. Preregister a matched three-backbone comparison on independently acquired human electrophysiology, cloze choices and expectability ratings. All nine-laboratory EEG forecasts and outcomes remain unopened. Published group-level directions in that replication study are known. This is a prospective cross-architecture generalization test on historically examined English and Spanish studies and a new laboratory replication.

Use Qwen3.5-2B, LFM3-1.7B-Base and Gemma 4 E2B with the project’s pinned revisions and official text-model normalization. Feed every complete original text once. Collect the middle and final native states at every printed-word end and sum the preceding native next-token negative log probabilities across that word. Confirm each native vocabulary projection against its official causal language-model forward; for Gemma this includes the final logit soft cap. Common printed-word support requires the tokenizer to give exactly the same visible prefix independently at every boundary in each of the three architectures. The acquisition texts include the English word norms and the original spelling variants in the EEG experiment, the Spanish word-resolution study, and all 160 original sentences of the nine-laboratory article/noun experiment. Store all raw features per text in FP16 and verify stable earlier states on suffix removal. No reader labels, reserved source-span markers or individual participant data enter the accelerator.

Fit identical readout structures separately per backbone. The compact representation uses five print features, native word surprisal and 24 training-only principal components of complete midpoint and terminal states at that word. Whiten and center all state channels using training words; prevent a large-variance individual component from determining cross-model comparisons. Freeze the standardised projection and all three readouts prior to their independent Spanish and multi-laboratory evaluation. Train with exactly the same existing English source folds, beta-binomial cloze counts, sampling-aware Gaussian ratings and Gaussian EEG for all three. Use the same empirical full-covariance method and equally determined surface-plus-surprisal comparator. Use identical original held words, outcome directions and mask per study. Primary quantitative endpoints are physiological rank correlation on the English held sentences, transported rank correlation on 8,640 Spanish word contexts and predicted noun-versus-article contrast on 80 independent English item families. Secondary analysis measures cloze/rating accuracy, paired calibration, and explicit cross-model differences. For each neural claim give the matched word-surprisal and trained surface comparator; show paired uncertainty over source sentences or lexical families. Learned signal beyond trained surface must be supported by its own paired interval. Multi-laboratory location ordering also requires comparable discrimination across stimulus families before supporting a mechanistic claim. Estimate performance of the same response heads under all three genuine architectures, with no dependence on target outcomes for representation construction or model selection.
