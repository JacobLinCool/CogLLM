# Teaching the population readout how the same printed word changes with context

The previous model improved likelihood across all word positions. For pairs of identical printed words, its advantage over calibrated lexical and surprisal cues was small or negative. Some historical comparisons evaluated the members of a pair using different cross-validation fits. This follow-up holds both sentences out of the same fit for every evaluated pair, learns directly from the human change across contexts, and tests transport to authentic Spanish word contrasts. The positive average Spanish N400 effect, weak item-wise agreement and previous English within-type analyses are known. New model coefficients and scores remain unobserved. We do not use individual English responses or Spanish outcomes to set hyperparameters.

Use the earlier Qwen population head’s native causal word covariates and frozen bottleneck. For each originally displayed case-sensitive word, pair distinct source sentences. Train the readout on feature differences between matching words, with each word type having equal total influence. Positive means greater negativity, a larger chance of omitting the expected word, or higher rated unpredictability. For EEG and ratings regress their standardized human mean differences against the same feature differences. The Gaussian variance is two residual units for EEG and two residual units plus the reported sampling variances of both rating means for rating. For cloze condition on the total number of observed misses in both contexts, using every nonempty human completion. The probability of allocating k misses to the first context is proportional to binomial(n₁,k) binomial(n₂,K−k) exp(k·Δxβ), with the observed K and all count-feasible k. The lexical intercept cancels from that likelihood. Multiply it by the within-type pair weights, omit contrasts for which only one allocation is possible, estimate the unique ridge optimum with precision 1/0.35² on standardized slopes, and verify its gradient. Correlated pairs share earlier readers and sentence responses. The objective is a composite conditional likelihood; its nominal curvature is a convergence check only. The held uncertainty resamples whole lexical types and source sentences. The estimator yields a change in a defined observed quantity, without assigning an absolute human load value to a word.

Hold out both sentences together. For the five original sentence folds, the 15 distinct excluded-fold sets leave either one fold or two folds out; fit each once and score only pairs with that exact membership. New conditional fits and matched conventional full-covariance fits see identical source rows, input features and six-surface-cue or contextual head capacity. Source PCA and all outcome variances use the available source rows in that fit. Report equal-type concordance and its paired interval across crossing sentence and lexical type clusters for each endpoint. Compare conditional contextual versus conventional contextual, the conditional contextual versus conditional calibrated-surface model, and both contextual models versus the conventional surface model. A word-only constant scores 0.5. Report exact-index pairs as a sensitivity. Preserve the full English unconditioned experiment as a separate observation.

Freeze the full-English conditional coefficients before scoring external physiological contrasts. On the preregistered 112 Spanish pseudoword families, compare the less-informative versus informative continuation at the identical category noun at position 18, with the earlier identical seventh word as a control. Compare group direction, family discrimination and family/reader variation against the same source-only conventional fits, the fitted surface-plus-surprisal model and raw whole-word surprisal. Place any transfer in its known context: the published Spanish experiment contains only 43 eligible EEG readers, and previous random reader splits found near-zero agreement on the ranking of the 112 noisy item differences. This is a follow-up on known stimuli, not an independently new human cohort.
