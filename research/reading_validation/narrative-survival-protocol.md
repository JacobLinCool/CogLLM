# Follow an open question across intervening narrative lines

An issue can still be open after the passage stops talking about it. A candidate explanation can also answer the most recent competitor by coincidence. On four previously studied German stories a direct pair scorer fell short of a recency rule (67.6% versus 70.8%). Here, fit a survival distribution for each prospective question origin using its whole period at risk. In training only, represent an annotator's actual wording of the question as an auxiliary target for the line that raised it and the line resolving it. A held story supplies only its prose. The existing results, including the proximity advantage, are known. This is a mechanism-driven follow-up rather than a new independent replication. [Source annotations](https://osf.io/m524v/) · [Original four-story test](narrative-course-results.md)

Use the same source lines, stored workbook hashes and study-level folds. A question's origin is its first definitely open line. For each recorded open spell, the date of resolution lies after the last definitely open line and no later than the next definite absence. Weight the probability mass of that complete interval. Treat spells reaching the document end as right censored; do not force a resolution at a paragraph or story boundary. Report any reopening after a definite absence and distinguish this accounting from the earlier first-origin summaries. Document the number of left-uncertain origins, absent closures and intervals spanning more than one line. Model the expected number of openings per line as a Poisson mean. Two questions raised on the same line can contribute two openings. A survival probability multiplies each possible earlier origin's estimated birth rate. Summing these terms yields a latent count on the scale of the study's questions, computable at each line without access to a preceding true label.

Model duration in log age, with additional prespecified piecewise slopes at ages 2, 8 and 32 lines. Use the same duration and observation model for the content-free control. Lexical and native models add visible features and direct affinities between the incoming line and the text at the opening. Represent the question authors' German wording by a fixed signed word and character n-gram hash. Learn shrinkage regressions from the opening line and the resolving line to this supervision in source stories only; use their predicted semantic match as an extra hazard feature. Center scale and fit these regressions on training material. When fitting a hazard on three source stories, cross-fit the question-wording regression for each source story on the remaining two. Fit its final two projections on the three source stories for use on the held fourth story. At no point can the held story's question wording, true active set, question count or an externally provided candidate join the article representation. Score the latent count and all candidate source lines in one left-to-right article pass. The fixed teacher target is calculated locally; no human question or reading response is uploaded for language-model inference.

Compare article-only estimates to (a) most recent open line for the pair test, (b) elapsed reading position for the count test, (c) the same source-fitted lifetime model with duration alone, and (d) a duration-and-lexical lifetime model with the same training exposures. Include the frozen native lifetime model without the question-wording loss; this identifies the contribution of the auxiliary source descriptions. For every endpoint publish each story result with equal-story summaries. The primary identity outcome is the probability of choosing the question that actually closes over a distinct question that remains open on the very same line. Also score the more demanding joint task without a known closing line: correctly link both the closing line and its original opening among every past line and every current line. Score the relative earlier-source belief where a closure is known, the proper interval/censoring log loss, interval count error, stock direction and the prevalence of exact opening/closing pairs. Four original stories determine the scope of all estimates.
