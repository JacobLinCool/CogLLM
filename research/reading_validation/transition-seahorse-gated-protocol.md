# Sharing evidence across a professional reader's first three summary judgments

The first SEAHORSE question asks whether a summary is comprehensible. For a reader who confirms comprehension, the next two questions ask separately whether the text is free of unnecessary repetition and whether it is grammatical. These three responses concern the same displayed summary. The original article first becomes visible at question 4. Use the original binary answers as three separate negative-response probabilities. The estimands are P(Q1=No | text), P(Q2=No | Q1=Yes, text), and P(Q3=No | Q1=Yes, text). Preserve all source skips and treat Unsure as indeterminate. Q2 and Q3 provide two marginal conditional likelihood contributions when present. Their score sum is a composite proper score. It does not require assuming independence of those two answers from the same judge. [Reader instructions and interface, §2.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.584.pdf)

We have already scored Q1 on these official test articles. The Q2 and Q3 held responses have not been examined in this project. This secondary analysis uses the same preexisting text exposures, overlapping-source exclusions and fixed 15-feature or 187-feature measurements. Duplicate-file judgments cannot enter training, tuning or testing here. Only English original train and validation responses may determine coefficients. Save forecast keys, probabilities, source task similarities, convergence and weights before opening the held Q2/Q3 grades. Include the coverage and participants' original answer counts, counting every systematically unasked question explicitly.

Compare two native fits and their two matched form fits. The independent fit learns separate standardized ridge logistic models with the same source article weights, fixed penalty .002 and validation-only intercept/slope calibration as the earlier Q1 observer. The adaptive fit learns a positive definite covariance across the three sets of feature slopes. Before fitting it, divide source articles into two deterministic groups by hash. Independently estimate each task's slopes in each half with the same ridge penalty. For each task, use the cosine between its two fitted slope vectors, clipped to [0,1], to measure reproducibility. Estimate task pattern similarity by the cosine Gram matrix of the mean half-vectors. For off-diagonal elements, multiply this similarity by the geometric mean of the two tasks' reproducibilities. Retain unit diagonal and apply a fixed 0.001 diagonal loading. This estimator remains positive definite, allows positive or negative sharing and defaults toward independent slopes for poorly reproduced source signals. With B the feature-by-task coefficient matrix and R the estimated covariance, minimize the sum of the three observed, article-equal binary log losses and (0.002/2)trace(BR⁻¹Bᵀ). Intercepts are free. A different source-fitted intercept and nonnegative slope for each question provides validation calibration. The covariance uses no validation or test grades. Require objective improvement and maximum first-order residual at most 2×10⁻⁶.

The primary comparison is the paired difference between adaptive and independent native observed-response log loss per presented summary, summing only truly observed binary answers. A summary with a No answer to Q1 contributes only that binary score. A Yes answer can contribute up to three scores according to availability. Report that contrast with a 95% original-article bootstrap interval. Report AUROC, negative-class average precision, absolute and per-task paired negative log loss. For the three individual native adaptive-versus-separate differences, also give simultaneous Bonferroni-adjusted 95% intervals. Compare the native measurements with matching document form and word predictability. The primary score speaks to the observed response instrument as a whole. A gain for one question alone does not establish a general benefit for all human responses. This experimental observer remains distinct from the released, independently evaluated Q1 writer head.
