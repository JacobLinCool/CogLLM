# Can other reading studies tell us how much a new outcome should borrow?

The frozen reader has two trained observation experts per endpoint. One learns state projections shared across studies and one learns a source-private projection. Each retains its original count, timing, graded, choice, event or paired-response likelihood. Previous aggregate outcomes for these two experts and per-endpoint coefficients are public in this project. The following new coefficient transfer and posterior updates, uncertainty analysis, and comparisons are specified before evaluating either on review rows. All analyses are retrospective within the existing supervised archive. No reserve is newly opened.

For endpoint e and material family f, let L_ei(a) be the negative log likelihood of its actual response or published sufficient statistics under the normalized geometric pool of the two predictive distributions. The coefficient a in [0,1] weights the shared expert. Gaussian distributions pool in natural-parameter space, event models pool across their original normalized categories, and paired measurements retain their covariance. Define calibration loss C_e(a) by summing, over observed material families, total row loss within each family divided by its actual observed number of trials. This gives every calibration family one unit of information. Treat exp(−C_e) as generalized evidence based on the study's own loss. Rows with grouped counts and within-group sums of squares are scored in their original likelihood. A family mean loss or a study mean loss never becomes an individual observation.

For each held-out source s, estimate a Beta(A,B) coefficient distribution from the CALIBRATE families of every *other* source. A contributing source has total mass one; endpoints with calibration support split that mass equally within source. Minimize the negative sum of the endpoint log integrals of exp(−C_e) under the Beta density, plus squared distance of log A and log B from log 2 divided by twice 1.5² per shape. Restrict A and B to [1,32], allowing uniform and monotone priors and excluding divergent endpoint densities. Integrate on an outcome-independent 97-point Gauss–Legendre grid. Recheck the coefficient and marginal evidence on a separate 193-point grid. Use the finer grid for all methods only if a coefficient changes by more than .0005 or a per-source objective by more than .005; report the numerical decision. Verified analytic gradients, several prespecified numerical optimizer starts, and projected-gradient checks establish convergence. Log-integral normalization is exact on the finite grid. The prior contributes one log-density term to the source-level fit, independent of the number of endpoints in that source.

Compare the transported no-target-calibration coefficient A/(A+B) with the posterior mean after multiplying this held-study-excluded prior by exp(−C_e) for that target alone. An unsupported endpoint receives its transported prior mean. Use a single coefficient in the original normalized predictive distribution. Distinguish this complete-source exclusion for *choosing the coefficient hyperprior* from the earlier fit of shared and private experts, whose supervised study labels remain part of their input training. An entirely unseen study without a trained observation link cannot be served by these two frozen experts.

Reconstruct fixed private (0), shared (1), midpoint (0.5), the published per-endpoint Beta(2,2) penalized mode, and the independent Beta(2,2) posterior mean on identical material. Fit separately for both historical and fresh source-family partitions and seeds 1729 and 2718. Freeze coefficients and calibration receipts before computing new evaluation losses. Preserve the distinct historical and fresh outcomes. The primary aggregate gives each source equal weight, each available endpoint equal weight within source, and the four split/seed analyses equal weight within source. The two seeds see the same people and are never resampled as independent studies. The fresh evaluation families originate from the original training pool and are separate from original development families, while the reconstructed experts reuse other historical data for fitting.

Three prespecified directional comparisons cover target-supported hierarchical coefficients versus (i) the published independently regularized coefficients and (ii) the fixed midpoint, and transported coefficients without target calibration versus (iii) the fixed midpoint. Outcome is the decrease in held negative log likelihood per actual observed trial. Use 10,000 whole-source bootstrap samples for marginal and three-comparison simultaneous intervals, and 100,000 source-level paired sign flips with Bonferroni control as a sensitivity under sign symmetry. Report unadjusted comparisons with the private and completely shared references as well. For each source show the number of eligible endpoints, source families, both material splits, both seeds, point losses and acquisition of calibration support. Give two-seed source-level uncertainty using a paired bootstrap over the original held material families within each split, preserving endpoint dependence, response denominators and trial weights. A one-family source supplies no material-resampling interval. Results apply to the studies represented here.
