# Can behavioral word responses improve physiology prediction with ten training sentences?

This internal missing-measurement experiment follows an inconclusive incremental likelihood comparison between empirical shared and diagonal priors under full measurement. The earlier full-data fold results for this corpus are known. The present sparse-fitting results have not been generated. Reserve all five existing sentence-level test folds. In each training split select exactly ten original training sentences by ascending SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string “CogLLM EEG small assay ” and the original sentence identifier. This choice never uses EEG, text difficulty or sentence length. The ten sentences are the only training examples with an available EEG endpoint. All eligible original training-sentence cloze choices and expectability ratings remain observed. A held sentence contributes no responses to any part of training.

Retain the established training-only Qwen representation, the exact Gaussian, beta-binomial and precision-weighted Gaussian observation laws, and the empirical covariance hyperparameters from the complete-data study. Absence of a physiological measurement removes its entire likelihood and Fisher-information block. Estimate physiological centering and scaling only from the sparse observed subset. Compare the learned full covariance with empirical diagonal and fixed diagonal priors; all three use the identical ten EEG calibration sentences. The primary comparison is the contextual full-versus-empirical-diagonal difference in predictive log density for all held English EEG words. Report word-level rank correlation, RMSE and sentence-cluster uncertainty for the primary effect. Report the behavioral endpoints and a fitted surface-plus-surprisal readout as secondary controls. A score gain on the full sample is insufficient to claim beneficial sharing if the paired clustered interval includes zero. Results apply to this single predetermined acquisition schedule.
