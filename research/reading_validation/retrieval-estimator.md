# Estimating source relevance and inconsistency from the visible prefix

The source model has two fitted linear maps. The first uses a conditional softmax over all completed earlier sentences. Its loss is the negative log probability of the constructed relevant premise, averaged over source variants with an actual choice. The second receives the correct earlier sentence during source training. Its binary and matched ranking losses distinguish contradiction from neutral and entailment under changes to the earlier text. Training-only standardization and a ridge of 0.02 on the average losses regularize both maps. At reading time the source of a conflict is unknown. Marginalize the conditional probability of contradiction over the model's distribution of earlier sentences. Return the most likely source separately. No model receives the index of the manipulated premise at inference.

Text-form features include the visible distance between sentences, previously read length, overlap and changes in negation and numbers. Semantic features add normalized native attention to the earlier sentence, cosine similarity of currently visible words to it, middle- and final-layer running sentence representations, their coordinatewise products and distances, and a fixed external contradiction projection. Each word compares only with complete earlier sentences and the portion of its own sentence already seen. The same head applies at each word. The training readout is supervised at completed NLI hypotheses, so generalization within a sentence must be checked separately. For the human first-report calibration use the marginal log odds at the sentence end, its change, its within-sentence maximum and the concentration of the inferred source distribution. Surprisal, from the original tied vocabulary projection, enters as a named comparator. No target scores choose the weights.

Weight reports within a news prompt, then prompts equally; use 10,000 paired prompt bootstrap samples. The location of the marked wording is provided for this particular evaluation.
