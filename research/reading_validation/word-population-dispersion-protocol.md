# Can supervision on changes improve a one-pass distribution of readers' next words?

The UCL-trained observer supplies a normalized distribution over 8,192 common spellings plus one residual category. Its measured external directional accuracy was similar to the backbone's. A distribution can put its most likely word in the right place while assigning the wrong spread to alternative responses. Fit one shared, four-feature readout for the local spread of that distribution. It mixes a softened, original and sharpened version of the population observer, using exponents 2/3, 1 and 3/2. A convex mixture keeps every forecast normalized and preserves its rank order. The gate receives an intercept, top category probability, residual-category probability and normalized Shannon entropy. The same gate and single native pass yield the next-choice likelihood and the derived curve. There is no inferred personal difficulty unit. [Original word responses](cloze-population-protocol.md)

All temperature-component probabilities used to fit the gate come from out-of-fold UCL sentences. No UCL sentence supplies predictions from a base expert trained on that sentence. The fixed all-source expert supplies predictions for every external Provo paragraph. Study A fits the gate with the log likelihood of the actual words readers supplied. Study B retains exactly that term and adds a binary conditional scoring rule for consecutive observed prefixes. At each prefix estimate D, the chance that two distinct sampled responses differ, using unbiased counts of response pairs. Across two consecutive prefixes compare two independently sampled response pairs. Among discordant comparisons, the probability that the later pair disagrees is proportional to D_later times (1 minus D_earlier). Score this identifiable change in collision odds with the forecast for the same event. Weight increments by their observed discordant probability, normalize within each task and give the two task averages equal weight. Repeated use of individual responses makes this a composite fitting criterion. Unknown respondent identity at a prefix never supplies a personal update. Use a fixed quadratic coefficient penalty of one unit divided by source prefixes, anchored at a gate with 80% original weight. Fit all structural coefficients jointly. Check gradients directly and report numerical stationarity. No architecture or weight is selected using external scores.

Before grading, write the source coefficients and digest of the external probability forecasts. The existing external results are known. Reuse every denominator-verified Provo prefix and its adjacent updates for a paired follow-up. Primary measures are mean absolute error of the change and actual word-choice log loss per observed elicitation. Give the latter only when the released response denominator is verified. Secondary measures are direction of nonzero changes and the level error. Compare the coupled fit with the unchanged population observer and the gate trained on choices alone. Use common paragraph bootstrap samples and simultaneous intervals over the four primary contrasts. Also show the fixed native baseline. The test estimates transfer to this previously examined corpus, conditional on a source-only design. A new reader cohort would be needed for independent confirmation of an improvement.
