# Informative context helped people, but the model had limited evidence about who benefited

At the identical unfamiliar noun, 44 Dutch readers translated it correctly on 96.3% of 1,486 first-practice trials when they had seen the diagnostic sentence first. Accuracy was 68.2% on 1,476 trials when that sentence appeared after the submitted response and 67.3% on 1,482 retrieval-only trials. The same-noun mean gain over the two uninformative-at-response conditions was 28.6 percentage points, with a crossed reader/word bootstrap interval of 22.5–34.9. In a separate group of 45 readers, the corresponding gain was 20.2 points (14.5–26.5) across 4,137 scored trials and 92 matched nouns. The first cohort used concrete matched carriers from the second experiment's materials because its own retrieval scripts sometimes contained inflection placeholders. In a single causal pass over each original sentence, Qwen assigned more probability to the *specific Dutch meaning* before the Swahili noun was visible. Among 101 eligible nouns, the first native token of the correct meaning ranked first on 51.5% of informative prefixes versus 6.9% of sparse prefixes and 5.0% after borrowing a similar-length clue from a different noun. Mean gain in conditional log probability was 2.476 nats against sparse context (word-cluster 95% interval 2.046–2.928) and 2.468 against the wrong context (2.057–2.862). There were 98 distinct onset classes for the 102 Dutch meanings and 93 scored words with unambiguous onsets. One sentence started with its Swahili noun and had no pre-noun native token to score. The absent difference between the sparse and late-cue prefix verifies that future information did not enter either forecast. Intervals resample both readers and words. In Experiment 2, participants rated their knowledge of each noun before practice. A model using that rating, practice condition and their interaction achieved 0.43768 nats on held-out words and people. Adding Qwen's correct-meaning score yielded 0.43932 nats; the gain was −0.00164 [−0.00341, −0.00001]. These results distinguish reproducing the average experimental manipulation from predicting which words or learners benefit most. The data establish when supportive context was available relative to a submitted response. They include no repeated within-trial judgment after the late clue, so its exact mental resolution token is unidentified.
